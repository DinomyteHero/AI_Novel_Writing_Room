# Colonel Maro Tess

## Role
Primary antagonist (operational); architect of the Vigil's field operations; knowing One Sith asset

## Want
Contain exposure, preserve useful assets, continue the larger operation.

## Need
Prove control is the only morality that survives crisis.

## Ghost
Former senior GAG operations officer. During the war, oversaw black-channel logistics and security operations that moved detainees, sealed cargo, evidence, classified personnel, Jacen-related material, and off-book transfers through emergency routes. Knew what the network was truly for. Saw Talon Veyr clearly as a young field agent — field instinct, movement, exits, timing, people-reading — and intended to bring him back to field duty after the Glasshook discipline taught him to see the whole operation rather than only the people in front of him. The war collapsed before that plan could complete. He believes Caedus was not wrong to seek control; he believes Caedus failed because he was too visible, rushed, and emotionally compromised. Post-war, the Vigil's project was the natural continuation.

## Arc
steadfast

## Voice
Plain, calm, operational. No villain poetry.

Good Tess: “Korda can carry the public version.” “He’s dead.” “That helps.” “Iresh failed.” “Completely?” “No.” “Skywalker moved as trained.” “Do not punish panic. Use it or remove it.” “Talon always did understand exits.” “You saved the people in front of you. You lost the people I needed you to think about.” Avoid Tess saying: “History belongs to those who manage its losses.” Better: “The report needs one responsible name. Korda’s will do.”

Almost none. If present, it is dry, administrative, and lethal.

Plain, measured, difficult to provoke. Does not posture. Does not explain more than required.

Tess should be terrifying because he is normal. Failure does not embarrass him; it gives him new categories.
