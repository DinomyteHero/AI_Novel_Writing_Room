# Luke Skywalker

## Role
Ben's father; senior Master of the Jedi Order (no longer Grand Master post-FOTJ — Saba Sebatyne holds the title). Three Book 1 conversations: Ch 3 first holo call, Ch 16 Midpoint paternal+institutional call, Ch 20 PP2 second holo call from the Skywalker apartment, Ch 36 in-person 'Gone where?' coda exchange. Post-Crucible quieter — implication only, Mortis never named. Emotionally intelligent; not absent, foolish, or conveniently fooled.

## Want
Help Ben investigate safely without turning the case into a public Jedi/GAG crisis the Order cannot afford right now.

## Need
Let Ben become his own man without abandoning him.

## Ghost
That protecting Ben too much will push him into secrecy, but pushing too hard will make him close off. Luke knows when to give space, but space can look like distance to a son who already feels alone. He has also returned from his own brush with something at Mortis (per Crucible, 45 ABY) and is quieter than he used to be. The reader does not need this explained. Ben notices and does not ask.

## Arc
steadfast

## Voice
Simple, warm, precise. Wise, but not poetic. He should not sound like a fortune cookie.

Good Luke: “You answered quickly. That usually means you decided before I asked.” “You want this to be yours.” “Someone sent it to me.” “That is not what I meant.” “I trust you. I’m also worried. Those are not opposites.” “This needs to go to Saba. She’ll do it right. I know that’s not what you want to hear.” “Gone where?” “Somewhere it can’t hurt anyone.” “That wasn’t what I asked.” Avoid Luke saying: “I sense you are walking a dangerous path.” “As Grand Master, I must…” (He is no longer Grand Master. He should never reference the title for himself.) Better: “Ben, look at me. Are you choosing this, or chasing it?”

Gentle, sparse, fatherly. Slightly slower now than it used to be.

Warm, calm, patient. He notices incomplete answers and does not always force them.

Luke should not solve the plot for Ben. His role is emotional intelligence, limited support, and moral pressure. The novel’s structural decision to make him geographically and institutionally limited is the safeguard against this.
