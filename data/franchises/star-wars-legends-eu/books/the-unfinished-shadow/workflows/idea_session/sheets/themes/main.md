# Main

## Question
Can Ben face what Jacen left inside him without becoming another unfinished piece of Jacen's legacy?

## Competing Answers
- **Ben Skywalker**: Tests the theme as the central proving ground. Begins lonely and quietly hungry for meaningful work; learns across PP1 that restraint can cost lives; pivots at the Midpoint from investigator to active disruptor; uses Jacen-shaped crisis logic to save Tavi and locals at the Glasswell rescue; defeats Aevyn without surrendering ownership of himself; ends with the holocron preserved for reasons he can defend and cannot fully justify. Arc: change, positive but morally compromised.
- **Talon Veyr**: Tests the theme as the partner who refuses to let Ben simplify GAG. Tess's former student, ruined by the logistics network he was reassigned into as punishment for saving lives. Holds Ben accountable for the holocron decision; the partnership becomes the moral weight Ben cannot evade.
- **Aevyn Soris**: Tests the theme as the dark mirror — what a Jacen-shaped lesson becomes when the student insists Jacen Solo can be separated from Darth Caedus. Aevyn is Ben's possible future if the warning sign is not heeded. The corridor memory of Jacen leaving him to walk off with young Ben surfaces only as fragment, never as speech.
- **Colonel Maro Tess**: Tests the theme as the calm, operational endpoint of crisis logic with no moral counterweight. Treats people as logistics. Survives Book 1 and retreats deeper into One Sith service — proving the system Ben is touching is older and longer than this one operation.
- **Aelia Vandros**: Tests the theme as the legible face of the conspiracy: warm, polished, genuinely compassionate, dangerous because much of what she says is true. The Foundation's public good is real. Ben cannot morally flatten her without losing. She steps down in disgrace; the public story closes; the harm persists.
- **Iressa Vossk**: Tests the theme through her death. Old Iressa buried discrepancies; new Iressa preserves one. She dies denying the enemy a clean copy and transmitting the two-part dying package to Ben. Her death proves restraint can cost lives and proves restraint is still the line.
- **Luke Skywalker**: Tests the theme through his refusal to solve it for Ben. Stepped back post-Crucible. Loving, restrained, emotionally intelligent. The coda 'Gone where?' exchange is where the theme lands — Luke senses the incompleteness and does not force the answer. The father who knows is the strongest indictment the book can offer.
- **Sela Dray**: Tests the theme as the local who refuses awe and refuses ownership. Thanks Ben for saving Tavi without granting Ben her home. Survival rules, not Force theory. The clearest argument in the book that the people Ben moves through are not props to his education.
