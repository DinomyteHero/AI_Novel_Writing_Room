The Force had been off-key for three months. Ben Skywalker was the only person in the Temple who couldn't stop hearing the discord.

He sat cross-legged on the meditation chamber floor, palms flat against cool stone. He tried again. The standard centering technique—breath, presence, release—was the first lesson for any initiate. He had practiced it since he was seven. He could do it in his sleep. He had done it in the back of a gunship with bolt-fire rattling the hull, in a detention cell on Coruscant’s lower levels, and on the floor of a Sith tomb while his cousin transformed into a stranger somewhere overhead.

The breath came. The presence followed. The release never arrived.

Instead, he found the hollow.

It sat in the same place it always did—southwest in his Force-sense. The sensation was meaningless, as the Force lacked cardinal directions, yet it persisted with the specificity of a compass needle. It wasn't dark. He had felt darkness often enough to know its texture: weight, heat, aggressive pressure. This was the opposite. This was the sensation of pressing a thumb against a load-bearing wall and feeling the durasteel give way.

He opened his eyes.

The chamber was silent. Carved stone, no windows, a single amber light strip along the baseboard. The Temple had been rebuilt twice in his lifetime, yet this room still smelled of recycled air—cool, mineral, and sterile. Outside these walls, the Temple hummed. Padawans ran through forms in the training rooms above. Two Knights walked the corridor, their presences easy and warm. Master Cilghal worked in her medical bay, her focus sharp and clinical.

None of them heard the noise.

Ben pressed his palms harder against the stone. A spike of heat moved through his chest. If he was the only one hearing it, his range had expanded beyond Council records, or he was simply broken enough to mistake static for a signal. The distinction mattered.

Three months ago, the wrongness was a faint hum he could lose in a crowd. Now it was a wire pulled past its tolerance. He woke at odd hours with a headache lodged behind his left eye—a dull throb that ignored medicine and vanished by dawn. His hands had developed a tremor. He hid it by burying them in his pockets or gripping a cup of caf. Four hours of sleep. The other four were spent in rooms like this, trying to determine if the galaxy was fracturing or if he was.

The quality of the sensation bothered him most.

He had spent weeks cataloging his memories like an investigator handling evidence. During the GAG years, the Force had carried the resonance of moral compromise. With Jacen, it had been a deliberate distortion, like a precision instrument being used as a club. Abeloth had been chaos—the Force screaming in frequencies it wasn't meant to carry.

This was different. It was structural.

The other disruptions had been events—wills imposing themselves on reality. This was happening to the shape of the Force itself. It had geometry: load paths, stress distributions, and convergence points. Somewhere in the Outer Rim, one of those points was failing. It wasn't a collapse. It was a slow, silent surrender of capacity.

He had worried he was projecting. His history was messy. Three years in the GAG, making choices he still couldn't justify. The things he had done at Jacen’s side. The Council recommended meditation and time. He had taken both. They helped with the trauma, but they didn't stop the noise. He didn't have the vocabulary to explain this without sounding like a Knight who had finally cracked.

So he told no one.

He could envision the conversation with his father perfectly. *I sense a structural anomaly. No, I can't find it on a star chart. No, I don't know the cause.* He could feel the careful concern that would follow. He would be redirected to the healers. The institutional machinery of the Order would decide that Ben Skywalker had simply seen too much.

He was not going to the healers.

He probed the hollow in his sense again, the way one might test a bruise. It remained. It had direction—that insistent southwest vector pointing toward the Outer Rim. It had depth. It was growing at a measurable rate.

The Jedi Order had survived Palpatine, Thrawn, and Caedus. It had rebuilt and endured. He trusted that legacy. He trusted the Council’s intelligence and his father’s perception. But he also trusted his own hands, and they told him something was wrong in a way the Council hadn't named.

Skywalkers rarely asked for help until they had exhausted every other option. He knew it was a flaw. But he couldn't walk into his father’s office and demand belief based on a three-month headache. He needed something concrete.

The door slid open.

Light from the corridor cut across the stone. A familiar presence entered—steady as gravity, warm as a ship running on clean power. Luke Skywalker stood in the doorway holding a datapad. 

It meant a mission.

The headache remained. The hollow was still there, patient and worsening. Ben uncrossed his legs and stood. For the first time in months, the wrongness had a destination.

"Dad," he said.

Luke held up the datapad. "I have something for you, Ben. It’s in the Outer Rim. A disturbance that doesn't match the usual patterns."

The compass needle clicked. Southwest. The hollow finally had a shape.

"Southwest?" Ben asked.

Luke’s eyes sharpened, his focus shifting as he read Ben’s presence. "Along the Rimma Trade Route. How did you know?"

Ben kept his hands steady. "I've been listening. I'm ready."

Luke studied him for a long moment. Ben felt the weight of his father’s perception—a silent, heavy pressure. Then Luke nodded.

"Good," his father said. "We leave in two hours."
