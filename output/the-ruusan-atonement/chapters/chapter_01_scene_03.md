The hangar bay held a dozen people and the vast, echoing silence of the empty space between them.

Ben stood at his ship’s ventral access panel. He balanced a datapad against his forearm, the screen’s blue glow carving sharp angles across his face. The pre-flight checklist was complete. It had been complete for twenty minutes. He remained motionless.

He scrolled.

Seff Hellin. Former Jedi Coalition, reliable in the field, and rarely prone to second-guessing. Ben’s thumb hovered over the name. During a mission, Seff reached out through the Force with instinctive frequency—a mental check-in, like a hand on a shoulder in the dark. He would feel Ben’s frayed edges. He would know something was wrong.

Ben scrolled past.

Natua Wan. An expert in ruins and pre-Republic artifacts who had cataloged three Sith sites on Ziost. She had been part of his father’s inner circle during the Abeloth crisis, and she reported anything that resembled a developing situation. Not out of disloyalty, but out of the exact brand of disciplined Jedi conduct Luke kept asking Ben to mirror.

The hangar smelled of fuel, scorched metal, and the stale, recycled air of a long-occupied bunker. A maintenance droid trundled past, its sensor dome swiveling toward Ben before dismissively turning away. Behind him, two technicians argued over a motivator calibration, their voices hushed to avoid a scene. Ordinary sounds. Ordinary smells. The sensory texture of a world where nothing was amiss.

Except for the hollow.

It sat in the back of his Force-sense like a room without furniture, walls, or a ceiling—a void where something essential should have been. It wasn't dark or threatening. It was simply absent, a silence so profound it made the rest of the Force feel deafening by comparison. He had carried it for three months. He had become adept at navigating around it, favoring the gap the way a runner favors a healed fracture.

He stopped scrolling.

*Desh Rolan.* Private security. Former GAG intelligence, specialized in threat assessment and personnel extraction. He was currently operating out of a contractor’s office in the lower levels of Nar Shaddaa, a location that spoke to the dwindling quality of his recent contracts.

Ben’s finger hesitated. This was the threshold. Here, the rationalization became a conscious act. He could still call Seff. He could still call Natua. He could still do this by the book.

He tapped the comm icon.

The line connected on the fourth chime. Desh had seen the ID and made him wait.

"Skywalker." The voice arrived with the flat, metallic rasp of long-range compression. "I wondered when you'd call."

Ben stared at the access panel. "I need a field partner. Outer Rim, two weeks minimum. Non-combat rated initially, though it might escalate."

"Non-combat." A pause followed, heavy with unspoken history. "What’s the brief?"

"Survey and report. I’m tracking an anomaly along the Rimma corridor. Uncharted system, possibly pre-Republic. I need someone who can handle logistics and equipment, and someone who doesn't generate—" He cut himself off.

"Doesn't generate what?"

The silence stretched. Ben caught his reflection in the darkened viewport: mouth set, shoulders rigid. He looked like a man making a choice he had already committed to hours ago.

"Complications," Ben said.

Another pause. Faintly, Ben heard the ambient hum of generator banks from Desh’s end. Desh was likely moving—pulling navigation data or checking a kit. He had always processed the things he didn't say through constant motion.

"Who’s the Jedi lead?" Desh asked.

Ben looked at the clean lines of his completed checklist. The question was a test, an exit ramp. Desh was giving him one last chance to mention the Council or the Temple.

"It’s just us," Ben said.

The silence lasted two seconds too long. Ben’s grip tightened on the datapad. This was the moment for Desh to push back, to ask the questions Ben couldn't answer, to find the limit of their history.

"Alright," Desh said.

Ben let out a breath he hadn't realized he was holding.

No interrogation. No demands for authorization. Just a simple acceptance, delivered in the same tone Desh had used in the Galactic Alliance Guard when backup failed to arrive. Back then, Desh had simply rebuilt the plan around the void. They had pulled three people from a collapsing facility and never mentioned the lack of support in the debrief. The work was the work.

But this wasn't the work. This was a secret, and Desh’s acceptance meant he understood the distinction.

"Departure in two hours," Ben said. "I’ll send coordinates for pickup."

"I need a day for equipment."

"Twenty-four hours. Meet me at the rendezvous."

"What’s the terrain?"

"Unknown. Pack for cold weather."

"I always pack for cold weather." The sound of a chair scraping back came through the speaker. "You’re buying the rations."

"Agreed."

The line cut.

Ben stood for a moment, datapad at his side. The hangar’s routine continued. A StealthX rolled toward the far bay—not his ship, but a Temple unit—and he watched it go with the detached interest of a ghost. He had bypassed the entire Jedi oversight structure in less than a minute. He had chosen the one man who wouldn't ask him to justify it. The thought was clean, devoid of guilt, and that was the true problem. It should have felt like a compromise. Instead, it felt like relief.

He tucked the feeling away and climbed into the cockpit.

***

Coruscant receded in the forward viewports, the city-planet’s sprawling lights shrinking into a single, brilliant spark. Ben calculated the hyperspace jump manually. The navcomp had the data ready, but he needed the rhythm of the arithmetic. Jump duration. Fuel reserves. Vector corrections for the Rimma’s gravity wells.

He centered himself, attempting the standard pre-jump ritual: settle the senses, clear distractions, establish a baseline.

The hollow remained.

What surprised him was the direction.

Until now, he had experienced the void as a weather pattern—an ambient pressure distributed across his awareness. He had mapped it as a zone of absence. But with Coruscant fading and the dark ahead, the geometry of the hollow shifted.

He reached out with the Force, testing the sensation like thin ice. The hollow didn't push back; it pulled. It was a current beneath still water, invisible until he stopped fighting it.

Southwest. Along the Rimma. Deep into the Outer Rim.

He hadn't been tracking the anomaly. He had been following a lure.

The navcomp chimed. The jump window was open.

Ben rested his hand on the lever. He wasn't hesitating; he was processing. Three months of vague wrongness had finally resolved into a point of origin. Whatever sat at the end of that vector had been calling him since before he knew how to listen.

He threw the lever.

Stars stretched into white lines. The hyperspace tunnel formed around him, a blue-white blur that most Force-sensitives experienced as a sensory blankness. Clean. Neutral.

The hollow persisted. Hyperspace usually muted the subtleties of the Force, drowning out specific signatures in the rush of the slipstream, but this absence was fundamental. It was indifferent to the speed of light.

Ben kept his hands on the controls. He didn't look at the bearing he’d memorized; he didn't have to. It sat in his mind like a compass needle that had finally found north—steady, patient, and absolutely certain.

He was going to it. He had always been going to it.

The thought arrived without drama. It waited for him to admit that the decision had been made the moment he stopped scrolling and found Desh’s name.
