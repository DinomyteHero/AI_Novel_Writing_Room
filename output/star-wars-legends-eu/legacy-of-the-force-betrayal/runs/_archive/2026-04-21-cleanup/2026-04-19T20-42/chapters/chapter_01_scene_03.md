The service passage was narrow enough that Ben could have touched both walls without stretching.

He didn't. He kept his hands tucked close and his back to the durasteel, tracking the line of Jacen's shoulder ahead of him. Overhead, a repulsorlift transport droned past on a low vector. Its engine wash shoved hot air down into the passage, carrying metal dust and something sharper—coolant cycling through the factory ventilation. Ben tasted it on his lips before he put a name to it.

His right hand dropped to his saber hilt. By touch, he found the activation stud, pressed his thumb against it without triggering it, then let go. He'd done that a hundred times in the training salle. Here, it landed differently.

He did it again. Same result. The hilt stayed dark.

Jacen had stopped at the far end. He stood with his shoulders squared, studying the angle around the corner toward the service entrance. He hadn't spoken since they'd left the scrub slope. Neither had Nelani, half a step behind Ben, her robe hem whispering over the permacrete in a sound so thin it almost wasn't there.

Ben watched Jacen's back and tried to read it. Nothing. Jacen was calm the way a wall was calm—there, solid, giving nothing away.

Then Jacen glanced back.

Not at Nelani. At Ben.

The look lasted maybe a second. Then Jacen faced forward again. No words. No hand signal. Just that glance, quick and exact, like checking whether a tool was where it was supposed to be.

Ben's throat worked once. Not fear. He was pretty sure it wasn't fear. More like the pressure before a spar, when the instructor said begin and everything stopped being practice. He was thirteen, in a service passage, with a missile factory around the corner, and Jacen had just looked at him like he was part of the plan.

He pushed off the wall.

"Where do you want me?"

The question came out steady. That almost surprised him. He'd expected his voice to do something stupid. It didn't.

Jacen didn't fully turn. He tipped his head just enough to speak over his shoulder. "East side of the hatch. Left of my position. You'll have a clear line to the interior if anyone tries to come out during the approach."

"Got it."

That was all. No explanation for the placement, no check on whether Ben was ready, no are you sure. Just the assignment, flat and clean, the same way Jacen might have told him which crate to move. Ben filed it away and checked his saber again—thumb to stud, release. Somewhere in the last half minute, the weight of it had settled. It was still real. It felt less huge.

Nelani moved up beside him. Her hood was back. In the dim spill from the passage mouth, her expression looked the same as it had on the slope: precise, unsatisfied, as if she were measuring something that refused to come out correctly. She didn't say anything to Ben. She looked toward the corner, then at Jacen's back, then toward the corner again.

Ben wondered if she was doing her own version of a saber check. He decided not to ask.

"Thirty seconds," Jacen said quietly. He wasn't looking at either of them. He watched the corner with that complete stillness that usually meant he was watching something else, too—the part Ben couldn't see yet. "The rotation brings the perimeter contact back to the north side. That's our window."

Ben started counting without meaning to. One. Two. Three. The coolant smell pulsed in and out with the ventilation cycle. Somewhere beyond the wall to his left, machinery kept up a low, repeating grind—industrial, ordinary, not a weapon. Just equipment doing its job. The factory sounded like a factory. That felt strange. He'd expected a target to sound more like one.

"Ben." Jacen's voice stayed low. "You're left of the hatch. Nelani has the approach. I have the contact at the entrance. You don't move until I move."

"Understood."

Nelani said nothing. Ben took that for her version of the same answer.

Jacen stepped around the corner.

Ben followed half a step back and left, exactly where he'd been put. The service entrance sat fifteen meters ahead, a flat panel cut into the factory wall. A single light over it cast a yellow cone onto the permacrete. The contact Jacen had mentioned was there too, a figure in gray with his back mostly to them, attention angled toward the approach road on the far side.

Jacen's pace never changed. He walked like he was crossing a room.

Ben matched his line and watched everything. The contact's posture—loose, unworried. The panel—sealed, no lock mechanism visible from here. The yellow light—enough to expose them for the last eight meters. Jacen had to have accounted for that. Ben logged it and kept moving.

His thumb found the activation stud again before he'd consciously reached for it. This time he left it there.

Ten meters. Eight. The contact shifted. Ben caught it in the shoulders—not a turn, just restlessness—and then Jacen stepped into the yellow cone. The contact started to turn. Jacen's hand came up with a precision that made the whole thing look almost administrative, and the contact dropped.

No shout. No wasted motion. Jacen caught him before he hit, then lowered him against the wall with the same economy he'd used for everything else.

Ben exhaled.

He hadn't noticed he'd stopped breathing.

Nelani was already moving past him toward the panel, hand at her belt. Then her saber hilt was in her grip, and the blade snapped on—blue, sudden, filling the passage mouth with hard color and a wash of heat that touched Ben's face a beat before the hum reached him.

He took his place left of the panel. His own blade stayed dark. His hand on the saber was steady.

This was real. The light made it real in a way the slope hadn't, the passage hadn't, the saber checks hadn't. Nelani's blade was real. The panel in front of them was real. He was exactly where Jacen had put him, with Jacen at his shoulder and the factory on the far side of the door.

Ben watched the blue light move over the durasteel and waited.
