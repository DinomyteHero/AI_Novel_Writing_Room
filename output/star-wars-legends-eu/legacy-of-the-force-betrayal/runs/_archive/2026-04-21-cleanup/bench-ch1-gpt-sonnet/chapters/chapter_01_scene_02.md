The turbolift doors slid open and warm air rolled over Jacen Solo, Ben Skywalker, and Testan ke Harran.

It carried sweat, machine lubricant, hot composite, propellant, explosive filler, scorched metal. Labor. Manufacture. Intent. Not rot, not hidden slaughter, not the sour bite of panic. This place smelled like an honest factory that had chosen dishonest work.

Jacen stepped out first.

He let his cloak trail a fraction longer than needed, then caught the next stride hard enough that the hem flared behind him. His boot heels rang on the catwalk grille. The sound traveled down into the manufacturing chamber and came back thin off duracrete partitions and machine housings. He didn't look over his shoulder to check whether Ben and Testan followed. He knew they would. A beat behind was exactly where he wanted them.

Below, eight assembly lanes ran the length of the chamber, each a bright strip of white conveyor and moving parts between gray barriers. Workers in protective gear bent over luminous-green spheres the size of human heads, fitting sensors into the warheads with the careful concentration of surgeons who understood that a tremor could become a memorial service. Farther downline, technicians plugged cable bundles into guidance units and studied flickering black-and-white monitor feeds. At the casing stations, men and women in stained coveralls tapped missile housings with rubber-headed hammers, listening for flaws.

The rhythm of it reached him through the metal under his boots. Conveyor motors. Hammer raps. The soft hiss of suit seals. A shouted exchange in Adumari somewhere below. Nothing in the pulse of this place said hidden. Hidden things ran off-beat. This factory kept time too well.

"Whalcome to Dammant Killers Cartann primary production facili-tee," Testan ke Harran announced behind him, broad and pleased and loud enough for the workers below to hear. His tunic was a bright, improbable arrangement of orange-and-purple diamonds over gold trousers, with a scarf that would have embarrassed a Hapan court musician. The beard, though, was clearly his pride: black, glossy, combed and conditioned until it held the sheen of expensive leather. "You ar looking at the finest concussion missile line on Adumar. Poss'bly in the whole Alliance, from a cairtin point of view."

Ben came up beside Testan with his expensive-vacationer holocam dangling from one hand, his face arranged in polite seriousness. "He says this is the finest missile line on Adumar," he told Jacen. "Maybe in the Alliance, if you ask the marketing department."

Testan blinked. "I did not say that."

"You implied it," Ben said.

Jacen kept his gaze on the floor below. "Then I'm fortunate I brought a translator."

Ben nodded solemnly. "I'm very gifted."

Testan gave Ben a suspicious glance, then seemed to decide the child was part of the Jedi theater and therefore safer to indulge than confront. "Young Bhen speaks fair Adumari for an offworldar."

"Thanks," Ben said. "I practiced on food menus."

Jacen moved farther along the catwalk, letting the chamber open beneath him in segments. Missile plants had their own logic: material intake, assembly, inspection, staging, security woven between. If there was an illegal corridor, it would either sit close to the shipping spine or hide behind some area the workers had learned not to look at twice. The future where they found it quickly depended on Testan's vanity. The future where they spent twenty minutes walking the catwalk in circles depended on the man's discipline. Vanity was easier to encourage.

Testan came up on his right, scarf fluttering in the warm draft from below. "Ar your regulations always so dramatic, Jedi Solo? I was told this would be an inspection, not a parade."

Ben answered before Jacen could. "That's just how he walks."

"I do not."

"You do, a little." Ben dropped his voice as if sharing a state secret. "He practices it in mirrors."

Jacen turned his head just enough to give Ben a measured look. "Thank you, Ben."

"You're welcome."

Testan barked a laugh in spite of himself, and the laugh loosened him another increment. "Good. Good. A Jedi with family to mock him. That makes him almost safe."

"Almost?" Ben asked.

"Nothing with a lightsaber is safe."

"That's fair."

Testan swept a hand toward the lanes below. "You see the warhead stations. Composite casing checks there. Guidance assembly there. Packaging and lot crating beyond the next barriars. We export under proper Alliance documantation. All legal. All taxed. All patriotic."

"Patriotic to whom?" Ben asked.

Testan looked at him sidelong. "To paying customars."

Ben lifted the holocam. "That sounds like something my mom would circle in a report."

Jacen heard the answer, filed the phrasing, and kept walking. Corellian-adjacent worlds had started talking in narrower circles lately—local duty, local sovereignty, local necessity. Even here, on Adumar, where every wall remembered pilots and duels and spectacle, the language had shifted. The Alliance was a customer. Maybe an overseer. Not automatically home.

He slowed near a cross-aisle and rested both hands lightly on the catwalk rail.

Below, two workers in hazmat suits eased a luminous-green warhead shell into a cradle. Another fed a long sensor wand into the opening with the care that bordered on tenderness. Dangerous work always made its own religion.

Testan said, "As you can see, ar workars observe all safety protocols."

Ben peered over the rail. "Do the safety protocols cover beard containment?"

Testan stared at him. "What?"

"For industrial environments. Hair contamination." Ben gestured at the beard. "That's a significant surface area."

"It is maintained to a high standard."

"Oh, I can see that. What do you use on it?"

"Oil."

Ben nodded as though this confirmed a theory. "I was guessing oil."

"Good oil."

"What kind?"

Testan drew himself up. "Imported."

Ben glanced at Jacen. "He says imported, which means expensive."

"I understood that part."

"Right. Sorry." Ben turned back to Testan. "Do you brush it with butter?"

Testan's expression moved through outrage, disbelief, and reluctant amusement in quick sequence. "Buttar."

"I'm asking."

"Of course not."

"It's very shiny."

"It is groomed."

"With imported oil."

"Yes."

"Not butter."

Testan huffed. "If I put buttar in my beard, I would smell like breakfast."

Ben considered that. "That does seem like a liability in a missile plant."

Jacen let the exchange run. Ben was doing exactly what he needed done: keeping Testan talking, slightly flattered, slightly annoyed, anchored to his own dignity. Around that dignity Jacen felt the man's thoughts moving in disordered loops. Pride in the factory. Irritation at offworld oversight. Pleasure at having an audience. Beneath all of it, quick sealed doors in the mind—not sealed well. Merely held shut by habit.

Jacen didn't press.

He listened instead to the shape of Testan's attention. Each time the guide swept a hand toward a lane, his thoughts touched that lane and bounced away clean. Again near the guidance stations, again near the casing checks. But when they neared the western wall the quality of that motion changed. A hitch. A turn aside. The mental equivalent of a man steering around a stain on the floor and hoping no one noticed him do it.

Ben said, "How many missiles does this place produce in a day?"

Testan took the bait with relief. Numbers were safer than secret spaces. "That depends on variant, loadout, customar specifications. At peak? Substantial. Enough that if a governmant is imprudent enough to annoy us, it regrats it."

Ben frowned. "You can say that on a tour?"

"I just did."

"Good point."

Testan stroked his beard. "And if you ar wondering, this facility supports many contracts."

"Corellian too?" Ben asked.

A pause. Small, but there.

"Many contracts," Testan repeated.

Ben gave him an innocent look. "That means yes."

"That means I do not discuss customars with children."

"I'm thirteen, not six."

"Then you ar old enough to hear no."

Jacen let the silence breathe. Testan was pulling his caution back around him. Time to close the gap.

He walked on, slowly at first, and let his awareness settle around the guide without pressing hard enough to feel like an assault. Testan's mind was disciplined only in the way a civilian's mind sometimes was: cultivated evasions, habits of discretion, no training in blocking a Jedi who had chosen to try. Jacen moved with care. A quiet interest here. A gentle weight there. He wasn't hunting words—words could lie. He wanted the image Testan kept steering around.

The catwalk curved with the wall. Gray panels. Utility conduits. An access hatch too small for cargo movement. A maintenance alcove holding nothing but tools and stale air.

Then the buried thing flickered.

Not the passage itself. Just memory residue: dark-blue jumpsuits, supervisors moving fast under stress, a broad gray panel descending from above with heavy finality. The perspective was from the catwalk. Testan had stood somewhere very near where Jacen stood now.

Jacen stopped.

Testan nearly took two more steps before he caught himself. "Jedi Solo?"

Jacen kept his eyes on the wall. The memory hung there in fragments—not a replay, but a possibility sharpened by guilt and repetition. The future where he walked past it led nowhere useful.

"There is something here," he said.

Ben went still at once.

Testan laughed too quickly. "Herar? A wall, certainly."

"Something wrong."

The two words changed the air between them.

Testan's amusement drained away. Beneath it Jacen felt the guide's mind clench hard around the buried image—too late, too rough. That kind of grip only outlined what it was meant to conceal.

"Wrong?" Testan said. "You'r inspectors now of morality in architecture?"

Jacen turned and regarded him for the first time since the turbolift. "What's behind this panel?"

"Structural support."

Ben stepped closer to the wall, studying it. His expression said curious apprentice. His readiness in the Force said he had moved from joke to assignment with nothing in between. "It does look a lot like the rest of the wall."

"That is how walls work," Testan snapped.

Jacen laid a hand flat against the panel. Warm metal. Vibration from machinery behind and below. Another trace from Testan's mind slipped free under the pressure of fear: dark-blue sleeves, a crate corner, warning stencils, the descending gray slab.

Not structural support.

He started walking again, faster now.

The ring of his heels changed pitch. His cloak snapped once in the heated airflow. He heard Ben and Testan fall in behind him, the guide scrambling to recover authority, Ben matching pace without needing to be told.

"Jedi Solo," Testan said, louder now, "you have no warrant to damage facili-tee property."

"Then this will be educational."

"Stop."

Jacen didn't.

He reached the panel that matched the memory—broad, gray, anonymous, its upper seam too clean for a wall that had been there long—and stepped aside. "Ben."

Ben handed him the holocam without looking. "Got it."

Then he was in front of the wall with his training blade out.

Testan made a strangled sound. "That is not legal."

Ben thumbed the activator. Blue light snapped out with the sharp hiss of ionized air. "It's my practice lightsaber."

Testan stared. "Your practice—"

Ben glanced back at Jacen. "He seems unclear on the concept."

"From a certain point of view," Jacen said, "it is true. He does practice with it."

Ben's mouth twitched. Then he set the blade to the panel.

The saber bit through durasteel with a sharp, hungry hiss. White-orange heat chased the cut line as Ben drew a meter-and-a-half circle, neat and fast, shoulders compact, motions economical in the way his parents had drilled into him. Molten droplets spattered the catwalk grille and cooled with small ticks. Testan backed away with both hands raised, half shielding his beard from the sparks.

"That is not an inspection," he said.

"No," Jacen said. "Now it isn't."

Ben finished the circle, stepped clear, and kicked the glowing disk.

It toppled inward with a booming clang.

Beyond it lay a passage packed floor to ceiling with transport containers.

Gray crates stacked in disciplined lots, stenciled with hazard markings and shipping codes. DAMMANT KILLER MODEL 16. Twenty-four to a lot. DANGER. DO NOT DROP.

For a heartbeat the whole factory seemed to hold its breath around the opening.

Then the alarms began.

Not loud enough to drown the machinery—sharp local bursts from inside the passage and somewhere deeper beyond it. Heavy doors started cycling farther in. Jacen felt the security response spread through the building like a muscle suddenly contracting.

Ben leaned in just far enough to give the holocam a clear angle. "That seems bad for them."

"It's conclusive for us," Jacen said.

Testan spun and ran.

He got three strides.

Jacen reached through the Force and hit him with a shove timed to the man's next step. Testan pitched forward, scarf flying, and folded hard into the catwalk rail before dropping in a heap. He didn't rise.

Ben winced. "You could've just tripped him."

"I did."

Boots thundered in the passage.

Jacen ignited his lightsaber. Green light flooded the gap, the catwalk, Ben's face for an instant. "Stay behind me."

Ben had the holocam up in one hand and his own saber held low in the other. "That was always the plan."

The first six security troops rounded the crate stacks at a run—orange jumpsuits under green carapace armor, helmets swept back in a style that would have looked rakish on a speeder bike and looked merely aggressive here. They came fast and in formation, rifles up, the narrow passage compressing them into their own problem.

"Jedi!" the lead trooper shouted. "Drop the weapons!"

Jacen stepped into the opening and began to walk forward.

Not a rush. Not a charge. A measured advance, blade moving in the butterfly pattern, the green arc folding and turning over itself until the passage filled with angles and reflected light and the troopers' own uncertainty.

The first volley came.

He caught the nearest bolt near the middle of the blade and sent it back high. It cracked against the commander's helmet and snapped his head sideways. The man crashed into a crate stack and went down in a tangle.

A second bolt deflected into a third trooper's shoulder plate and spun him into his companion.

Two more bolts crossed in front of Jacen and found the center of the squad—because the men behind had fired through the men ahead.

The smell changed immediately: burned synthcloth, cooked armor seals, a brief ugly trace of scorched flesh under hot metal.

Jacen kept walking.

Behind him, Ben gave a short involuntary snicker.

Jacen said over his shoulder, "Something amusing?"

"You're doing the walk again."

Another volley. He twisted the lightsaber and folded the bolts away from the crate stencils, sending one into the passage ceiling where it burst into hot sparks. "Could you speak up?" he called to the troopers. "I'm a little deaf."

The men in front hesitated. That hesitation did more damage than the saber had. The second rank tried to push through. The first rank tried to duck aside. Rifles crossed. Someone fired on reflex. A green-armored trooper spun and went down.

Ben's snicker sharpened into a real laugh for half a second, then cut off as he remembered himself. Good. If Ben could laugh, he wasn't freezing.

Jacen reached the fallen commander and stepped past him without looking down. A trooper lunged in too close for a shot, trying to use the passage's tight geometry to jam his rifle muzzle into Jacen's ribs. Jacen angled his shoulder, trapped the barrel under his left forearm, and brought the lightsaber hilt into the man's helmet. The trooper dropped.

Another burst came from deeper in the passage. Jacen caught two, three, four bolts, choosing return lines that sent them into armor and bulkhead—never into the crates. The future where a ricochet found a transport container ended in a considerably brighter report than he cared to contemplate.

The rest of the first wave broke.

They retreated over their own wounded, dragging one man by the harness straps. More boots pounded farther in.

"Second team," Ben said. "They learn fast?"

"They learn in batches."

Ben had the holocam up now, peeking past Jacen's shoulder. "I've got the crates. I've got the labels. I've got the men trying to kill us. Very complete package."

"Keep it steady."

"That's easy for you to say."

The second wave of six hit the passage firing before they had clean lines. Jacen gave ground two steps to improve his angle, then returned the bolts low and center. Armor rang. Men cursed. Two dropped to the deck. Another slammed backward into a crate tower and nearly brought twenty-four missiles down on his own squad.

"Watch your fire!" a trooper shouted.

"Excellent advice," Jacen said.

Ben leaned sideways to get the insignia and hazard markings in frame. "This is Ben Skywalker," he said into the holocam in a voice that was aiming for official and landing somewhere near earnest panic with good posture. "Jedi Knight Jacen Solo and I are in a secret part of the Dammant Killers plant in Cartann. These are DAMMANT KILLER MODEL 16 transport containers, and their guys are trying to kill us, which is probably not standard public-tour procedure."

A blaster bolt flashed for the holocam. Ben yanked it back. "That was close."

"Keep narrating," Jacen said.

"Really?"

"Yes."

"It makes me a target."

"It makes the recording useful."

Ben muttered, "That also sounds like something my mom would say," and raised the holocam again. "For the record, those troops opened fire first."

A trooper broke from cover along the crate stacks and lunged into the opening. Jacen pivoted and clipped the rifle from his hands with a precise strike that separated barrel from stock without touching flesh. The trooper stared at the ruined halves. Jacen kicked him back into two others.

"Also for the record," Ben added, "we object."

The second wave dissolved into confusion. Heavier boots pounded farther in—more numerous, more deliberate. Eight this time, by sound and spread. Jacen didn't need to count long. The facility had committed its passage response. Good. Fewer surprises ahead.

Bad, too. It meant they had to move now.

He stepped back from the opening. "Ben. Down the passage."

Ben didn't ask whether that meant forward toward the troops or forward past them. He tucked the holocam, dropped behind Jacen's left shoulder, and moved when Jacen moved.

They advanced.

There was no room for flourish. The hidden passage was barely wide enough for two men abreast, lined on both sides with enough munitions to punish carelessness permanently. Jacen held the butterfly pattern tighter—smaller arcs, economical turns at wrist and elbow. Each bolt became a decision. Ceiling. Armor. Deck. Dead wall. Never the crates.

A trooper near the rear had more discipline than the rest. He waited until his fellows crouched and fired on a rising angle, trying to catch Ben above Jacen's guard. Jacen read the shot before the trigger completed its pull. He raised the saber, knocked the bolt into the passage light strip, and the strip burst in a shower of white fragments and sudden shadow.

"Thanks," Ben said.

"You're welcome."

"I feel very protected."

"That's the idea."

The third wave pressed with more sense. Two went prone behind fallen comrades and fired between legs while the rear rank shot high. The pattern narrowed Jacen's options. He moved forward through it rather than back, accepting proximity over distance, turning bolts with minimal blade travel. One struck a chest plate. One burned into a forearm guard. One hit the deck near a boot and filled the passage with smoke and sparks. The men farther in faltered at the smell of their own side going down.

Jacen used that hesitation.

He surged the last three meters, drove his shoulder into the nearest standing trooper, and spun him into the prone shooters. Bodies tangled. Rifles skittered across the deck. Ben, still exactly where he was supposed to be, nearly bumped Jacen's back and corrected with a half step.

"Sorry."

"Don't stop."

"I wasn't planning to."

They pushed past the midpoint of the hidden stockpile. Crates rose on both sides like mute walls. Shipping tags. Lot numbers. Destinations buried in customs coding that said little and implied plenty. Jacen needed only a glimpse of route prefixes to recognize Corellian handling patterns underneath. This wasn't local surplus. This was pipeline.

Plant the hook. Follow later.

At the far end of the passage a rail opened onto a second manufacturing chamber below—larger than the first, louder, and now in the early stages of panic. White conveyor belts glowed beneath them. Workers were abandoning their stations and running. Opalescent shell casings moved on one line in neat cradles, indifferent to the commotion.

"Rail," Ben said.

"I see it."

Blasterfire chased them from farther in. Jacen turned at the opening and held the corner, lightsaber flicking left-right-high, making a green lid over the passage mouth while Ben raised the holocam again.

"This is still Ben Skywalker," Ben said. "We have now reached another part of the secret part. There are more assembly lines down there. If anyone watching this later wants to know whether they hid the missiles on purpose, I'd say yes."

"That commentary is informal," Jacen said.

"It's good commentary."

"It is."

Ben panned the holocam across the chamber: workers fleeing, lines still running, the rail, the passage mouth farther in with orange-and-green troopers stacking up to fire. A bolt streaked past his shoulder and splashed across Jacen's lightsaber in a green flare.

Ben ducked. "Definitely trying to kill us."

"Your report is becoming repetitive."

"That's because their behavior lacks variety."

A squad leader shouted from the passage mouth: "Cut them off below!"

Jacen glanced over the rail.

No troops on the floor yet. That would change in seconds. The fastest route out was ugly but workable: over the rail, onto the moving conveyor, sprint to the maintenance portal at the end of the line where finished sections passed through into the next compartment. Small for adults. Fine for Ben. Tight but manageable for Jacen if he folded correctly. The future where they took the stairs gave security time to bracket them from both sides.

"Ben," he said. "Over."

Ben looked down at the belt, then at the small portal at the far end. "You picked this route on purpose?"

"It's there."

"That's not the same thing."

"Go."

Ben slung the holocam, planted a hand on the rail, and vaulted.

For a horrible half second he was just a thin boy in free fall above an assembly line.

Then he landed on a conveyor cradle between opalescent shell casings, knees buckling, arms windmilling once, and recovered into a sprint that would have made Han proud and Mara pretend not to be. He hit the belt running, saber off so he could use his left hand for balance. Good choice.

"Nice landing," Jacen called.

Ben shouted back, "I'm giving it a generous pass."

Blasterfire intensified.

Troopers poured out onto the rail farther in, angling for a downward shot at Ben. Jacen widened his stance and turned the saber into a rapid vertical weave, catching shots and sending them into the opposite wall, the rail supports, empty ceiling. The lines for dropping several men were right there. Chest, throat, visor. He kept their heads down instead.

A bolt came in from low and right, cast from the chamber floor at an angle that would have sent it into a neighboring worker station if he deflected it conventionally. Jacen shifted his grip toward the pommel and caught it near the hilt, absorbing the energy flare close enough that the heat crossed his knuckles and bit into his palm through the glove.

Pain flashed, sharp and clean.

He held the saber steady until the bolt bled away into light.

No time to look at the glove. No point.

Ben was halfway to the portal. The belt carried shell casings beneath him with mechanical calm. He glanced back once, saw Jacen still at the rail, and kept going. Obedience again. Trust again. No wasted question.

"Jacen," he called, "Uncle Han says you never want to fight the enemy on ground he's chosen if you can avoid it."

"He's right."

"So this counts as avoiding it?"

"From a certain point of view."

Ben laughed once. "You're using that too much."

"It's a good phrase."

The first trooper on the rail farther in tried bravery and closed to arm's reach. Jacen back-kicked him in the knee without turning. Armor cracked. The man went down shouting. Another reached past him with a rifle; Jacen couldn't tell stun from kill and didn't much care. He trapped the barrel under his arm, twisted, and sent the weapon spinning over the rail.

Three more bolts came from the passage mouth. He angled the saber and fed them into the deck at the rear rank's feet. Hot fragments bounced off greaves and shins. The line broke.

That was all the opening he needed.

Jacen vaulted the rail.

He landed harder than Ben had, boots slamming onto the moving conveyor between two opalescent shell casings, left knee flexing deep to take the impact. The belt tugged him backward for an instant before his stride caught up with it. Fire snapped overhead from the rail and chewed sparks out of the machinery farther in.

Ahead, Ben dropped to a crouch at the portal and squeezed through, holocam first, then shoulders, then hips. His boots vanished last.

Jacen ran.

The portal was built for maintenance droids or small workers, not Jedi in field gear and a cloak. He yanked the cloak free with his scorched hand, ignored the sting, and heard blasterfire hammer the conveyor housings farther in. Shell casings rang and skidded in their cradles. Another second on the belt and it would become a trap.

He reached the portal, turned one shoulder, and dove after Ben, squeezing through the diminutive opening while behind them the surviving troopers kept firing into the conveyor belt they had just vaulted.
