The training blade caught him across the shoulder guard half a second after Ben's body insisted the parry should have connected. The impact wasn't hard—low power setting, standard drill—but it landed solid enough to register on the scoring band. Third time this week.

Ben deactivated his saber. The blade retracted with a snap-hiss that arrived on schedule, unlike the defensive reflex that had failed to materialize.

"You're bleeding speed," his partner said. The Knight—Ben hadn't caught his name at the start of the rotation, just another face in the evening drill queue—lowered his own training blade. Sweat darkened the collar of his tunic, but his breathing was steady, controlled. "Want another pass?"

"Not unless you're trying to pad your win record." Ben rotated his shoulder, testing the joint. The armor had absorbed the kinetic impact, but the point had registered in the Temple's sparring log, another blemish on a week that didn't need more data points suggesting Ben Skywalker was off his game. "Long week. I'm done."

The Knight nodded, already heading for the equipment rack. He racked his blade with the casual efficiency of someone who hadn't just tagged a Skywalker in a standard exchange. "Tomorrow, then."

"Sure."

The salle door hissed shut. Ben stood alone with the ventilation's steady hum and the distant percussion of the Temple's late shift moving through corridors three levels down. The empty space pressed in around him, the acoustics subtly wrong—like a room where the sound had shifted just enough to make your own voice feel foreign.

He'd told himself it was fatigue. Told himself that since Abeloth, since the fallout, since the endless debriefs and the reconstruction of an Order that kept looking at him like he might explain what his father had done. Told himself he was just tired. But tired didn't make you miss a parry you'd executed ten thousand times since childhood. Tired didn't make the ambient presence of the Temple arrive like an echo, half a beat behind the strike—a vibration that reached his awareness after his muscles had already committed to motion.

Ben exhaled, rolling his neck. Then he set his feet in the opening stance of the Fourth Form, hands empty at his sides.

No blade. Just the motion.

He moved through the sequence slowly, letting muscle memory carry him through the thrusts and pivots without the counterpoint of a lightsaber's weight. The forms were older than the Temple, older than the Ruusan Reformations, passed down through generations of Jedi who had trusted the rhythm of the Force to meet them at the apex of each strike. His breathing steadied—four seconds in, four seconds out—the basic cadence that was supposed to align with the Temple's ambient presence.

The rhythm arrived late.

Ben's weight shifted into a lunge that should have coincided with the pulse of the Force through the salle, but the pulse lagged. His foot hit the mat a fraction early, destabilizing the stance, and he stumbled. Not much—a hitch in his balance, a catch in his breath, his hand flailing slightly for a guard that existed only in muscle memory—but enough to break the form's integrity. He recovered into a neutral stance, sweat prickling at his hairline, and tried again.

The same delay. The same stumble on the transition from the third to the fourth position.

He stopped, sweat cooling on his palms. The salle's Force presence hung in the air like a subsonic vibration, present but delayed, syncopated against the beat his body knew by reflex. It wasn't silence. More like a room where the bass note kept dropping out, leaving the melody hanging incomplete, or a comlink where the voice arrived a syllable behind the lip movement.

Not fatigue.

The admission came sharp and unwelcome, edged with the particular irritation of a Skywalker confronting his own limitations. Ben leaned against the wall, letting the duracrete cool his back through the sweat-damp training tunic. He'd been handling this privately for seven days, adjusting his timing, compensating for the lag, treating it like a personal malfunction rather than a symptom of something the Council kept dismissing as residual disturbance from the recent crises.

But residual disturbances didn't cost you exchanges in the salle. They didn't make you lie to other Knights about why your guard had dropped, didn't make you calculate whether the lag was getting worse or if you were just losing the ability to hide it.

Ben closed his eyes, listening to the Temple breathe around him—the distant lift chimes, the whir of a cleaning droid passing in the corridor, the subharmonic thrum of a thousand Jedi going about their evening routines. All of it arriving on time, crisp and synchronized, except the Force itself, which kept arriving like a delayed transmission, half a beat behind where it should be.

He should say something. The thought surfaced and sank just as quickly. Explaining it would require words he didn't have, descriptions that would sound like trauma or excuse or the kind of mystical babble that made the Council's new military liaisons roll their eyes. The Council had measured the background fluctuations and found them within tolerance. Other Knights weren't stumbling. Just him.

Or just him and the ones who weren't admitting it.

Ben pushed off the wall, shaking out his arms. The wrongness persisted, that half-beat lag in the ambient field stealing timing he couldn't afford to lose. He needed to run the diagnostics again, check the Temple's Force saturation levels, compare them against the baseline readings from before the crises, before the disturbances, before everything had started arriving out of phase. He could do that in the meditation chamber, alone, where no one would see him testing whether his own perception had fractured or if something outside himself had shifted.

The door hissed open.

Luke stood in the doorway with a datapad and an expression Ben knew meant a mission.
