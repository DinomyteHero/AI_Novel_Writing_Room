The training blade caught him across the shoulder before he could bring his guard up.

Ben stepped back. Rendell lowered his weapon, waiting.

"Good," Ben said, which was the wrong word for it.

The hit itself hadn't hurt — training settings, padded edge, nothing he'd feel tomorrow. What stung was the gap between where his guard had been and where it should have been. Half a second. Maybe less. The kind of margin that didn't exist in his reflexes, or hadn't, until this week.

Third time.

Rendell was already moving into reset position, the question implicit in the way he held his blade — again? — and Ben pressed two fingers against his shoulder like he was checking for damage he knew wasn't there.

"I'm calling it," he said. "Fatigue's catching up."

It wasn't quite a lie. He was tired. He'd been tired all week, the same grinding background static that he'd been attributing to everything except what he suspected it actually was. Late nights. Post-mission tension. The residual noise that had been rattling around the Temple since Abeloth, the kind of thing Masters discussed in careful language and Knights mostly pretended not to notice.

Rendell nodded, accepting the out without pressing. He was a good sparring partner — reliable footwork, clean form, and the social grace not to make a thing of it when someone tapped out. Ben watched him rack his training saber and pull a towel off the bench, and waited for the door to close behind him.

Down the corridor, a turbolift chimed. Somewhere farther out, the late shift was moving through the Temple's bones — the soft percussion of boots on durasteel, a cleaning droid's low hum working its way along the adjacent hall. Coruscant at this hour was still Coruscant. The city didn't notice what was happening in the Force any more than it noticed anything else.

Ben stood in the middle of the salle and didn't move for a moment.

Then he unclipped his lightsaber from his belt and held it without igniting it.

The form he chose was one he'd been drilling since he was fourteen — Shii-Cho, first sequence, the one Jedi used to teach children because the movements were slow enough to feel each transition. He wasn't a child and he wasn't interested in Shii-Cho, but the sequence was simple enough that his body could run it on reflex, which was the point. If something was pulling at his timing, this was where he'd find it.

He moved through the opening stance. His shoulders settled. His feet found the familiar spacing on the floor.

The Force was there. It was always there — the ambient presence the Temple carried, warm and layered, the accumulated practice of decades pressing into the walls. He'd grown up inside it. He knew the texture of it the way he knew the sound of his own breathing.

He moved into the first transition.

The lag hit between the second and third position.

Not absence — the Force didn't vanish. It was more that his body reached for the next beat and the field arrived a half-step behind, like a note played slightly flat. Not wrong enough to break the form. Wrong enough that he felt it, the way you felt a step that was two centimeters lower than you'd expected, the small lurch of a body that had prepared for something that wasn't quite there.

He stopped. Started again from the opening stance.

Second transition. The lag again, in the same place, the same half-beat delay in the ambient field that his reflexes had been compensating for all week without his conscious mind catching up to what the compensation cost. He hadn't been dropping his guard. He'd been running on a rhythm that was no longer the rhythm of the field around him, and his body had been absorbing the mismatch until it couldn't anymore.

Ben lowered the unlit lightsaber.

He stood there for a moment, not moving, while the cleaning droid's hum faded down the corridor and the turbolift chimed again, distant, someone else's night continuing normally.

The fatigue explanation had been comfortable. Convenient. Abeloth's wake had left the Force strange in ways the Council was still cataloguing, and blaming the week was something any Knight could do without it meaning anything. He'd been doing it. He was aware, now, that he'd been doing it.

He ran the transition a third time, slowly, and the lag was there — not in him, not in his footwork or his grip or the muscle memory he'd spent years building. The field itself was off. A half-beat behind. Like a musician he trusted playing a note that wasn't quite the note.

His chest tightened.

Not the field around Coruscant generally. He'd have heard about it if the entire Temple was running off-pitch. Whatever this was, it was quieter than that, and more specific, and he didn't have a word for it yet that wasn't either alarming or embarrassing.

He clipped the lightsaber back to his belt and went to the bench. Sat down. Picked up his water bottle and put it back down without drinking from it.

Rendell hadn't felt it. He'd been standing in the same salle, moving through the same Force-saturated space, and his timing had been clean. His footwork had been exactly where it should be. If the field were off, Rendell would have felt it too, and Ben would have seen it in his movements.

Which left the alternative.

Ben pressed the heel of his hand against his sternum, briefly, and then stopped doing that.

He'd been handling it privately because the options for handling it publicly were bad. Either something was wrong with the Force in a way that the Order hadn't noticed yet, which was its own category of problem, or something was wrong with him specifically, which was a different category of problem and one he had less interest in discussing with anyone. Post-Abeloth jitter. Residual sensitivity. The kind of thing that got a Knight sent to a Healer and kept there while someone else took the mission.

He was still sitting there when he heard footsteps in the corridor — not the cleaning droid, not the late shift's anonymous shuffle. A specific cadence. Unhurried.

The door opened.

Luke stood in the doorway with a datapad in his hand and the expression Ben had been seeing his whole life — the one that meant the evening had just changed shape.
