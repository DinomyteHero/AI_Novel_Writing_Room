Ben's guard dropped a half-second late. Rendell's training blade cracked across his shoulder with a flat thwack that echoed off the salle walls. The impact jolted through muscle and bone, clean and stinging, the kind of hit that said his reflexes had betrayed him again.

He reset his stance by instinct, lightsaber humming low in his grip. The weapon felt right. The salle felt wrong. That half-beat lag in the ambient field pulled at the edge of his attention like a missed step on familiar stairs. He shook it off, or tried to. Rendell was already circling, boots whispering against the polished floor, waiting for the next exchange.

"Again?" Rendell asked. His voice carried the mild curiosity of a man who'd scored three touches this week on an opponent who usually handed them out instead.

Ben forced a breath. Sweat slicked his palms inside the practice gloves. The recycled air of the Temple carried the faint ozone bite of charged training blades and the distant chime of a turbolift somewhere down the corridor. Late-shift foot traffic, probably. Coruscant never slept; neither did its Jedi, not entirely.

"Not tonight," Ben said. He thumbed the activator. The blade died with a snap-hiss. "I'm calling it. Legs feel like they've got yesterday's patrol still in them."

It wasn't quite a lie. The week had been long—patrol rotations, Council briefings, the usual grind of a Knight who kept volunteering for the parts no one else wanted. But fatigue didn't explain the lag. Fatigue didn't make the Force arrive just behind the rhythm his body had drilled into reflex years ago.

Rendell powered down his own blade. The younger Knight gave a shallow nod, the kind that accepted the out without digging. "Fair enough. You buying the next round in the refectory if I beat you four times next session?"

"Only if you buy when I remind you why that's not happening." Ben kept his tone light, the dry edge he used when he didn't want questions. It worked. Rendell chuckled once, wiped his face with a sleeve, and headed for the equipment rack. A cleaning droid whirred past in the outer corridor, its tread a soft mechanical complaint against the durasteel plating. Normal sounds. Normal night. Ben waited until the doors hissed shut behind the other man before he let the mask slip.

Alone now, the salle felt larger. Echoes of their bout still hung in the air, the faint burn of exertion mixing with the metallic tang of the floor polish. He paced a slow circuit, boots marking time. The walls rose high, etched with the faint scars of generations of training. Coruscant pressed in beyond them—city lights bleeding through the high transparisteel slits, the endless hum of a planet that never powered down. He could almost hear the late-shift footsteps fading toward the turbolifts, Knights and staff cycling through their own private exhaustions.

He stopped at the center of the mat. No blade this time. Just the form. Shii-Cho first, then the opening sequences of Ataru. Muscle memory didn't need the ignition. His arms cut the air in precise arcs, feet shifting with the old drilled economy. Bend, pivot, reach. The movements should have flowed like water over stone. Instead, the lag caught him again—right as he transitioned into the low sweep. His body knew the timing. The Force didn't. It arrived a half-beat behind, like a note played slightly flat in a song he'd known since he could walk.

The sensation lodged under his ribs, a tight vibration that wasn't quite pain. He repeated the sequence slower. Same result. The ambient field dragged, stealing the beat he couldn't spare. His chest tightened. Not fatigue. Not the long week. Something else. The frustration rose sharp and clean, the way a Knight got frustrated with a tool that had always worked before. His own body, his own connection—failing him in front of someone else this time. Rendell hadn't said anything, but he'd seen. Word traveled in the Temple like coolant through ducts. Sooner or later, someone would ask.

Ben rolled his shoulders, feeling the bruise from the hit bloom under his tunic. He could have called for a rematch. Pushed through, proved it was nothing. Instead he'd waved it off. Handled it privately, the way he handled most things these days. The isolation settled heavier than the bruise. Other Knights didn't feel this drag. They meditated, they trained, they moved through the Temple with the steady confidence of people who trusted the ground under their feet. He didn't anymore. Not fully. And saying it aloud would only make him sound like the one still carrying scars from Abeloth, or worse—from everything before that. Traumatized. Unreliable. A Skywalker with a private ghost.

He ran the form again. Arms slicing, feet pivoting. The lag hit on the third transition, that same half-beat theft in the field around him. It wasn't in his head. It was out there, in the ambient rhythm that had always been steady before. The realization didn't arrive in words. It landed in his body first—a sharpening behind his eyes, a shift in the way his breath sat in his lungs. Not me. Not just me. The frustration curdled toward something colder, deeper. He didn't name it. Naming it would make it real in a way he wasn't ready for yet.

A soft chime sounded from the corridor. Another turbolift. More foot traffic heading home or out to the night patrols. The Temple lived its own quiet life at this hour—droids sweeping, late meditators in the chambers down the hall, the faint pulse of a million lives stacked into the city-planet beyond these walls. Ben had always liked that part. The galaxy didn't stop for Jedi problems. It kept moving, messy and loud and real.

He straightened from the last stance, arms loose at his sides. The salle felt smaller again, the weight of the evening pressing in. Whatever this was, it had cost him tonight. In front of a witness. He couldn't keep pretending it was nothing. Not if it kept stealing beats he couldn't afford to lose.

Luke appeared in the doorway with a datapad and an expression Ben knew meant a mission.
