Ben's guard dropped a half-second late.

Rendell's training saber cracked across his shoulder, the impact vibrating through padding and muscle like a misstruck chord. Ben staggered back a step, boots scraping the salle floor. Sweat slicked his palms; he tightened his grip on the hilt.

Not again.

The hum of their blades filled the empty training salle, a low thrum undercut by that persistent drag in the ambient field—a half-beat lag, like a note played slightly flat. It had tugged at him all week, faint enough to blame on long shifts or the Temple's late-hour quiet. Tonight, it had stolen a reflex he'd owned since he was twelve.

Rendell lowered his blade, brow furrowed under the salle's stark lighting. "You all right? That wasn't even a full extension."

Ben deactivated his saber with a flick, the hilt cooling in his hand. He forced a grin, the kind that usually bought him an out. "Fatigue. Been pulling doubles since the Abeloth cleanup. Call it?"

Rendell hesitated, circling back to center like he always did after a clean hit. The man was solid—mid-Knight, no frills, the sort who showed up for sessions because forms didn't lie. "One more? You're sharper than this."

"Not tonight." Ben clipped the hilt to his belt, rolling his shoulder to test the bruise forming beneath. The lag echoed in his chest, off-rhythm. Rendell didn't feel it; no one did. Explaining would sound like excuses, or worse—cracks from the GAG days he wouldn't drag into the open. "Go grab a meal. I'll lock up."

Rendell lingered a beat, then nodded. "Tomorrow, then. Don't let the week win." He deactivated his own saber and headed for the door, boots echoing faintly against the polished stone. The panel hissed open, spilling corridor light and the distant chime of a turbolift. Foot traffic murmured beyond—late-shift Knights wrapping meditations, a cleaning droid's servos whining in pursuit.

The door sealed. Silence dropped, broken only by Ben's breathing.

He paced the length of the salle, shadow stretching long under the overheads. Muscle memory first. No Force, no blade—just the form. He dropped into Soresu opening stance, arms loose, feet planted. Slow through the sequence: high guard, pivot, low sweep.

His body flowed clean for three breaths. Palms still damp, boots gripping the floor's faint grit. Then the lag hit—a half-beat pull in the ambient field, yanking his timing like a string snapped slack. His elbow dipped a fraction late, the sweep turning sloppy.

Ben froze mid-form, chest tightening. Not fatigue. Not the week.

He reset, eyes on the far wall's etched Jedi crest. Again: high guard. The rhythm his body knew by reflex buckled the same way, the field arriving a half-second behind. Sweat traced his spine now, cold against the salle's recycled air. This wasn't him fraying. It lived out there, in the Force itself.

Frustration knotted low in his gut, sharp as a blade pommel. Skywalker reflexes shouldn't miss. Knights didn't flinch at shadows. And no one else felt this off-note drag—not Rendell, not the patrols he'd shadowed last shift. Say it aloud, and he'd sound unreliable, still shaking off Abeloth's echoes. Better alone, testing until it made sense.

He paced faster, boots thudding. The salle felt smaller, walls pressing with the Temple's under-hum: vents cycling, a faint vibro-mop drone from the corridor. Late evening, and Coruscant kept moving—merchants haggling in lower levels, speeders cutting night lanes beyond the ziggurat's transparisteel. Life went on. His didn't.

One more form. No blade. He ignited nothing, just moved—pivot, thrust, recovery. The lag stole the beat again, predictable now. His shoulder burned from Rendell's hit, a reminder that private problems leaked when you pushed too long.

Ben stopped, hands on knees, breath ragged. Enough pretending. The field was wrong, structural in a way exhaustion couldn't touch. Chest tight, focus sharpening like a lens snap. Not him failing. Something else.

Distant turbolift chime again, closer this time. Voices overlapped in the hall—two Knights trading shift notes, a laugh cutting short. The cleaning droid whirred past, its treads sucking up grit.

He straightened, wiping sweat from his brow. Reluctant lift in his chest—maybe time to log it. Maybe.

The door panel hissed open.

Luke stood in the doorway, datapad in hand and that mission-ready set to his shoulders—the one Ben knew from a dozen handoffs.
