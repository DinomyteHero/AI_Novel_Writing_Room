The training blade caught him across the shoulder before his guard could drop.

Not a graze. A solid strike. The emerald emitter washed over his tunic, the impact jarring through his collarbone and rattling his teeth. Ben stepped back, boots skidding on the polished durasteel floor, and lowered his own practice weapon. The steady hum of the saber vibrated up his wrist, a familiar frequency he had felt since childhood. Everything else felt wrong.

Rendell deactivated his blade and stepped out of the exchange. He was breathing hard, but his posture was loose, balanced. A clean hit. He had earned it.

Ben rolled his shoulder. The sting was sharp, precise. It was the kind of mistake that would leave a bruise, but the bruise wasn't what bothered him. The timing was. His body knew the parry. His muscles had drilled the motion ten thousand times across a dozen different temple floors. The delay had come from somewhere else. A fractional hesitation in the ambient field, pulling at his attention just as he committed to the block. Like a metronome ticking a half-beat slow.

Third time this week.

"You're drifting, Skywalker," Rendell said, wiping sweat from his brow with the back of his glove.

Ben thumbed the activation switch. The training saber's hum died. "Late shift," he said. "Coruscant traffic's bleeding into my sleep cycle. I keep waking up to the mag-lev lines."

It wasn't entirely a lie, but it wasn't the truth either. Rendell nodded anyway, accepting the excuse with the easy grace of a man who hadn't felt what Ben had felt. The Temple corridors outside the salle still carried the muffled foot traffic of the evening rotation. A turbolift chimed in the distance, followed by the low, rhythmic whir of a maintenance droid buffing the hallway floor. Normal. Steady. The kind of background noise that meant the city was breathing and the Order was holding its shape.

"Rematch?" Rendell asked, already shifting his weight forward.

"I'll owe you one," Ben said, keeping his tone light. He managed a wry half-smile, the kind that usually worked to close a subject before it opened. "Let me stretch out. I'll buy you caf in the mess if I lose my footing again before lightsout."

Rendell smirked, clipped his emitter to his belt, and headed for the exit. The transparisteel panel slid shut behind him, leaving the salle quiet except for the recycled air cycling through the overhead vents.

Ben stood alone in the center of the mat. He flexed his fingers, feeling the phantom weight of the hilt. He shouldn't be making rookie mistakes. He was a Knight. The Order had spent months cleaning up after the recent crises, patching wounds, running diagnostics, and telling everyone to get some rest. The background disturbance had been logged as residual static, a lingering echo from campaigns that felt like they belonged to a different decade. Knights were walking around with headaches and calling it fatigue. Ben had accepted that. He had filed it away. He had told himself it was just the usual Skywalker tendency to overthink the quiet.

He started pacing. The durasteel floor was cool through his boots. He rolled his neck, working out the tension, and dropped into the opening stance of Form III. No blade. Just the shape of the movements.

He drew his left foot back, pivoted his hips, and swept his arm through a standard deflection arc. The motion was smooth. His joints tracked correctly. He closed his eyes and reached for the rhythm of the room.

The lag hit him on the third beat.

He pushed his weight forward, expecting the ambient current to catch him, to meet his motion with the usual steady resistance. It didn't. The space around him felt slack. The Force arrived a half-second behind his body, pulling at the edges of his awareness like a loose thread on a drumhead. His center of gravity shifted. He overextended his right foot, catching himself only by dropping his shoulder and resetting his stance.

He opened his eyes. His palms were slick with sweat.

It wasn't fatigue.

The realization didn't arrive as a thought. It landed in his chest, a tight, cold knot that made him straighten his spine. He had been blaming his own head, his own sleep schedule, the lingering weight of things that didn't need names. But his reflexes hadn't failed. The timing had. The field itself was dragging.

He ran the sequence again, slower this time. He focused on the transition from guard to attack, feeling for the exact moment the current should snap into place. It came again. A fractional delay. A missed step in a rhythm that had held true since he was a padawan swinging a wooden practice stick in the Temple gardens. It wasn't in his muscles. It was structural. It was in the walls, in the air, in the space between his boots and the floor. The wrongness was a half-beat lag in the ambient field, steady and undeniable.

Ben stopped moving. He let his arms fall to his sides and just stood there, breathing through the quiet. The isolation pressed in. He could walk to the archives. He could mention it to the shift commander over caf. He could describe the sensation in clean, professional terms and watch the person across the table nod politely while filing him under 'post-campaign adjustment.' They didn't feel it. The other Knights walked the same halls, meditated in the same chambers, and slept through the same nights without missing a beat. If he said it out loud, he sounded like he was chasing ghosts. Or worse, like he was breaking.

He rubbed the back of his neck. The sweat on his palms felt cold. He wasn't broken. He was just the only one listening to a rhythm everyone else had stopped hearing.

The heavy durasteel door at the far end of the salle hissed open.

Luke stood in the threshold, holding a datapad against his chest. He wore his standard travel fatigues, the fabric still crisp, his posture relaxed but ready. The ambient noise of the corridor faded behind him as the door sealed shut. He didn't speak immediately. He just looked at Ben, taking in the sweat, the unlit practice saber on the floor, the set of his shoulders.

Ben felt the tight knot in his chest loosen, just a fraction. Reluctance and relief tangled together. The privacy he'd been guarding was gone, but so was the weight of carrying it alone. He straightened, wiping his hands on his tunic.

Luke's expression was calm, familiar, and utterly serious. Ben knew exactly what it meant.
