The training blade hissed, a blur of violet light that should have been easy to parry. Ben saw the opening, his muscles already coiled to snap his own saber upward in a textbook Shien deflection. 

Then the world slipped.

It wasn't a physical stumble. It was a sudden, jarring dissonance in his perception, like a heavy door slamming shut three rooms away. The timing of the universe stuttered. His guard stayed low, his reaction arriving a heartbeat after the opportunity had vanished. 

The blunt edge of the practice weapon cracked against Ben’s shoulder. The sting was sharp, radiating heat through his tunic, but the bruise to his ego burned worse. 

"Point," the other Knight said, pulling back into a neutral guard. The man’s breathing was heavy, his face slick with sweat under the salle’s harsh overheads. "You're getting slow, Skywalker. Or maybe I’m just getting better."

Ben suppressed a grimace, rubbing the sore spot on his deltoid. This was the third time in a week. He could see the Knight waiting, the violet blade humming with a steady, mechanical drone that felt mocking.

"Maybe both," Ben said, his voice dry. He thumbed the power setting on his hilt, and the blue glow died with a metallic click. "But let's call it for tonight. I’m hitting a wall."

"Another round? You usually don't quit until you've pinned me to the floor."

"Not tonight," Ben said, forced a thin smile. "Lack of sleep. Go get some stim-caf, I’ll lock up."

The Knight shrugged, deactivated his saber, and clipped it to his belt. He didn't push. No one pushed a Skywalker when they looked that tired. He offered a brief nod and headed for the locker rooms, his boots echoing against the polished floor until the heavy doors hissed shut behind him.

Ben stood alone in the center of the salle. The silence of the Jedi Temple was never truly silent; it was a layering of distant machinery, the ventilation's steady rush, and the collective presence of hundreds of Jedi. Usually, it felt like a calm sea. 

Tonight, it felt like a cracked bell.

He didn't ignite his saber. Instead, he began to move. He stepped into the first form of Shii-Cho, his empty hand gripped as if holding a hilt. He lunged, pivoted, and swept his arm through the air. 

His body was perfect. His hamstrings were tight, his weight distribution was exact, and his muscle memory fired with the precision of a droid. He moved faster, transitioning into Ataru, a series of rapid-fire strikes and spins. 

There. 

He felt it again. A momentary void where the Force should have been a guide. It was a shudder in the foundation of things, a vibrational mismatch that had nothing to do with his own exhaustion. It was as if the underlying framework of the galaxy had a loose bolt, rattling every time he tried to lean on it.

He stopped mid-turn, breathing hard. The sweat on his palms felt cold. 

He’d felt something like this before. He remembered the greasy, suffocating presence of Jacen as he’d spiraled into Caedus. He remembered the way the air seemed to thin and sour around Abeloth, as if reality itself were being digested. This wasn't an emotional disturbance. It wasn't the anger of a Sith or the grief of a planet. 

It was structural. 

The Council spoke of the 'post-war echo,' a lingering trauma in the Force from the decade of disasters they’d barely survived. They called it background noise. They told the younger Knights to meditate through the static.

Ben wiped his forehead with the back of his hand. They were wrong. This wasn't static. It was a fracture. And if he was the only one feeling it this clearly, it meant he was either more sensitive to the break, or he was finally losing his grip on the legacy he was supposed to uphold. 

He began the forms again, slower this time. He focused on the gap. He tried to reach into that discordant note, to find the edges of the stutter. Every time he brushed against it, his stomach did a slow, nauseous roll. It was a silence that shouldn't exist—a hollowed-out space in the middle of a crowded room.

He was a Skywalker. He was supposed to fix things. But how did you repair a hole in the way the universe held itself together?

The sound of the salle doors sliding open cut through his concentration. Ben didn't jump, but his hand instinctively twitched toward his belt.

He turned to see a familiar silhouette framed against the corridor light. Luke Skywalker stood in the doorway, his posture straight but his shoulders carrying the familiar weight of a dozen different burdens. He held a datapad in one hand, the screen’s blue light casting sharp shadows across his face. He didn't say a word, but the way he stood there—ready, expectant—sent a different kind of jolt through Ben.

Luke appeared in the doorway with a datapad and an expression Ben knows means a mission.
