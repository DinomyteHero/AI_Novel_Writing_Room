The training saber caught him across the shoulder plate. It wasn’t a hard strike, but it wasn’t supposed to connect at all. Ben’s guard dropped a half-second late. His parry arrived just after Rendell’s blade had already cleared the line. The plastic hummed against his armor, a sharp crack that echoed off the durasteel walls of the salle.

Ben reset his stance. His palms were slick with sweat. He rolled his shoulder, feeling the dull impact where the strike had landed. Third time this week.

Rendell stepped back, lowering his own training blade. “You’re drifting.”

“I’m adjusting,” Ben said. He kept his breathing even. The Force should have given him the read a fraction of a second earlier, a clear signal to pivot his weight and meet the strike. Instead, the ambient field had arrived late. A half-beat lag, like a chord played slightly flat in a room he knew by heart.

Ben thumbed the activation stud on his hilt. The emerald training blade snapped off with a soft hiss. He tucked the hilt into his belt and waved a hand before Rendell could reset his stance.

“Call it a day,” Ben said. “Long shift.”

Rendell frowned, wiping his own brow with the back of his glove. “We can run it again. Your footwork is clean. You’re just slow on the draw.”

“Slow on the draw, fast on the excuses.” Ben offered a crooked smile, the kind he used when he wanted a conversation to end. “I’ll buy you caf in the mess tomorrow. You can tell me how I’m losing my edge.”

Rendell studied him for a second. He powered down his saber, the blue glow dying out. “Don’t overdo it, Ben. You look like you haven’t slept since Tuesday.”

“Tuesday was three days ago,” Ben said. “I’m fine.”

Rendell tossed him a towel from the nearby bench. Ben caught it one-handed. The other Knight headed for the exit, boots scuffing against the polished floor. The door slid open to reveal the corridor beyond. A scrubber droid whirred past, its brushes leaving wet tracks on the stone. Down the hall, the low murmur of late-shift foot traffic filtered through the archway, punctuated by the soft chime of a departing turbolift. Coruscant didn’t sleep. The Temple just changed shifts.

The door slid shut. Silence settled over the salle, thick and recycled-air cool.

Ben wiped his neck and dropped the towel onto the bench. He paced to the center of the mat, bare feet finding the familiar seams in the flooring. He didn’t reach for his saber this time. He didn’t need a blade to feel the rhythm.

He settled into the opening stance of Form I, palms open, weight balanced. Slowly, he moved through the sequence. Step, pivot, draw, strike. The muscle memory held. His body knew exactly where to go, exactly how to shift his center of gravity to meet an imaginary opponent.

But the Force didn’t match.

He pushed into the second movement, feeling for the familiar pull of the field. It arrived late. That half-beat lag again, dragging against his timing. He adjusted, trying to ride the delay, but it wasn’t a delay he could predict. It was a gap. The ambient vibration slipped out of sync, a note held too long before it finally dropped. His foot came down on empty air, and his balance fractured for a fraction of a second before he corrected it.

He stopped.

He stood perfectly still, chest rising and falling. He ran the sequence again. Same lag. He shifted to Form III, defensive, grounded. The lag held. He tried a rapid series of footwork drills, forcing his body to react without thinking. The gap widened. It wasn’t fatigue. Fatigue was a heavy leg, a slow breath, a mind that fogged under pressure. This was different. The ambient field was dragging, pulling half a second behind the rhythm his body had known by reflex for twenty years. A skipped beat in a metronome he’d trusted since childhood.

He lowered his hands. The frustration sat heavy in his ribs, clean and sharp. He wasn’t failing. The ground was moving.

He walked to the edge of the mat, pacing the length of the salle. He kept his hands loose, rolling his wrists, testing the tension in his forearms. If he couldn’t rely on the field to tell him where to step, he’d have to rely on the floor. Simple physics. Old habits. He closed his eyes and listened. The ventilation hummed. A distant door hissed open somewhere on the lower level. Beneath it all, the wrongness waited. It didn’t announce itself. It just pulled the tempo out from under him.

He opened his eyes. The ceiling lights cast long, sharp shadows across the polished floor. He was tired of guessing. Tired of pretending a skipped beat was just a long week. Tired of carrying a problem that made him look unreliable to everyone else.

A soft scrape of boots sounded at the threshold. Ben didn’t turn immediately. He knew the gait.

He straightened, rolling his shoulders out, and looked toward the doorway.

Luke appeared in the doorway with a datapad and an expression Ben knew meant a mission.
