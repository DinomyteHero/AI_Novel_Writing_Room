Kolven filled the viewport in bands of green and blue, cloud cover broken in white curls over a temperate world that looked fit for postcards, tourist brochures, and bad investment pitches. From orbit, it should have been ordinary.

Desh made it sound like a corpse.

"Surface temp variance of eleven degrees inside the same lat band," he said from the sensor console. "Moisture readings stable, except they aren't. Chronometric sync is drifting by point-three seconds every six minutes and getting worse. Bioscan says avian population present. Audio sweep says no birds."

Ben kept one hand on the co-pilot yoke and watched the world turn. "So the scans are lying."

Desh didn't look up. "I'd feel better if they were."

That earned the smallest breath of laughter from Kael, slouched in the jumpseat behind them. "Comforting man, our Desh."

Sera stood braced by the hatch, rigid as a soldier on parade. "The reports from the out-system relay said settlement structures were still visible."

"Structures are there," Desh said. "Street grid too. No power signatures. No active transmitters. Nothing moving bigger than small mammals."

Torin leaned over the back of Ben's chair, his warm voice undercutting the numbers by half a shade. "Green world, blue seas, empty town, broken clocks. I've visited worse."

"You say that about everything," Ben said.

"It's a gift."

The joke landed and died fast. Kolven kept turning below them, too clean from this height. Ben tasted metal at the back of his tongue. Recycled air carried the ship's usual mix of coolant, worn circuitry, old caf, and the faint oil scent that never quite left the cockpit. Nothing in it explained the taste.

He tightened his grip on the yoke.

The Steady Returns dropped lower. Continents widened. A coastline slid past. Inland, a patch of geometry emerged between dark-green growth and pale roads: blocks, straight lines, the fossil of a town.

Desh's console clicked a warning tone.

"Sync drift just doubled," he said. "I'm losing calibration on three environmental channels."

Ben frowned at the instruments. "From atmospheric interference?"

Desh gave him a flat look. "If the atmosphere wants to explain how a clock loses time in chunks, I'm listening."

Ben reached, almost out of habit, toward the Force.

Something answered.

Then nothing he knew followed it.

He drew his hand back from the yoke before he realized he'd tensed hard enough to ache. Not yet. He wanted ground under his boots before he tested that again.

Torin noticed anyway. Of course he did. "Bad?"

Ben kept his eyes on the viewport. "Wrong."

Sera's gaze shifted to him, sharp and measuring, then to the world below. "Land us inside the settlement perimeter. We need the center."

"Happy to hear someone else volunteer the obvious dangerous plan," Kael said.

Ben angled the ship down through a hole in the clouds. The town rose to meet them. Rooflines intact. Towers of vine and flowering brush spilling over duracrete. Streets traceable under green spread. No smoke. No motion in windows. No glint of repulsors, no scavenger camps, nothing that said living people had ever tried to come back.

And no birds.

A place this green should have been noisy enough to rattle the hull.

Instead the cockpit took on that sealed, listening quiet ships got right before landing, except this silence came from outside and pressed inward.

The landing struts hit with a muted thump.

For a moment no one moved.

Then Desh unstrapped. "I'm bringing hard instruments. If my chrono starts telling me it's my birthday, somebody shoot it."

"Can I shoot you instead?" Kael asked.

"You can file a request."

Ben killed the engines and stood. The deck felt level. His inner ear disagreed.

The hatch opened on warm air with a wet vegetal smell underneath it, rich and overripe. Sunlight pooled across a cracked boulevard. A three-story administration block faced them with half its windows gone opaque from moss and dust, the whole western wall drowned in vines. The vines should have been chasing light.

They climbed the shadowed side instead.

Ben stepped down the ramp and the pressure under his sternum deepened, a steady inward push. The duracrete beneath his boots was solid. His balance dipped anyway, that brief stomach-lurch of missing a stair in the dark.

Beside him, Sera had already gone still in the alert way of a soldier reading a battlefield. "There," she said.

Ben followed her line of sight.

Flowers opened in a strip of shade along the lee side of the street, pale violet and fat with color where the sun never touched. Across from them, in full light, the same species hung closed and dull.

Torin moved a step closer to a wall draped in green. "You weren't exaggerating."

"I am wounded," Desh said, coming down the ramp with a compact scanner in one hand and his chrono in the other.

Kael paused at the bottom and tipped his head back toward a tangle of branches overhead. "No nests."

Ben listened. Wind moved leaves. Somewhere far off, water tapped metal in a slow irregular beat. No calls. No wing flutter. Even insects seemed to have second thoughts.

The town square lay two blocks ahead, marked by a dry fountain and a ring of low municipal buildings half buried in growth. They moved toward it in a loose spread, Desh and Kael covering flanks by habit, Sera watching the buildings as if one might remember how to be occupied.

Ben kept trying not to reach.

The Force had always been there before he consciously touched it. A background certainty, not loud, not constant in any dramatic sense, just present the way balance was present, or gravity, or the knowledge that one action carried a different weight from another. He didn't have to think about it to stand in relation to it.

Here, relation kept slipping.

He passed a storefront whose transparisteel was split by roots. A vine on the frame uncurled while he watched, tendril easing out, then drawing back the other way, reversing course with no hurry at all. Not fast enough to count as a trick of the eye. Slow enough to be undeniable.

Desh stopped dead. "Did that just—"

"Yes," Ben said.

"Good," Desh muttered. "Wanted confirmation before I started bargaining with my childhood religion."

"You had a childhood religion?" Torin asked.

"Three. My mother liked options."

That got a short huff out of Ben. Tension cracked for half a second and sealed again.

They entered the square.

The fountain at its center held a bowl of black water furred over with green. Ben watched a leaf touch the surface and drift inward against the ripple moving outward. Around the square, vines crawled across facades in layers, some threading into open doors, others peeling off brick and creeping back down toward the ground. Shade and sunlight had traded jobs. Growth followed rules from somewhere else.

Sera circled once, slow and intent. "This was deliberate."

Kael looked at her. "Meaning?"

"Not spread. Placement." She pointed to the square, then the streets feeding it. "A test pattern. Containment edges. Observation lines."

Ben looked again and saw it. The denser wrongness centered here, then feathered outward in streets and blocks. Veraine hadn't merely damaged Kolven. She had used it.

His mouth went dry.

If she could do this to a settlement world to see what happened—

"Ben," Torin said quietly.

He knew why. Everyone else had already turned toward him without making it obvious. Jedi business. He hated that phrase and lived inside it anyway.

Ben stepped to the dead fountain and planted his boots. Warm air touched his face. Leaves scraped stone nearby. The pressure in his chest set hard as a fist.

He reached for the Force.

It came at once.

Presence. Power. Vast, immediate, close enough to drown in.

And no axis.

No lift toward one choice or warning against another. No fine grain, no familiar contour, no inner alignment clicking against what lay outside him. Every trained instinct went out and found nothing to catch on. The ground of sense dropped away.

Ben's fingers flexed once at his sides.

The Force was there. Raw and open and answerable. He could touch it. He could move with it.

But there was no shape to the touch. No direction inside the answer. Light and dark did not vanish into each other; they were simply absent as bearings. Comparison itself had gone missing.

His breath roughened.

Hollowness ran through the bones of his arms, a stripped-out feeling, as if something that had always lined them had been pulled free while leaving the flesh intact. The square tilted without moving. His own thoughts rose loud in the space the silence left behind.

Take more. Push harder. If there's no warning, there's no limit.

He opened his eyes. He hadn't noticed closing them.

Sera stood three meters away, hands loose but ready. Torin had shifted half a step forward. Kael watched with that unreadable stillness of his. Desh looked openly alarmed and tried to hide it by checking instruments.

"Ben?" Torin asked.

Ben swallowed. "Don't." His voice came out flatter than he intended. "Don't use it unless you have to."

Torin's brows drew together. "That bad?"

Ben looked at him and searched for the right words. None of the usual ones fit. "You can reach it," he said. "It reaches back. That's all."

For once, Torin had no answer ready.

Sera moved to the fountain's rim and extended a hand, careful, controlled. Ben saw the exact moment she touched it. Her shoulders locked. Not dramatic. Worse than dramatic. A fighter discovering the floor under a stance had been removed.

She lowered her hand at once.

"There is no ordering principle here," she said, each word clipped hard. Formal voice, tighter than before. "No correction. No restraint."

Kael rubbed the center of his chest with two fingers, almost absentmindedly. "Well, that's excellent."

Desh glanced up from his chrono. "You want excellent, try this." He held it out.

The second hand moved. Stopped. Jumped ahead two marks. Held. Then dragged backward by half a tick before settling and moving on.

No one spoke.

Desh blew air through his nose. "That's a mechanical movement. Springs and gears. I trust springs and gears. Springs and gears and I have a working relationship."

"Break the relationship gently," Kael said.

His hand was still at his chest.

Ben noticed. "What is it?"

Kael looked down, surprised to be asked. "Nothing I can point to." His mouth pulled sideways. "That's the problem, I think."

Sera's attention snapped to him.

Kael shrugged one shoulder, uncomfortable under it. "Feels like I got news somebody died. Only there's no somebody." He dropped his hand. "No face. No name. Just the hit."

The words settled over the square heavier than they should have. Desh's expression changed by a fraction. Not agreement. Recognition.

Ben's stomach knotted. Desh with his drifting chrono, Kael with grief that had nowhere to land, Sera standing too straight because if she bent even slightly she might test the Force again out of reflex and find that same emptiness. The zone hurt people whether they could name the wound or not.

He made his hands stay open at his sides.

The urge to reach again came hard and stupid. Probe farther. Define the edges. Force the unknown into a map. His whole life had trained him to meet uncertainty by leaning into the Force until the shape of the right next move emerged.

There was no right next move waiting here.

Only his own mind.

He could think of a dozen uses for raw power without conscience threaded through it. Efficient uses. Clean ones. Decisive ones. The thoughts came quick now, unbuffered, stripped of the quiet friction that usually met them before they became intent.

Veraine had stood in this.

Veraine had chosen more.

A vine on the fountain's edge crept backward over stone while Ben watched, green tip retreating into its own coil. Somewhere nearby, water tapped metal again. The skipped beat of Desh's chrono sounded too loud in the square.

Ben stared at the dead black pool and heard himself think with nothing between the thought and the hearing. No softening. No pressure from elsewhere. No instinctive recoil that wasn't his own.

The thoughts were his. All of them. Including the ones the Force usually softened.
