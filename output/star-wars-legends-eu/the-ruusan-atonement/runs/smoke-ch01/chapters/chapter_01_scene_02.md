Luke's office smelled like old flimsiplast and recycled air and the particular kind of stillness that accumulated in rooms where important decisions got made quietly. Ben had been in this room a hundred times. He still paused in the doorway for half a second every time, the way you paused before stepping into cold water.

"Close it behind you," Luke said.

Ben closed the door.

His father was seated behind the desk, the holocomm dark, a datapad face-down on the surface in front of him. He looked like he'd been waiting, which meant he'd been doing something else entirely and had set it aside the moment Ben's footsteps reached the corridor. That particular quality of attention — present before it was visible — was one of the things Ben had grown up unable to replicate and had spent years pretending not to notice.

The office was lit warmer than the Temple's standard evening cycle. Luke's Force presence filled the room the way heat filled a space, vast and familiar, the closest thing to home Ben had left in the galaxy. Under it, faint but persistent, the wrongness was there — a sour note running beneath the warmth, present even here, in the most Force-saturated building on Coruscant.

Ben sat in the chair across the desk without being invited.

Luke slid the datapad across the surface without preamble.

It was a survey compilation — seventeen flagged reports, the header stamps running from six months ago to the current week. Ben picked it up. The blue glow of the display caught the edge of the desk as he tilted it toward him. The readings were sourced from six different survey teams, three different Outer Rim sectors, and one deep-space relay station that had been dormant since before the Yuuzhan Vong War. All flagged for the same anomaly classification: *Force distortion, unspecified origin, no proximate cause identified.*

Ben scrolled.

The third report was from a Knight he knew slightly, Maren Sollis, who had a reputation for careful documentation. She'd written: *Distortion presents as directional absence rather than active interference. No corruption signature. No dark side resonance. Recommend follow-up survey.* The fourth report used different language and arrived at the same description. The seventh was a single line: *Something is wrong with the Force here and I don't know how to write it down.*

He zoomed in on reports nine, twelve, and fifteen. The coordinates formed a rough corridor — Outer Rim, running roughly spinward. The anomaly readings in those three were the strongest, the language the most strained, each surveyor reaching for a different metaphor and landing in the same territory. An absence. A gap. A place where something should be and wasn't.

Ben set the datapad on the desk.

"You want a survey team," he said.

"I want you," Luke said. "Survey team is optional."

There it was. Ben looked at his father across the desk and waited.

"The reports started six months ago," Luke said. "I've been watching the pattern develop. The distortion is directional — it has a bearing, and the bearing has been consistent. Three of those teams recommended follow-up. I haven't sent anyone back because I wanted a complete picture first." He paused. "I have a complete enough picture now."

"What's your read on it?"

Luke was quiet for a moment. Not hesitating — considering, which was a different thing. "Old wound," he said. "The Outer Rim has places where the Force doesn't sit right. Ruusan. The Maw. Katarr. This reads like something similar — a location where some historical event left a mark." He met Ben's eyes. "Probably not urgent. Worth investigating."

*Probably not urgent.* Ben kept his face neutral.

"I feel it here," he said. "Right now, in this room. Not a location effect."

Luke's expression didn't change, exactly, but something behind it did. "So do I," he said.

The admission landed with more weight than Ben had expected. He'd been half-braced for his father to say he didn't, to confirm that Ben was the only one carrying this particular piece of dread. Instead Luke had said *so do I* with the same calm he used to discuss weather patterns and supply manifests, and Ben understood: his father felt it, and had categorized it, and had arrived at a different conclusion.

"You think it's a wound," Ben said.

"I think it's consistent with a wound. Old damage surfacing." Luke's tone was measured. "The Force has long memory. Sometimes things that were sealed don't stay sealed."

That was true. Ben knew it was true. He also knew what old damage felt like — he'd felt the Force around Myrkr, around the places where Centerpoint had been, and none of them had felt like this. Old wounds had weight, gravity, the specific heaviness of something that had once been violent and had settled into scar tissue. What he was feeling now was absence without weight. Not a scar. A missing piece.

He thought of Jacen, near the end, when the darkness in him had stopped feeling like darkness and started feeling like nothing at all. He thought of Abeloth's wake — not the screaming, but the silence after. He thought of Vestara's face the last time he'd reached for her in the Force and found the connection scraped clean.

He didn't say any of that.

"I'll take the survey," he said.

"The corridor runs through six systems. I'd suggest starting at the spinward end and working in — the strongest readings are toward the center." Luke pulled the datapad back and added a route overlay, then slid it across again. "Standard survey protocols. Report when you have something concrete."

Ben looked at the route. It ran directly along the bearing he'd been tracking for three weeks. He'd known it would.

"Anything specific you want documented?"

"Whatever you find." Luke's voice carried something careful in it — not reluctance, exactly, but awareness. "Use your judgment."

Ben nodded. He stood, picked up the datapad, and moved to the window. Coruscant's nightside spread out below the Temple in its usual organized blaze, a hundred million lit windows and transit lanes and the slow crawl of upper-atmosphere traffic. From up here it looked clean. Ordered. Everything in its assigned place.

The wrongness was there in the glass, reflected back at him — not visible, just present, the way you were aware of a room's temperature without looking at a gauge.

Behind him, Luke hadn't moved.

There was a version of this conversation where Ben turned around and said *I think this is more than a wound and I shouldn't go alone.* He was aware of it the way you were aware of a door in your peripheral vision. He didn't turn around.

"I'll file a departure plan in the morning," he said.

"Good." A pause. "Ben."

He looked back.

Luke's expression had shifted into something that wasn't the Grand Master. It lasted about two seconds. "Be careful."

"I will," Ben said.

He set the datapad on the edge of the desk, picked it back up, and headed for the door. Luke didn't call him back. Didn't suggest a partner, didn't revise the assignment, didn't say the thing that was sitting in the space between them like a third person in the room.

Neither of them did.
