Ben's guard dropped a half-second late. The training blade cracked across his shoulder with a solid thump that rang through the salle. He staggered one step, then caught himself before the momentum carried him into the wall.

The other Knight lowered his saber. "Got you again."

Ben thumbed the activation switch. The blade hissed out. He rolled his shoulder once, testing the sting. "Yeah. You did."

The Knight's stance eased, but his eyes stayed sharp. "Rematch? You've been off all week. I can go easy if you want."

"No need." Ben forced a grin that felt borrowed from someone else's face. "Call it fatigue. Long week. I'll buy the next round in the refectory if you keep quiet about it."

The Knight shrugged, deactivated his own weapon, and headed for the door. "Your secret's safe. Get some rest, Skywalker. Temple needs you sharp."

Bootsteps faded down the corridor. A cleaning droid whirred past in the distance, its brushes whispering against duracrete. The salle doors slid shut with a soft click, sealing Ben inside with the echo of that missed beat still vibrating in his teeth.

He paced the length of the room, lightsaber hilt heavy in his palm. The walls rose high, carved with the old reliefs of Jedi from centuries past. Their stone faces watched without judgment. Ben stopped at the far end, rolled his neck, and began the first form.

No ignition. Just the body moving through positions it had known since he was small enough to trip over his own feet. Step, pivot, reach, guard. Muscle memory should have carried him. Instead the half-beat lag in the ambient field pulled at every transition. His hand closed a fraction late on an imaginary hilt. His foot planted a hair too heavy. The rhythm he had drilled into bone fractured again and again.

He pushed through the second form. Sweat cooled along his spine. The distant murmur of late-shift foot traffic filtered through the walls—Knights and staff heading for quarters or night duties. A lift chimed somewhere down the hall. Normal sounds. Ben kept moving.

The third form brought the stumble. His balance tipped as the lag stole the timing on a turn. He caught the wall with one hand, breath steady but jaw locked tight. This wasn't residue from Abeloth or the other messes that still clung to the Order like smoke. Those had faded months ago. This was new. Persistent. And it had just cost him three touches in front of a peer who would mention it in the duty logs if he wasn't careful.

Ben straightened. The salle felt larger now, emptier. He set the training saber on the bench and ran the fourth form again, slower. Eyes closed. The half-beat lag followed him like a shadow half a step behind the music. Every time the field should have met his intent, it arrived late. Not enough to see with the naked eye. Enough to make a Knight miss a block he had never missed before.

He finished the sequence and stopped. Sweat trickled down his temple. The stone at his back felt cool through the thin tunic. For a long minute he simply stood there, listening to the delayed pulse of the place. The Temple had always hummed in time with him. Tonight it answered a fraction off, and the gap widened with every breath.

Frustration coiled tight under his ribs. He had spent the week telling himself it was nothing. A trick of overwork. The kind of thing any Knight might feel after back-to-back missions and too many late nights in the archives. But Knights didn't lose exchanges they had won a thousand times. They didn't feel the world slip out of joint during forms older than their bloodline.

The thought brought a dry twist to his mouth. Measuring himself against legacy again. As if the name alone should have insulated him from a simple timing error. His mother would have laughed at that. His father would have told him to stop thinking and start fixing it.

Ben exhaled. The isolation pressed heavier now. The other Knights saw fatigue. He saw something eating away at the edges of what he could trust about himself. Admitting it aloud meant reports. Questions. A conversation that would end with someone senior patting him on the shoulder and suggesting meditation cycles he had already tried. Easier to carry it alone. Safer.

He pushed off the wall. One more form. The lag hit harder this time, yanking the beat out from under his stance like a rug pulled from beneath his feet. He froze mid-motion, arms half-raised, and let the frustration settle into something closer to acceptance. It was real. It was here. And pretending otherwise had just become a luxury he could no longer afford.

The doors whispered open behind him.

Ben turned. Luke stood in the doorway, datapad in hand, expression already sliding into the focused lines that meant off-world business and no time to waste.
