The training blade caught him across the shoulder, and Ben stepped back.

Not a dramatic stumble. Just a step. The kind that said *acknowledged* without saying anything else. He brought his saber up in a belated guard that would have been useful half a second ago, then let the tip drop toward the floor.

"Again?" his partner asked. Davan, third year as a Knight, decent footwork, the sort of fighter who won by patience rather than flash. He was breathing harder than Ben was, which would have felt better if Ben had won.

"I'm good." Ben deactivated the training saber and the low buzz died between them. "Call it."

Davan gave him a look — not suspicious, just the ordinary concern of someone who'd watched a sparring partner take three hits in a row — and then clipped his own saber to his belt. "Early morning tomorrow anyway."

"Yeah."

The door hissed shut behind him. Ben stood in the middle of the salle and listened to the sound of his own breathing even out.

Distant, down the corridor: a cleaning droid's soft mechanical scrape, the lift chime on the far side of the floor, the muffled voices of the late shift carrying on without him. The Temple at this hour had its own particular quality, the kind of alive-but-quieting that Ben had always found useful for thinking. Right now it wasn't helping.

He walked to the equipment wall and hung the training saber on its peg. Rolled his shoulder once — the hit had landed across the muscle, nothing structural, nothing that would matter in the morning. That wasn't the problem.

The problem was the lag.

It had started six days ago. Not a disturbance in any sense the Order's current vocabulary covered cleanly; the Abeloth aftermath had left everyone's Force sensitivity running a little hot, a little raw, and the consensus among the senior Knights was that the residual turbulence was settling. Ben had nodded along with that consensus because it was easier than explaining what he was actually feeling, which was — not turbulence. Not rawness. More like reaching for a handhold that was exactly where it should be and finding it a half-beat late. Not missing. Just *late*. The handhold was there. The timing was wrong.

Three times this week it had cost him a beat in the salle. Tonight it had cost him one in front of someone else.

He pulled his tunic straight and stayed in the middle of the room.

The smart move was to go back to his quarters and sleep. He'd logged more salle hours this week than any two Knights combined, which was either dedication or avoidance, and he was self-aware enough to know which one it was. The problem with sleep was that sleep didn't tell him anything.

He ignited his saber.

The blue blade threw familiar light across the salle's pale walls. Ben settled into opening stance — Shii-Cho, the first form, the one he'd drilled so deep into reflex that he could run it half-asleep and still hit every mark — and moved.

The form was clean. His body knew it the way it knew how to breathe. The blade tracked through its angles, and for the first eight counts Ben felt nothing except the good mechanical satisfaction of muscle memory executing without interference.

Then the ninth count.

The Force presence in the room — the ambient field that every trained Knight learned to use the way a pilot used peripheral vision, not consciously, just *there* — arrived late. Not absent. Late. A half-beat behind the moment his body needed it, like a second voice coming in slightly after the downbeat. His weight shifted to compensate, and the compensation threw the line of the form, and he had to stop.

He lowered the blade.

Stood there with sweat cooling on the back of his neck and the distant cleaning droid still scraping away down the corridor and the form half-finished in the air around him like an accusation.

*Fatigue* was the word he'd been using. It was a good word. It explained everything without requiring him to say anything uncomfortable to anyone who might write it in a report. *Fatigue* meant he was working hard and needed rest, which was true. *Fatigue* meant the solution was sleep and a lighter schedule for a week, which was the kind of solution that didn't require a conversation with his father.

The problem with *fatigue* was that it didn't explain the ninth count.

He walked to the wall and put his back against it. Deactivated the blade. The salle went back to its nighttime quiet, the pale walls absorbing the last of the light, and Ben looked at the ceiling and tried to be honest with himself for thirty seconds, which was harder than it sounded.

The lag was real. It had been real for six days. It wasn't getting better, and the spar tonight was the first time it had been visible to another person, and if it happened again in a context where *fatigue* wasn't a plausible cover story, someone was going to ask a question he didn't have a clean answer to. He was a Knight. Knights were reliable. That was the entire job — show up, be reliable, do the work. His father had built a new Order on that premise, and Ben had spent five years proving he could hold up his end of it without the Skywalker name doing the heavy lifting.

Five years, and now he was losing timing drills to a disturbance he couldn't name.

He pushed off the wall and ran the form again from the beginning. Didn't ignite the blade this time — just the body, just the movement, testing whether the problem was in the Force or in him. The first eight counts were clean. The ninth count: the lag, the compensation, the stumble.

Not him. Not fatigue.

He stopped at count nine and held the position. His arm was extended at the angle the form required, his weight distributed the way ten years of drilling had made automatic, and the Force was *there*, he could feel it, but it was arriving like an echo instead of a signal. Like the room was taking a breath after he'd already exhaled.

He'd been handling this privately because private problems stayed private until they became someone else's problem. That was practical. That was sensible. That was also, he acknowledged, a reflex he'd inherited from a family that had historically been terrible at asking for help until the galaxy was on fire.

He lowered his arm.

The corridor light shifted — the salle door opening — and Ben turned.

Luke stood in the doorway with a datapad in one hand and the expression Ben had been seeing since he was twelve years old, the one that meant *pack light, we're leaving soon*.
