The training blade caught him across the shoulder, a solid smack of energy and sound.

Ben's own blade dropped a half-second later, his guard arriving late to a place it should have been by reflex. The hum of both training sabers filled the salle, then cut to silence as he thumbed his off. A dull ache bloomed under his collarbone where the padding hadn't fully dispersed the impact. Third time this week.

"Point," his sparring partner said, a Rodian Knight whose name Ben kept forgetting. The Rodian's large black eyes blinked once, a gesture of polite finality. He deactivated his own blade.

"Yeah," Ben said. He rolled his shoulder, working out the sting. His skin felt hot under his tunic. "Good match."

It wasn't. He'd missed the parry. Not a complex feint, not a superior technique—just the basic Ataru riposte he'd drilled since he was twelve. His body had moved, but the Force's timing, the ambient rhythm of the space around him, had arrived late. Like listening to a transmission with a persistent half-second lag. He heard the note, but the beat was already gone.

"Again?" the Rodian asked, already settling into a ready stance.

"Next time," Ben said, clipping his training saber to his belt. "Long week."

The Rodian nodded, a Human mannerism he'd adopted. "The Vexis cleanup reports. I heard." He didn't press. He gave a short bow, collected his water canteen from the bench by the wall, and left.

The salle doors hissed shut. The ambient soundscape shifted: the distant metallic whir of a cleaning droid in the corridor, the low thrum of the Temple's environmental systems, the faint echo of boots on polished stone floors somewhere several levels down. The late shift. The Temple never truly slept.

Ben stood in the center of the empty training floor. The ache in his shoulder was a punctuation mark. He ran a hand through his hair, sweat-damp at the temples. *Long week.* An understatement. The Vexis mission had been a mess—a collapsed smuggling ring, too many blaster burns on civilian housing, the sour tang of fear in the air for days after. The Council had called it a successful intervention. Ben remembered the way the Force had felt afterward: unsettled, like a pond after a stone's throw. Everyone said it was residue. Lingering emotional trauma from the civilians, maybe some dark-side artifact the smugglers had been hoarding. The galaxy had been through a lot. The Force was allowed to be sore.

But that was two weeks ago. The wrongness hadn't faded. It had settled in.

He began to pace. The smooth floor was cool through his bootsoles. He'd been blaming fatigue, the accumulated grind of back-to-back assignments, the low-grade headache that came from reading too many incident reports under bad lighting. Skywalkers weren't supposed to get tired, the narrative went. They were supposed to be relentless. He'd leaned into that, let the assumption do the talking for him.

His pacing turned into movement. Without thinking, he fell into the opening sequence of Form III, his body tracing the familiar arcs and pivots through the air. No blade. Just the geometry of it, the muscle memory deeper than thought. Step, turn, arm extending in a precise block, weight shifting forward for the counter.

His foot landed a fraction late.

He didn't stumble, but the rhythm fractured. The sequence demanded a fluid transition from the block into a spinning sidestep. His body executed the spin, but the *timing* was off. The salle's own Force presence—the accumulated calm of generations of meditation and practice, the architectural echo of peace—usually flowed around him, a current he could feel and match. Now it felt like wading through still water that pushed back a half-beat after he moved. He completed the spin, but it was a correction, not a continuation.

He stopped. Sweat cooled on the back of his neck.

Fatigue didn't do that. Fatigue made you clumsy, made your arms heavy, fogged your thoughts. It didn't steal the beat between intention and action. It didn't make the world feel like a badly synced holodrama.

He started the sequence again, slower this time. Deliberate. He focused on the physical mechanics: the tension in his calf as he pushed off, the swing of his arm, the pivot of his hip. He pushed the Force to the background, treating it like atmospheric noise.

Halfway through, the lag snatched at him again. It wasn't a push or a pull. It was an absence, a silent gap where the expected resonance should have been. His next step hit the floor a hair too soon, throwing his balance forward. He had to drop his stance to recover, his palm slapping lightly against the mat.

He straightened up, breathing evenly. His heart thumped a steady, annoyed rhythm against his ribs. *Alright.* Not fatigue.

He walked to the wall, leaning his shoulder blades against the cool permacrete. The salle was vast and quiet, illuminated by soft overhead panels that cast long shadows from the equipment racks. He could hear the cleaning drum moving closer, its brushes whirring against the corridor floor outside.

He wanted, suddenly and sharply, for someone to walk in and see it. Not the stumble—the cause. For another Knight to frown and say, *You feel that? That lag?* So he wouldn't have to be the one to say it first. So he wouldn't have to voice a concern that sounded, even in his own head, like the paranoid worry of a Knight who'd seen one too many cosmic horrors. *The Force is out of sync for me.* It sounded like a flaw in his perception, not a fact about the galaxy.

He was a Skywalker. His perceptions were supposed to be the baseline.

A dry, humorless smirk touched his lips. There it was. The measuring stick. He could almost hear his mother's voice, pragmatic and unforgiving: *If your instrument is broken, Ben, you fix it. You don't blame the orchestra.*

But what if the orchestra was the problem?

The doors at the far end of the salle hissed open.

Ben pushed off the wall, turning. Luke stood in the doorway, a datapad in one hand. He wore his usual simple tunic and trousers, but his posture was the one Ben recognized—shoulders set, focus already extending past the room, into the galaxy. It was the mission-ready posture. The *we-have-work* posture.

Luke's eyes found him across the distance. He didn't smile, but his expression wasn't stern. It was assessing, specific. He lifted the datapad slightly.

Ben felt the reluctant hope uncurl in his chest, a live thing. A problem he could move toward was better than a wrongness he could only wait inside.
