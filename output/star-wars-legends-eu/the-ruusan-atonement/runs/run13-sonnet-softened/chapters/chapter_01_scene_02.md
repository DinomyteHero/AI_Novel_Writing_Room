The datapad clicked against the desk.

Luke said nothing. Seventeen survey reports glowed amber in the blue light—his silent challenge: *tell me I'm wrong to worry.*

Ben lifted it.

The office never changed. Functional chairs. A scarred desk. A holocomm wearing a pale ring into the durasteel. Real books on real shelves. Beyond the window, Coruscant’s afternoon smog burned copper. The Force here felt like standing too close to a star—vast, warm, his father’s gravity pulling at everything.

Underneath, a sour note. Off-key. Patient. Waiting.

He scrolled.

The flags defied classification. No dark-side taint. No nexus bleed. No residue of mass death he knew too well. The readings showed absence wearing presence like a mask—holes instruments built to detect everything insisted were real.

He zoomed in on three. Thumb hovering over the last.

Same sourness. Captured six months ago by Knight Feliss: *anomalous Force-field degradation, no visible cause, no dark side contamination, recommend follow-up.* No follow-up happened. The report sat in the amber queue until Luke fished it out.

"Seventeen," Ben said.

"Confirmed." Luke’s tone stayed level. "More exist. Surveyors blamed calibration drift."

"Where?"

"Outer Rim. Ruusan Corridor. Triangulation’s messy. I need boots."

Ben set the datapad down—not shoved, just placed. The careful border of someone who’d learned not to grab for anything. "You feel it too."

Luke chose his words. "Yes."

"What flavor?"

Outside, a transport banked through the haze, running lights winking. "Like a tide slipping out," Luke said. "Slow. Natural. After Ruusan. After Malachor. The Force kept breathing. Impressions fade across centuries. This feels like the tail of something ancient."

Ben stared at the pad.

Darrpa Sector. Six months back. A tide, maybe. To him, the same shape had cracked Jacen’s presence years ago—hairline at first, then structural. He’d learned to notice the quiet cracks.

He kept that to himself.

"Available Knights?"

"Fewer than I’d like." Luke met his eyes. "Cato Neimoidia still burns. Three teams on the Hydian Way. One Knight left who reads anomalies, isn’t deployed, and knows the difference between echo and escalation."

The words hung between them.

Ben felt his shoulders square—not quite unconscious. The way his body always responded when Luke handed him something sharp. "Diplomatic way to hand me a survey run."

"I’ve had practice."

The joke fell flat. Ben stood, moved to the window. Far below, Coruscant kept being Coruscant—a metal hive that had outlasted every flag. From up here, it looked eternal. He knew better.

Luke’s presence filled the room behind him. Warm star he’d steered by since childhood. Under it, the sour note hummed on, unresolved.

Dad heard tide. Ben heard wall getting ready to fall.

He turned. "Outer Rim. Standard parameters?"

"Standard. Check-ins every three days. Contact at Terminus—a pilot named Rolan. Knows the Corridor."

"Timeline?"

"Follow the readings." Luke’s gaze flicked, almost father. "If it spikes, I want the ping."

"You’ll get it."

He would. He just wasn’t sure they’d define *spike* the same way.

Luke held the look a breath longer, then nodded—Grand Master mode re-engaged.

Ben didn’t suggest a partner. Luke didn’t ask.

The sour note kept humming beneath the Temple’s warmth. Neither of them spoke it aloud.
