The hangar reeked of hot metal and stale recycled air—the lingering stench of a thousand ships that had come and gone without ever quite taking the smell with them.

Ben ran his fingers along the port side of his scout, checking each fuel coupling by hand despite the diagnostic flashing green. Old habit. He pressed the connection point, confirmed the seal, moved to the next.

The datapad rested in his left hand.

He hadn’t opened the Jedi roster yet. Meant to. The Council’s available list would have six or seven names—Knights between assignments, Outer Rim veterans, Corridor specialists. He knew most of them. Could call any.

His thumb swiped across the screen.

Twelve names. Amber tags. He scrolled through them like a report he already knew was wrong—searching for something to disprove, finding nothing. Dorset had survived three trauma-site surveys and would arrive convinced the readings were residue. Pallav would demand a preliminary assessment before breaking orbit. Reen—solid Jedi, sharp instincts—but she’d defer to her Master, who sat on the subcommittee that had labeled the anomaly a background echo.

Ben closed the roster.

The datapad weighed more now, like it might drag his arm down if he held it much longer. He set it on the deck plating and moved to the repulsor vent housing, crouched, checked the grille. Clean. His fingers found his encrypted contacts without his eyes following.

The relief hit like Corellian whiskey—sharp, fleeting, gone before he could taste it.

---

Desh answered on the third tone.

"Rolan." The long-range channel flattened his voice, compressed it. Behind it, the ambient hum of a mid-grade cantina leaked through—clinking ceramics, overlapping conversation.

"It’s Ben." He hauled himself aboard, stowed his pack with unnecessary force. "You busy?"

"Deeply." A pause. "Watching a man try to convince a Rodian his cargo manifest isn’t forged. It’s almost art."

Ben wedged into the cockpit and initiated the power-up sequence. Overhead switches, left to right. Repulsorlifts to standby. "I need a co-pilot."

"For."

"Outer Rim run. Off-book." He pulled up departure protocols and opened a channel to Temple Control. "Week, maybe two."

The cantina noise dimmed. Desh had moved somewhere quieter.

"Off-book as in paperwork-later, or off-book as in I should probably not tell anyone?"

"Somewhere in between."

"Generous." Another pause. Longer. "Where?"

"Terminus Station first. Then the Corridor."

Silence stretched between them, taut as a tripwire. Ben watched the repulsorlift readout climb toward operational range. He didn’t fill the gap.

"When?" Desh asked.

"I’m leaving in ten."

"Of course you are." The dryness looped back into something almost fond. "I’ll need to pack."

"You’re already packed."

"I’m always packed. That’s not the point." A door creaked. Footsteps on duracrete. "You could give a man twelve hours."

*Temple Control, Jedi Scout Seven-Seven-Aurek, requesting departure clearance, Bay Fourteen.* Ben subvocalized it into the comm, got a confirmation tone, fed the flight path to the nav-computer. "So that’s a yes."

"That’s a yes." The cantina sounds vanished. "Terminus in—what, forty standard hours?"

"Thirty-eight if the Perlemian’s clear."

"It won’t be. Bulk freighter convoy at the Hydian junction. Forty-two."

Ben’s mouth twitched before he could stop it. "Forty-two."

"Don’t be late." Desh cut the channel.

Ben held the comm a moment before setting it down. The unasked questions hung between them, a language they’d spoken for years without voicing the important words. *Off-book* carried weight from a time when it had meant the difference between surviving the night or not. Desh understood without details.

It was the most honest conversation Ben would have all day.

---

*Scout Seven-Seven-Aurek, Bay Fourteen, you are cleared for departure. Maintain two-hundred-meter separation through the inner lanes.*

"Copy." Ben engaged the repulsorlifts. The ship lifted clean, no hesitation. He nudged it toward the hangar mouth.

The Temple hangar buzzed at mid-afternoon. Three other ships cycled through—a Jedi transport crammed with padawans visible through the viewport, a diplomatic shuttle from the Outer Council, a maintenance sled crawling across the deck at half speed. Ben flew past without stopping. The hangar crew moved around him with practiced indifference. Just another departure.

He cleared the bay doors and hit the atmospheric thrusters.

Coruscant sprawled beneath him. From this angle—climbing hard, banking east toward the orbital lanes—the city looked like circuitry, every surface threaded with lines and nodes and the ceaseless motion of traffic. Vast. Patient. Indifferent to any one ship leaving.

He set his course and let the autopilot handle the lane merges. Traffic thinned as he climbed. The planet’s comm chatter faded to static, then to the clean silence of low orbit.

The wrongness remained. It had clung to him all morning—the sour, structural ache that defied direction and resisted centering. He’d learned not to fight it. He breathed around it the way you breathe around a bruise.

The nav-computer confirmed the Perlemian jump point. Thirty seconds to optimal position.

Ben reached for the hyperdrive controls.

*Scout Seven-Seven-Aurek, this is Orbital Control. You are cleared for hyperspace departure. Safe travels.*

"Copy. Departing." He pulled the levers.

The stars stretched.

For the first second—the standard compression of light into lines, the familiar non-sensation of hyperspace—everything was normal. The wrongness blurred at the edges the way Force impressions usually did. Hyperspace had always been a kind of static, the Force present but muffled, like a signal through interference.

Then the instruments flickered.

Not catastrophically. A single sweep across the sensor board—the readings stuttered, recalibrated, settled. The nav-computer chirped once and held its course.

And the wrongness didn’t fade.

It sharpened.

Ben’s hand stayed on the controls. The sensation shifted from a diffuse ache to something with edges, something with direction. The way a loose tooth suddenly hurt in one specific place when pressed. The way a sound you couldn’t locate became a sound you could track.

It was pulling.

Not toward Coruscant. Not toward any of the Jedi sites behind him. Forward. Along the bearing the nav-computer had already plotted. Out toward the Corridor, the Outer Rim, toward whatever was generating seventeen amber flags and a silence that shouldn’t exist.

He’d thought he was following the mission parameters.

The starlines stretched around him, blue-white and steady, and Ben kept his hands on the controls and didn’t move, while the wrongness pulled like a needle finding north.
