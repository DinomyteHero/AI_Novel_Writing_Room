The datapad landed on the desk with a soft *thud*.

"Seventeen flagged reports," Luke said. "Six weeks."

Ben picked it up. Blue light crawled across his fingers as he scrolled—survey vectors, atmospheric readings, deep-space telemetry from a dozen ships along the Outer Rim corridor. Each bore the same mark: *Anomalous Force resonance. Consistent with post-Abeloth residual disturbance. No action required.*

"They're all filing it the same way," Ben said.

"The same way you filed it."

Ben's thumb stilled on the screen. "I filed it three times. There's a difference."

"There is." Luke reclined in his chair. "Also, I heard about the sparring session."

"Good news travels fast."

"Korren mentioned it in her practice log. She was worried." A beat. "I told her you were probably fine."

"Probably." Ben returned to the screen. "Thanks for the confidence."

He kept scrolling. Past routine deviations, past readings that could be stellar drift or gravitational noise, until he found the three that matched what he'd felt in the salle—the same flat, sour note, like biting into something ripe that turned rotten. He zoomed, cross-referencing coordinates.

There. And there. And there.

"These three," he said, turning the datapad. "Different ships, different survey windows. Two weeks apart. Look at the coordinates."

Luke leaned in.

"They're not random scatter," Ben said. "They're on a line." He traced the bearing with one finger—southwest, deep into the Outer Rim, past the last reliable charts. "Whatever's generating this has a source."

Luke studied the screen. "I've felt it too."

Ben's grip tightened. "Since when?"

"A ripple," Luke said. "An echo of something old." He folded his hands. "My first thought was Malachor. Or Nathema—some old wound reopening. The dark side leaves scars."

"That's not what this is."

Luke's gaze lifted—not challenging, but listening.

Ben stood. The office shrank around the shape of what he needed to explain. Luke's Force presence filled the space—vast, warm, familiar as gravity. Right now, it only made the sour note underneath worse by contrast. Even here, in the Temple's administrative heart, it waited. Faint. Patient. Wrong.

"Dark side corruption has a texture," he said. "You know what I mean—thick. Pressurized. Like the Force compensating for damage." He turned back. "This isn't that. No pressure. No weight. More like—" He hunted for the word. "Like reaching for a wall and finding air."

Behind him, Luke's chair creaked.

"I've felt that twice before," Ben said. "Once when Jacen was—" He didn't finish. He didn't need to. "And once during Abeloth, when the Force went sideways for six hours and everyone thought they'd imagined it." He faced his father. "I wasn't imagining it then either."

Luke's expression shifted—not dramatically, but something settled, a careful attention different from the briefing posture of two minutes ago.

"No," Luke said quietly. "You weren't."

The acknowledgment hit harder than Ben expected. He'd braced for gentle redirect, another *post-Abeloth residual*, the institutional refrain filed against his name three times running. Getting the opposite left him wordless.

"You feel a ripple," Ben said. "I feel a void. That's not a small difference."

Luke was quiet. Then: "It might not be."

He reached across the desk and rotated the datapad, calling up a survey route—pre-plotted, running along the Outer Rim's southwestern corridor. The line tracked the bearing Ben had traced.

"I need someone to run this route," Luke said. "Standard survey. Six systems, deep-range scans, Force-observation logs." He slid the datapad back. "The Order needs data. Someone who knows what to look for."

Ben didn't take it. "You're calling this routine."

"To the Temple, it is." A measured pause. "To you and me, it's something else."

That was as close as his father would get to *I believe you*. Ben lifted the datapad.

The route followed the bearing exactly. Six systems, the last at the edge of reliable charts, where the Outer Rim frayed into deep dark. Whoever had plotted it knew where to look.

"How long have you been planning this?" Ben asked.

"Since your second report." Luke's mouth quirked. "I waited to see if you'd request it."

"I was about to go without requesting it."

"I know." Not accusatory. Not surprised. "That's why I'm assigning it."

Ben studied the route again. Six weeks minimum. Alone in a survey ship, chasing a void the rest of the Order had dismissed. His shoulder still ached from Korren's blade.

He could have asked for a partner. Could have said *this might be bigger than one Jedi* and watched Luke agree, seen backup assigned, turned this into a proper operation. He saw it—the shape of the other path.

He set the datapad on the desk's edge. Picked it up with his other hand, screen glowing steady.

Luke watched. Didn't speak.

"Survey mission," Ben said. "Deep-range scans. Force-observation logs. Standard protocol."

"Standard protocol," Luke confirmed.

The office held its breath. Outside, Coruscant's traffic streamed on, indifferent to the sour note threading beneath the Temple's harmonics. Luke's presence filled the room, warm and vast, the same gravity it had always been.

Ben tucked the datapad under his arm.

Neither said another word.
