The hit landed clean.

Ben registered it half a second after the training blade seared across his shoulder—the gap between the Force going flat and his guard finally moving to close it. Muscle memory had tried to save him. The Force had not cooperated.

He staggered back two steps, more from shock than impact. The salle hummed with the low thrum of a dozen ignited blades, Ben standing in the center with his shoulder burning and his training saber still lit—a detail that felt like a personal rebuke.

"Skywalker." His sparring partner powered down, concern sharpening her voice. "You good?"

"Terrific." He deactivated his blade. "Third time this week someone’s tagged me across the shoulder. I’m building a collection."

She didn’t laugh. Genuine concern was harder to deflect than irritation. "Seriously. You telegraphed that parry by half a beat. You never do that."

"I’m getting old." Twenty-four. "Early onset. Very tragic. Go get some dinner—I’m going to run forms."

She studied him with the particular look Jedi Knights reserved for moments of necessary interference. Then she clipped her saber to her belt and left.

Ben waited for the doors to hiss shut behind her.

The salle stretched long and high, a space that swallowed sound and let it die slowly. Sconces lined the walls, casting even, pale light over the practice floor. Late as it was, the Temple’s corridors beyond lay quiet, and the Force here carried the settled weight of thousands—Masters in their quarters, Knights on rotation, younglings asleep in the creche. He’d grown up with that weight. It was as familiar as the ventilation system’s drone.

The flat note was not.

He pressed a hand to his shoulder where the blade had cut through fabric—a shallow gash, nothing worth mentioning. What was worth mentioning was the half-second before it, when the Force had stuttered like a failing circuit and his body had moved on instinct, too slow, too late.

Three times this week.

He unclipped his hilt, turning it over without igniting it. Then he moved.

The kata was third-form—forty-two positions demanding precise footwork and pivoting. He ran it clean. His body knew the angles; his weight shifted without thought, his feet found their marks, his grip adjusted with each rotation. Nothing broken. Nothing slow.

He stopped at position thirty-one—a low guard with a forward lean—and held. Then he straightened and ran it again from the start, faster.

Same result. Flawless mechanics. No hesitation.

The problem wasn’t his muscles. It wasn’t his mind. The problem was the gap between his instincts and the Force that should have been sharpening them—and that gap only opened when the flat note hit.

Ben lowered his hilt and stood motionless in the salle’s center.

He reached out carefully, probing like he’d been taught to test damaged terrain—not pushing, just listening. The Temple’s presences were all there, steady and warm and entirely unhelpful. He slipped past them, extending his awareness outward through Coruscant’s upper levels, through the haze of millions of lives stacked atop one another, through the planet’s deep geological pulse. All fine. All normal.

Then he found it.

Not on Coruscant. Not in the system. The flat note was an echo of something farther out, radiating inward like the ghost of a sound already spent. He followed the bearing without thinking, the way you follow a scent to its source—and the bearing pointed outward. Past the Colonies. Past the Expansion Region. Past the Mid Rim, into the Outer Rim’s deep dark, where the galactic arm frayed and charts grew sparse.

He lingered on it.

The wrongness had a texture he recognized—one he hadn’t admitted to himself until now. It wasn’t the sick weight of dark side corruption. He knew that touch well enough. It wasn’t grief or violence or the residue of mass death, though he’d felt those too. This was different. Structural. Like a building’s load-bearing frame—the kind of absence that didn’t announce itself until the ceiling moved.

He’d felt something like it twice before. Once when Jacen Solo had been reshaping himself into something unrecognizable, and the Force around him had taken on a quality of wrongness everyone dismissed as dark side proximity. Once during Abeloth, when the Force itself seemed to lose its footing.

Ben had been young during Jacen’s fall. He’d been paying close attention during Abeloth. He knew the difference between a wound and a distortion. This wasn’t either. It was more like—a missing keystone. Something that should have been there, providing structure, and wasn’t.

He exhaled through his nose and stared at the salle’s far wall, which told him nothing.

The Order had been running on reduced capacity since Abeloth. Masters stretched thin, Knights pulling double rotations, every disturbance filtered through the assumption that the galaxy was still recovering. A reasonable assumption. Probably even correct, most of the time.

But he’d filed three incident reports in the last two months about this specific anomaly. All three had come back with the same response: *Noted. Consistent with post-Abeloth residual disturbance. No action required.* Which was the institutional equivalent of *You’re not wrong, but you’re not the priority.*

He could escalate. Push harder. Pull his father into it. Make it impossible to ignore. He could also be wrong—still carrying enough scar tissue from the last two years that his perception ran hot, finding damage where there was only background noise.

Ben clipped his hilt back to his belt.

The bearing was there. The texture was real. And if he was right, the last thing the Order needed was another Skywalker raising alarms on a hunch and a bad week of sparring. He’d trace it further before saying anything. Get something concrete. Handle it alone.

The salle doors opened.

Luke Skywalker stood in the threshold, a datapad loose in one hand, his posture radiating readiness—not urgency, but decision already made. Ben had seen that posture his entire life. He knew exactly what it meant.
