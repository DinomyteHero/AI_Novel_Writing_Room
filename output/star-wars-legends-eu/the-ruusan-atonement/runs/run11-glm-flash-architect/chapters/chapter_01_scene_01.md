The training saber cracked across his shoulder—hiss of energy searing through padded fabric. Ben staggered two steps sideways before catching himself on the salle’s polished floor. His sparring partner, a Knight whose name had slipped his mind since morning drills, stood with blade still raised, her weight shifted wrong for a follow-through. She’d expected him to block. He’d expected himself to block too.

"Third time this week," she said. Lowered her weapon. "You’re distracted."

Ben deactivated his saber. The weapon’s hum died. Sudden silence pressed against his eardrums. "Understatement." He rolled his shoulder where the blade had connected. Training sabers ran low power—more sting than damage—but the hit had landed clean because he’d left the quadrant wide open. A child could’ve exploited that gap.

His mother would’ve had him doing blindfolded deflect-drills for a month if she were still here.

He flexed his fingers. The shaking was subtle. Manageable. The kind of thing you could hide if you kept your hands busy.

"It’s the post-Abeloth adjustment," the Knight offered. Helpful. Most of them tried these days—the ones who’d stayed on Coruscant through the madness and the ones who’d returned. They’d all learned to translate each other’s silences into something like normalcy. "Half the Order’s still recalibrating. The Council’s been running—"

"I know." Ben clipped his saber to his belt. "Thanks for the session."

She hesitated. He felt her weighing whether to push, whether to offer platitudes about recovery cycles or meditation techniques or the Force reasserting itself after trauma. He kept his expression neutral, his shoulders loose. After a moment, she took the exit he’d left her.

"Get some rest, Skywalker."

The salle door sealed behind her. Ben stood alone in the half-dark.

Temple evening cycle had dimmed the overhead panels to soft amber, catching the scuff marks on the training mats. Outside the massive transparisteel viewport, Coruscant’s skyline glittered through atmospheric haze—a million lives pressing against the glass, each a bright spark in the Force’s hum.

If you could still hear the hum.

Ben started pacing. Bootsteps on the floor felt too loud without the saber’s hum to fill the space. He moved through Djem So’s opening stance—weight forward, blade hand high, center low—without igniting the weapon. His body knew the forms the way it knew how to breathe. Muscle memory carved into him since childhood.

The problem wasn’t his body.

He shifted into the second sequence. Block. Redirect. Counter. Movements flowed, each step leading to the next, each breath aligned to the pattern that had kept Jedi alive for millennia. His feet knew where to go. His hands knew where to reach. The map was still there, printed into his bones.

But the Force—

He reached for it. The way he had ten thousand times. The way he had when he was barely old enough to hold a training remote. The way he had when the galaxy still made sense and Jacen hadn’t fallen. The Force was there. He could feel it. The vast presence connecting every living thing. The current that had carried his family for generations.

It was there. And it was wrong.

Not dark. Not tainted. Not the cold wrongness of the dark side or Abeloth’s dislocation. This was a note played slightly flat. A load-bearing wall beginning to groan. Structural. Underneath the surface. Something shifting.

His hand closed on empty air where the parry should’ve connected with something real.

Ben stopped in the salle’s center. Breathing too fast. He forced it slow—four counts in, four counts out. The way his mother had taught him before she taught him anything else.

The Temple’s presence pressed against his awareness. Dozens of Jedi in meditation or sleep, their signatures layered into a background wash most Knights stopped noticing after their first year. He’d always found it grounding. A reminder he wasn’t alone. The Order endured. The galaxy kept spinning even when his world tilted.

Tonight the presence felt thin. Not absent—never absent—but stretched, like a voice holding a note past its resolution.

He crossed to the viewport. Coruscant spread below—sharp edges, artificial light—and somewhere out there, the wrongness sharpened too. He’d felt it first three months ago. A whisper. A flicker. The kind of thing you dismissed as fatigue or stress or a bad dream’s afterimage. He’d tried to dismiss it. For weeks, he told himself it was nothing. Told himself the Council was right to classify these reports as background noise, echo residue, the galaxy’s slow healing from Abeloth’s damage.

But echoes didn’t have direction. This did.

He could feel it now if he stopped fighting and listened. A pull. A gravity. Something out there in the black beyond the Outer Rim, calling with a voice that wasn’t a voice, pulling with a hand that wasn’t a hand. It tugged at him the way a cracked foundation tugged at the walls above it—not with force, but with structural failure’s patient inevitability.

He knew this flavor. Had tasted it in the worst year of his life, standing in the shadow of a man who’d been his cousin, his friend, his cautionary tale. Jacen’s fall had left scars in the Force that hadn’t healed. Abeloth’s presence had torn holes that still hadn’t closed. This wasn’t either of those things—but it rhymed.

That was what scared him.

The Temple’s presence dropped away for half a second. Just his own breathing. Just Coruscant’s distant thrum. Just the weight of wrongness pressing against his awareness like a door that wouldn’t stay shut.

Then it returned. He released the breath he’d been holding. The moment passed.

But he couldn’t un-know what he’d heard in that silence. The lattice was groaning. Something was coming apart. And he stood in the salle with shaking hands and a flat note in the Force where his certainty used to be. Handling it alone. Because that’s what Skywalkers did. The safe way.

The door behind him opened.

Ben turned. Luke Skywalker stood framed in the corridor’s warm light, datapad in one hand, an expression Ben knew too well—the one that meant coordinates, a destination, a problem that needed solving.

The look that meant mission.
