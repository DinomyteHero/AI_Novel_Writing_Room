Ben sat in the cockpit with preflight green across the board and the Temple partner roster glowing in the lower corner of his datapad.

His thumb slid past it.

Not after a pause. Not after a debate. Straight past, opening his personal contacts with the smooth certainty of a habit that had gotten a little too old to be flattering.

The choice left a small, tight pull under his ribs. He ignored that with the same efficiency.

Outside the canopy, the south hangar moved through its usual controlled mess. A loader droid trundled by with a crate rack humming under strain. Voices bounced off durasteel walls—service techs calling clearances, somebody in the next lane arguing cheerfully with a fueling unit that was almost certainly blameless. The air tasted of hot circuitry and hydraulic fluid, cut through by recycled chill venting through the bay.

Ben scrolled once, found Desh Rolan, and keyed the message.

*South bay. Departure in twenty. Outer Rim run. Available?*

He sent it before he could improve it into something less honest.

The reply came back before he finished checking his fuel margins.

A chime. Then Desh's voice, thin with comm compression and long-range static.

"Skywalker. That your idea of a social invitation?"

The tight pull under Ben's ribs eased by a fraction. "You free?"

A brief crackle. Ben could picture him already moving while he talked—Desh rarely wasted time sitting still when a question had an obvious answer.

"For you? That depends. Am I packing for weather, paperwork, or people shooting at us?"

"Unknown."

"The worst category." He sounded almost pleased by that. "How fast?"

"Twenty minutes."

"That's rude."

"You can say no."

"I could," Desh agreed. "I won't. Send docking lane if you change hangars."

The channel clicked off.

That was it. No *why me*. No *why not a Jedi*. No careful circling around the fact that a Knight on the duty board would have made more sense on paper and less sense anywhere else that mattered.

Ben set the datapad into its slot and looked for a moment at the darkened screen. Desh had always had a gift for accepting the shape of a bad plan and improving its odds without spending breath on the speech before the mission. It was a useful trait in private security. It had been an even more useful trait in the GAG, back when Ben had been young enough to wear Alliance black and mistake being useful to dangerous adults for a career path.

He keyed the docking lane over anyway. Professional courtesy. Also because if he sat there appreciating the silence too long, he might have to explain it to himself.

The starboard hatch indicator flashed amber, then green. Fast even for Desh.

Ben hit the internal lock release and heard boots on the boarding ramp a moment later—brisk and unhurried at the same time. Desh climbed into the cockpit in a dark field jacket with a compact duffel over one shoulder, looking exactly like a man who had packed in four minutes and still remembered the things that mattered.

He dropped the bag behind the copilot's seat, swept the active displays once, and settled in without ceremony. Mid-thirties, Human, calm eyes, compact build. He wore competence the way some people wore armor—without asking to be noticed for it.

"You look terrible," Desh said.

Ben started the port repulsors. "Good to see you too."

"I mean it professionally."

"That's kinder than I expected."

Desh strapped in and tested his restraints with a quick tug. "I save the personal insults for longer trips."

Ben brought the systems from standby to live flight. Engine vibration climbed through the hull and into the seat frame, familiar and steadying. "I'm touched."

"Do not make this emotional."

Ben snorted before he could stop himself. The sound loosened something in his chest. Desh had a way of cutting through tension without pretending it wasn't there, which was rarer than it had any right to be.

Temple control fed him departure clearance. He acknowledged, nudged the throttle, and the ship lifted cleanly from the deck.

The hangar dropped away in measured increments. Guidelights swept the canopy—red, amber, green. A pair of techs near the lane marker waved him through, one with a hydrospanner still in his glove. Ben dipped the nose in return and angled into the departure corridor.

Coruscant opened ahead in layers of traffic and light. Lanes braided between towers. Civilian speeders flashed past in ordered streams, courier craft cutting cleaner lines through the flow. Far below, the planet went on with its day, which was one of Coruscant's more irritating qualities—the center of galactic life could make almost anything feel private by refusing to notice it.

Desh pulled up the nav repeat on his side and gave the route a quick scan. "Outer Rim run," he said. "You want the official version or the useful version?"

"Useful."

"Good. I dislike paperwork with a spiritual component."

Ben took them through vector correction and into the climb. "Temple assignment. Track anomaly markers, verify local noise, file updates."

Desh glanced at him. "And the useful version?"

Ben watched a customs cutter slide across a higher lane and waited until it cleared. "Same, but with less confidence."

"Ah." Desh folded his arms loosely. "One of those."

*One of those* covered a lot of ground between them. It covered strange intelligence work, half-legal insertion jobs, and the years when *trust me* had too often meant *you'll understand after the damage is done*. Desh did not press. He had learned that in the same institution Ben had, which was not an advertisement for the institution.

The wrongness was still there.

It had ridden with him into the cockpit, through checklist cadence and comm chatter, through the easy banter that usually filed the edge off his nerves. A thin drag at the back of his perception—not loud, just persistent. On most trips, distance helped. Transit smeared local disturbances. Hyperspace usually washed Force oddities smooth unless they were sitting directly on top of you.

This one stayed put.

Ben adjusted pitch and fed more power to the rear thrusters. "You armed?"

Desh patted the side of his jacket. "Within reason."

"That answer has never reassured anyone."

"It should. The unreasonable gear costs extra."

They broke atmosphere with a brief shudder across the hull. Coruscant fell behind them into dark and glitter, the planet's night side a sprawl of jeweled circuitry. Ben aligned with the jump vector, checked for lane conflicts, and rolled his shoulders once against a knot he'd been carrying since the training salle.

Desh was quiet for a moment, watching him in the reflection off his display. "You planning to tell me what I'm walking into?"

"When I know."

"That bad, then."

"Yeah."

Desh nodded, accepting the shape of that too. "All right."

No sermon. No demand for certainty Ben didn't have to give. Just *all right*, with the old operational weight underneath it: *then we go see*.

Ben set the final coordinates, confirmed the hyperdrive solution, and wrapped his hands around the controls. The wrongness pressed once—faint, stubborn, present.

He drove the ship into hyperspace.

Stars snapped to white lines and vanished into blue. The jump hit with its usual gut-lurch, then settled into the steady deck hum of sustained transit. Instrument lights flickered once across the main board—a minor power ripple, gone before it could mean anything. The hyperdrive readouts held.

Ben waited for the wrongness to drop away.

It didn't.

His grip shifted on the controls before he had words for it. Not tighter. More braced.

Beside him, Desh was running through system status, unaware of anything beyond what the instruments could tell him. Ben envied that for half a second. Clean data. Either the board was lying or it wasn't.

The Force wasn't smoothing itself out in jump. The thin drag held—and then it sharpened.

Not louder. Cleaner.

Ben went still.

For weeks it had sat under everything like a note struck wrong and left unresolved. Here, in hyperspace, where it should have blurred into distance, it did the opposite. The smear pulled taut. The wrong note found its place in the chord.

Ahead.

Straight ahead, down the bearing he had already plotted, fixed with the hard certainty of a compass needle that had finally found north. Not trailing back toward Coruscant. Not diffused around him. Forward—waiting at the far end of the lane his own hands had chosen.
