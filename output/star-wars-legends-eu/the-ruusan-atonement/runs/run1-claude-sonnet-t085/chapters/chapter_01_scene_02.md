The datapad’s glow was the color of a fresh bruise.

Ben stood at the edge of Luke’s desk, hands at his sides. Seventeen survey flags across fourteen months, each cataloged with the same clinical notation: *anomalous Force readings, non-standard, origin undetermined.* It was the language of a scout who lacked better words. Ben understood that. He had been searching for the right vocabulary himself for three months.

The office smelled of cold tea and recycled air. Underneath lay a particular staleness—the sediment of years of heavy decisions. No decorations. A few datapads, a holocomm unit, and a window overlooking Coruscant’s upper cityscape. Outside, traffic lanes ran amber in the twilight. The Grand Master of the Jedi Order kept the most unadorned office in the Temple. Ben usually found this either principled or strategic, depending on his mood.

Tonight, he couldn't decide.

"Walk me through what you're seeing," Luke said.

He stood behind the desk. Ben had learned to read that posture. Seated meant administrative; standing meant he was still working the problem.

Ben pulled the datapad toward him and scrolled to the third report. "A survey team out of Ord Mantell flagged this first. They described the Force as—" he checked the notation— "*diffuse.* An odd choice." He set the pad down. "The next six reports use different language. Thin. Quiet. One pilot says it felt like flying through dead air. But they're all pointing at the same corridor, all within twelve parsecs of each other along the Darpa Sector run."

"And your read?"

Ben’s fingers gripped the datapad’s edge. He had rehearsed this since Luke handed him the file twenty minutes ago. Technical framing was safer. It was the kind of delivery a Jedi Knight could manage without revealing how long he had been turning this over in the hours before dawn.

"It isn't dark," he said. "That’s the first thing. There’s no weight, no pull, nothing that reads as corruption. It’s more like—" He paused. The silence stretched. Luke waited with a quality of attention that turned the quiet into pressure. "You know how a hyperdrive sounds when the motivator loses alignment? It’s still running. It’s still generating output. But the harmonic is off. Something inside isn't meshing."

Luke remained still. His presence in the Force was vast and warm, a steady baseline from someone who had spent decades learning not to transmit. Ben kept his own presence contracted. It was a reflex now, the Force equivalent of keeping his hands in his pockets.

"I feel it too," Luke said.

Ben looked up. His father’s expression remained fixed, but the weight in the room shifted.

"I’ve been feeling it for some time." Luke moved to the window. Amber traffic reflected off the transparisteel, catching the silver in his hair. "It feels like a fading echo. Something from the war, perhaps. The Ruusan campaign left marks on the Force that took generations to settle. This could be residual."

*Residual.* Ben tested the word against his own senses. Luke had watched the Force heal from Palpatine, from Abeloth, from a century of Sith war. He took the long view. Scars faded; the Force endured.

But Ben had felt Jacen’s corruption before anyone named it—that slow pressure drop, the way his cousin’s presence had felt like a load-bearing beam giving way. He had recognized Abeloth as a wrongness before she became a threat. Vestara had taught him that darkness didn't always announce itself with heat. Sometimes it arrived as erosion.

His father knew how to read things that pushed back. Ben was reading something that was simply gone.

"It isn't fading," Ben said. He kept his voice level but swiped across the datapad, pulling up the overlay. Two maps appeared side by side. "The reports are getting more frequent. The readings aren't diminishing. They're expanding." He gestured at the data. "Ord Mantell’s survey boundary versus last month’s. Twelve parsecs became nineteen."

Luke turned from the window to study the maps. His expression settled into careful neutrality—the look of a man who had already considered this possibility and had not yet decided how to act.

"You think it's a leak," Luke said.

The word landed poorly. Not because it was wrong, but because it was Ben’s word—the one he had guarded in private for three months. Hearing his father name it first made Ben’s chest tighten.

"Yeah," Ben said. "I do."

Luke studied him. He wasn't looking for suspicion, but weighing Ben’s confirmation against his own internal tally. Ben had grown up under that gaze. He still didn't know if it meant his father saw more than he let on, or less than Ben feared.

"The survey route along the Darpa Sector corridor," Luke said. "One Knight can move through it more quietly than a task force. Fewer disruptions. Cleaner data."

Ben nodded once. Professional. He understood the logic, and he understood the subtext: a single Knight was expendable. If something went wrong, a solo loss was an acceptable risk.

He thought about the shape of Luke’s darkness—it had always possessed direction and moral mass. This thing in the Darpa Sector had neither. It sat in the Force like a gap. Not a wound, but a structural failure. Luke called it residual because his perception was calibrated to things that shouted. This thing was silent. It was a release.

Ben kept these thoughts to himself. Correcting Luke meant admitting he needed Luke to be right. He needed his father to have answers so he wouldn't be alone with the void.

"I can have a route plotted by 0600," he said.

"Good." Luke moved back to the desk. He reached past Ben to close the survey overlay. For a moment, his hand rested on the desk’s edge—not quite a gesture, not quite stillness. "The Council will want a preliminary report within the week. Whatever you find."

"Understood."

Ben picked up the datapad. The blue glow made the lines in his palm look like deep canyons. He felt his father’s presence, still vast, but carrying a question. Or perhaps a weight Luke had carried alone until Ben walked through the door.

Seconds ticked past. Long enough for Ben to recognize the invitation.

*Take someone with you. Don't go alone.*

Luke didn't say it. He had chosen to send Ben, but he left the door open for Ben to ask for help. It was an opportunity to break the distance that had held steady since the Lost Tribe crisis—since Ben had learned that even Skywalkers could fail.

Ben didn't ask. He accepted the solo assignment, a silent confirmation of the space between them. The choice felt like freedom, but it tasted like ash.

"Get some rest before you ship out," Luke said.

"I will." Ben turned toward the door. "Thank you for the assignment."

He meant it. His father had handed him something that mattered. The fact that Ben planned to carry it in ways Luke hadn't sanctioned didn't change the warmth of that trust. It just sat beside it, unresolved.

The door slid shut.

In the corridor, the Temple’s ambient harmonics settled around him—that deep resonance of a place where thousands had touched the Force over millennia. Ben stood and listened past the hum, searching for a specific frequency.

Out along the Darpa Sector, something that should have held fast was letting go. He felt it like a note played in the wrong key: persistent, patient, and continuing.

He walked toward the hangar alone.
