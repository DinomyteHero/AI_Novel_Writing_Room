Luke lifted the datapad a fraction in greeting instead of answer, blue light washing over his hand and the front edge of his robe. Numbers and route markers glowed across the screen.

Ben stepped in. The office door sealed behind him with a soft hiss.

Luke's rooms had never tried to impress anyone. Desk. Holocomm. Two chairs that looked chosen for endurance rather than style. Shelves with actual flimsi among the data crystals, because apparently being Grand Master did not cure a person of old habits. The broad window beyond the desk gave Coruscant's night traffic in thin streams of white and red. Recycled air carried tea, old paper, and the metallic bite of Temple climate control.

Under all of it sat the same sour wrongness that had dogged Ben in the salle.

Luke looked up. Warmth came off him through the Force the way it always had—steady, familiar, big enough to lean on if Ben let himself. There was distance in it, too. Not coldness. More the careful spacing of a man who had learned that being both father and Grand Master meant disappointing one role no matter what he chose.

"Close enough that you came down in person," Ben said. "I'm guessing this isn't about my sparkling performance with a training blade."

"One of the advantages of being Grand Master is access to eyewitness accounts." Luke held out the datapad. "Take a look."

Ben crossed to the desk. The screen held a string of reports, seventeen entries stacked in clean columns. Survey teams. Patrol routes. Fringe listening posts. A scatter along the Outer Rim and the edge of the Hydian corridor. Each was tagged, time-stamped, and flagged in yellow.

He took the datapad. His fingers tightened on the edge before he loosened them again.

"Seventeen?" he asked.

"In the last six standard weeks."

Ben lowered himself into the chair without taking his eyes off the screen. "That seems like enough for concern and not enough for panic. Very Jedi."

Luke's mouth twitched. "We aspire to balanced overreaction."

Ben snorted once and scrolled.

The first report was from a two-Jedi survey team checking residual effects near a world that had hosted a Lost Tribe safehouse. No hostile contact. No measurable environmental event. Both Jedi noted transient disruption in Force feedback during active perception. The language was clinical, which did not hide the unease underneath.

The second came from a medical convoy escort. Similar note. Brief deadening at the edge of awareness. No mechanical cause found. The convoy's instruments showed a shallow dip in local sensor noise that their techs had written off as interference.

Ben flicked down, faster now.

A map opened under his hand. Seventeen points. Spread wide, but not random. A line wanted to exist there. Three of the points pulled his attention at once. He zoomed in.

Same timestamp drift.
Same not-quite-empty patch in the readouts.
Same tiny drop where the report language turned from "disturbance" to "absence."

His stomach drew tight.

Luke watched from across the desk, saying nothing.

Ben enlarged the first of the three. "Who flagged these?"

"I did."

Ben checked the second. Then the third. "Good."

That came out sharper than he intended. He set the pad flatter against his palm and made himself keep his voice even. "Because these three match what I've been getting. Not all seventeen. These."

Luke leaned back a little. "Why those?"

For one brief pulse, Dad rose up in Ben's throat, attached to an old reflex: ask, explain, trust him to hear the rest. Ben tapped the first report instead.

"Because the others read like fallout," he said. "People still picking up old damage, local contamination, stress, any of the dozen fun leftovers the galaxy keeps producing. These three don't."

He tapped the second and third in turn.

"These have shape. The Force is still there, but the answer slips. For a moment you push and nothing pushes back."

Luke's gaze narrowed, not in disagreement. In focus.

Ben looked down at the glowing reports again. The blue light sharpened the bones in his hand. "I've felt versions of it before."

"Where?"

There was no point being delicate with shared family history. The archives had that covered.

"Around Jacen," Ben said. "Not all the time. At the worst points." He kept scrolling, not because he needed more data but because sitting still would make the office too quiet. "And around Abeloth. Different scale. Different cause. Same missing resistance." He hesitated on the third name, then gave it anyway. "Vestara, once or twice. Not her exactly. What she was willing to stand close to."

Luke was silent long enough for the traffic beyond the window to register as moving stars.

Ben set the datapad on the desk. "I know how that sounds."

"It sounds like pattern recognition," Luke said.

The words landed warmer than Ben had expected. Enough that some of the tightness in his chest eased before he could stop it.

Luke folded his hands. "I've been feeling it too."

Ben's head came up.

Not surprise. Not quite. More the click of two parts meeting after grinding past each other for too long.

"In the Temple?" Ben asked.

"And elsewhere." Luke glanced at the datapad. "Mostly at range. Brief contact. A pressure change and then it's gone."

Ben studied him. Luke's presence in the Force remained broad and familiar, but now that the admission was out, Ben could sense the strain under it—the controlled attention of someone who had been testing a problem from six angles and refusing to name it too soon.

"What do you think it is?" Ben asked.

Luke let out a breath through his nose. "An anomaly moving along established routes. Possibly tied to old damage sites, possibly to something using those routes for cover. We don't have enough yet to call it a threat with a clear source."

Ben almost smiled. There was the Grand Master answer. Precise. Responsible. Built to survive committee review.

He looked back at the map. "You think it can be surveyed."

"I think it has to be surveyed before we make worse decisions."

Fair. Also irritating.

Ben rose with the datapad and walked to the window. The durasteel frame was cool under his hand. Below, Coruscant traffic streamed in layered lanes, every pilot convinced someone else was the dangerous one.

Behind him, Luke stayed at the desk.

Ben kept his eyes on the city. "And you want me to run the line."

"Yes."

Solo, then. Luke had not said it yet, but the shape of the briefing already had.

Ben angled the datapad, bringing up the route. Outer Rim corridor touches. Listening posts. Survey buoys. A chain long enough to keep one person busy and far enough from Coruscant that "check in if needed" became a nice phrase with limited practical value.

He heard his own voice from years ago, younger and more annoyed: so your plan is to throw me at the problem and hope I learn something useful on the way down?

He was older now. More polite. Usually.

"What's the official version?" he asked.

Luke answered at once. "Routine anomaly survey under Grand Master authority. Confirm reports, gather readings, inspect sites with strongest correlation, avoid engagement if a hostile actor presents. Return with data."

Ben glanced over his shoulder. "That's almost dull enough to work."

"That was the goal."

There it was, the brief warmth the scene card had promised the universe and Ben had not been consulted on. It passed between them cleanly. No speeches. No confessions. Just the old ease showing itself for a second before both of them let it go.

Ben looked back at the route.

"You said established routes," he said. "These points track too neatly for random spread. If someone's moving through the corridor and this is residue, they're not leaving much."

"Agreed."

"If it's not residue, then the route itself matters."

"Also agreed."

Ben nodded once. "Then this won't stay a survey for long."

"No," Luke said. "It may not."

The wrongness pressed at the edge of Ben's awareness even here, faint and unpleasant, like a taste left in a cup after the tea was gone. He thought of the hitch in the salle, the instant where intention had met nothing. He thought of those three reports and the way they lined up with what his own instincts had been trying to tell him.

Luke had felt it too. That mattered more than Ben wanted to admit.

It did not change the rest.

He set the datapad against the window frame and studied the route a final time. Long run. Sparse support. Plenty of room for a pilot, a Jedi, and a bad idea to get acquainted. In fairness, that described half the missions worth taking.

Ben tapped the authorization field. The assignment flashed green.

"I'll take it," he said.

Luke inclined his head. "Leave when you're ready."

Ben picked up the datapad again and turned from the window with a crisp nod, already fitting the mission around what he would need, what he would pack, which ship made the most sense for a run like this. He did not ask for another Jedi.

Luke did not offer one.

Neither of them said what sat between those two choices.
