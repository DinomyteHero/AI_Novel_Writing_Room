Ben’s guard dropped a half-second late.

Rendell’s training blade snapped across his shoulder with a flat crack that cut through the salle’s hush like a hatch slamming shut. The strike hit harder than a sparring touch ought to, which meant Rendell had already committed before Ben’s block got there.

Ben stepped back and rolled the shoulder once, as if that would shake loose the sting. It wasn’t pain so much as the clean jolt of a mistake his reflexes had no business making. Even now, the disturbance tugged at his attention—not a sound, exactly, but the salle’s Force presence sliding in a beat behind the rhythm his body had trusted for years. A note coming in flat. Late.

Third time this week.

Rendell killed his training saber and brushed sweat-damp hair off his forehead. “You all right?”

“Fine.” Ben kept his voice easy, almost lazy, and deactivated his own blade in one smooth motion before clipping it to his belt. “Call it fatigue. Long week.”

Not a lie, not entirely. The week had been long. He’d been tired since Tuesday, or maybe since the thing at the edge of his senses started worrying at him like a half-heard question he couldn’t quite catch. But fatigue was a word another Knight could accept without further discussion, without the awkward pause that came with trying to explain something he couldn’t name.

Rendell lingered, saber still in hand, clearly deciding whether to push.

Ben let him.

“Another round?” Rendell asked.

“Tomorrow.” Ben gave him a half-smile that missed his eyes by a long way. “Let me get some sleep first.”

Rendell nodded, accepting the out or at least deciding not to force the issue, and crossed the salle to rack his saber. His boots thudded softly on the padded floor. Out in the corridor, a cleaning droid hummed past with a thin mechanical whine, and somewhere deeper in the Temple a turbolift chimed its arrival on another level. The late shift was still moving through the building—Knights, Masters, support staff, everyone keeping to their own jobs in the hour between evening and night.

Ben stayed in the center of the salle until Rendell’s footsteps faded.

Then he dropped the smile.

His shoulder ached where the blade had landed. Not injury—the training weapons were set to sting, not to break—but the precise, ugly feeling of a mistake his body should never have let through. He had blocked that strike since he was fourteen. The form lived in muscle and bone by now; his body knew it before his thoughts did.

Tonight it had come a half-second late, and the gap had been there in the middle of it.

He paced the salle once, then again, measuring distance with the sound of his own boots. Empty room now. No audience, no rematch waiting, no one to explain anything to. Just Ben, the quiet hum of Temple air, and the thing at the edge of his senses.

He stopped in the center of the training circle and lifted his saber without igniting it.

The form was an old one, something his father had shown him years ago—a slow sequence built for control rather than speed. Ben moved into the first stance, then the second, letting his body find the positions from memory while he kept half his attention on the field around him. The salle’s Force presence was steady, familiar, the same calm current he had known since he was old enough to notice it.

He reached the third stance and felt the delay.

Nothing dramatic. Just a half-second between his motion and the field answering it, like accompaniment sliding in after the beat instead of on it. He kept going, moving through the rest of the form deliberately, and the lag followed him from transition to transition.

The problem wasn’t in him.

That landed as a physical jolt more than a thought—a tightening in his chest, a narrowing of focus, the hard edge of readiness before a fight. All week he’d been carrying it like a private failure, some frayed thread in his own perception, some leftover echo of old crises that everyone else had already moved past. But his body knew the forms. His body had known them longer than he had words for them. And his body was on time.

The Force was not.

Ben stood still in the center of the circle, unlit saber in hand, and did not put the feeling into words. *Dread* sounded too neat, too polished—something that belonged in a debrief, not here in an empty salle while a cleaning droid trundled past outside. He wasn’t ready to name it. He wasn’t sure he wanted to.

But fatigue was no longer a believable answer.

Whatever this was, it sat outside him. Something was off in the Force itself, something that had been pulling at his attention all week and was now costing him beats he couldn’t afford. He didn’t know what it was, or why no one else seemed to feel it. He didn’t know whether the problem was the galaxy or his own head—whether he was catching something real or whether the years since Abeloth had left a crack in his perception that was only now widening.

That uncertainty was worse than the sensation itself.

He ran the form again, faster this time, and the lag stayed with him—half a beat behind every shift, every turn, the Force arriving late to its own rhythm. His body compensated without asking permission, adjusting to what was there instead of what should have been, and that adjustment felt like failure even when it kept him upright.

He stopped in mid-stance and lowered the saber.

The salle had gone quiet again. The cleaning droid was gone. The turbolifts had stopped chiming, and the late-shift traffic had thinned to nothing. Ben stood alone in the empty circle, breathing evenly, and let the feeling sit there without trying to sand it down into an excuse.

He didn’t know what it was. He didn’t know why he was the only one who seemed to feel it. He didn’t know whether saying it out loud would make it real or just make him sound like a Knight who couldn’t trust his own senses.

But it wasn’t fatigue. It wasn’t him.

The door hissed open behind him.

Ben turned. His father stood in the doorway, datapad in one hand, the other loose at his side. Luke Skywalker looked calm—the sort of calm that meant he’d already read whatever was on the pad and had moved on to the next problem. His presence in the Force was steady and warm, broad in the way it had always been broad, and for a heartbeat Ben felt the discord ease, as if Luke’s attention had shoved it back.

Then Luke met his eyes, and something in his expression shifted. Not much. Just enough.

Ben knew that look. A mission was coming.

The half-heard note settled back into his chest as he faced the door.
