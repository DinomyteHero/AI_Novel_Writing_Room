His guard came up late.

The training blade slapped across Ben's shoulder with a hard, humming crack, and the sting bit through his tunic a heartbeat before the salle speakers chirped the point. He had time to register three things in quick order: the heat in the contact line, the fact that his own saber was still half a move from where it needed to be, and the familiar hitch at the edge of the Force—that maddening half-beat delay that had been dogging him all week.

He stepped back on reflex and shut down his blade before irritation could turn into something sloppier. Blue light snapped out. The room seemed brighter without it.

His sparring partner lowered his own saber a fraction, eyebrows lifting. "You all right?"

"Fine." Ben rolled his shoulder once. Bad idea. The line of the hit answered with a sharper throb. "Nice shot."

The other Knight didn't look particularly pleased with himself. More cautious than pleased, which was worse. Ben was used to people taking points off him. He was not used to them looking like they'd just watched a lift cable fray.

"You want another pass?"

Ben glanced at the floor markings, at the mirrored wall beyond them, anywhere but at the other man's face. Sweat had turned his palms slick on the hilt. He adjusted his grip, buying half a second he didn't need and couldn't use.

"No." He clipped the saber to his belt. "I'm done."

The Knight tilted his head. "Done done, or—"

"Fatigue," Ben said. "Long week."

That, at least, was true enough to pass inspection. The Temple had been running hot for months, people crossing paths in corridors with datapads, unfinished meals, and the pinched look of too many small crises stacked on top of old large ones. Background static in the Force after Abeloth, after everything else, had become the sort of phrase people used and then kept walking.

The Knight nodded slowly. "Right. Get some rest."

"I hear that's popular."

That got the quick huff Ben had aimed for. Not much, but enough to make the exchange feel normal. The other man saluted with two fingers against his brow, turned off the salle line, and headed for the door. His boots thudded across the floor, then softened on the corridor runner outside. A lift chimed somewhere beyond. Voices drifted past, late-shift low and tired, then moved on.

The door slid shut.

Ben stood in the middle of the training circle with the dead weight of the saber at his hip and listened to the room settle. Ventilation breathed through the high grilles. The overhead panels gave everything a clean white wash that left nowhere to hide. Along the far wall, a maintenance droid nosed out from an alcove, gave the empty floor a doubtful scan, and retreated again as if deciding this was still a Human problem.

"Long week," Ben muttered.

The excuse sat sour on his tongue. Not because it was a lie. Because it was a coward's fraction of the truth, shaved down until it fit in polite conversation.

He unhooked the training saber and set it on the bench by the wall. Then he stripped off his outer tunic and tossed that beside it. The air against his damp shirt felt cool enough to raise the small hairs along his forearms.

Maybe it was fatigue. Maybe his concentration was frayed. Maybe spending the last several days trying not to think about the same wrong note in the Force had done exactly what trying not to think usually did and made him think about nothing else. That would be neat. Embarrassing, but neat.

Which was probably why he didn't believe it for a second.

Ben walked back to the center mark, planted his feet, and let his breathing even out. No blade this time. No hum in his hand to mask anything. Just the shape of the form and the rhythm his body had learned years ago, drilled in salles and cargo bays and forest clearings and every other place Jedi ended up pretending was a training room.

He brought his empty hands up into guard.

The first sequence flowed cleanly enough. Step, turn, cut line without a blade, recover. His shoulders loosened. His boots whispered across the floor. For two moves, three, maybe four, he almost managed to forget why he was doing this.

Then the lag touched him again.

Not in his muscles. Those were where they belonged. Not in the floor, or the air, or any injury the training blade had left behind. It came from the place that should have met him without asking, the surrounding press of the Force in a room full of years of practice and discipline and countless small acts of focus. The salle had always had a rhythm to it. Every training space in the Temple did. Tonight it answered him late.

Half a beat. No more than that.

Enough.

His next pivot overshot. He corrected, tried to fold the mistake into the form, and missed the timing on the follow-up anyway. Empty hand cut through empty air. Useless.

He stopped, exhaled through his nose, and reset.

Again.

The second run went worse because now he was waiting for the miss. Anticipation crowded the edges of everything. When he moved, he could feel the delay coming, the way you could hear a song dragging just behind tempo and know the next line would land wrong before it arrived. He compensated too early, then too late. By the end of the sequence his stance had widened without permission, bare feet gripping at the floor as if traction could fix a problem that wasn't in his legs.

He lowered his hands.

The room answered in that same crooked rhythm. Temple stone, old circuits in the walls, breath, pulse, the distant stream of beings moving through the corridors outside—it all should have nested together. Tonight one layer kept arriving after the rest.

Ben scrubbed a hand over his face and started pacing the edge of the circle.

Three times this week. No, four, if he counted the meditation chamber two nights ago, when he'd come out of a simple centering exercise with a headache and the nasty suspicion that the floor under his concentration had shifted while he was standing on it. He had not counted that one, because counting it would have required admitting there was something to count.

He snorted once under his breath. Good work, Skywalker. Ignore the problem and maybe it'll get shy and leave.

The joke landed flat in the empty room.

He turned back in, slower this time, and raised his arms for Soresu transitions. Minimal commitment. Pure timing. If his body was off, this would show it. If the hitch was somewhere else, this would show that too.

He moved.

Guard. Angle. Check. Recover.

For one precious instant everything lined up. He nearly smiled.

Then the next beat vanished under him.

No stumble this time. No dramatic failure. Worse than that. His body kept going while the rest of him reached for a cue that hadn't arrived yet. The gap opened small and clean, and in that gap he knew with a clarity he had been dodging since the week began that he was not imagining this, not overtrained, not carrying leftover strain in a way that would go away if he slept another six hours and drank more caf.

His hands dropped.

A tight coil formed low in his gut and pulled hard. He stood very still, listening to the room breathe one count behind him.

The Temple had weathered stranger things than one Jedi Knight having an off week. It had weathered invasions, schisms, exiles, dark side eruptions, political disasters, and the ordinary grind of too many people asking the Force for answers on too little sleep. Ben knew all the reasonable explanations. He had been reciting them to himself for days.

None of them helped when the next beat went missing.

He bent, braced his hands on his thighs, and kept his eyes on the floor until the urge to swear at the room passed. The polished surface reflected only his boots and a slice of bright overhead light. No answers there. Convenient.

If he said something now, it would become real in ways he might not be able to pull back. It would become concern, watchfulness, maybe reassignment. It would become people being careful around him. Worse, it might become that look—kind, patient, impossible to fight—the one reserved for beings everyone suspected were still carrying damage they hadn't finished naming yet.

Ben straightened slowly.

The half-beat throbbed at the edge of his awareness, stubborn as a drip in the walls.

He reached for his saber on the bench and stopped short when a presence touched the doorway behind him, familiar enough to recognize before he turned.

Luke stood in the entrance with a datapad in one hand and an expression Ben knew meant a mission.
