The hangar smelled like coolant and hot metal, the way all hangars did, and the *Gallant Reach* sat fueled and ready in bay twelve with her running lights already cycling. Ben had handled that himself rather than logging the request through the Temple's departure system. Small thing. Easier to not have a record of when he'd decided to go.

He dropped into the pilot's seat and pulled up the datapad.

The Jedi roster populated automatically—the system's default assumption that a Knight departing solo needed a partner flagged and assigned. Ben scrolled past Tiran Holt, who was good in a fight but asked questions the whole time. Past Zori Vel, who would comm his father inside six hours. Past Davin Naas, who he actually liked, which was precisely the problem—Davin would notice things, and Ben didn't need anyone noticing things yet.

He kept scrolling.

His personal contacts list opened with three names at the top. The third was Desh Rolan, tagged with a Coruscant exchange and a secondary relay for when he was off-world. Ben tapped it before he finished the thought telling him to think about this more carefully.

The comm connected on the fourth ring.

"Rolan." The voice was flat, slightly distorted by the long-range relay, with the particular quality of someone who answered comms the same way at 0300 as at midday.

"It's Ben."

A beat. "Skywalker. You're either in trouble or you have work."

"Work. Outer Rim survey run, open timeline, probably three to four standard weeks. I need someone who can handle logistics and security without requiring a briefing on Force metaphysics."

"How much of the Outer Rim?"

"Corridor sector. Ruusan vicinity to start, then following a bearing."

Another beat, shorter. "When?"

"I'm sitting in the hangar."

"Of course you are." Something shifted in the background—a chair, maybe, or a bag pulled from a shelf. "My rate went up since the Bothawui job."

"I know. I already approved it."

"You approved a rate you don't know yet."

"I know your rates, Desh."

A sound that wasn't quite a laugh. "Bay number?"

"Twelve. I'll file your clearance now."

"Give me forty minutes."

The comm clicked off. Ben set the datapad on the console and looked at the hangar's far wall, where a maintenance droid ran a diagnostic sweep along the belly of a shuttle three bays down, its work light throwing a slow yellow arc across the deck plating.

Forty minutes. He'd expected forty minutes.

He'd known Desh Rolan for nine years, which meant he'd known him since the GAG—since the years when the Galactic Alliance Guard had been the thing you didn't mention at Temple dinners, the joint operation that had made everyone's hands dirty in the name of preventing something worse. Ben had been young enough that his memories of it ran more to impression than detail: the particular silence that fell when someone mentioned Jacen's name around the veterans, the way certain Jedi had watched him for two years after the war like they were waiting for him to become something. Desh had been a junior intelligence analyst then, one of dozens of non-Force-sensitives who'd done the actual work while the Force-users collected the operational briefings. They'd met because Desh had flagged an error in a threat assessment Ben had signed off on, and instead of routing the correction through channels, Desh had walked it over personally and laid out the problem without making it a confrontation.

Ben had remembered that. In the years since, he'd come to understand it was just how Desh operated—direct, efficient, no performance in either direction.

The forty minutes passed. Ben ran pre-flight checks.

Desh arrived at thirty-seven, a duffel over one shoulder and a compact case in his off hand that Ben recognized as his field kit. He stowed both in the cargo compartment without being asked, came forward, and dropped into the co-pilot's seat.

"You eat?" he said.

"Not recently."

"I brought rations. They're bad, but they exist." He pulled up the secondary nav display and started orienting himself. "Ruusan vicinity. What are we looking for?"

"Anomalous Force readings along the Corridor. Seventeen flagged survey reports. I'm following a bearing."

Desh nodded at the nav display. "I'll plot alternates in case the bearing curves."

That was it. No *why aren't you bringing a Jedi*, no *does your father know*, no *what exactly does anomalous mean in this context*. The absence of those questions was not an absence of understanding—Desh's silence on the subject carried exactly the weight of someone who knew which doors not to open. Ben had spent nine years appreciating that weight. He didn't examine it now, either.

"Departure in five," Ben said.

"Ready when you are."

The *Gallant Reach* lifted on repulsors and angled toward the bay exit. Coruscant's perpetual amber light pressed through the viewport as the ship cleared the hangar mouth, and Ben fed power to the drives and pointed her nose at the sky. The city-planet fell away in layers—the mid-level traffic lanes, the upper-atmosphere transports, the thinning haze where atmosphere gave way to black. Coruscant Station control gave him a departure vector and he took it, threading between a bulk freighter and a passenger liner before the traffic lanes spread out and the planet became a curve of amber light behind him.

The wrongness was present. It had been present since the Temple, since the meeting with his father, since the first survey report had put a shape to what he'd been half-perceiving for weeks. He'd gotten used to it the way you got used to a high-pitched tone in a room—background noise until you deliberately listened for it.

He listened for it now.

Still there. Still sour, still directionless, still patient in the way that things without volition were patient.

He punched in the hyperspace coordinates and engaged the drive.

The stars stretched and collapsed into the tunnel. The *Gallant Reach* shuddered once as the realspace-hyperspace boundary passed through the hull, and Ben waited for the wrongness to do what it always did in hyperspace—fade, or at least soften, the way all Force perceptions softened when you were outside normal space and the usual reference points didn't apply.

It didn't fade.

The instruments flickered—a half-second dropout across three secondary systems, nothing critical, the kind of thing you'd log and not worry about. Ben wasn't watching the instruments. The wrongness had shifted. Still sour, still the same low register, but it had acquired a direction the way a compass needle acquires direction when you stop moving: a distinct, specific pull toward the forward-port quarter, toward whatever waited at the end of the bearing he was already flying.

Not louder. Not stronger. Just *pointed*.

Ben kept his hands on the controls and looked at the hyperspace tunnel and said nothing.

In the co-pilot's seat, Desh was already pulling up the alternate nav plots, his eyes on his own display, apparently unconcerned with whatever the Jedi next to him was not saying.
