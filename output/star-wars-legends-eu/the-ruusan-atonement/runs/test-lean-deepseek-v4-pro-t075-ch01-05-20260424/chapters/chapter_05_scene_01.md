Ben's hand found the stasis release before he'd finished deciding.

The lever was cold under his fingers, older than the Order he served, older than the language the display panels were written in. He pulled it. The mechanism resisted for half a second — a thousand years of disuse catching in the gears — and then gave with a sound like a held breath released.

The first chamber's field dissolved.

Blue light collapsed inward and was gone, and the woman inside opened her eyes.

She didn't blink. Didn't orient. Her body moved before her mind caught up — knees bending, weight dropping, hands coming up in a guard position that Ben didn't recognize but understood immediately. Combat crouch. The posture of someone who had woken into danger more times than she'd woken into safety.

Her eyes found his. Gray, steady, cataloguing him the way he was cataloguing her — threat assessment, not recognition.

And then her presence hit him.

Sharp. That was the first word his mind reached for and discarded. Sharp wasn't right. Sharp was a blade, and this was a blade that had been whetted against something harder than stone. Disciplined — yes, that was closer. A signature shaped by training that Ben could feel the age of without being able to name the era. The way you could tell a building was old without knowing the architectural style. The way a voice carried an accent from a region you'd never visited.

She was Jedi. He knew that before she spoke, before she moved again, before anything else registered. But she was Jedi the way Gavran's fortress was Jedi — from a time when the word meant something different than it meant to him.

"Identify yourself."

Her voice was hoarse from a millennium of disuse. The words came out scraped raw, but the syntax was precise — complete sentences, formal address, the particular cadence of someone who had given orders in combat and expected them to be followed. No contractions. No hesitation.

Ben opened his mouth to answer.

The second chamber's field collapsed before he could.

The man who emerged was broader than the woman — shoulders that had carried weight, hands that had held something heavier than a lightsaber. He staggered, caught himself on the doorframe with one hand, and stood there breathing for a long moment with his head bowed. The posture of someone who had been holding a line and had only just realized the line was gone.

His presence arrived warm.

That was the only word for it. Not hot — not the aggressive heat of anger or the banked-coal heat of barely controlled power. Warm. The kind of warmth that made you want to step closer, that promised safety and shelter and someone who would carry the weight if you let him. It settled into the room like a stone settling into water, and Ben felt it in his chest before he felt it in the Force — a pressure that was not pressure, a weight that was not weight, something that had been sunk deep and left there long enough to grow roots.

The contrast with the woman's presence was immediate and unsettling. Where she was an edge, he was a foundation. Where she was ready for combat, he was still finding his balance. Where she had snapped into a fighting stance, he was leaning on a doorframe with his head bowed, and Ben understood without knowing how that this man had been holding something for a very long time and had only just let go.

"Ben." Desh's voice was quiet, from somewhere behind him and to the left. "Three. The third one's field is flickering."

Ben didn't turn. He couldn't. The woman was still watching him from her crouch, her gray eyes tracking his face and his hands and the lightsaber at his belt with the same tactical precision she'd used to assess the room. The man was straightening now, his breathing evening out, his head coming up to reveal a face that was younger than Ben had expected — not young, but younger than the weight in his presence suggested. His eyes found the woman first, not Ben, and something passed between them that Ben couldn't read.

"Your name," the woman said. Not a question this time. A demand.

"Ben Skywalker."

The name meant nothing to her. He could see it land and fail to connect — no recognition, no reaction, no flicker of the thing that usually happened when people heard *Skywalker* in the presence of a Jedi. She was still waiting, still assessing, still holding her combat crouch with the ease of someone who could hold it for hours.

"You are Jedi-trained."

"Yes."

"Not of my Order."

"No."

She absorbed that. Her eyes moved past him — to Desh, to the emergency lighting, to the ring of chambers and the nine dark ones and the empty one with its shattered viewport. She catalogued each of them in turn, and Ben watched her do the arithmetic.

"What year is it?"

He told her.

The room held its breath.

He saw it land on each of them differently. The woman — Sera, he knew without knowing, the name arriving with the same certainty as the edge of her presence — went still in a way that was not stillness. The man — Torin, warm and heavy and settling — closed his eyes and kept them closed for a long moment, his jaw tightening around a number that was too large to hold. And the third presence, the one he hadn't seen yet, the one whose field was still flickering in its chamber —

The third presence went sharp and quiet and very, very still.

Ben turned.

The third chamber's field had dissolved while he was watching the others. The man inside had exited in silence — no stagger, no crouch, no hand on the doorframe. He was standing with his back against the wall of the stasis ring, his weight settled onto his rear foot, his eyes moving across the room in a pattern that Ben recognized from a dozen tactical briefings. Cataloguing exits. Identifying threats. Mapping a space he had never seen with the particular attention of someone who had learned to do it before he learned to speak.

His presence was clipped. Guarded. Trained in a register Ben had not encountered before — not Jedi, not quite, but not Sith either. Something that had been shaped by discipline and then compressed. A voice that had learned to speak quietly because loud voices drew attention, and attention was dangerous.

He didn't speak. He didn't move. He stood with his back against the wall and his eyes tracking the room, and his silence was the loudest thing in it.

Sera straightened from her crouch. The movement was slow, deliberate — someone who had decided, provisionally, that the immediate threat had passed and was now reassessing. Her eyes went to Torin first, and something in her face shifted — recognition, relief, a thousand other things that Ben couldn't name and didn't have the right to witness. Then to the man against the wall. Her jaw tightened.

"Kael."

Not a greeting. An acknowledgment. The man — Kael — didn't respond. His eyes found hers for a moment and then moved on, still cataloguing, still mapping, still refusing to commit to anything more than observation.

Torin opened his eyes.

He looked at Sera first — the same recognition, the same relief, the same thousand things Ben couldn't name. Then at Kael, and his expression flickered in a way that Ben read as complicated. Then at Ben, and his gaze was steady in a way that felt like being weighed.

And then he looked at the ring of chambers.

The nine dark ones. The shattered one. The emergency lighting flickering across the scoring that radiated outward from the empty chamber's viewport, the damage marks that Desh had traced with his fingers, the BREACH warning still blinking amber on the monitoring panel.

Torin was the last to orient. He looked at the nine dark chambers, the empty one, and said quietly: "Who got out?"
