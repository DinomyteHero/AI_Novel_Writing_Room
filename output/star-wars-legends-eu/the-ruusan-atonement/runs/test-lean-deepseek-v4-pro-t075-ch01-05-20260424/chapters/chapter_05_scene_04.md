The command table was a slab of ancient stone that had probably served the same function when Gavran's people still walked these halls. Ben spread the data across it in layers—Corridor maps bleeding amber light across the surface, seal readings that spiked and dipped in patterns he couldn't yet read, the chamber file with its occupant designation field sitting blank where a name should be.

The wrongness pressed at the edges of his perception. Not the focused pressure of the empty chamber below, but something wider now—diffused through the fortress like smoke through a ventilation system. He couldn't get a grip on it. The Force around him felt the way a note played slightly flat felt, not wrong enough to name but wrong enough to notice, and the noticing didn't help.

Sera and Torin worked through the archive materials beside him. She moved with the precision of someone who had organized tactical intelligence for decades—which she had, Ben reminded himself, even if those decades were a thousand years old. Her hands sorted datacards and physical folios into categories Ben hadn't asked for but immediately recognized: seal infrastructure, Corridor geography, personnel records, threat assessment. Everything in its place.

Torin worked differently. His arrangement sprawled—connected items touching, categories overlapping, the whole thing reading less like a filing system and more like a story he was telling himself about what they were looking at. He paused over a folio, tilted his head, and set it beside another folio from a different stack entirely. The connection wasn't obvious to Ben, but Torin's hands moved with the confidence of someone who had done this a thousand times before. Literally.

"Gavran's survey notes," Torin said, tapping the first folio. "And the supply manifests from the same period." He tapped the second. "He was provisioning for a longer deployment than the official records show. Someone was planning to stay."

Ben filed that. The warmth in Torin's voice was still there—the same narrative ease he'd carried in the archive—and it still landed one degree off from where Ben expected it. Not wrong, exactly. Just aimed at something Ben couldn't see.

Sera didn't look up from her sorting. "The provisioning was for the anchors. Gavran knew the seal would need maintenance beyond his lifetime." She set another datacard in the threat-assessment pile with a click that sounded final. "He was not planning to stay. He was planning for us to stay."

The distinction hung in the air. Ben watched Sera's hands—still precise, still controlled—and saw what the precision was doing. Armor. She was organizing the archive the way she'd organized herself since waking: everything in its place because the alternative was looking at what the place had cost her.

He didn't comment. He turned back to the command table and the chamber file with its name-shaped blank.

"Desh."

"Already on it." Desh was at the secondary console, his hands moving across the interface with the particular rhythm of someone who'd spent years pulling intelligence from systems that didn't want to give it up. "The monitoring packets are corrupted, but the corruption's patterned—whoever scrubbed the logs didn't have time to do it right. I can reconstruct the response-quadrant data by morning."

"Morning."

"That's the earliest I can give you. The packets are a millennium old and someone tried to eat them on the way out." Desh glanced up, his expression flat. "The name's in there. I just need time to pull it out."

The timeline crystallized. Ben felt it settle into place like a lock engaging—the escapee had two months of head start, the seal was failing regardless, and the survivors were already organizing the archive as if departure was assumed. Sera's precision. Torin's sprawl. Kael's silence from the corner where he'd been standing since they entered, watching the room with the particular attention of someone who'd decided not to volunteer anything until he knew what the volunteering would cost.

The choice was being made around him.

Ben pulled out his datapad. The motion felt automatic—his hands moving before his conscious mind had finished the calculation. He opened a new message addressed to Luke's personal comm code and started typing.

*Survey complete. Significant structural anomaly at target site. Delaying return to investigate further. Will update when I have more.*

He read the words once. Twice. The gap between what he'd written and what he knew sat in his chest like something he'd swallowed wrong. No mention of the survivors. No mention of the seal. No mention of the empty chamber and the two-month head start and the methodical access pattern of someone who'd been planning their exit for centuries.

He didn't write the word *help*.

His thumb hovered over the send key. The wrongness pressed at the edges of his perception—the missing-handrail sensation, the bad footing—and his instinct said *call Luke, call him now, tell him everything*. It was the instinct his training had built. The instinct his father would have followed. The instinct that had kept Jedi alive for a thousand years by making sure no one carried a crisis alone.

Ben sent the message.

The datapad chimed confirmation. The transmission was already routing through the fortress's comm array, bouncing toward Coruscant through relays that would deliver it to Luke's desk within the hour. The reply window would close before Desh finished reconstructing the packets. Before the escapee's name resolved. Before Ben had enough information to justify what he'd just done.

He didn't name it as a choice. The pattern had closed too fast for naming. But his hands had known what they were doing before his mind caught up, and the weight of it settled onto his shoulders with the particular finality of something he couldn't take back.

"Everyone to the ship." His voice came out steady. "We're lifting in ten."

The *Steady Returns* strained off the plateau with a groan that ran through the deck plates and into Ben's boots. Five people in a ship built for two—the engines were compensating, but he could feel the effort in the vibration beneath his feet. The fortress shrank in the viewport behind them, its dark stone bleeding into the darker mountainside, and the wrongness that had pressed at him inside its walls diffused into the wider sky.

Still present. Still spread. Still unreliable.

Sera stood at the cockpit's secondary console, her hands hovering above the interface without touching it. The controls were modern—standard YT-series layout, nothing exotic—but she was reading them the way you read a language you hadn't spoken in a thousand years. Close. Restrained. Working it out.

Torin leaned against the bulkhead behind her, observing. His warmth was still there, but it had acquired a watchfulness that Ben hadn't seen in the archive. He was cataloguing the ship the way he'd catalogued the folios—making connections Ben couldn't follow.

Kael had found the jump seat in the aft compartment and strapped himself in without being asked. His silence was deliberate. Ben had spent enough time around quiet people to know the difference between silence that meant nothing and silence that meant *I'm waiting*.

Desh was already running the nav computer, his hands moving across the interface with the same efficiency he'd shown at the monitoring console. "Corridor heading's locked. First node is six hours out." He didn't ask if Ben was sure. He didn't ask what they were going to do when they got there.

The plateau fell away beneath them. The fortress became a shadow on the mountainside. Somewhere ahead, unnamed and patient and methodical, someone was executing a plan that had been centuries in the making. The monitoring packets would resolve her identity overnight—the name sitting in corrupted data that Desh was already reconstructing—but by then they would already be in pursuit.

Ben braced for what came next. The choice was made. The *Steady Returns* was climbing.
