The monitoring station on Level 1 smelled like burnt wiring and old stone. Ben stood over the console Desh had coaxed back to life, watching the display flicker through its startup sequence in a language that predated Basic. The amber light cast long shadows across the room, catching the dust still settling from their descent through the fortress.

"Empty chamber," Ben said. "Show me the logs."

The console hesitated — a lag that was either age or damage — then complied. A timeline unspooled across the screen, date markers in Old Galactic Standard, status flags in a color code Ben didn't recognize. The earliest entries were clean. Methodical. Someone had checked this chamber the way you check a lock you trust but can't afford to ignore.

He scrolled forward. The entries continued — regular intervals, the same status flags, the same steady green. Months became years. Years became decades.

"Skip to the end," Desh said from his left. He'd found a secondary terminal and was running his own search, one hand braced against the console's edge. "Whatever happened, it happened recently."

Ben jumped the display forward. The timeline blurred, dates streaming past in a cascade of amber light, and then —

The entries stopped.

Not gradually. Not with the kind of degradation you'd expect from a millennium-old monitoring system. The readable data ended cleanly, two months back by the console's internal clock, and everything after was corrupted. Not broken. *Corrupted*. The distinction sat in Ben's chest like a stone.

"Desh."

"I see it." Desh leaned closer, his face lit from below by the display's glow. "That's not system failure. System failure leaves fragments — partial entries, error codes, something. This is blank."

Ben's shoulders tightened. The wrongness he'd been feeling since they entered the fortress was still there — the missing-handrail sensation, the bad footing — but it had acquired a direction. Someone had erased the record. Someone who knew the system well enough to scrub it clean.

"Two months," Ben said. "Whoever broke out had two months before we even knew the seal was failing."

"More than that." Desh tapped his own display. "I've got terminal access logs from the upper levels. Someone's been moving through the fortress — Level 3, Level 2, the supply stores on Level 1. Methodical. They knew exactly what they were looking for and where to find it."

Ben crossed to Desh's terminal. The access pattern was unmistakable: a single user, moving with purpose, hitting each station once and only once. No backtracking. No hesitation. The timestamps showed a steady progression over weeks — not the frantic scrambling of someone fleeing, but the deliberate pace of someone executing a plan.

"Supplies," Ben said. "Equipment. They weren't just escaping. They were preparing."

"For what?"

Ben didn't answer. He didn't have an answer. The question hung in the recycled air between them, and the weight of it pressed against his ribs.

Sera's voice cut through the silence. "Here."

She was standing at the entrance to the empty chamber, one hand resting against the doorframe. The erosion marks were visible even from the monitoring station — grooves carved into the stone, not by violence but by patience. Centuries of patient work, the Force applied in increments so small that the monitoring system had never flagged them as a breach. The damage didn't read as desperate. It read as inevitable.

Sera stepped inside. Her fingers traced the deepest groove, following its path from the door's edge to the chamber's center, and Ben saw her face change. It was a half-second break — a tightening around her eyes, a catch in the precision of her movement — and then it was gone. She steadied herself against the wall, her expression smoothing back into the composed register she'd worn since waking.

She didn't explain. Ben didn't ask.

The silence between them carried the weight of what she'd seen and chosen not to say, and Ben filed it alongside the corrupted logs and the methodical access pattern and the two months of silence. The evidence was accumulating. The explanation wasn't.

Torin stood in the doorway.

He'd been quiet since they'd left the archive — not withdrawn, exactly, but observing. The way you observe a room you've been in before and found changed. His gaze moved across the erosion marks with the particular attention of someone who knew what he was looking at, and when he spoke, his voice carried the same narrative warmth it had in the archive. The same ease.

"Patient," he said. "Whoever it was. They didn't break the seal — they wore it down. One thread at a time. Centuries of one thread at a time." He tilted his head, and the ghost of a smile crossed his face. "You have to admire the discipline."

The word landed wrong.

It was a small thing — an inflection, a warmth-adjacent note that sat one degree off from where it should. Torin's voice was still warm. Still easy. But the warmth was aimed at the wrong target, and Ben's attention caught on it the way a musician catches a flat note in a familiar piece. He didn't interpret it. He didn't have a place to put it. He filed it — a small dissonance at the edge of his awareness — and let it pass.

Kael moved.

He'd been standing against the far wall, silent since they'd entered the chamber, and his movement was so minimal that Ben almost missed it. A shift of weight. A glance at Torin that lasted half a second and then returned to the erosion marks. Kael didn't speak. He didn't need to. The glance was enough — the particular quality of a man who had noticed something and decided, for reasons he wasn't sharing, not to name it.

Ben's focus tightened. The group was carrying three separate reactions to the same evidence, and none of them were saying what they meant. Sera's half-second break. Torin's off-note warmth. Kael's silent glance. The empty chamber was telling them something, and they were all reading it differently.

He turned back to the console. "Map," he said. "Overlay the activity timestamps."

The display shifted. The fortress schematic bloomed in amber light — Level 3, Level 2, Level 1, the supply stores, the archive, the hangar bay. The escapee's activity appeared as a series of markers, each timestamped, each connected by the steady progression of someone who had never once doubled back.

The pattern was clear. The direction was clearer.

"The Corridor," Ben said.

The word landed in the room like a stone dropping into still water. The escapee wasn't hiding in the fortress. The escapee wasn't waiting to be found. The escapee was already weeks ahead, moving along the same route Ben had charted from Gavran's survey data, and the distance between them was measured in time they couldn't recover.

Forward pressure settled onto Ben's shoulders. Not despair — his mother's son didn't do despair — but the particular weight of responsibility that came from knowing the threat was already in motion and he was already behind. The fortress was no longer the primary theater. The Corridor was. And somewhere ahead of them, unnamed and patient and methodical, someone was executing a plan that had been centuries in the making.

"Desh," Ben said. His voice was steady. "Pull every monitoring packet from the last three months. Access logs, supply manifests, navigation data — anything that shows where they went and what they took. I want a reconstruction by first light."

Desh nodded once. He didn't ask if Ben was sure. He didn't ask what they were going to do. He was already reaching for the console, his hands moving across the interface with the efficiency of someone who had spent years pulling intelligence from systems that didn't want to give it up.

Ben looked at the map again. The Corridor stretched across the display, a line of uncharted space running from the fortress to the seal's outer boundary. The escapee's markers ended at the hangar bay — the last timestamp two months old, the trail cold, the name still withheld.

Two months of silence. One wrong note he couldn't place. A threat already moving.

Ben braced for what came next.
