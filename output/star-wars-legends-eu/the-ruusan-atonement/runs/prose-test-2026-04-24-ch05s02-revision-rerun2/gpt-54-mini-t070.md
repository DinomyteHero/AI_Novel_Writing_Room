Sera crossed to the archive console before Ben could stop her.

Her hand went to the seam under the upper display. Thumb, forefinger, two taps, a drag, then a pause that looked almost like hesitation until the console answered her and the panel brightened. The sequence was too fast for him to follow cleanly. It had the hard snap of muscle memory.

The archive woke with a low, steady hum. Holo-emitters in the console breathed out pale blue light, and half a dozen file markers rippled into place across the main display. Ben’s chest tightened on the first tone. Not fear. Not exactly. More like the floor had shifted by an inch and his body knew it before his mind did.

Sera did not look back. “There.”

Desh made a small sound beside Ben. “She always do that like she was born with a lockpick in her sleeve?”

“Probably,” Ben said.

The word came out thin. He could hear the archive now: the hum, a faint click from a relay inside the console, the soft scrape of Sera’s boot as she shifted her weight. He could also hear the sealed Codex fragment in its case near the wall, faintly lit and untouched, like it was listening too.

Sera leaned in over the display. Her face was all angles and concentration. She scrolled through files with short, exact motions, then stopped on a block of archaic Basic so formal it looked ceremonial. Ben could read Basic fine. He could not read this quickly.

Torin stepped up beside her, one hand already lifting as if he had done this a thousand times and meant to do it one more. “That is the Veranthos index. I wondered if they would have tagged it under protocol or penalty.”

“Apparently both,” Desh said.

Torin’s mouth quirked. “The archivists were feeling generous.”

Ben watched Torin’s fingers draw the file into the center of the display. The gesture was so practiced it made Ben’s skin prickle. Not because it was elegant. Because it was familiar. Wrongly familiar, like seeing someone use a tool they had no business knowing.

The index unfolded into columns. Titles. Subsections. Containment terms. Compliance clauses. Penance language written so densely it looked like law had been folded over itself until it cut.

Ben’s eyes snagged on a line, then another. Chamber-bound anchors. Central keystone. Gavran.

Thirteen total, if the count was read the way the file read it: twelve in chambers, one at the core. Not a room full of volunteers. Not a chorus. A structure.

He felt Desh glance at him. “That bad?”

Ben kept his eyes on the display. “I am deciding how much I dislike a thousand-year-old filing system.”

Desh snorted. “Fair.”

Torin brushed one open hand across the lower edge of the display. The protocol expanded, and his voice changed with it. Not louder. Warmer. As if he had stopped reading a file and started remembering a corridor. “Veranthos had a phrase for this. You would not have liked the phrase.”

Sera did not look up. “I do not like most phrases.”

“No, but this one earned special treatment.” Torin’s smile came and went. “They called it a keeping measure. Then they got embarrassed and called it a restraint, which was worse.”

Ben’s gaze moved from the text to Torin’s hand. The hand was steady. The hand had the easy confidence of someone touching old scar tissue in a place where he knew the scar would still answer.

Modern Jedi training, Ben thought, and then the thought snagged.

No. Not modern training. The history he had inherited. His father’s Order. The one that had come after all this. The one that had learned to distrust armies, to centralize instruction, to keep the Jedi from becoming warlords again after the New Sith Wars and Ruusan and everything the histories had wrapped around those names. Real history. Public history. The part everybody knew.

He swallowed and looked back at the display. The file heading sat there in black text on blue light.

VERANTHOS CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL.

There it was. Not denied. Not hidden from him now. Just waiting.

Desh leaned in enough to read the upper lines. “That is not what I expected.”

“What did you expect?” Ben asked.

“A map. A diagram. Something with arrows and someone named after a mineral.”

Ben almost laughed. It came out as air through his nose. The sound loosened the tightness in his chest by a fraction, enough to keep him from doing something stupid like grabbing the console.

Sera scrolled lower. Her jaw was set hard enough to cast a shadow under her cheekbone. “The file is intact.”

“Good,” Ben said.

Her eyes flicked to him, cool and precise. “Do not sound pleased. Intact records are often the worst kind.”

That got a real breath out of him. Short, sharp. “I will put that in my notes.”

“You keep notes?”

“Only the ones that make me look competent.”

Torin gave a low laugh, but it faded a beat too fast. Ben saw it. Tiny thing. Still there.

Sera was still reading. “Containment protocols. Anchoring terms. Limit states. Penance restrictions.”

The last two words landed with enough weight to make Ben’s shoulders go rigid.

Desh said, “That sounds cheerful.”

“It was not intended to be.” Sera’s tone did not change. “The wording is exact. The procedure was exact.”

Ben glanced at her. “You know this code.”

“I know the access sequence.” She shut the display with a flick of her hand, then reopened a lower layer. “And the register. The language is Veranthos administrative Basic. Pre-Reformation forms. High legal script in the margin notes.”

Desh gave him a side look. “I understood maybe half of that.”

“Good,” Ben said. “Then I am not the only one.”

Torin reached in again, and this time Ben saw the file title shift. Not the whole thing. Just one designation in the line where a name should have sat. One term replaced by another with a short, clean motion of Torin’s finger.

Architect disappeared.

Keeper remained.

Ben stared. “You just changed that.”

Torin’s expression was open, almost apologetic, and somehow that made it worse. “No. I corrected it.”

“On purpose?”

“I rarely do things by accident these days.”

Kael had been quiet in the back of the room so long Ben had stopped tracking him as movement and started tracking him as absence. Now he was at Sera’s shoulder, eyes narrowed at the display. He reached past her without asking, tapped the line once, and adjusted the designation again.

Not architect. Not even keeper, exactly.

Custodian.

Then he stepped back as if he had touched a hot plate.

The room seemed to tighten around that single word.

Ben looked at him. Kael met his eyes for one brief second, then looked away first. The correction sat on the display with the hard little finality of a lock closing.

“That matters to you,” Ben said.

Kael shrugged one shoulder. “Words matter.”

It was the most he had offered since Ben had met him. Maybe the most he planned to offer today.

Torin let out a breath through his nose. “He is right. Gavran kept it. Engineered it, too, by the looks of that. But he did not build himself into a god and hang a title on the wall.”

Sera’s mouth tightened. “No.”

The word was flat. Exact. She was still looking at the file, not at any of them. “He was custodian of the protocol. The protocol itself was for containment.”

Ben felt the shape of the statement before he fully caught the words. He knew what was coming because the room had already given him enough pieces to guess at the outline. His body reacted first anyway. A drop, like missing a step in dark. One hand flexed once at his side.

Sera lifted her chin a fraction and finished it in the same composed voice. “And penance.”

The archive hummed on. The display did not flicker. No alarm sounded. Nothing dramatic happened at all.

That was the worst part.

Ben heard his own voice answer before he had chosen the words. “The Reformations.”

Sera’s eyes shifted to him. Torin went still. Desh looked from one face to another, caught the change in the room, and said nothing.

Ben heard himself continue, more carefully now. “After Ruusan. The public settlement. The demilitarization, the end of Jedi Lords, the centralized training, the Republic oversight. That came later.”

Sera did not nod. Did not confirm. She did not need to. The archive did that for her, line by line, in a language older than the politics Ben had been taught to attach to it.

He felt the connection land before he had words for it. Not a thought. A drop in his center. A clean, sick clarity.

The later settlement was real. Ruusan was real. The reforms were real. The Order had changed itself, or been forced to change, and the histories he had grown up on were not wrong there.

They were incomplete.

Not by accident. By design.

Ben stared at the file, then at Sera, and the air in the room seemed to sharpen. The hidden protocol sat beneath the public settlement like a sealed chamber under fresh stone. Same names. Different purpose. A private wound carried forward under an honest one.

His mind tried to run ahead. Why penance? For what? What had the old Order done that needed to be buried in law and preserved in a containment file and carried for a thousand years without a name?

He did not get answers. Not yet.

What he got was three separate people standing in front of him, and the room rearranging itself around that fact.

Sera was the instrument. Precise. Exact. The one who could touch the lock and name what it was without softening a single edge.

Torin was the keeper. Warm enough to make the old names feel human, and dangerous for that very reason.

Kael was the correction. One word. No explanation. Enough to make the whole display shift.

Ben looked at them and understood, with a clarity that sat cold and clean in his gut, that he was not standing in a room full of records anymore.

He was standing in a room full of witnesses.

Sera’s hand rested on the console. The light from the display caught the sharp line of her knuckles. She looked at the file once more, then back at Ben.

“Containment,” she said. “And penance.”

Ben heard the word before he meant to. “Reformations.”

Sera did not say what the penance was for. She did not need to. Ben could read the rest of it off her face.
