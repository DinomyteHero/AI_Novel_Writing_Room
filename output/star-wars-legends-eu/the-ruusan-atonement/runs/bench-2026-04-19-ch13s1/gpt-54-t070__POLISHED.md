Kolven filled the viewport in clean bands of blue and green, white cloud laid across it in soft swirls that belonged on a travel brochure or an agricultural survey packet. Temperate world. Settlement-ready. The sort of place people picked when they wanted their grandchildren to forget the word frontier.

Desh looked down at the sensor board and said, "I've got a forty-two degree spread across six kilometers."

Ben kept one hand on the co-pilot yoke and leaned closer to the glass. "Maybe the planet's trying a new climate package."

"No birds," Kael said from the bulkhead behind them.

Ben glanced up. "That your scientific assessment?"

Kael shrugged. "You notice birds when they're missing."

He was right. A world this green should have had motion over the tree line, little flickers and wheels and stupid territorial dives at the ship. The sky stayed empty.

Sera stood behind the pilot seats with her hands clasped behind her back, posture straight enough to shame the chair. "Repeat the scan."

Desh did. His voice stayed flat, the way it always did when flat was the only useful shape. "Surface temperatures contradict visual exposure. North-facing walls are retaining more heat than southern. Vegetation signatures overlap in ways they shouldn't. Blooming and dieback in the same cluster. Chronometric beacon reflections are dirty."

"Dirty how?" Torin asked.

Desh frowned at the display. "How would you like it explained? Technical or honest?"

"Honest," Ben said.

Desh tapped the screen with one blunt finger. "Like the numbers don't agree on what time they were taken."

That got the cockpit quiet.

Below them, sunlight flashed on water. A river cut a bright line through a spread of low structures and gridded streets. From orbit the settlement looked abandoned, not ruined. No scorch marks. No impact scars. No blackened bite taken out of the terrain. Veraine had done this without touching a turbolaser.

Ben rolled his shoulders once and let them settle. "Any power signatures?"

"Nothing stable."

"Life signs?"

Desh gave him a look. "Plant mass. Insects, maybe. No large animal movement. No Humans. No anything else I'd put money on."

Torin moved up between the seats and braced a hand on the frame, broad and easy as ever. "Well. Good news. Nobody's shooting at us."

Sera didn't look at him. "Yet."

Torin smiled. "There's the team spirit."

Ben guided the Steady Returns lower. The landing pads came into view: cracked circles of duracrete near a row of squat prefab structures with roofs half-buried in green. Vines covered the walls in thick ropes. Pretty from up here. Too pretty. The place had the polished stillness of a room staged for guests who were never going to arrive.

The ship dropped through the last of the cloud. Ben reached, out of habit more than thought, brushing for the familiar spread of minds, weather, life, that constant quiet map under everything.

The Force answered.

And gave him nothing he knew how to use.

He tightened his grip on the yoke. Not fear. Not yet. More like the first step onto stairs in the dark when there isn't a stair where your foot expects one.

"Ben?" Desh said.

"Here." His voice came out level. "Taking us in."

He set the ship down on a pad split by weeds thick as cables. The landing struts met duracrete with a solid thump. Normal sound. Normal vibration. Ben waited for the rest of normal to catch up.

It didn't.

The hatch cycled open. Cool air came in, damp with growing things and the mineral bite of old concrete after shade. No birdcall. No rustle from anything larger than leaves moving against one another. Even the insects kept their distance from the opening ramp.

Ben clipped his lightsaber to his belt and started down first. The soles of his boots hit the landing pad with a blunt, familiar contact. Duracrete. Fine grit. A seam where the surface had buckled upward. Everything his body touched said world. His balance said drop.

He stopped at the foot of the ramp.

The street beyond the pad was still traceable between low homes and a caved-in market shelter. Doorframes stood. Windows stared black. Vines climbed one building, then in the span of a breath drew back from a sunlit edge and fed themselves into shadow instead. Not fast. Not dramatic. Just enough that his eyes caught the motion and refused to file it anywhere useful.

Behind him, Sera came down the ramp and halted hard enough that her robe brushed his arm. "Do you see that?"

"Wish I didn't."

Torin stepped out next, scanning left and right. "I've had worse welcomes."

Kael followed and stood still with his hands loose at his sides, head tilted. "No birds," he said again, softer this time, like the silence had earned a second look.

Desh came last carrying a compact field kit in one hand. He swept the area once with his eyes, then once with the scanner, and seemed to trust his eyes less after the second pass than before it. "Temperature dropped three degrees between the ramp and here."

"We walked six meters," Ben said.

"I know."

Sera had gone rigid beside him. Not statue-still. Braced. Ben knew that look from sparring rings and firefights alike: hold the line, define the problem, deny it the right to move you.

He wished he had a line to hold.

Usually the Force offered one whether he asked or not. A pressure, a pull, a warning texture under a choice. Not commands. Never that. Just shape. The edge of wrong. The clean grain of right. Here he reached and touched presence with no direction in it at all. Energy. Potential. Near enough to feel against his skin from the inside.

No axis.

Ben stepped off the pad and into the street.

His stomach dipped. Not motion. Not vertigo. The ground was flat under him. His body knew exactly where his feet were. The rest of him did not. The old reflex to center himself through the Force fired and ran into open space.

Torin moved past him a pace, slower now. "Ben."

"I know."

Torin looked back, warmth gone from his face. "Do you?"

Ben almost answered with something dry. It died before it reached his mouth.

Across the street, pale flowers had opened in a strip of shadow beneath a collapsed awning. The same vine on the sunlit side hung closed and colorless. Sera crossed to the wall and knelt without touching it.

"These should be dead," she said.

"That covers a lot of this place," Kael muttered.

Sera shot him a look sharp enough to cut cable. "Their roots are on the opposite side of the wall."

Kael peered over from a careful distance. "Then they're ambitious."

Torin huffed a single laugh. The sound stopped fast, as if he'd heard how alone it was.

Ben turned in a slow circle. Streets. Intact corners. A child's repulsor toy half-sunk in creeping moss. An apartment block with laundry lines still strung between balconies, empty and clean, no cloth left on them. He kept expecting the place to declare itself—to show the damage in a way the eye could understand. Fire. Blight. Corpses. Something with edges.

Instead there was drift. While watched.

A vine on the market shelter crept downward, then checked, then threaded itself sideways along a crack. Ben stared at it until his eyes watered.

Desh crouched near the landing pad and took a chrono from his kit. Old-school, metal case, second hand under a plain glass face. He thumbed it awake and held it out.

"Tell me I need calibration," he said.

Ben went over. The second hand moved, paused, jumped two marks, ticked once, then held again.

Torin joined them. "Faulty mechanism."

Desh snorted. "Mechanical backup. Shielded case. I keep it because digital systems lie in new and exciting ways." He lifted the chrono closer to his ear, then looked at Ben. "This one's trying for classic."

Ben listened. Tick. Tick. Gap. Tick.

The gap sat wrong in his teeth.

Desh lowered the watch. For the first time since orbit, some strain showed through the practical calm. It wasn't panic. It looked meaner than that, because it had nowhere to go. "I don't like this place."

Ben almost said, None of us do. The words stalled.

What pressed below his ribs wasn't his own reaction, not entirely. Desh had no Force-sense for this. No trained language. No old Jedi categories to brace with. He had a watch that skipped, a silent sky, and a settlement that had gone bad in ways his hands couldn't fix. The weight in him had the blunt pull of grief, but grief for what? The homes were empty. The people had been gone for who knew how long. The loss in the air refused to attach to any face, any body, any event.

Ben turned toward him to offer reassurance and found nothing to offer with any honesty behind it.

Not calm. Not certainty. Not the usual, steady undercurrent that said this is wrong and can be mended, this is dangerous and can be navigated, this is dark and must be resisted. He had words. He did not have the shape beneath them.

Sera rose from the wall and came back toward the group. "The growth is still changing."

Torin looked at the chrono, then at the buildings around them. "Field test."

Nobody argued.

Veraine had done this here first. Taken a world people had meant to live on and stripped it into a lesson. See what holds. See what doesn't. See how large the wound can be made before the galaxy notices it is bleeding.

Ben's hand drifted toward his saber, stopped there. Metal. Weight. A thing built to answer a problem. He left it clipped.

Kael stared down the street, toward the river. "I hate places where the silence feels busy."

Ben looked at him. "That one sounded rehearsed."

"I've had practice."

Torin faced Ben fully now. "Can you read the edge of it?"

Sera did the same, and that was worse. Torin's question carried concern. Sera's silence carried expectation. Ben had been the one moving first, sensing first, naming the thing under the thing, since he was old enough to know there was a difference.

He planted his feet shoulder-width apart on the cracked duracrete. "Maybe."

Desh tucked the chrono away but kept his hand on the pocket, as if he could pin time in place by pressure. "You want us back on the ship for this?"

"No." Ben looked at the street, the moving vines, the flowers blooming where light didn't reach. "Stay where I can see you."

Sera's gaze narrowed. "If the zone reacts—"

"It already is," Kael said.

Ben shut his eyes.

The first reach had been reflex, half-formed, the mental equivalent of checking a railing in the dark. This one he did on purpose. Breathe in. Let the noise of his own body settle. Open the familiar door.

The Force came at once.

Present. Full. Vast enough to drown in.

No light. No dark. No warning edge. No current carrying his thoughts into proportion. He touched power without contour. Touched life without moral weight. Touched the world and got back response with nothing to compare itself to. Every trained instinct in him leaped toward recognition and struck emptiness. Not absence of the Force. Absence of what had always made sense of it.

The drop hit him in the body.

His knees unlocked. He caught himself before he stumbled, boots scraping on grit. The ground under him stayed firm. The ground of sense was gone.

He opened his eyes to keep from pitching into that open interior fall.

Torin had taken half a step forward. Stopped. His hands were open, useless at his sides.

Sera's face had gone pale under the severity. "Ben."

He heard her. The word arrived stripped down to sound and concern in a Human voice. It did not carry the usual echo of intent behind it. He had never noticed that echo before because it had always been there.

"Don't," he said, and his own voice sounded bare to him.

Desh shifted his weight. "Talk to me."

Ben stared at the street because it was a street, because walls were walls, because if he named enough solid things maybe one of them would reach inside and brace him. Moss on the curb. Split drain cover. Blue paint flaking from a doorway. His heart beating too hard. Sweat cooling under his collar in the damp air.

The Force remained wide open around all of it.

Unshaped.

For one awful instant, relief tried to rise with the terror. No pull. No correction. No quiet pressure against the worst angles of his own mind. Nothing but himself standing in it.

He understood then what Veraine had touched and called freedom.

Ben stood in the street on Kolven with the others watching him and heard his thoughts come back without any softening at all. Not guided. Not weighed. Not checked against anything outside him. In the silence of the de-structured Force, Ben hears himself think without the safety net of Force intuition for the first time. The thoughts are his. All of them. Including the ones the Force usually softens.
