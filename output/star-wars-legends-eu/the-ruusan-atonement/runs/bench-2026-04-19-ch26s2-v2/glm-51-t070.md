The void had no edges.

Ben stood at the threshold—where the caldera's rim met the absence beyond—and reached for Sera's presence the way a man in a dark room reaches for a wall. His fingers found nothing. His senses found less. The Force inside the Scar was present but unstructured, present but wrong, like water that had forgotten the shape of its container. He could feel the lattice straining around the wound, the new resonance holding against the pull, and he could feel Sera somewhere in that formless dark, moving with the precise determination of someone who had waited a thousand years to refuse a pattern.

He should be in there with her. The thought scraped across his mind, sharp and useless. The Skywalker instinct: fix it yourself, bear the cost yourself, control the outcome yourself. His mother's son. His father's grandson. The whole lineage of people who looked at other people's suffering and thought, *I should be the one holding the weight.* Maybe that was the real family tradition—not the Force sensitivity, not the propensity for trouble, but the bone-deep conviction that love meant taking the hit so someone else didn't have to.

Well. That was working out great for Torin.

Ben planted his feet and reached deeper into the resonance, adding his focus to the seal Kael was holding at the perimeter. Kael's presence flickered at the edge of his awareness—steady, watchful, the man's particular talent for finding the angle that kept everyone alive expressing itself in the way he braced the lattice without trying to control it. No heroics. No sacrifice. Just the structural equivalent of a hand on a load-bearing wall. A lesson Ben was still learning.

Inside the void, Sera found the Scar.

Ben felt it as a shift in pressure—not physical, not exactly, but the Force-equivalent of atmospheric change before a storm broke. The wound pulsed. The lattice around it flexed. And at its edge, standing with his hands pressed flat against the tear in reality, was Torin Hal.

His Force signature was already half-gone.

Ben's stomach dropped. He could feel it now—the way Torin's presence was being drawn into the wound, thread by thread, his identity dissolving into the containment like ink spreading through water. His eyes were open. They did not see Sera. They did not see anything. The man who had laughed easily, who had told stories with self-deprecating warmth, who had made this group feel like a team—he was already disappearing into the Scar, and the part of him that remained was a vessel more than a person. A container. Structural rebar, holding the wound closed with the only material he had left.

Ben's hands twitched at his sides. The impulse to reach in himself almost overwhelmed him—pull the man out, bear the weight, do what Skywalkers did. He held back. Not because he trusted the plan. Because Sera had earned this. A thousand years of waiting to refuse a pattern, and this was the refusal. If he reached in now, he would be making the same choice Torin had made. The same choice Jacen had made. The same choice every Jedi Lord had made when they decided that self-sacrifice was the highest form of love.

He held back. It cost him.

Sera did not hesitate.

Ben felt her move through the void—not the way movement worked in open air, but the way a blade moves through water, deliberate and cutting. She reached Torin's position and placed her hands over his.

The technique resisted.

Ben's breath caught. The Force-locks were built to seal, to contain, to hold. A thousand years of discipline, refined across generations of Jedi who had never imagined the locks could be turned the other way. Sera was trying to unseal—to pull Torin free instead of binding him—and the lattice pushed back with structural fury, the containment infrastructure recognizing her touch and trying to complete the pattern it had been designed for. The locks wanted to close. They wanted to hold. They wanted to do what they had always done.

She refused.

Ben felt the reversal like a physical lurch in his chest. The locks, designed to hold, began to release. The discipline that had imprisoned her for a millennium—her prison, her training, the thing that had made her useful to Gavran and the thing that had kept her conscious in the dark—turned inside out. She was not sealing. She was unsealing. She was using the same technique, the same precision, the same thousand-year expertise, for the opposite purpose. The same hands that had held things closed were now pulling them apart.

The wound screamed.

Not audibly. Not in words. But Ben felt the Scar's pull intensify, felt the lattice shudder as the containment structure destabilized, felt the resonance he and Kael were holding threaten to shear. His hands clenched at his sides, the impulse to reach in and steady it himself almost overwhelming. He could feel Sera's presence locked with Torin's, could feel the adhesive bond between the man and the wound stretching—

—and beginning to tear.

The separation was not clean. It was not elegant. It was the Force-equivalent of ripping a bandage off a wound that had healed around the fabric, pulling free what had fused together. Ben felt it in his chest like a shared ache, felt his muscles tense as he shifted his stance to channel more focus into the resonance, felt a wave of nausea roll through him as Torin's Force signature—permanently altered, permanently reduced—came loose from the Scar.

The wound pulled at him. The Scar wanted to keep what it had claimed. Sera's hands tightened over Torin's, and Ben felt her refusal like a physical force—not the cold discipline of the Force-locks, but something hotter. Something that had burned in the dark for a thousand years, waiting for the chance to choose differently.

She pulled.

The adhesive tore.

Torin's presence separated from the wound in a single, violent instant, and Ben felt the new seal reassert itself around the Scar—felt the lattice flex, felt the resonance steady, felt Kael's anchor hold and his own brace hold and the containment infrastructure close around the wound without Torin's presence to sustain it. The resonance hummed with the strain of what had just happened, but it held. The wound was contained. The seal was intact.

The man who had held it was free.

Sera and Torin emerged from the void together.

Ben's throat tightened. He could see them now—not just feel them, but see them—Sera's hands still pressed over Torin's, her grip the only thing keeping him upright. Torin's eyes were open. They had been open the whole time. But now they found Sera's face.

For a heartbeat, warmth flickered there. Recognition. The man Ben had traveled with, the one who had laughed at his own mistakes and treated Ben with genuine affection, surfaced like a diver breaking water. Ben saw Sera's name form on Torin's lips without sound.

Then the warmth faded.

It did not collapse. It did not shatter. It simply settled into something quieter, something smaller. The way a fire settles into coals. The way a voice settles into a whisper when the person speaking has forgotten why they were shouting.

Torin pulled free. Alive. His eyes find Sera's face. For a moment—just a moment—the warmth returns. Then it fades to something quieter. He knows her. But the way he knows her is different. Diminished. Present but smaller. The caldera didn't consume him. It replaced the parts that made him Torin.
