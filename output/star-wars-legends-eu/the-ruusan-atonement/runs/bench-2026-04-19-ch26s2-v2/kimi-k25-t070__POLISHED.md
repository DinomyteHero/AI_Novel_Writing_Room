The threshold had no edge. Ben stood at the boundary where Veranthos's broken crust ended and the Scar began, and the transition offered no resistance under his boots—just a slow fade from volcanic rock into absence. The void didn't swallow sound; it removed the conditions that allowed vibration to travel. His breathing existed only in the tightness of his chest, the expansion of his ribs, the rush of blood past his eardrums. No echo. No air currents brushing his skin. The Force existed here, but without the lattice that gave it shape, without the directional push and pull that Jedi used to navigate. It pressed against his awareness like water at impossible depth, directionless, temperatureless, textureless.

He reached inward, not with his hands—he'd learned that lesson three hours ago when his fingers had passed through the threshold and gone numb for twenty minutes—but with the part of him that had always mapped the galaxy through the Force's structure. That map failed here. No up. No down. No north. The usual constellation of living things, the background radiation of sentient thought, the gravitational signatures of mass and energy—all of it flattened into a single plane of undifferentiated presence. Except for Sera. A point of disciplined intensity moving through the formless dark, her presence sharp as a blade held against the throat of infinity.

Ben shifted his weight, boots grinding on stone that wasn't there. The absurdity struck him—that he was essentially standing guard at the entrance to nothing, prepared to fight something that had no shape, no mass, no weakness a lightsaber could exploit. His hand rested on his lightsaber hilt more from habit than tactical necessity. If something came out of the void that Sera couldn't handle, his plasma blade would offer about as much resistance as a polite request.

Then he felt Torin. Or what remained of him.

At the Scar's center, where the wound in the Force opened raw and weeping into the de-structured dark, Torin stood with his hands pressed flat against the absence. Ben's perception of him came sideways—through the way the void bent around his silhouette, through the slow drain of his Force signature into the lattice that Gavran had built a thousand years ago to hold this place together. Torin's eyes were open. Ben knew this without seeing them, knew it through the hollow way his friend's presence broadcast outward, eyes fixed on something that wasn't there, seeing nothing, registering nothing. His signature had already thinned, spreading into the containment structure like ink diffusing in water.

Ben's stomach clenched. The control instinct screamed at him—*reach in, grab him, pull him out*—but his mother's training overrode it with the brutal efficiency of a circuit breaker. Sera was already moving. To interfere now, to break the perimeter and plunge his own unprotected presence into the void, would collapse the resonance he and Kael had spent hours establishing. He forced his hands to stay at his sides, fingers twitching against empty air, muscles trembling with the suppressed urge to act.

Sera reached Torin. Ben felt the moment of contact as a disturbance in the void's equilibrium—a pressure wave where two solid objects collided in a space that shouldn't permit collision, a ripple in the formless soup. She placed her hands over Torin's, covering his fingers where they pressed against the wound. The gesture looked intimate from outside, a lover's touch, a blessing. From inside, Ben knew it was violence.

She reversed the technique.

The Force-lock discipline Sera had spent a millennium perfecting—the art of sealing, of binding, of holding things in stasis against the entropy of time and the corruption of the dark side—turned one hundred eighty degrees. Ben felt it through the resonance they shared, felt the precise moment her intention shifted from *contain* to *release*, from *hold* to *surrender*. The lattice resisted. It had been built to accept Torin's presence as structural reinforcement, to weave his Force signature into the containment architecture like rebar in ferrocrete. Now Sera demanded it unweave, demanded the structure release its grip on the living man it had half-digested.

The void shuddered. Not physically—there was no physics here, no matter to vibrate—but the metaphysical structure that underpinned the Scar buckled, flexed, screamed in frequencies that Ben felt as nausea behind his eyes. He staggered, his connection to the resonance snapping taut like a cable under sudden load, the strain transmitting down his spine and into his heels. He fed more focus into the matrix, stabilizing the perimeter while Sera attacked the center, pouring his will into the container they'd built to replace the ancient anchors. Kael, somewhere to his left, made a noise between a grunt and a growl, his own presence thickening like concrete poured into a foundation, rough and heavy and immovable, holding the edges while Sera tore at the center.

Sera pulled.

The separation tore through the Force with a sound that wasn't sound—a sensation of adhesive ripping free from skin, of surgical tape stripped from hair, of Velcro torn apart fiber by fiber in slow, agonizing increments. Torin's presence, which had been half-integrated into the wound's containment lattice, began to detach. The technique fought back. The lattice had grown around him, through him, into the spaces between his thoughts, threading his identity through the seal's infrastructure. Extracting him was surgery performed without anesthesia, on a patient who couldn't scream, while the operating room collapsed around them, each fiber of connection resisting separation with the desperate grip of roots torn from dry earth.

Ben's breath stopped. His own hands ached in sympathy, joints throbbing as if he'd gripped something too hot and refused to let go, skin burning with the friction of resistance. He channeled more energy into the resonance, feeling the strain in his lower back, the burn in his thighs as he shifted his stance to ground the overflow, teeth grinding, sweat stinging his eyes despite the cold. The new seal—they'd built it to contain the wound without anchors, without the living rebar that Gavran had used—wavered at the edges, the lattice they'd constructed straining to compensate for the sudden absence of Torin's integrated presence. For one infinite second, Ben thought it would collapse entirely, that Sera's refusal to accept the pattern would destroy everything they'd worked to preserve, that the wound would open fully and swallow them all in a cascade of de-structured Force.

Then Torin came free.

The extraction hit Ben like a punch to the sternum, a physical shockwave that rocked him back on his heels. He gasped, sucking in air that tasted of sulfur and metal. Torin's Force signature, which had been diffuse and spreading, a smear of identity across the containment lattice, suddenly contracted into something small and specific. But wrong. The texture had changed. Where before Torin's presence had radiated warmth, command, the easy confidence of a man who carried too much and asked for help too little—now it was quiet. Diminished. Present, but occupying less space in the Force, like a room after the furniture has been removed.

Sera drew him out. They moved together through the void, two shapes resolving from the formless dark, becoming distinct as they approached the threshold, Sera's arms around his torso, guiding him like a sleepwalker. Ben watched them emerge—not with his eyes, which saw only the visual spectrum, the play of light on dust, but with the part of him that recognized people by the shape of their souls.

They crossed the boundary. Veranthos's harsh gravity reasserted itself with brutal immediacy. Torin's knees buckled. Sera caught him, her arms tightening around his torso, her cheek pressed against his shoulder. Ben stepped forward to help, then stopped. This wasn't his moment to touch. This was Sera's victory, Sera's cost, Sera's love expressed through the discipline turned backward.

Torin pulled free. Alive. His eyes find Sera's face. For a moment—just a moment—the warmth returns. Then it fades to something quieter. He knows her. But the way he knows her is different. Diminished. Present but smaller. The caldera didn't consume him. It replaced the parts that made him Torin.
