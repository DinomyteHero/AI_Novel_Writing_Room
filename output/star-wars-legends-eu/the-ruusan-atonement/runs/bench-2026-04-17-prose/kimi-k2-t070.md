The training blade caught Ben across the shoulder with a crack that echoed through the empty salle. He stumbled back, blinking sweat from his eyes as the familiar weight of failure settled in his gut.

"Third time this week, Skywalker."

Ben deactivated his training saber and rolled his shoulder, testing the bruise already forming under his tunic. The other Knight—tall, human, probably had a name—stood relaxed in guard position, blade humming at ready. Ben had been paired with him for three sessions now and kept forgetting to ask.

"Long day," Ben said, wiping sweat from his forehead. The excuse felt hollow even as he said it. The truth sat heavier: the Force had stuttered again, that flat note striking just as he'd needed perfect timing. Like trying to dance to music that kept skipping half-beats.

"You're favoring your left side." The Knight's blade dipped slightly. "Distracted?"

Ben almost laughed. Distracted didn't cover it. The wrongness had been building for weeks now, a discord running beneath everything like a ship's engine slightly out of alignment. Everyone else seemed to think it was residual disturbance from the latest galactic crisis—background noise that would fade. But Ben had grown up around Jacen's fall and Abeloth's chaos. He knew what real Force disturbance felt like. This was different. Structural.

"Fatigue," he said instead, clipping his training saber to his belt. "Been pushing too hard."

The Knight nodded, accepting the lie. They always did. Being Luke Skywalker's son came with certain expectations—chief among them that any failure was temporary, easily corrected with more focus and discipline. Ben had learned to let people believe what they wanted.

"I'll catch you tomorrow," Ben added, already moving toward the equipment racks.

The Knight departed without argument, his footsteps echoing down the corridor until only the salle's ambient hum remained. Ben waited until the door sealed before letting his shoulders sag.

Alone now. Thank the Force.

He paced the training circle, boots scuffing against the polished floor. The salle felt different empty—larger, more honest. No witnesses to what he was about to test.

Ben drew a deep breath and began the opening sequence of Ataru, moving through the forms without igniting his blade. Just muscle memory and balance and the precise timing that had earned him his Knight's braid at nineteen. His body knew this dance. Had known it since childhood when his mother first put a training saber in his hands.

Forward roll. Spring up. Spin kick. The movements flowed clean and sharp, no hesitation, no missteps. His shoulder ached where the training blade had landed, but the motion was pure.

So it wasn't physical. Good. That left one other possibility.

Ben stopped in the center of the circle, breathing steady, and reached for the Force.

The familiar presence wrapped around him like warm water—or tried to. Instead of the clean connection he'd known since childhood, the Force hit that flat note again. Not absence, not exactly. More like... distortion. As if someone had taken the galaxy's fundamental harmony and played it slightly wrong.

He'd felt something like this before. During Jacen's transformation into Darth Caedus, when his cousin had started making choices that bent reality around them. Later, during the Abeloth crisis, when the very structure of the Force had seemed to fray at the edges. But those had been emotional wrongness, darkness bleeding through the light. This felt mechanical. Like the Force itself had developed a fault line.

Ben opened his eyes and found his hands were shaking.

The Temple's ambient presence pressed around him—dozens of Jedi meditating, training, living their lives with no apparent awareness of the discord humming beneath everything. He could walk into the Council chamber right now and explain what he was sensing, and they'd probably suggest more meditation and maybe a refresher course on Force philosophy.

Because they weren't feeling it. Not like this.

The isolation hit him like a physical blow. Whatever was happening to the Force, whatever crack ran through its structure, he was alone in perceiving it. Which meant either he was broken—trauma finally catching up with a decade of galactic crises—or the galaxy was broken in a way no one else could see yet.

Ben's money was on the second option, but he'd been wrong before.

He started pacing again, shorter strides this time, trying to walk off the restless energy that came with having no clear enemy to fight. The Council would want evidence. Proof that this wasn't just another Knight struggling with post-crisis adjustment. They'd want data and analysis and probably a psychological evaluation before they'd take his word that the Force itself was misfiring.

But Ben had grown up in a family that saved the galaxy on a regular basis. He knew how to trust his instincts, even when they pointed toward conclusions that seemed impossible.

The flat note sounded again, making him stumble mid-stride. Not painful—worse. Wrong in a way that made his teeth ache and his skin crawl. Like the universe had developed a hairline fracture and he was the only one who could see the stress patterns.

Boots scuffed in the corridor outside.

Ben straightened automatically, smoothing his tunic and schooling his expression into something that wouldn't worry his father. Because Luke Skywalker was brilliant and compassionate and had literally rewritten Jedi doctrine, but he was also the man who'd once tried to rebuild the Order while fighting a war and raising two kids and dealing with his own trauma. Ben had learned early that some problems were better solved before they became Luke's problems too.

The door slid open.

Luke stood in the corridor, datapad in hand and that particular set to his shoulders that meant he'd been reading mission briefings. The same posture he'd worn during the Yuuzhan Vong war, during Jacen's fall, during every crisis that had shaped Ben's childhood into a series of urgent departures.

Ben met his father's eyes and felt the familiar mix of love and resignation. Whatever was happening to the Force—whatever crack ran through reality itself—he wouldn't be investigating it alone much longer.
