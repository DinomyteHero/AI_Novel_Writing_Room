The hangar reeked of scorched metal and departure—thruster wash still clinging to the air, recycled currents hissing through vents wide enough to crawl through. Ben kept his back to most of it. Crouched by the Rover’s port landing strut, he twisted a repulsor coupling that didn’t need twisting, his datapad balanced on one knee.

The roster glowed on the screen.

He skipped past Jysella Horn first. Capable. Meticulous. She’d demand daily check-ins and a full briefing on the Codex before they broke orbit—and she’d be right. He kept scrolling.

Taryn Zel. Sharp instincts, sharper reflexes. She’d spend the first three legs questioning why Luke’s son chased a Force echo into the Unknown Regions without Council approval, framing every doubt as concern. He didn’t have the patience for concern today.

The third name he dismissed without reading.

He closed the roster.

His personal contacts hid under *maintenance vendors*—a leftover label from GAG days he’d never bothered updating. He opened it, found Desh Rolan three entries down, and called before he stood.

The coupling was fine. Had been fine. He pocketed the hydrospanner anyway.

---

The comm connected on the fourth chirp. Background noise bled through immediately—wet, mechanical clatter from a busy spaceport, a voice snapping about a cargo manifest two stalls over.

"Rolan." Flat. Alert. Desh answered calls the way he entered rooms: no wasted motion.

"It’s Ben. You free?"

A pause. Not hesitation—calculation. "Define free."

"Unknown Regions. Few weeks, maybe. Need a second set of hands unattached to the Order."

Another pause, shorter. "Pay?"

"Standard security rate plus hazard."

"What’s the hazard?"

Ben weighed the question. "Unclear. Force-related. Possibly ancient."

"How ancient?"

"Pre-Reformation ancient."

Silence. Then: "So the interesting kind."

Ben’s shoulders sagged without permission. He leaned against the Rover’s hull, durasteel warm through his jacket. "That’s one word for it."

"When do you leave?"

"Twenty minutes."

"I’m on Nar Shaddaa." A crate thudded down. "Send jump coordinates. I’ll meet you at the first waypoint."

"Terminus Station. Six hours."

"Copy." A beat. "Ben."

"Yeah."

"You eat today?"

Ben glanced at the empty equipment case doubling as a workbench. "Caf."

"So no." The line clicked off.

Ben stared at the dead comm. The tightness in his chest—carried since the sparring salle, the briefing, the seventeen identical reports—had eased, just a fraction. Not gone. Rearranged.

He remembered the night they’d tailed a Black Sun lieutenant in the lower levels—Desh running thermal scans while Ben watched through macrobinoculars. Three hours in, word came the lieutenant had kids in the next room. Desh hadn’t asked if Ben would authorize the breach. He’d tightened the scan perimeter and waited. When Ben called it off, Desh only packed gear. No questions. No judgment. Just the work.

That was Desh. Two years in GAG, operating where the line between security and something harder to name had blurred beyond recognition, and Desh had never once asked Ben to justify a call. Not when it was wrong. Not when it cost. He’d asked *what*, *when*, *how*. Never *why*. Ben had learned to return the favor. It was the closest either of them got to a creed.

"Still talking to tools instead of people, I see."

Ben spun. Master Durron stood twenty meters away, arms crossed beside a maintenance skiff, that familiar mix of curiosity and concern tightening Ben’s jaw.

"Master Durron."

"Solo again?" Durron’s gaze flicked to the Rover’s open hatch, back to Ben. "Council assigned Jysella to this."

"Plans changed."

"Whose?" Durron stepped closer, voice low. "Ben, you’ve been chasing ghosts for weeks. The Force—"

"Is telling me to move fast." Ben jammed the hydrospanner into his belt pouch. "Jysella would want three days of briefings before we left orbit."

"She’d want to know why Skywalker’s son’s charging into potentially hostile territory without backup."

Ben met his eyes. "I’ve got backup."

"Rolan doesn’t count." Durron’s mouth curled. "Not Force-sensitive. Can’t—"

"He can keep me alive long enough for the Force-sensitive part to matter." Ben snatched his flight bag from the deck. "I know what I’m doing."

"Do you?" Durron blocked his path. "From where I’m standing, it looks like you’re running from something."

The words landed like a punch. Heat flooded Ben’s face, fingers curling. "I’m running toward something. There’s a difference."

"Then take a Jedi partner. Someone who can—"

"No." The refusal came sharper than intended. Ben forced calm. "I appreciate the concern, Master, but this isn’t your call."

Durron studied him. "Your father—"

"If you’ll excuse me, I’ve got a departure window." Ben stepped around him, heading for the Rover’s ramp. "I’ll file reports when I have something worth reporting."

He didn’t look back. Couldn’t. The tightness had returned, heavier, and his hands trembled as he triggered the hatch. Behind him, Durron’s boots retreated across the deck, each step a small victory in a fight Ben wasn’t sure he was winning.

---

The Rover lifted clean, rotated on its axis, and slipped into the departure lane with the methodical patience of a ship that had made this run before. Ben watched Coruscant shrink in the rear sensor display—spires first, then the broader mass, then the block of the planet itself, until the whole world became a disc of amber light below.

He pulled up the nav chart and set the vector southwest.

The city-glow faded as he breached atmosphere. Stars blinked on one by one, then all at once. Traffic thinned. He ran the departure checklist by rote, attention split between instruments and the faint wrongness lodged behind his sternum for weeks.

It was still there. Of course it was.

He finished the checklist, confirmed Terminus Station, and reached for the hyperspace levers.

The stars stretched.

Starlines blazed white across the viewport, collapsed into the flat, blue-white tunnel of hyperspace. Instruments settled. Drives dropped to cruise pitch.

Ben closed his eyes and reached out.

In hyperspace, the Force went quiet—muffled, like sound through thick plating. Expected. Physics. The anomaly should’ve dissolved into background noise the moment the ship crossed the threshold.

It didn’t.

He felt it like a wire pulled taut—not a sound, not a sight, but tension. Direction. The hollow that should’ve resonated had stopped being hollow. It had become a *line*, running southwest through the blue-white void, pulling with steady, patient pressure that cared nothing for Ben’s readiness.

His eyes opened.

Instruments read normal. Hyperspace normal. Nav chart matched his route, his waypoints.

But the pull wasn’t following his course. It ran parallel, slightly ahead, like a current beneath still water.

Ben sat back in the pilot’s seat.

He’d left Coruscant to follow a bearing. Told himself that. Told his father that, in reports and waypoints and *I’ll report back with something the Council can use.* A controlled investigation. Observation.

The Force hadn’t gotten the memo.
