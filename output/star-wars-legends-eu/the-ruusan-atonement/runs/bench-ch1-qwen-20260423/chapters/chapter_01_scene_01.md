The training blade caught him across the shoulder a half-second too late.

Ben took the low-power hit through his flight suit, a sharp, clean sting that had no business being there. He reset his stance, boots squeaking on the polished salle floor, and watched Rendell step back. The other Knight’s blue practice blade held steady, humming its familiar note. Rendell’s breathing hadn’t even changed.

Ben’s guard had dropped. Not a flinch. Not a tactical mistake. Just a gap in time where his reflexes should have been.

Third time this week.

He lowered his own training saber, thumb finding the activation plate. The blue light snapped off with a soft hiss. The room seemed to go still around the edges. The Temple’s presence reached him a beat late, sliding into place after his own rhythm had already moved on. Not dread. Something smaller, and more infuriating. A timing slip. A note struck just behind the beat. His body knew the tempo by heart, and the field kept missing it by a breath.

"Again?" Rendell asked. He wiped sweat from his brow with the back of his wrist. "You gave me the opening, but I can’t claim it if you weren’t trying."

"I'm trying," Ben said. He rolled his shoulder, working out the phantom sting. "It’s just been a long week. My head’s somewhere else."

"Somewhere else usually means you’re thinking three moves ahead. Tonight it just means you’re slow." Rendell clipped his own training weapon to his belt. "Get some sleep, Ben. We’ll pick it up Thursday."

"Thursday works." Ben managed a thin smile. "I’ll try to be conscious for it."

Rendell gave him a brief nod and headed for the equipment racks. The side door sighed shut behind him. Ben waited until the latch clicked before he let his shoulders sag.

Fatigue. That was the story he’d been telling himself since Monday. He’d been running on recycled caf and broken sleep, which meant half the Order was probably doing the same. He could blame exhaustion. He could blame the long rotation on the outer terraces. He could blame anything except the thing he actually noticed, the thing that kept hooking his attention by the ribs.

He crossed to the center of the mat. The salle was empty now. Through the high transparisteel viewport, he caught the distant chime of a turbolift arriving on the training level, then the low mechanical whir of a cleaning droid working its way down the hall. Coruscant never really slept, and neither did the Jedi. Late-shift foot traffic murmured somewhere beyond the wall. Ben stood in the middle of it all and let his breathing slow.

He didn’t ignite the blade. He simply raised his hands and began the opening sequence of Soresu.

Step. Shift. Parry.

His muscles knew the geometry. They’d been drilled into him since he was tall enough to hold a hilt. Mara’s voice lived in his joints, sharp and uncompromising. Defend the line. Trust the rhythm.

He moved through the first three forms. Clean. Precise. Muscle memory doing the heavy lifting.

Then he opened himself to the Force.

The lag hit like a missed footing on a dark stair.

His body braced for the familiar surge of support, the invisible catch that met a falling weight, and nothing arrived when it should have. The field was there—he could feel it, heavy and present—but it came in late, just enough to unsettle balance, just enough to make the next step feel unmoored. He corrected automatically, shifting his weight, but the wrongness stayed at the edge of awareness. Not a color. Not a shadow. A vibration out of phase. A metronome ticking just after the beat.

He stopped moving.

He’d been telling himself it was stress. That the lingering static from the Abeloth years was finally catching up to him. That maybe he’d been pushing too hard for too long, ignoring the small warnings his own nerves had been sending. He had a dozen tidy explanations, all of them available and all of them wrong enough to annoy him.

Stress didn’t make the Temple’s presence drag behind his footsteps. Stress didn’t steal a beat from a form he could run in his sleep.

He stood still, listening to his breathing. The salle felt too large now. The wrongness wasn’t in his head. It was in the room, in the floor beneath his boots, in the air between the distant corridor sounds. Something was off-key. He didn’t know what. He didn’t have a name for it. He only knew, with a cold, physical certainty, that it wasn’t him.

He ran the sequence again. Slower this time. Letting the lag come. Letting his body adjust to the delayed rhythm. It felt like walking through water. It felt like waiting for a door that refused to open. He swallowed the frustration, sharp and familiar, and kept moving. If the Force was late, he’d compensate. He always compensated. That was supposed to be the whole point of practice, wasn’t it?

Footsteps sounded in the corridor outside. Deliberate. Unhurried.

Ben didn’t break form. He finished the sequence, lowered his hands, and turned.

Luke appeared in the doorway with a datapad and an expression Ben knew meant a mission.
