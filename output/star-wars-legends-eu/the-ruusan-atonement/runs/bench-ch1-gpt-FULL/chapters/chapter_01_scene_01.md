The training blade cracked across Ben's left shoulder hard enough to numb his arm.

He hissed through his teeth and gave ground on pure reflex, boots scraping the salle floor. The blue practice blade in his hand came up a half-second too late to matter. Too late to parry, too late to turn the hit, too late in a way that should not have happened at all.

Third time this week.

His partner had already stepped back into guard, doing the polite thing and pretending the exchange had been clean and ordinary. The training sabers hummed between them. Sweat crept down Ben's spine under his tunic, and his grip felt slick.

"You all right?"

"Fine." Ben rolled his shoulder once and winced at the answer his body gave him. "Again."

They came together.

A high cut. Ben caught it. Low feint, inside line, pivot—

The wrongness brushed through the Force like a note half a step off, faint but enough. Enough to tug his timing crooked. His blade drifted wider than he intended. He corrected, a fraction behind. The other Knight's training saber tapped his ribs.

Not hard enough to count as anything serious. Hard enough to count.

His partner lowered the blade a few centimeters. "You sure?"

Ben killed his saber. The snap-hiss of deactivation sounded louder than it should have. "I said I'm fine."

The other Knight waited.

Ben exhaled once through his nose, then hooked the inactive hilt at his belt. "Actually, no. I'm done. Long day."

That earned a nod, neutral and mercifully uninterested. "Tomorrow, then."

"Assuming my pride survives the night."

A corner of the Knight's mouth twitched. "Risky assumption."

He clipped his own saber away and headed for the exit. A moment later the door slid shut behind him, and the room settled into quiet.

Not silence. The Temple never had silence. Ventilation whispered through the walls. Somewhere down the corridor came a muffled burst of laughter, then footsteps. The salle lights buzzed at the edge of hearing. Under it all sat the familiar pressure of so many presences packed into one place—Jedi moving, studying, healing, arguing, meditating. Most days that shared nearness steadied him.

Tonight it only made the off-note stand out.

Ben flexed his left hand. The shoulder had already started to throb, a blunt pulse with each heartbeat. Another bruise to add to the collection. At this rate he was going to look as if he'd lost a disagreement with a cargo loader.

He crossed to the center line again and stood still until his breathing evened out.

Fatigue would have been convenient. Fatigue made sense. Late session, too many hours, too little sleep, everyone still pretending the galaxy had returned to normal because the latest disaster was at least no longer exploding in public. Fatigue was clean. Fatigue didn't leave a scratch on his confidence.

This did.

He drew the hilt back into his hand but didn't ignite it. The cool metal settled against his palm. He set his feet and moved through the opening of a basic form, slow and exact. Cut, recover, turn. The motions lived in muscle and bone by now, drilled so deep he could have done them half-conscious.

First sequence, clean.

Second, almost clean.

On the turn into the third, his balance shifted wrong. Not much. A heel landing a finger-width off, his center line drifting, the recovery taking thought when it should have taken none.

Ben stopped.

Sweat slid from his temple to his jaw. He wiped it off with the back of his wrist and started again, slower this time. No blade, no opponent, no need to read anyone but himself. If his body was off, he'd feel it. If he was leaning too hard on the Force for timing, that would show too.

Step. Rotate. Guard high. Drop. Pivot.

There. Again.

Not clumsiness. An interruption.

He changed the sequence, stripped it down to bare transitions. His boots whispered over the mat. On one pass the movement flowed. On the next, the same warped note passed through him and shaved the edge off his certainty. Tiny delay. Tiny correction. In a salle, with a training blade. In a real fight, tiny got people killed.

Ben lowered the hilt and listened.

The wrongness wasn't loud. That was the problem. It threaded through everything else, thin and persistent, never strong enough for anyone to treat as urgent. Most of the Order had a shelf full of reasons to dismiss it. After Abeloth, after the wars, after the mess with the Lost Tribe and the things before that, strange currents in the Force had become part of the background. Disturbance. Residue. Old damage taking its time to settle.

Maybe for them.

For him, it had edges.

He moved again, faster now, pushing. If it was trauma, if his mind was grabbing at echoes and naming them threats, pressure ought to make the cracks show. He cut through the air, turned, dropped into a low guard, rose into a diagonal strike that ended with his shoulder protesting.

The wrong note touched the motion and the whole pattern came loose in his hands.

Ben halted so sharply the rubberized sole of his right boot chirped on the floor.

He knew this.

Not the exact shape. Not the source. But the quality of it. The way it didn't feel angry or hungry or wounded in the usual ways. No surge of malice. No emotional stain. This sat underneath all that, where the Force arranged itself into something living minds could trust.

Jacen had brushed that layer sometimes near the end, during the moments when his choices stopped reading like choices and started feeling like pieces forced into place.

Abeloth had torn at it from the other direction, making everything near her ring false.

Ben's stomach tightened. He rolled his bruised shoulder again, harder this time, as if pain might knock the thought loose. It stayed put.

He'd spent enough years around impossible things to know when one had come back wearing a new mask.

The practical move would be to report it. Log the incidents. Ask for a med check, then a meditation review, then a polite discussion with several Masters about whether lingering exposure to galactic-scale nightmares had left Ben Skywalker hearing ghosts in the walls.

He could picture that conversation too well.

You're overextended.

Take two days off rotations.

Center yourself.

As if he hadn't tried. Walking meditations in the upper gardens before dawn. Breath discipline. Sensory narrowing. Full immersion in the currents around the Temple until his own thoughts thinned out and the larger whole carried him. Sometimes it helped for an hour. Then the note came back, and with it the private question he was getting tired of asking.

Was the Force off, or was he?

Ben set the hilt against his forearm and pressed until the metal bit into skin. Grounding. Physical. Useful. He looked around the empty salle: scuffed floor, wall racks, the mirrored panel at the far end turned dark with the lights angled away. No mystery there. No answers either.

He imagined filing the report anyway. He even took one step toward the wall console.

Then he stopped.

Because once it was out, it would stop being his problem and start being everyone else's interpretation of his problem. Observation. Evaluation. Concern. Maybe all of it deserved. None of it helpful if he was right and this thing slipped away while the Order discussed categories.

Also because Skywalkers had a long, decorated history of bringing uncomfortable Force problems to the room only after they'd already become disasters. Family tradition was less a lesson and more a warning label, and Ben had spent most of his life trying not to add fresh evidence.

Which, he admitted while staring at the console and not moving, was a pretty solid reason to ask for backup.

His mouth tightened at that. Fine. Good. Excellent. He was arguing with himself in an empty salle. Definitely the mark of a stable Jedi Knight.

Ben turned away from the console.

He reset his stance in the center of the room and ran the opening sequence once more. This time he kept it simple, no speed, no tests. The motion steadied his breathing. His shoulder burned on the cross-body recovery. The wrong note remained, thin and stubborn, but he marked where it pressed hardest and held that in memory.

If he couldn't trust the whole shape of it yet, he could trust pattern. Timing. Recurrence. Evidence.

And until he had enough of that to bring someone more than a bruise and a bad instinct, he'd handle it himself.

The thought sat badly. Still, it locked into place.

Ben lowered the hilt and let his arms hang. The salle had cooled while he worked; sweat dampened the collar of his tunic, and recycled air brushed chill across his neck. Out in the corridor, footsteps approached, measured and familiar.

He looked up.

Luke stood in the doorway with a datapad and an expression Ben knew meant a mission.
