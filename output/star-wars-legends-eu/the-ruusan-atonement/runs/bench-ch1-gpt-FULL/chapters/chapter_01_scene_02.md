Luke lifted the datapad in greeting instead of a hand. "You were about to file a report."

Ben stopped in the doorway. "I was about to consider filing a report."

"That serious?"

"Depends how much faith you have in my wounded pride."

Luke's mouth edged toward a smile. "Come in."

His office looked the way it always did: functional, used, never arranged to impress anyone. Desk, chairs, wall shelves with too many actual paper files mixed in among data chips, a holocomm unit pushed to one side. Coruscant burned beyond the transparisteel in layered bands of traffic and light. Luke's presence filled the room before anything else did—vast, steady, familiar enough to make Ben think *Dad* for one unguarded beat before he locked that away and crossed to the desk.

Luke sat, then slid the datapad over the polished surface.

Blue light washed across Ben's fingers as he picked it up. The display held a line of flagged survey reports, seventeen entries stacked in clean columns, each tagged from a different point along the Outer Rim. Dates. Coordinates. Brief notes. Instrument irregularities, navigation drift, one field team reporting meditation interference severe enough to abort site work.

Ben lowered himself into the chair and started scrolling.

"Seventeen?" he asked.

"In the last eight months," Luke said. "Scattered enough to pass for noise if you read them one at a time."

Ben flicked through the summaries. Most were the usual kind of things that built Jedi survey logs: odd sensor returns, local accounts no one trusted yet, teams checking old sites for residual trouble after the galaxy's latest round of disasters. Then three entries caught and held.

He expanded the first. Rural moon. No major dark-side history on file. Survey Knight reporting no pressure, no malice, no disturbance she could define. Only degraded timing during simple telekinetic exercises and a "flat" response in meditation.

Ben enlarged the sensor graph, then the location trace.

Second report. Merchant waystation near a secondary hyperlane. Civilian complaints about experienced navigators misjudging routine docking approaches. Jedi observer noted a faint internal dislocation during saber drills. Nothing external enough to trigger an alarm.

Third. A dead world farther spinward. Old battlefield remains, mostly looted centuries ago. The field notes were terse, almost irritated. Force contact present but off in a way the reporting Master did not consider dark-side contamination.

Ben's shoulders drew tight. The chair gave a soft creak as he leaned closer.

Those three sat in his head with ugly precision. Not because the wording matched what he'd felt in the salle. Jedi wrote around things in their own styles. But the shape underneath was the same. No anger in it. No hunger. No stain you could point to and call corruption. Just that sour little drag under the rest, shaving certainty off familiar motions.

Luke watched him over folded hands. He wasn't crowding the silence. He never had to.

Ben zoomed the map wider. Seventeen points curved across the Rim and down along a corridor that looked almost accidental until the eye started connecting them.

"You've already run trajectories," he said.

"Yes."

"And?"

"No clean line." Luke nodded at the display. "But enough of a bearing to justify a closer look."

Ben tapped the three expanded reports. "These aren't the same as the others."

"How so?"

"The rest are messy." He scrolled once, dismissing a report with atmospheric interference and another with local Force folklore muddying the read. "Could be equipment. Could be bad note-taking. Could be a team trying too hard to be useful." He touched the first of the three again. "These aren't doing that."

Luke's gaze shifted to the text. "Because they're uncertain?"

"Because they're trying not to overstate it." Ben looked up. "Whoever wrote these knew something was wrong and didn't have a category for it."

A beat passed.

Luke leaned back a fraction. "That tracks my read."

The words landed harder than they should have. Ben had come in half-ready to defend himself against patient concern, Temple procedure, maybe a recommendation for rest. Instead Luke was here already, several steps ahead, datapad in hand.

"You've felt it," Ben said.

"Yes."

Not imagined. Not stress. Not one more private malfunction he'd have to package into a neat report and hand over for review. The knot between his shoulders loosened, quick and sharp enough to almost hurt.

Luke reached for his own cup on the desk, found it empty, and set it down again. "Not the way you have, I think."

Ben let that sit for a second. Outside the window, traffic streams moved in red and white bands around distant towers. Inside the office, recycled air carried the dry, metallic bite of the Temple systems. Under Luke's familiar steadiness, the wrong note still pressed at the edge of things, faint and sour.

"How are you reading it?" Ben asked.

Luke considered before answering. "As a distributed anomaly. Localized pockets, same broad profile, no clear source event. It may be environmental. It may be tied to old damage in places we haven't surveyed closely enough. Until we know more, it's a mapping problem."

Ben stared at him.

A mapping problem. Clean. Responsible. Grand Master language for something that sat under Ben's skin like a bad memory with fresh teeth.

He looked back at the three reports and felt old names pass through him in quick, unwelcome flashes. Jacen, near the end, when choice had started coming off him stripped of its human edges. Abeloth warping every instinct that should have held true. Vestara standing close enough to matter while Ben kept trying to decide which parts of her were real and which parts were angles. Different disasters. Different scars. Same buried wrongness in all of them, brushed only for moments, never this wide.

He expanded the dead world report again and heard his own voice come out flatter than he meant. "It isn't environmental."

Luke's eyes lifted. "Tell me."

Ben rubbed a thumb along the datapad's edge. "The places don't matter as much as the effect. People keep describing the same loss from different directions. Timing goes off. Judgment goes off. Not enough to panic anybody. Enough to make trained people miss by a fraction." He met Luke's gaze. "That's not weather."

"No," Luke said. "It probably isn't."

Probably. Ben almost smiled at that. Grand Masters got to keep room in the sentence.

Luke folded his arms. "You've seen a version of this before."

It wasn't a question, but it carried one.

Ben nodded once. "Pieces of it."

"Where?"

He could have said enough. He could have left the names buried and let Luke infer the rest. Instead he heard himself answer with stripped-down honesty. "Jacen. Abeloth." A brief pause. "Vestara, once or twice."

Luke didn't move much. He never did when something hit home. Just a slight stilling, like the room had narrowed around him.

"That's a hard list," he said.

"Yeah."

Another small silence. Warmer than the ones before. Worse for that reason.

Luke tapped the side of the datapad. "Which is one reason I asked you here."

There it was. The mission waiting underneath the talk.

Ben set the pad down long enough to look at him fully. "You want me to trace the route."

"I want you to survey the flagged sites along this bearing, compare live impressions against the reports, and send updates at each stop." Luke's tone stayed even, administrative by habit and choice. "Master Horn is tied up in the Core. Kam and Tionne are already handling a separate archive recovery. The teams closest to these sectors are short on senior Knights." He paused. "And I thought some time outside the Temple might help."

Breathing room, offered with a mission packet attached.

Ben picked the datapad up again so his hands had something to do. "So this is either a critical assignment or a fatherly attempt to get me out of the building before I start taking out support beams with a practice saber."

Luke's mouth twitched. "If you damage the Temple, I'll put that in the formal record."

"Good. I like fairness in command."

The brief warmth eased the room open a notch. It would have been easy, right there, to say assign a second Knight. Easy to make it sound practical. Route coverage. Cross-checking perceptions. Standard caution. The reasons lined up neatly.

They also sounded too much like asking not to carry it alone.

Ben hated that they sounded like that.

Luke was watching him, not pushing, not rescuing him from the choice by making it himself. That had its own weight. Maybe he trusted Ben. Maybe he was giving him room. Maybe he was doing the Skywalker thing again—sending one person into a problem bigger than it looked and hoping judgment would cover the gap.

Ben didn't know which answer bothered him more.

He rose with the datapad in hand and crossed to the window. Coruscant spread beneath him in impossible depth, towers lit all the way to the horizon, traffic lanes threading between them in constant motion. He touched the cool transparisteel with his free hand. The contact grounded him. So did the faint ache in his bruised shoulder.

The wrongness still sat there, thin and ugly, even this high above the city.

"When do I leave?" he asked.

"Soon as you're ready. We can have a ship prepped within the hour."

Ben looked out at the moving lights instead of back at the desk. One more opening remained. Ask for another Knight. Ask Luke what he really thought this was. Ask for the conversation underneath the briefing.

He let all three go.

"I'll take it," he said. "Solo."

Behind him, Luke did not answer at once.

Then: "All right."

Ben stayed with his hand against the transparisteel, datapad resting against his leg. Luke didn't suggest anyone else. Ben didn't turn around and ask. The silence between them held the shape of that missing offer, and neither of them touched it.
