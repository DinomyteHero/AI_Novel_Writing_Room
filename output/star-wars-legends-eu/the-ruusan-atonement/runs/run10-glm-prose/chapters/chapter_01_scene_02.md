The datapad smacked Luke’s desk. Seventeen survey reports glowed amber—ANOMALY: UNCLASSIFIED.

Ben Skywalker picked it up. Blue light sliced shadows across his knuckles as he scrolled. Outer Rim patrols usually netted bored pirates, mynock nests, or a smuggler who’d nudged a nav buoy off course. Not seventeen reports from different sectors describing the same impossible readings.

“Same bearing?”

Luke nodded. “Every one.”

Ben zoomed in on the third file: gravimetric distortion, sensor ghosts, clocks slipping seconds. The seventh repeated the language; the eleventh added chrono drift. Three carried Jedi marginalia—*unusual resonance, perceptual irregularities*. His thumb stilled on a Knight’s note: *A presence that should be there but is not.*

The wrongness slithered up his spine. He’d tasted it for weeks—pressure behind the eyes, a drone refusing to resolve. Seeing it charted made it worse.

“You feel it too.” Not a question.

Luke’s shoulders squared—the tiny shift that meant a father weighing disclosure against a Grand Master’s duty. “Yes.”

Ben opened his senses. Luke filled the office like sunlight through high windows—vast, warm, familiar—yet the wrongness threaded the glow, a hairline fracture only visible if you knew where to look.

“Background noise,” Luke said. “Echo residue from the war. The galaxy’s still settling.”

Ben’s jaw clenched. The explanation was reasonable, almost comforting. It was also wrong.

“Dad—” He caught himself, slid the mask back on. “Luke. This isn’t residual.”

Luke’s stillness sharpened; he listened with more than ears now.

“I’ve walked Dathomir, the Maw, the scars Jacen left. This isn’t contamination. It’s quieter. Patient.” He tapped the pad. “These graphs don’t match a wound. They match a hole.”

The word hung. Luke’s expression didn’t flicker, yet his presence drew in a notch, the way a hand closes before a choice.

“Absence,” Luke echoed.

“Like something’s missing. Not dark, not broken. Gone.” Ben kept his voice flat, clinical. “A gap.”

Luke leaned back; the holocomm painted silver across his beard. “The Council reviewed the data. Consensus tags it residual dissipation.”

Of course. Their taxonomy had slots for darkness, wounds, nexuses—nothing for emptiness. File it under the nearest heading and move on.

Ben stepped to the window. Coruscant stacked itself below—vertical rivers of light, the Senate dome a pale moon. Somewhere down there the galaxy carried on, unaware the Force was fraying at the seams.

Distance helped him breathe. The wrongness pressed against his skull—three months ago a whisper, now a headache he couldn’t un-hear.

“I’ll run the survey route.”

The silence stretched.

“Solo?” Luke’s voice carried that particular careful weight, the one that said he already knew the answer.

One Knight, one ship, eight coordinates. Every protocol said build a team, bring backup. Ben’s instincts screamed the same. He stayed at the glass.

“Yes.”

Luke studied him with those eyes that had ended wars. Ben met the reflection—same blue, same impossible expectations. He’d inherited the gaze and the reflex to fill silence with action instead of truth.

“Eight sites,” Luke said. “Six to eight weeks, depending on early findings.”

“I know the drill.”

“Desh Rolan will meet you at Terminus. He’s cataloguing spatial anomalies along that vector—non-Force data that lines up.”

Desh was sharp. And curious. He’d ask questions Ben preferred not to answer.

“Acceptable.”

Luke rose. For a heartbeat he was just Dad—man who’d sent his son into fire before and would again because the galaxy asked, and because he trusted Ben to walk out.

“The absence you’re describing,” Luke said. “You’re sure it’s not contamination?”

“I’m sure it doesn’t match any I’ve seen.”

Distinction mattered. He’d catalogued plenty—Dathomir nightsisters, Maw shadows, the footprints Jacen left in wet cement. This was cleaner. Colder. And it was getting closer to something he couldn’t yet name.

Luke joined him at the window. Their reflections overlapped—father and son, Grand Master and Knight, two men who loved each other through briefing packets and mission parameters.

“Report through standard channels,” Luke said.

“Standard channels.”

Neither added that standard meant the Council, who’d already decided. Neither mentioned Ben would carry a gap in the Force aboard a single starfighter. Neither spoke the obvious: Luke had offered solo, and Ben had taken it without asking for more.

The wrongness kept humming, patient, off-key.

“Morning departure,” Ben said.

Luke’s hand brushed his shoulder—warm, brief, saying things words would only break. Then he returned to his desk, and Ben walked out. The door hissed shut, sealing the silence between them.
