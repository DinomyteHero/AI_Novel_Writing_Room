The ground hummed.

Not wind. Not machinery Ben could name—just a low vibration climbing through his boot soles, steady as a heartbeat. He stopped. The hum didn’t.

Desh halted two paces ahead, checked his chrono, and scowled. "Running two seconds fast. Started the moment we crested the ridge."

"Note it."

"Already did." Desh pocketed the device and kept walking.

The structure squatted in a shallow bowl of stone—either scooped naturally or cratered by artifice. Ben couldn’t decide anymore. The plateau had been shaped, not chosen. Blast-berms curved toward an entrance set mostly underground. The visible face was a single slab of gray-black rock laced with metal crossbeams that looked more like sutures than architecture. No windows. No ornament. Only a shallow groove above the doorway repeated the interlocking-circles motif he’d photographed on two dead worlds.

He’d quit logging coincidences after the third system.

"Jedi temple?" Desh asked.

"No."

"Certain of that."

Ben flicked a hand at the berms, the kill-box approach, the facade’s refusal of handholds, shadows, mercy. "Temples open inward. This points outward."

Desh studied the angles. "Bunker."

"Yes."

"Wonderful. A thousand years of Jedi history and nobody filed the bunker blueprints."

They advanced on foot, slow, hands loose. The wrongness had clamped behind Ben’s ears since the ridge—not the diffuse drag of the thin places they’d hiked through, but something local, something that felt like standing beside a dam at midnight and hearing the wall groan. The Force slid sideways here. Reach for distance, get depth. Ask for clarity, receive static. He kept his perceptions shallow.

The blast doors were sealed.

Ben swept his scanner across the seam. No manual release. No exterior panel. The readout showed power—active, not residual—cells trickling current after a millennium. Someone had built this to outlast civilizations. They hadn’t bet on a short game.

He holstered the scanner and waited.

Nothing.

"Knock?" Desh murmured.

"Patience."

The mechanism found him when it was ready. A line of emitters he’d mistaken for trim flared along the jamb—cold, crackling blue that moved like liquid metal. It crawled from boots to crown, slow, clinical, then lingered at his sternum. Pressure, not light, as if a palm pressed flat to his breastbone and held.

The doors stayed shut.

Ben didn’t move. The scan narrowed, ran again.

*Different,* he realized. *I taste wrong to it.*

He offered nothing. Performed nothing. Simply existed with the Force moving through him the way it always did—his own flavor, inherited from two parents and warped by everything since. The emitters fired a third pass.

Gears screamed like a ship tearing apart in atmosphere—massive, unwilling, metal protesting centuries of stillness. The seam appeared. The slabs parted finger-widths, then meters.

Cold exhaled from the gap. Stale. Pressurized. The taste of air recycled until it went flat.

Desh’s blaster cleared leather before the echo died. He ducked right, back to the wall. Ben took left.

Dark. Then not.

Lights rose in reluctant stages—amber, then white—answering Ben’s presence the way the doors had, measuring before yielding. The room revealed itself as neither chapel nor archive but a command node built by someone who expected to fight from it.

Tactical holotables dominated the floor. Empty weapon racks lined the far wall. Every surface chose utility over comfort to the point of hostility—no cushions, no murals, no curve that didn’t serve a tactical reason. The Jedi vocabulary survived if you knew it: certain ratios, certain alloys, the way power conduits hugged corners. But every softening element had been planed away, leaving bone and purpose.

Ben stood inside it and felt the place settle on his shoulders like wet canvas.

"Cozy," Desh said, already sliding along the perimeter, muzzle high. "Really channels the serenity."

"It’s a fortress."

"Box meant to keep something in—or out. Can’t tell which." Desh cleared the last corner and lowered his blaster a fraction. "No contacts. Whatever lived here left long ago."

Ben crossed to the central console. Dust lay thick as felt, untouched since before his bloodline began. He didn’t trust the Force with it. He used his palm, dragging a clean stripe that revealed dark alloy beneath.

The console woke.

Not gradual. Every screen, every emitter, every dormant system drank power at once. The holotable stuttered alive, vomiting static and text that scrolled too fast to read.

Desh yanked his earpiece free. "Broadcasting on a dead channel—straight into my receiver. Volume’s wrong. Frequency’s wrong—" He winced, palm pressed to his skull. "Sounds like someone screaming through static."

Ben leaned over the projection. The letters cycled, archaic High Galactic shorthand over a Basic skeleton he knew from pre-Ruusan logs, compressed for low-bandwidth relays. Same loop, endlessly, shouting into a galaxy that had stopped listening centuries ago.

He read once. Again.

The wrongness clamped his teeth. Drummed against his sternum. Offered no useful sense except the certainty that this place had screamed since before the last Sith War—and no modern ear had known to listen.

On the next pass, the translation locked.

Two words.

*SEAL FAILING.*
