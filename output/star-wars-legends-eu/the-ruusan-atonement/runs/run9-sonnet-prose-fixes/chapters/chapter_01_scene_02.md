The datapad skidded across the desk—Luke’s version of a gentle shove.

Ben caught it. Blue light spilled over his knuckles: seventeen reports, each stamped with the same amber tag—ANOMALY—UNCLASSIFIED. Leave it to the Order to file existential dread in triplicate, headers aligned.

He scrolled.

Coordinates snaked along an Outer Rim vector he’d been tracing on mental star charts for weeks, the ones that kept him awake at two in the morning. Seeing the corridor rendered in official font—time-stamped, cross-referenced, sealed with the Temple crest—sent a jolt through him, sour and confirming. Like a med-scan that doesn’t heal but proves you’re not imagining things.

“Third report this month from that sector,” Luke said. He reclined, elbows on the arms of his chair, fingers loosely braided—relaxed on the surface.

“Third flagged one.” Ben pinched the display, zooming on the three tallest spikes. “The analysts ignore these?”

“They didn’t think escalation—”

“How many, Dad.”

A beat. “Eleven.”

Ben’s fingers flexed. He laid the pad flat and stared at the triplet of crests. They aligned with the tally in his head close enough to tighten the muscles between his shoulder blades.

“What’s the official theory?”

“Residual echo, probably. Leftovers from Mortis, spatial scarring.” Luke’s tone stayed even, the sound of a man reciting a conclusion he hadn’t endorsed. “Master Cilghal wants a meditation circle to establish a baseline before we act.”

Of course. Ben swallowed the snort. “Timeline?”

“Six or eight weeks to gather the right Masters, then another—”

“Problem’s been steepening for three months.” Ben tapped earliest to latest. “That slope isn’t drift; it’s acceleration.”

Silence answered. The office vents exhaled recycled air, laced with old flimsi and the sharp-herb tea Luke drank when sleep evaded him.

Luke leaned forward, not arguing—just weighing.

“I feel it,” he said.

Ben went rigid.

“Whatever’s out there—I feel it too.” Luke’s presence, wide and warm as a childhood blanket, flickered like a candle in a draft. “To me it’s pressure, distant. A deep-structure disturbance, decades from fruition.”

“It’s not pressure.” The words came rough. Ben lifted the pad again, needing somewhere to put his hands. “Pressure has a source, a vector. This is—” He stopped.

How to name an absence? A room with a wall quietly removed, cold seeping in from every direction at once.

He’d tried with two senior Knights. Both had given the polite nod of people filing away a symptom.

“Different,” he finished.

Luke studied him—not Grand Master to Knight, but father to son. Harder to bear.

“Your history gives you a different filter,” Luke said. Not a dismissal, an acknowledgment that still managed to box the thing. “Jacen. Abeloth. Vestara. You’ve seen what the Force can become.”

“One way to put it.”

“Wrong?”

Ben set the pad down with a plastic click and crossed to the transparisteel. Coruscant layered itself below—skylanes stacked ten deep, running lights blurred by smog. A freighter banked toward the merchant corridor, and beneath the Temple’s steady chord, the wrongness hummed, thin and patient, like a toothache in winter air.

It had reached the heart of the Order.

“No,” he said. “It’s not wrong.”

Behind him, the chair creaked—Luke shifting, choosing words.

“I want a survey run along that bearing,” Luke said. “Eight waypoints, standard protocol. Two, maybe three months.”

Ben’s pulse jumped. He tracked the freighter until it vanished into haze. “Solo?”

“I figured you’d rather—” Luke paused. “You’ve been running quiet checks for weeks. I wasn’t going to hand you a minder.”

Gift-wrapped autonomy. Ben saw the ribbon, wanted it anyway.

“Bearing cuts through rough space,” Luke added. “I can build you a team if—”

“No.” Too quick. He steadied his voice. “Solo is quicker. Fewer scheduling knots up front.”

True. Not the truth.

The truth was he didn’t know how to describe the pull in his head without watching another set of features arrange themselves into careful concern. He’d caught that look from Tass an hour ago in the salle; he wasn’t collecting more.

And beneath that: he’d misread before. Jacen had felt like family until he’d tried to rule. Vestara had felt like a partner until she’d chosen Sith. Some recalibrations had sharpened him; others had left scars that pulled wrong. Better to test this alone, no audience, no second guesses.

“Eight waypoints,” he said. “I’ll file a preliminary jump chain tonight, refine as I go.”

“Report from each site.” Not a suggestion.

“Standard.” He stepped away from the window. “I’ll need a ship and full clearance—flagged and buried data.”

“Ready by dawn.”

Ben returned to the desk, lifted the pad, offered it back.

“Master Skywalker.”

Luke took it, fingers brushing Ben’s. Neither moved.

“Ben,” Luke said. Only that.

Ben gave a single professional nod and headed for the door.
