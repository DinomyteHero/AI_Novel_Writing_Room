The cockpit held the stale tang of recycled air and hydraulic fluid, the familiar scent of a ship that had spent too many hours in atmosphere and too many more waiting for departure. Ben Skywalker ran his thumb across the datapad’s surface, the Jedi partner roster glowing soft blue against the darkness of the preflight checklist.

Three names. Three Knights who owed him favors, who would answer if he called. His thumb paused over the first contact, then moved past it.

The personal list opened instead. Desh Rolan’s designation sat third from the top, below a string of calls he hadn’t returned in months.

Ben keyed the comm. Long-range delay stretched the seconds thin before the connection caught.

“Skywalker.” Desh’s voice crackled through, dry as ever, the fatigue of someone living on stim-patches and bad coffee buried in the consonants. “You’re a long way from the Temple.”

“I need a second body.” Ben kept his tone flat, operational. “Outer Rim transit. Deniable work.”

A pause. Not the kind that meant hesitation. Ben knew that pause; he had heard it from officers weighing risk against obligation. This was different, the stillness of someone already measuring the angle.

“Deniable,” Desh said at last. The word had weight, the particular gravity of something neither of them named. “You bypassed the roster.”

Ben said nothing. He didn’t need to.

A faint snort came over the comm, half amusement, half recognition. “That so?” Desh said. “You calling because I’m convenient, or because you need someone who won’t ask you to justify your bad habits?”

Ben’s mouth twitched once, not quite a smile. “Coordinates.”

There was a beat of static, then Desh’s voice again, easier now. Not warmer, exactly, but easier. The tension of explanation neither of them would voice slid out of the line between them. “I’m at Kuat. I can intercept at Fondor if your timeline allows.”

“It does.”

“All right. I’ll be at the orbital transfer point in eighteen hours. Don’t be late, Skywalker. I hate waiting.”

The comm clicked dead.

Ben set the datapad aside and ran the preflight sequence from memory, fingers moving across the console without hesitation. The ship answered his touch, engines cycling through startup with a low vibration he knew in his bones.

The hangar bay opened ahead. Service techs called to one another in the clipped shorthand of ground crews, their voices bouncing off durasteel and rising to the vaulted ceiling. Ben guided the vessel through departure protocol, feeling the Temple recede behind him as the ship angled toward open sky. He had done this before. Too many times, maybe. The old Galactic Alliance Guard had trained people to move fast, to work without ceremony, to trust a partner at your shoulder because the alternative was dead weight. That history lived in the bones of the order now, in the people who remembered it and the ones who only inherited the habits.

He kept the ship steady and did not let himself linger on why that thought had surfaced now.

The wrongness in the Force remained. It had been there since he entered the hangar, a pressure behind his eyes that he could blame on the Temple’s density at first—the weight of all that life, all those bright presences layered on top of one another. It should have thinned as he climbed. It should have broken apart when the ship rose through the atmosphere and Coruscant’s endless light fell away beneath him.

It didn’t.

Ben’s fingers tightened on the controls. The disturbance sharpened instead, gaining edges it hadn’t had on the ground. A restlessness moved under it, faint but undeniable, and the feeling made the hair at the back of his neck lift. The hyperspace jump would clear it. The jump always cleared it. Force signatures did not survive lightspeed. The math didn’t work; the physics didn’t allow it.

He punched the coordinates into the navcomputer and hit the hyperspace initiator.

The stars snapped into lines. The ship shuddered through the transition, instruments flickering as the hyperdrive engaged. And the wrongness stayed.

Ben’s hands gripped the console. The pressure didn’t sink into the background thrum of hyperspace travel. It sharpened, gaining weight and direction. A pull. Not the diffuse unease he’d carried from the Temple, but something specific, something aimed—like a compass needle turning, then settling on true north.

His breath caught.

The Force didn’t work this way. Disturbances faded. They didn’t follow. They didn’t pull.

But this one did.

Ben braced against the seat, the wrongness settling into a bearing he couldn’t yet name. Something waited at the far end of it. Something that had been waiting since he left the Temple, since he bypassed the roster, since he called Desh instead of a Knight who would have asked questions he couldn’t answer.

The ship drove on through hyperspace. The wrongness held its course.

As Ben left Coruscant, it did not diminish. It took direction.
