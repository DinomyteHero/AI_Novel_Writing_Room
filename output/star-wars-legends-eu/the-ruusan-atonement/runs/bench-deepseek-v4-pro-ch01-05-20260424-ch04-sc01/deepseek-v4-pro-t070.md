The cold cut through his jacket before they were halfway across the plateau.

Ben's breath steamed in the thin air and vanished, snatched away by a wind that hadn't stopped for a thousand years. The altitude registered as a low burn in his lungs — not pain, not yet, but the kind of pressure that promised it if he pushed harder. He didn't push. The fortress demanded a slower approach, and his body had already decided to give it one.

Desh walked three paces to his left, close enough to read each other's faces without speaking. Neither of them spoke. The wrongness that had drawn them to this world was stronger here, concentrated in a way that made Ben's skin want to pull tighter against his bones. It wasn't hostile. It was directional — a pressure moving through the mountain like a held breath, waiting to be released.

The structure ahead read as geology from the ridge. From here, at the edge of the plateau where the ground leveled into a flat apron of wind-scoured stone, the angles gave it away.

Ben stopped. "There."

Desh followed his line of sight, squinted against the sharp light. "That's not a mountain."

"No."

The fortress had been built so precisely into the mountainside that the join was nearly seamless — weathered stone meeting weathered stone, the same patina on both, the same thousand-year weathering. But the fortress stone was too regular. The angles were too deliberate. A natural formation didn't taper into that particular geometry, didn't carry that particular weight in the way it sat against the sky.

No symbols marked the exterior. No Jedi crest, no Republic seal, nothing that would tell a survey team or a salvage crew what they were looking at. The secrecy was a fact of the design, not an aesthetic choice. Whoever built this had wanted it found only by someone who already knew what they were looking for.

Ben started walking again. The wind pressed against his chest.

The entry mechanism was set into the rock at waist height, a recessed panel of alloy that hadn't oxidized in the thin air. Ben knelt beside it. The metal was cold enough to sting even through his glove.

"Anything?" Desh asked.

"Give me a minute."

He pulled off the glove and pressed his palm flat against the panel.

The mechanism pushed back.

Not physically — the stone didn't move, the panel didn't shift — but in the Force, the sensation was unmistakable. A subtle repulsion, like pressing against a held breath. The light beneath the alloy pulsed amber once, twice, scanning.

Ben held his hand steady. The amber deepened.

"Problem?" Desh's voice was calm, the operational flat he defaulted to when things started going wrong.

"It's not reading me."

He tried again, reaching for the technique he'd been trained in — the modern Jedi signature, the calibrated presence that opened Temple doors and identified him to Alliance systems. The mechanism's pulse stuttered, flickered, then held amber.

Refused.

Ben's jaw tightened. The frustration arrived as a sharp physical sensation, a narrowing of focus that collapsed the awed distance he'd been carrying since the plateau. He could feel the mechanism's pulse under his palm now, a heartbeat of old technology, still alive after a millennium. It knew what it was looking for.

He wasn't it.

"Can you force it?" Desh asked.

"Maybe. I don't think I should." Ben's hand stayed pressed against the stone. The wrongness here was discriminating — not hostile, not a trap waiting to spring, but specific. It had been built to admit something, and whatever that something was, a modern Jedi Knight wasn't it. "It's not trying to keep me out. It's waiting for something I'm not giving it."

"So give it something else."

Ben closed his eyes.

He stopped trying to impose his technique — stopped reaching with the training, the discipline, the careful calibration of a Jedi who'd learned control before he'd learned anything else. He opened his palm flat against the mechanism and reached deeper, past the modern signature, into something older.

The bloodline.

He didn't have a word for it. He'd never needed one. But he'd felt it before — in the Temple on Coruscant, in the way certain old systems responded to his father before they responded to anyone else. A recognition that wasn't about training or rank or anything you could earn. Something the Jedi Lords had built their fortresses to admit.

The amber light flickered.

Hesitated.

Shifted to green.

Ben felt it in the Force before he saw it — a recognition, not a surrender. The wrongness here acknowledged him. The sensation wasn't comforting. It was unsettling in its specificity, like a door that had been waiting for someone who looked enough like him to pass.

His stance widened without him deciding to. His shoulders braced.

"Green's good," Desh said. "Green means open, right?"

"I think so."

The fortress door ground open.

The sound was low and sustained, stone grinding against stone after a thousand years of silence. Air spilled out through the widening gap — ancient air, sealed since the Jedi Lords had walked these halls, carrying a weight that Ben's lungs registered immediately. Staleness. Dust. The particular cold of rock that had never known sunlight.

Ben dropped his hand from the mechanism. The green light held.

Desh pulled a glow rod from his belt and thumbed it on. The beam cut through the darkness inside, picking out a corridor of the same weathered stone, walls that angled inward as they rose, a ceiling lost in shadow. "No welcoming committee."

"Not yet."

Ben reached into the Force ahead of them, feeling for movement, for presence, for the shape of the wrongness that had drawn them here. It wasn't a void. It wasn't a wound. It was structured — concentrated, directional, moving through the mountain like a held breath that had been waiting to exhale.

Something was alive in here.

He didn't say it. Desh would know soon enough, or he wouldn't, and saying it wouldn't change what they were about to walk into. Ben stepped across the threshold, and the ancient air closed around him like a hand that had been waiting a very long time.

Behind him, Desh swept the corridor with the glow rod and followed.
