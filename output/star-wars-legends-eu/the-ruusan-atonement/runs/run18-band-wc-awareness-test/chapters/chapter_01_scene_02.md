The datapad clicked against the desk.

Ben picked it up. Seventeen amber flags marched down the screen, each stamped across eight months of Outer Rim surveys. He scrolled through headers: *Anomalous Force resonance, vector 227-mark-14. Unclassified disturbance, consistent bearing. Residual post-Abeloth signature—recommend monitoring.*

“Recommend monitoring.” He exhaled the words. “Translation: we’re clueless but need to sound official.”

Luke’s face stayed neutral, but his shoulders sagged in agreement. “After Abeloth, the Council leans conservative. Anything odd gets the soft label.”

“Seventeen soft labels pointing the same quadrant didn’t raise alarms?”

“They raised mine.” Luke laced his fingers together. “Which is why you’re here.”

Ben zoomed in on three clustered reports. The coordinates formed a vector, not a scatter. The wrongness settled at the base of his skull—thin, sour, wedged beneath his father’s warm tide like vinegar under honey. He set the pad down, then picked it up again, zooming deeper.

“These three.” He angled the screen toward Luke. “Not Abeloth residue. Residue burns hot—angry scar tissue. This is cold.”

Luke leaned closer, his breath hitching. “An absence.”

“An extraction.” Ben’s thumb left a smudge on the display. “Scars still bleed. This is a wound that never closed, edges smooth as if carved by a scalpel.” He scraped his chair back and stood. “I’ve tasted corruption. I’ve walked wounds. This doesn’t push—it pulls. Directional. Quiet.”

Luke let the silence stretch. The office—dim, cluttered with holo disks and star charts yellowed with age—swallowed the quiet whole.

“I feel it too,” Luke said.

The words landed heavier than Ben expected. He turned to the window, his shoulders locking in place.

“Months,” Luke continued. “The surveys only confirmed the bearing. I read it as history—old scar tissue from Ruusan, Malachor, Nathema. The Outer Rim is layered with ancient grief.”

Ben weighed the city’s glow against memories of Jacen’s hollowed core, Abeloth’s vacuumed hunger, Vestara’s masked absence. He kept the last one locked behind his teeth.

“History pushes,” he said. “This tugs. Different flavor.”

Luke exhaled through his nose. “You saw the Ankhural reference—*lattice extraction.*”

“Now I’ve felt the tug inside the Temple.” Ben tapped the datapad. “Don’t know what it means, and I already hate it.”

“So go find out.” Luke slid the pad back; a mission brief overlay glowed above the survey list—route, gear, report intervals. Solo format. Ben read it without touching.

“Solo,” he said, the word bitter on his tongue.

“You’re calibrated to absence better than anyone.” Luke’s mouth twisted around the euphemism: *you’ve been hollowed enough to recognize hollow.*

Ben lifted the pad. Clean margins. Wide leash. A gift, and a confession Luke couldn’t follow where the pull led. Thirty years old. Knight rank. Survivor of fractures that would have shattered most Masters. The assignment wasn’t praise—it was a key to a door Luke couldn’t walk through.

He moved to the transparisteel. Traffic streams stitched the night, layer upon layer of ten-thousand-year sediment. The wrongness hummed southwest—patient, precise.

“Survey runs straight down the bearing,” he said.

“It does.” Luke’s voice carried that careful neutrality, the kind that meant he was already three steps ahead in his head.

“If the vector leaves the protocol map?”

“Use your judgment.”

He almost asked for backup. Almost suggested a second shadow ship. The words formed behind his teeth—*I could take Jaina, or Zekk, or even*—but he swallowed them. Seventeen reports. Eight months. One line. The math felt final, like a door clicking shut.

He turned from the glow. “Morning departure.”

Luke nodded. “Authorization’s filed.”

Nothing else. Ben tucked the datapad under his arm. Luke’s presence remained a steady sun, withholding nothing because it had already given everything it could.

No partner requested. None offered. The silence between those facts hung in the room like smoke, and neither of them waved it away.
