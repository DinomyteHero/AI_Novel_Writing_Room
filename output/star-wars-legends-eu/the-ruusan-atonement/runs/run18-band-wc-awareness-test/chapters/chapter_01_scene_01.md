The training blade slashed across Ben’s shoulder. One second to register the irony before the sting hit.

Third time this week.

He retreated, letting the bout collapse rather than admit defeat. His training saber deactivated with a quiet hum. Nita Qwel—three years his junior, barely suppressing a grin—held her stance with disciplined precision.

"Again?"

"I'm finished." Ben rolled his shoulder. It would bruise. He’d earned worse. "Good strike."

"I had the angle." Generous. They both knew better.

"You had the angle." True as far as it went. He clipped the hilt to his belt and strode toward the water station, letting the motion end the conversation. Nita was solid. Right now, she was also the last person he wanted to justify himself to.

She didn’t press. The doors hissed shut behind her thirty seconds later. He exhaled through his nose.

Empty salle. Late enough that evening sessions had cleared out, early enough the night crew hadn’t started. Just him and the long room—scuffed panels, overhead lights set to training-cool blue.

He drank, not thinking about the hit.

*Three times.*

First time, distraction. Everyone had too much on their minds this long after Abeloth. Second time, he’d run extra katas and blamed fatigue. This time he’d been rested, focused, and still dropped his guard half a second late because something in the Force had gone flat—like reaching for a handhold that wasn’t there.

He set the cup down harder than intended.

Nita Qwel shouldn’t land that strike. Not at that range, not from that angle, not when he’d read her weight shift a full beat early. His body knew the counter. His hands started moving. Then the floor went wrong—not physically, but underneath—and his timing unraveled.

He unclipped his saber and stood in the center.

No ignition. Just the hilt, dense and familiar, emitter pointed at the floor.

He began Shien Form III’s opening sequence. Not for drill—he could run it blind. Muscle memory deeper than Force assistance. He wanted the seam between what his body could do alone and where the Force was supposed to slot in.

First movements clean. Footwork, pivot, guard, the long sweep into rising cut. Air moved around the dead hilt exactly right.

He pushed into transitions that needed force speed, reached for the half-second of anticipation that should be waiting—

And found nothing.

Not resistance. Not interference. Nothing. A pocket of absence where texture should be, like a thumb pressed into soft material. He stumbled, caught himself on one hand, stayed there.

His palm stuck to the panel.

He stood, ran it again from the top. This time he tracked the absence. Third transition. Every time. Same place, same hollow.

The back of his neck prickled.

Post-Abeloth residual disturbance—official language, the explanation that got nods at briefings. Galaxy still settling. Sensitives still recalibrating. The Order had agreed any odd perception was probably grief, PTSD, the long shadow of a war that had bent reality. Ben had half-believed it, because the alternative sounded like *the rookie who cried Sith.*

Except.

He let the wrongness press against his perception and recognized it.

Not the shape. Not what it was. The *flavor*—a presence scraped clean of something essential. He’d tasted it around Jacen near the end, when hollow spaces had opened inside his cousin’s resonance. Around Abeloth, amplified to nausea, a scream with something cut out. Years learning force-corruption, and this wasn’t that.

Quieter. Specific. Not a wound, not infection. More like someone had removed a load-bearing strut and the rest still stood from habit, not knowing it should fall.

He was crouching without deciding to, training hilt loose, staring at nothing.

Not PTSD. Certain in the way the Force stopped arguing and simply presented fact. His PTSD had a shape he knew—flinch, hypervigilance, the specific chill when someone moved like Jacen. This was structural. External. Something happening *to* the Force, not to him.

Which meant either every other sensitive in the Temple felt it and dismissed it, or he alone felt it clearly enough to name.

Neither option comforted.

He exhaled. Shoulder still stung. He imagined walking into a briefing and announcing he’d lost a spar because the Force had a missing step, and pictured the response.

*You think something's wrong with the Force itself?*

*Yes.*

*Based on what?*

*It feels like Jacen did before he stopped being Jacen.*

He could hear the silence.

The salle doors hissed.

Ben looked up.

Luke Skywalker stood in the doorway holding a datapad, wearing the gravity that meant the mission was already decided and only the telling remained.
