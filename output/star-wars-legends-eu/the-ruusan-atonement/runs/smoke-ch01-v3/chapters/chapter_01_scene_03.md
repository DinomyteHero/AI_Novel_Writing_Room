The preflight was done. Ben sat with it anyway.

The *Ranger's Debt* hummed around him, the cockpit lit in the cool blue of standby systems, and the Jedi Temple hangar stretched out beyond the viewport — high ceilings, the echo of a hydrospanner somewhere in the middle distance, a maintenance crew working on a Defender-class three bays over. Nobody paying attention to him. He appreciated that.

His datapad rested on his knee. The Jedi partner roster was open.

He scrolled past Corvin first. Good pilot, reliable in the field, had a habit of wanting to talk through every Force reading in real time. Then Arev, who'd filed the most recent of the three matching reports and would therefore have opinions about her own work that Ben would have to manage. Then Pellan herself, whose instincts he actually trusted — which meant she'd notice when his instincts were off, and she'd say something, and he'd have to explain why he was reading the anomaly the way he was instead of the standard protocol way, and that conversation would take longer than the mission.

Practical reasons. All of them practical.

He powered down the roster and opened his encrypted personal contacts.

The guilt was brief. He smothered it before it could get organized.

Desh picked up on the third ping. Long-range comm crackled with the slight delay of a Coruscant-to-Nar-Shaddaa bounce, and then his voice came through, dry as recycled air.

"Either you're in trouble or you're bored. Those are the only two reasons you call at this hour."

"Neither," Ben said. "I have a job."

A pause. The comm hissed softly. "Survey work?"

"Outer Rim sweep. Anomaly investigation. Officially routine, actually not."

"Mm." Another pause, shorter. "How long?"

"Unknown. Weeks, probably. Could stretch."

"Pay?"

"Jedi Order rates. I'll get you cleared as a contracted specialist."

"That's worse than GAG rates and GAG rates were insulting."

"GAG doesn't exist anymore."

"My point exactly."

Ben almost smiled. Desh had spent four years with the Galactic Alliance Guard in the era when the Guard was doing work that nobody in the Alliance wanted to officially acknowledge — the kind of surveillance and extraction operations that kept the peace by methods the Senate preferred not to examine. He'd left before the dissolution, which meant he'd gotten out while the leaving was still voluntary. Ben had met him during the period when the Guard's shadow work had briefly intersected with a Jedi operation that nobody was supposed to remember. They'd pulled each other out of a basement on Druckenwell. Neither of them had filed a full report. Some things were easier to leave in the basement.

Desh understood unspoken things. It was what made him useful. It was also why Ben had called him instead of Pellan.

"I leave in an hour," Ben said. "You're on Nar Shaddaa?"

"Close enough. Send me the pickup coordinates."

"Sent."

A beat. On the other end, Ben could hear the faint sound of something being set down — a glass, maybe, or a tool. Then Desh said, with the mild tone of a man confirming a lunch reservation: "This the one you can't take a Jedi on?"

Ben kept his voice even. "It's the one that needs a specialist."

"Right." Desh's tone didn't change. "Send me the survey parameters. I'll read them on the way."

The comm clicked off.

Ben set the datapad on the co-pilot seat and started the ignition sequence. The *Ranger's Debt* came alive beneath him — repulsorlifts cycling up, the hull vibrating in that particular way that meant the engines were warm and ready. He ran through the departure checklist the way he always did, methodically, touching each control in sequence. There was a version of flying that was all Force intuition, reading the ship and the space ahead like an extension of perception. He wasn't doing that version right now. He was doing the mechanical version, the one that was just switches and readouts and a series of steps that ended in the ship being somewhere else.

The hangar crew didn't look up as he lifted off.

He angled the *Ranger's Debt* up through the hangar bay's exit corridor, the ceiling lights strobing past in a regular rhythm, and then he was through and above and Coruscant opened around him — the planet-wide grid of it, towers and transit lanes and the Temple dome falling away behind his port side. He didn't look at the Temple. He watched the traffic lanes and slotted into the orbital approach queue, climbing through the atmospheric strata until the air thinned and the stars sharpened and the planet below became a curved edge of light.

The tension in his shoulders had been there since the briefing. He hadn't noticed it until it started to ease.

He plotted the first jump — Coruscant to the Hydian outer spur, standard route, nothing that would flag in the departure logs as unusual. The navi-computer returned a four-hour transit estimate. He locked in the coordinates.

The wrongness was still there. It had been there all morning, the faint off-note underneath everything, the thing that wasn't corruption or distortion but pure absence. He'd gotten used to it the way you got used to a draft in a room — present, consistent, ignorable if you didn't focus on it.

He pulled the hyperdrive lever.

The stars stretched.

The *Ranger's Debt* punched through, and the tunnel of hyperspace folded around the cockpit — blue-white and constant, the visual static that was supposed to be the only thing you could perceive in transit, because hyperspace was a layer of reality where the Force went quiet and even the most sensitive Jedi flew on instruments alone.

His instruments flickered. A single stutter across three readouts, there and gone.

The wrongness didn't vanish.

It was still there — not fainter, not muffled by the transit layer the way it should have been. And it wasn't ambient anymore. It wasn't the sourceless background drone he'd been tracking for six weeks. It was directional. He could feel it the way you felt a current when you'd been standing in still water — the same water, but now it had somewhere to go, and it was going there, and it was taking him with it.

The navi-computer held steady. The *Ranger's Debt* was on course.

Ben sat in the blue-white of hyperspace and looked at the readouts and didn't move for a long moment.

Ahead. The wrongness was pulling ahead, out along the Hydian corridor, toward something specific and patient and waiting. Not drawing him. Not threatening him. Just pointing, the way a compass pointed, with the indifferent certainty of a thing that had always known where north was.

He'd thought he was investigating an anomaly.

The *Ranger's Debt* hummed around him, steady and oblivious, and the wrongness in the Force held its direction.
