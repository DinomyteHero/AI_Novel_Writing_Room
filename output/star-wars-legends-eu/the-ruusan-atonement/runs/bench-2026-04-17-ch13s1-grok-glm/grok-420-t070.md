Ben watched the planet swell in the viewport, a disk of green continents and blue seas that should have looked peaceful. The Steady Returns held steady orbit while the scanners painted a different picture. Desh leaned over the console, voice flat.

“Vegetation index is inverted. Canopy reads as shadow growth. No avian returns, no mammalian either. Ground temp’s nominal, but the biomass distribution makes zero sense.”

Ben kept his hands loose on the armrests. The world looked temperate, almost inviting. Yet the data insisted something had chewed the edges off reality. He cross-checked the readings himself. Same contradiction. “We expected damage. Not this.”

Torin stood at the rear of the cockpit, arms folded, saying nothing. Sera’s reflection ghosted the glass beside Ben—posture rigid, eyes narrowed on the curve of the horizon. Kael slouched against the bulkhead, watching everyone else watch the planet.

Desh tapped another control. “Surface looks intact from up here. Streets, buildings, the old landing grid. But the growth patterns are backward. Like the light’s coming from the wrong direction.”

Ben exhaled through his nose. Routine arrival, he told himself. They had seen broken nodes before. This one simply wore its damage on the outside. The lie sat easier than the alternative.

They took the shuttle down in silence. The descent felt normal—vibration through the deck, the muted roar of atmosphere. When the ramp dropped, humid air rolled in carrying the scent of turned soil and overripe fruit. Ben stepped onto cracked permacrete. His boots rang against stone that should have been overgrown but wasn’t.

The settlement stretched away in orderly lines. Walls still stood. Roofs remained mostly whole. Yet vines climbed every surface in spirals that twisted against gravity, leaves pale on the sunward side. Flowers—deep violet—clustered in the darkest alleys where no light reached. No wind stirred them. No birds called. The quiet pressed in until Ben’s own pulse sounded loud.

Sera moved beside him, gaze sweeping rooftops out of old habit. “This is deliberate. A test run.”

Torin crouched, ran gloved fingers along a seam where moss should have grown but grew instead in tight rings around the base of a streetlamp. “The Order never recorded anything like this. Not even at the old battlefields.”

Kael stayed near the ramp, hands in his pockets. He studied the inverted growth without comment.

Ben pushed his awareness outward the way he had ten thousand times. He expected the familiar brush of currents, the subtle pressure of life against life. Instead his reach met presence without shape. The Force flooded the space—raw, humming potential—but offered no reflection. No direction. No light, no shadow, no lattice to guide the current. It simply existed, like glare in a room with no surfaces.

His stomach lurched. The absence of resistance felt worse than emptiness. He stood on solid ground yet the floor had vanished beneath every instinct he owned. Vertigo tilted the world sideways. He locked his knees before he could stagger.

Desh walked a slow circle, eyes on the chrono strapped to his wrist. The second hand jerked, paused, then skipped forward three beats at once. He frowned. “Time’s stuttering. Three seconds lost in the last minute. Not drift—erasure.” He looked up, face pale. “And there’s this weight in my chest. Grief, but nothing to pin it on. Like I lost something I can’t name.”

Ben heard the words but they arrived at a distance. The Force remained present, a silent roar with no voice. He tried again, reaching for the old centering exercise. Breathe in, find the thread of calm, let the lattice align the self. Nothing answered. The technique spun in vacuum. His own thoughts crowded forward without the usual softening filter, sharp and uninvited.

*This is what she wants. A galaxy without guardrails. Without the lie that tells us what matters.*

The thought carried no judgment from outside himself. It simply sat there, cold and complete. Ben’s pulse spiked. He had never noticed how much of his balance came from that external handrail until it disappeared.

Sera’s voice cut through. “Ben?”

He blinked, refocused on her face. She watched him the way she watched unsecured hatches—ready for anything.

“I’m here,” he said. The words tasted thin. “It’s not gone. It’s… unstructured. Like trying to see when light has nothing to strike.”

Torin straightened, brushing dirt from his gloves. His usual easy smile had tightened at the corners. “We trained for corrupted sites. Malachor. Nathema. This is different. The dark doesn’t pull. Nothing pulls.”

Kael spoke for the first time, voice low. “Means nothing pushes, either. No intuition. No warning. Just us and whatever we drag inside our own skulls.”

Desh tapped the chrono again. Another skip. The grief in his expression deepened, a man mourning without a corpse. “Whatever she did here, it’s bleeding into baseline reality. Not just for you lot. For everyone.”

Ben turned in a slow circle. The flowers in the alley drank shadow like water. A vine across the street looped backward on itself, leaves pointing at the ground. The air carried the faint mineral bite of old stone and something sweeter, almost fermented. His skin prickled as though the humidity itself had weight.

He tried the centering breath again. Inhale. Hold. Release. The rhythm stayed mechanical. No answering warmth rose to meet him. The thoughts kept coming, faster now, unbuffered.

*You always believed the Order’s lattice was natural. What if Veraine is right? What if we’ve spent a thousand years enforcing a cage?*

The idea should have horrified him. Instead it arrived neutral, a simple proposition. His own mind without the usual editorial voice felt like standing in an empty hangar with every echo belonging to him alone. The realization landed hard: these were not new thoughts. They had always lived beneath the surface, padded by the Force’s quiet assurance that light had weight and dark had consequence. Without that, every doubt carried equal mass.

Sera stepped closer. “Your color’s off. Talk to us.”

Ben swallowed. The words came out steadier than he expected. “It’s all still there. The power. The potential. But the shape is missing. I reach and my hand keeps going. No resistance. No… direction.”

Torin’s jaw worked. He looked at the sky as if it might offer answers. “Then we adapt. The way we always have.”

Kael gave a soft snort. “Adapt to what? Gravity pointing sideways? You can’t adapt to your own compass dissolving.”

Desh closed the chrono’s cover with a click. The sound cracked loud in the unnatural stillness. “Whatever clock this place runs on is broken. And that grief I mentioned? It’s getting heavier. Like the whole settlement died and forgot to fall over.”

Ben felt the words settle against his ribs. The de-structured zone pressed in from every side—present, alive with energy, yet utterly without hierarchy. No moral gravity. No instinct to warn him which way danger lay. He could lift a speeder with a thought or slit a throat with the same indifference, and the Force would not flinch. The knowledge scraped raw places he had never examined in daylight.

He tried one last time to find the old currents. Nothing. Only the roar of possibility without boundary. The absence of the handrail left him swaying though his boots stayed planted.

Sera watched him with that archaic intensity, as if she could will the lattice back into place through sheer discipline. Torin had turned away, studying the backward vines like they might reveal a tactical solution. Kael waited, eyes half-lidded, already calculating angles no one else wanted to see. Desh stood solid, the only one grounded in measurable things—skipping seconds and nameless grief.

Ben’s next breath shook on the way out. The thoughts kept arriving, unsoftened.

*Jacen tried to impose order once. Look where that led. Maybe tearing it down is the honest choice. Maybe—*

He cut the thread himself. The effort cost him. Sweat gathered at his temples despite the mild air. For the first time in his life the silence inside his head belonged entirely to him. All of it. The strategic calculations, the old angers, the quiet fears that the Skywalker name demanded perfection he could never deliver. No gentle current rose to blunt the edges. No luminous assurance that the light would balance the scales.

Only Ben, standing on a street where flowers bloomed in darkness and time itself stuttered, listening to every thought the Force had once kept quiet.
