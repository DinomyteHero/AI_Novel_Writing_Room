The hangar thrummed—repulsorlifts cycling, deck crew barking clearances. Pad seven’s lights striped the *Steady Returns* in alternating amber and green, like a beast that had learned to ignore clocks. Ben’s fingers danced over the pre-flight sequence by touch, muscle memory from a childhood crammed into cockpits. His datapad leaned against the nav-computer, twelve names glowing on the screen.

He swiped past Torvan Res. “Full briefing,” he muttered. “Council loops.” The name vanished.

Yela Maris—sharp, unshakable. Her report had waved off Sernpidal’s wrongness as lingering trauma. He’d read it twice, hunting for gaps. His thumb pressed harder than necessary.

The third name carried his father’s recent praise. Ben’s hand moved before the words registered.

The roster dissolved. Personal contacts appeared. Desh Rolan sat between a dead relay and a Nar Shaddaa cantina that no longer existed.

He hit connect.

“Rolan.” Static and metal-on-metal clanks. “You’re wasting a profitable afternoon.”

“Define profitable.”

“Paid by the hour, easy job. My favorite kind.” A pause; something heavy hit decking. “What do you need?”

Ben adjusted throttle trim. “Six weeks, maybe eight. Outer Rim survey. Off rotation.”

“Off rotation.” Not a question. “How far?”

“Council knows I’m running it. Not the details.”

Clanks resumed. “Danger?”

“Unknown. Probably low.” His hand hovered over the yoke. “Maybe not.”

“At least you’re honest.” The clanks stopped. “Ship?”

“Mine.”

“Good. I hate commercial when people shoot.” A beat. “Anyone shooting yet?”

“Not yet.”

“Launch window?”

Ben checked the checklist. “Forty minutes.”

Thinking air. Not dead. He completed an item, moved to the next.

“Need two hours,” Desh said. “Close a job, grab gear. Meet me commercial terminal, level six arrivals.”

“Leaving from the Temple.”

“Then you wait at the Temple.” Brief pause. “Don’t touch the galley till I vet your stores. Last time you editorialized against caf.”

“I didn’t forget. I declared it a war crime.”

“War crime, Skywalker.”

Ben closed the sequence and leaned back. Tension unspooled from his shoulders in one slow exhale. Two hours longer than he wanted; exactly long enough to stop second-guessing the call he hadn’t made.

Desh hadn’t asked why no Jedi. Hadn’t asked why off-rotation in any way that needed an answer. Just danger and groceries—questions that mattered.

Beyond the transparisteel, Coruscant’s afternoon traffic stacked in holding patterns. The wrongness pressed beneath it all, a low-pressure ache behind his sternum. Directionless weight. He’d stopped trying to pin it down.

He went to count ration packs.

---

Ninety minutes later, Desh climbed the ramp—duffel over shoulder, crate under arm. One glance at the galley, zero words. Worse than complaint.

“There’s caf.”

Desh dropped two extra pouches onto the counter like evidence. “Now there’s enough. Strap in.”

Ben was already in the cockpit.

The *Returns* lifted from pad seven amid routine chatter. Ben flew manual—hands needed work. Temple shrank to a speck, then Coruscant’s amber haze swallowed everything.

He punched throttle.

Stars snapped into cold focus. Ben fed jump coordinates, spooled hyperdrive. Desh rode copilot, scanning secondaries unasked.

“You’ll brief me,” Desh said.

“Eventually.”

“Good enough.”

Charge peaked. Ben yanked the lever.

Starlines.

Instruments flickered—power redistribution, sensor reset, motivator hum settling to cruise. Normal.

The wrongness remained.

Hand still on the column, Ben felt it settle behind his sternum—unchanged yet sharper, pointing like a compass needle that had waited for quiet. Whatever lay beyond the Rim, past the chart’s blank, it wasn’t waiting to be found.

It already knew he was coming.
