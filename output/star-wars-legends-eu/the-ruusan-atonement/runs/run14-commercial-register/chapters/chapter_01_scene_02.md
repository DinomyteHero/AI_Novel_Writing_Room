Luke’s office held the quiet of work that had outlasted dinner—datapads fanned across the desk like scattered playing cards, the holocomm dormant in its cradle, Coruscant’s amber grid pressing against the transparisteel behind him. Ben had grown up in rooms like this: spaces that belonged to the Order first and to people second, where holorecords and lightsabers shared drawers with caf-stained mugs.

Luke nudged a datapad across the scarred surface.

"Seventeen."

Ben thumbed it awake. Blue light washed his knuckles as he scrolled. Seventeen survey reports, each flagged ANOMALY—UNCLASSIFIED, filed over seven months by four different crews. "You’ve been busy. Three Masters had to bury these in the residual index."

"Four." Luke’s correction carried the weight of a man who’d spent decades being underestimated. "The fourth signature’s mine."

Ben’s fingers stilled on the screen. "You filed a false report?"

"My own survey. Six weeks ago." Luke’s mouth held that small, tired smile—half apology, half challenge. "Wanted to see what you’d notice before I told you."

The datapad suddenly felt heavier. "And if I’d missed it?"

"You didn’t."

Ben kept scrolling, letting the silence stretch. Third cluster—Darrpa sector, three separate teams, four months apart, same waveform. Distortion. Displacement. Absence. All three cross-referenced to post-Abeloth residue. All three wrong.

He tapped the gradient zoom. "They call it an echo. It isn’t. Echoes fade. This climbs."

Luke leaned in, elbows claiming desk space. His Force presence spread warm and familiar, the same pressure Ben had swum through as a kid learning to float. Beneath it, almost masked, the wrongness hummed—flat, patient, a single sour note under the Temple’s chord.

"I feel it." Luke’s eyes stayed on the screen. "The cadence matches residual trauma. Abeloth’s wake is still settling; pockets of instability cling to the Outer Rim. This could be one."

Ben set the datapad aside with deliberate care. "Could be."

Luke caught the tone instantly. "But you don’t."

"Gradient’s wrong for residue." Ben folded his arms, fingertips digging into his sleeves. "Three teams, three lexicons, same tag—that deserves a look. If it were Abeloth fallout, it would’ve flattened by now."

"Survey route threads the bearing these reports sketch." Luke’s voice carried that careful neutrality Ben recognized from a hundred briefings. "Eight stops, standard recon, six to eight weeks depending on early returns. I want you to fly it."

Ben crossed to the window. The city layered itself in vertical terraces of neon and stone, sunset bleeding to amber behind transparisteel. Beyond the skyline, the wrongness waited—directional, steady, a wall slightly out of plumb.

"How many pilots?"

The question came out sharper than intended.

"Route specs a single craft." Luke’s answer came too quickly, too smooth. "Staffing’s your call."

Latitude wrapped in permission. No mention of backup, no suggestion of partners—just the open sky and the expectation that a Skywalker would know what to do with both.

Ben’s reflection stared back from the window—same jawline as Luke, same eyes, same tendency to carry the galaxy’s weight like it belonged to him personally. He should propose someone. The thought arrived, polite, and left without waiting for acknowledgment.

"I’ll take it."

Luke dipped his head. "Coordinates are on the second screen."

The silence stretched between them, thick with everything they weren’t saying. Outside, Coruscant kept moving, indifferent and vast, while the wrongness hummed under everything like a note held too long—almost forgotten, never quite.
