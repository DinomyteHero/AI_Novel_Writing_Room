The datapad was already on the desk when Ben walked in.

Luke didn't look up from the screen, but his hand moved — a small gesture, two fingers, *come here* — and Ben crossed the office without breaking stride. The door whispered shut behind him. Coruscant traffic bled through the transparisteel as a low wash of sound, speeder engines and distant comm-chatter, the kind of noise you stopped hearing after the first year in the Temple.

His father's presence in the Force was warm. Familiar in the way gravity was familiar — something you forgot you were standing in until it shifted.

"You wanted to see me."

Luke turned the datapad around and slid it across the desk.

Seventeen reports. Ben picked up the pad and scrolled. Survey data, Outer Rim, clustered along a bearing he recognized before his eyes reached the coordinates. The wrongness was there in the numbers — not named, not diagnosed, but flagged. Anomalous Force-readings. Instrumentation drift that didn't match any known interference pattern. Three separate survey teams had noted it, and none of them had used the same words.

His thumb stopped on the third entry. The readings matched. Not approximately — exactly. The same sour note he'd been carrying under his ribs for weeks, the one that made the Temple's usual harmonics feel like a recording played slightly off-speed.

He hadn't told anyone. He hadn't known how to start.

"When did these come in?"

"The first one crossed my desk six weeks ago." Luke's voice was even. Grand Master voice, but the version he used when the room was empty and the door was closed. "I sat on it. The pattern wasn't clear yet."

Ben kept scrolling. The datapad's blue glow threw sharp shadows across his knuckles. "It's clear now."

"I wanted to be sure before I brought it to you."

*Brought it to you.* Not *brought it to the Council.* Not *raised it with the Masters.* Ben looked up, and his father was watching him with an expression that had nothing to do with institutional protocol.

"You've felt it," Ben said. It wasn't a question.

Luke nodded once. "Not the way you have. I don't think anyone feels it the way you have." He paused, and the pause was the thing Ben noticed — the half-second where his father chose his words instead of reaching for the first ones that came. "It's been building. I've been aware of it as a disturbance. An anomaly. Something that doesn't fit the map."

Ben heard the difference. *Anomaly.* The word sat in his chest like a stone that wasn't his to carry. His father was describing a problem. Ben was recognizing a register he'd lived inside since he was a boy — the wrongness that had been there after Jacen, after the war, after everything. The thing that felt less like a disturbance and more like a wound that had been waiting for someone to notice it was still bleeding.

He didn't say that. He set the datapad down.

"The bearing runs through unclaimed space for most of the route. A few old hyperspace lanes, but nothing that's been charted in the last century." He could hear himself slipping into mission-assessment cadence, the clean rhythm of tactical thinking. It was easier than the other thing. "I can take a shuttle. Run the survey, see if the readings get stronger closer to the source."

Luke didn't answer immediately. His hands rested on the desk, still, the way they always were when he was weighing something. Ben had spent his whole life learning to read those silences.

"You'd be going alone," Luke said.

"I know."

"There's no backup on this route. No support infrastructure. If something goes wrong — "

"Dad." The word came out before Ben could stop it, and he saw the flicker in his father's expression — there and gone, a warmth that didn't quite reach the surface. "I know what I'm signing up for."

Luke held his gaze for a long moment. Then he reached across the desk and tapped the datapad, pulling up the full mission brief. "I've already cleared the assignment. The Council knows you're running a survey. They don't know what I think you're going to find."

"What *do* you think I'm going to find?"

"I think you already know. I think you've known longer than I have." Luke's voice was quiet now, stripped of the Grand Master register. Just a man in an office, talking to his son. "And I think you need to be the one who goes."

Ben stood up. He didn't mean to — his body made the decision before his mind caught up, carrying him toward the window. The traffic below was a river of light, endless streams of speeders threading through the Coruscant canyons. People going home. People going to work. People who had never felt the wrongness and never would, because it didn't live in the kind of space they could touch.

He should ask for a partner. The mission brief allowed for it — a second Knight, someone with survey experience, someone who could watch his back. The words were right there, waiting for him to say them.

He didn't.

"I'll leave tomorrow." His shoulders settled into a readiness that was also a closing. The brief connection with his father was real — he could still feel it, warm and steady at his back — but the distance was necessary. He needed the window. He needed the space. He needed to be the one who went, and he needed to go alone, and he needed his father to understand that without making him explain it.

Luke didn't push.

"The mission packet's on the datapad. Ship's prepped in Hangar Seven." He paused again, and when he spoke, his voice carried something Ben couldn't name — not sadness, not quite. Weight. "Ben. Whatever you find out there — "

"I'll report in."

"That's not what I was going to say."

Ben turned from the window. His father hadn't moved. His hands were still on the desk, still in the same position, and his expression was the one he wore when he was choosing not to say something that mattered.

"Come back," Luke said.

Two words. Not an order. Not a Grand Master's directive. Just a father who had lost too many people, asking his son to be careful.

Ben nodded. "I will."

He picked up the datapad. The blue glow winked out as he tucked it under his arm, and the office was suddenly dimmer — just the ambient light of the city, the faint hum of the Temple's systems, the steady warmth of his father's presence in the Force.

He didn't suggest a partner. Luke didn't offer one. The silence around that choice settled into the room like something that had weight, something that would stay there long after Ben was gone.

He walked to the door. His hand was on the release when Luke spoke again.

"Ben."

He stopped.

"You don't have to carry everything alone."

Ben didn't turn around. If he turned around, he'd see his father's face, and he wasn't sure he could hold the distance if he saw his father's face.

"I know," he said. And then, quieter: "Neither do you."

The door opened. Ben stepped through it, and the corridor swallowed him, and the office was quiet again behind him.
