The training saber grazed Ben’s shoulder—clean, parallel, impossible. He’d read Vesh’s feint, started the counter, but a half-beat slipped away and the blade found its mark anyway.

Heat seared through his tunic.

“Ben.” Vesh lowered her weapon, surprise bleeding into concern. “You’re off.”

“Got that memo.” He killed his blade and rolled the joint. Muscles worked. No bruise. No excuse. “Third hit this week. Maybe I’m finally past it.”

“Twenty-eight isn’t old.”

“For a Skywalker, it’s middle age.” The joke fell flat before it landed.

Vesh didn’t smile. “Again?”

“Rain check.” He clipped the saber to his belt and stepped out of the circle. “Need to cool off.”

She studied him a second too long, towel draped over her shoulder, then left through the side hatch. The door thudded behind her.

Evening light slanted through the high windows, catching motes in the recycled air. Ben stayed on the padded floor, listening to the Temple’s air handlers wheeze.

The wrongness hummed—a note tuned flat.

Three months. Not loud enough to name, just enough to snag his rhythm and cost him a parry. In sparring, a heartbeat meant the difference between a block and a bruised shoulder.

He began with footwork. Form Three, first sequence—no blade, only muscle. Weight back. Pivot. Slide. Mara had drilled the angles into him before he’d ever held a real saber.

No hitch. No drag.

Form Five. Wide stances. Power sweeps. He pushed until sweat stung his eyes and his shoulders burned.

Nothing. Timing clean.

He stopped center-mat, breathing hard, palms pressed together.

Not the body, then.

He widened his stance, hands loose at his sides, and let perception stretch. The Temple pressed in—hundreds of lives stacked like glow-lamps: a circle three floors up, initiates fidgeting through katas, archivists murmuring over datacrons. All of it humming in the background, warm and ordinary.

Underneath, the sour note.

He listened without touching—never touching. It crouched at the edge of sense, a stone under still water, warping the current without breaking skin. No pulse. No color. Just a steady pressure that made his molars ache.

The Council filed it under “background noise”—aftershocks of Abeloth, war trauma, the galaxy’s migraine. Amber tag. Quarterly review. Known variable. He’d signed the reports like a good Knight.

He hadn’t mentioned the compass needle.

Echoes fade. This grew a vector. Every week the bearing sharpened, pointing somewhere he couldn’t yet see.

He reached farther, past Temple walls, past the comfortable white noise of Jedi routines. The sour pitch narrowed. His pulse stumbled—

—and for one stomach-lurching instant he wasn’t on the mat. He was fourteen, breathing ash aboard the Jade Shadow, and the Force around Jacen felt torn—not dark, but something bleeding that belonged to neither side. The boundary had split; through the gap oozed a wrong that turned his stomach inside out.

Jacen’s face—his cousin, not the Sith mask—smiled like nothing had changed.

Ben’s eyes flew open. His hands trembled; he spread his fingers until the shaking stopped.

Not the same. Jacen was ash. Abeloth gone. Galaxy stitched and scarred.

Yet the flat note hadn’t faded. It had settled—patient, foundational, like a crack in permacrete. You can live over a fracture for years, ignore it, hang art on the walls. Eventually the shift wins and the roof sags.

The word hit him like a rifle butt: *structural.*

Not residue. Not contamination. Not noise to file and forget. Something wrong with the Force itself—not how it was used, but how it *was*. If that held, every Council briefing was written on wishful parchment.

He shut his eyes. Breath shallow. He dragged it deeper, one lungful at a time, and felt the knowledge settle across his shoulders like a lead mantle.

If he was right—and the ache behind his sternum said he was—he listened alone. He’d stood beside the tear once before; he alone recognized the scar reopening.

Take it to the Council? They’d already ruled. Reverse himself and he admitted either perjury or fracture—Skywalker perception finally cracked. Neither earned a hearing.

Solve it himself, then.

The thought should have felt lonely. Instead it felt inherited. Father had rebuilt the Order alone. Mother had hunted shadows no quorum would sanction. Ben had watched. Learned the taste of that solitude.

His weight now. His crack to seal.

He opened his eyes.

The salle doors hissed. Luke Skywalker stood in the threshold, datapad in hand, shoulders set to a pitch Ben could read before he could spell. Mission-ready. No greeting. No joke.

That face meant trouble had arrived.
