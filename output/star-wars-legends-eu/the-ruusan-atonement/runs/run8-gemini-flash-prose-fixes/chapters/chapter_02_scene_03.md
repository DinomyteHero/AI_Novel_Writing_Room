The second world was a vertical nightmare of ice and jagged slate, worlds away from the sun-scorched dust of the first survey site. Freezing mist clung to the underside of a low, violet canopy. The air tasted of wet iron.

Ben Skywalker ignored the sleet stinging his cheeks. He crouched beneath a basalt overhang, gloved fingers scraping away rime from a flat stone face. The rock was metamorphic, layered like old parchment, yet the marks carved into its surface were impossibly familiar.

Interlocking circles. A jagged vertical line.

The same hand had been here. The same tool had shaped this stone. Ben traced the groove of the vertical strike—and the world tilted. Depth perception flickered. The overhang seemed to retreat kilometers while the ground rose to meet his chest. A parallax error of the soul. The Force didn’t flow here. It stuttered, leaving a hollow ringing in his inner ear. The stone beneath his hand vibrated like a struck bell.

"Temperature’s dropping," Desh Rolan said. His voice was flat. He stood ten meters back, boots crunching on frozen moss. A handheld thermal scanner hung from his grip, its display furrowed in his brow. "But only in a three-meter radius around that slab. Outside this circle, it’s a balmy four degrees. Inside? Cryogenic. My sensors are throwing a fit, Ben."

Ben pulled his hand back. The phantom vibration still rattled his bones. "The acoustics are off again."

"Off is an understatement." Desh tossed a pebble toward the basalt slab. Instead of a sharp clack, the impact produced a dull, heavy thud—echoing from a point five meters behind the rock. "Sounds like we’re in a duracrete vault, not an open ridge. I don’t like it when physics takes a holiday. Let’s get the imagery and clear out before the mountain decides up is down."

Ben stood. His legs felt leaden. He took the high-resolution scans with steady hands, though his mind was already light-years distant. One site was an anomaly. Two sites, separated by hundreds of systems but unified by a singular, disturbing architectural dialect—that was a trail.

"We’re done here."

They climbed in silence to the landing clearing where the *Steady Returns* waited, its engines ticking as they cooled. The transition back into recycled air and mechanical hum felt like a reprieve. Ben shed his thermal cloak and headed straight for the cockpit, movements efficient, driven by a growing, cold clarity.

Desh was already at the nav-station, fingers dancing across the interface. "I’ve been running the numbers since we lifted. The ice world we just left and the ruins on WR-7741. Overlaying the coordinates now."

A holographic star chart bloomed between them—a sea of blue and gold pinpricks. Two red dots pulsed in the Darrpa Sector. Desh swiped his hand. A bright white vector line snapped into existence, connecting the two points.

"A perfect straight edge." His voice lost its usual dry edge. "Ancient outposts usually follow trade routes or resource veins. These? They don’t care about local geography. They’re markers on a heading."

Ben leaned over the console, hands braced against its edge. "Show me the survey route. The official mission data."

Desh’s fingers hesitated. "You sure about that?"

"Pull it."

The Jedi-green line appeared, lying directly on top of Desh’s white vector. Not parallel. Not similar. Exact.

The cockpit ventilation suddenly seemed inadequate. Ben’s pulse hammered in his throat. "Extend the bearing. Past the second site."

"Ben—"

"Just do it."

Desh’s hands moved with practiced precision. The white vector elongated, slicing through charted systems of the Outer Rim. It bypassed known hubs, ignored established hyperspace lanes, and kept going. It drove past the edge of the Republic’s furthest beacons.

"Ben." Desh’s voice was low. "The line’s leaving the map."

The holographic display shifted, zooming out until the galaxy’s familiar clusters shrank toward the center. The vector line continued its unwavering path into unmapped void. It pointed toward a sector of gravity wells and radiation clouds that had deterred scouts for a thousand years.

Ben reached past Desh. Fingers hovered over the navicomputer’s input. He punched in the coordinates for the extended bearing, forcing the computer to render the empty space at the line’s end.

The screen flickered, processing against its deepest archives. No name. No trade route. Just a ghost—a cluster of faint, unconfirmed gravity signatures that shouldn’t have existed.

The bearing pointed to systems that didn’t appear on any chart Ben had ever seen.

Something was out there.
