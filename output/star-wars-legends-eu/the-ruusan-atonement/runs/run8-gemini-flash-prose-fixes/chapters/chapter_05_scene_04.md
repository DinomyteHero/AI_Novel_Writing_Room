The command table of the *Steady Returns* flickered with overlapping ghosts: sapphire maps, amber schematics, a violet thread that sliced across both. Ben nudged the thread toward the Ruusan Corridor, systems that hadn’t tasted steel since his grandfather’s day.

Beside him, Sera and Torin worked in silence—crystal passed, port slotted, archive decrypted, priority sorted. One rhythm, worn smooth by campaigns neither of them recalled. Ben watched their shoulders brush, their hands move in parallel circuits that left him outside the pattern.

“Three fronts.” The ship’s processors hummed beneath his words. He tapped red icons that pulsed like infected cells. “Anchor nodes are cascading. Nav buoys report drift, channels dropping to static. The lattice seal is losing its grip on local physics.”

His finger traced the violet line. “Veraine has sixty-three days on us. She’s not running; she’s shopping. If she tags the remaining nodes first, she won’t wait for the seal to die. She’ll pry it open and name the price.”

Desh leaned from the cockpit arch, wiping his knuckles with a rag that had once been white. He surveyed the four Force-sensitives crammed into a space meant for two. “Scrubbers maxed, thermostat dumped. We’re breathing each other’s exhalations. Anyone hyperventilating, do it on a schedule.”

He aimed the wrench at Kael, statuesque in the corner. “You—webbing, now. Physics doesn’t bargain with existential despair.”

Kael’s eyelids lowered a fraction. He slid into the jump seat, sleeves swallowing his hands.

Sera straightened, braid flicking her shoulder blade. “Nearest node is thirty lights out—minor fortress, edge of the Darrpa Sector.” Her gaze locked onto Ben. “Your Council’s response window?”

Ben’s hand stilled over the display. “We aren’t calling them.”

The air thickened. Sera’s eyes narrowed, tallying risks he hadn’t named. “Explain. Sector-wide collapse. You’re a Knight, not a fleet. You can’t hold that line with sheer will.”

“They’d come in waving rule books,” Ben said. The words left a metallic tang. “Forty years of light-side doctrine colliding with a place where the Force forgets its own name. They’d step off the ramp confident, die confused. Veraine would harvest them before they finished the landing checklist.”

He swept his stare across the survivors—three witnesses to a war crime the Order never recorded. “I’m the only one who knows you exist. Summon the Council and this becomes a hearing. Weeks of ethics memos while the galaxy frays. Not on my watch. I’ll drive.”

Torin’s voice was nearly a sigh. “You talk like a man renting a disaster he plans to own.”

The Battle Master’s hands rested on the table’s edge, scars glowing pale in the holo-light. “Gavran swore the lattice was a cage we forged because we feared the wild. Draw the line bright enough, he said, and the galaxy would behave.”

Ben realigned a node marker. “Lines matter. Without them, power has no compass. Jedi stay in the light because the alternative consumes the weak first.”

Torin lifted his head. “You think the Force recognizes your maps?”

“I’ve tested them.” Ben’s fingers tapped the console. “Every right choice echoes back warm. Every wrong one—cold.”

Torin’s face didn’t argue; it folded, like a tent collapsing in slow motion. He stared at the map again, the rasp in his throat older than the ship around them. “Gavran thought so too. Before.”

Ben logged the name: Gavran, architect of the Silence—another link in a chain forged from blood and holocron ash. He tagged it for later, attention already sliding to the nav console. He missed Torin’s shoulders sagging, missed Sera’s knuckles whitening on her unlit hilt.

Mission first. Ghosts later.

He stepped into the alcove beside the engineering panel, datapad warm against his palm. Thumb hovered.

*Investigating environmental instability Darrpa Sector. Recovered survivors from uncharted installation. Proceeding to secondary verification. Standard protocols intact.*

No mention of Veraine. No mention of the seal. No mention that the survivors were a millennium old and carried a war crime in their pockets. He was shielding his father from a truth that would crack the Order’s foundation. Skywalker burdens came with reinforced spines.

Transmit.

The bar crawled green, then vanished. Bridge burned.

“Strap in.” Desh’s voice cut through the hum. “We’re fat and cranky, but she’ll fly.”

Ben dropped into the co-pilot seat. Beyond the transparisteel, the plateau’s fused stone caught sunrise like broken glass. Engines shrieked; deck plates jittered. Five heartbeats and a cargo hold of secrets climbed toward vacuum.

The fortress dwindled to a lid on a wound that refused to close. Space opened, cold and familiar, but the wrongness didn’t fade—it thinned, spreading like oil on water.

The nav screen pulsed with the first anchor’s coordinates. The hunger had a name, a vector, and competition.

Ship’s nose aligned. Thrusters bit. The *Steady Returns* lurched into the Corridor, trailing a lie vast enough to eclipse a sector.
