The walls of the twelfth chamber showed no frantic gouges. Ben traced a groove with his gloved fingers—smooth as river stone, deep enough to swallow his fingertip. The cut ran straight, geometric, part of a pattern carved by pressure so patient it had outlasted carbonite. Not rage. Erosion.

The Force here pressed against his awareness like a bruise. In the other cells, survivors left warm residue—jagged exhaustion, dull endurance. This space tasted of cold iron and salt. Analytical hunger. Something that had spent a thousand years not enduring its cage but dissecting it.

"She didn't break it," Ben said. The vault swallowed his voice. "She unmade it."

He keyed the console set into the doorframe. Red text flooded the tiny screen—centuries of flags, warnings, power spikes. The earliest timestamp predated Ruusan by two hundred years.

"Gavran’s logs," Sera said. She wasn’t looking at the display; her gaze had fixed on the bare floor where the anchor's bolts had been sheared off. "He flagged her Priority One-Alpha. Knew she'd feel the seal slip first."

Ben filtered by prisoner tag. One file remained intact.

Darth Veraine. Brotherhood of Darkness. Specialization: Alchemic Strategy.

The name meant nothing to the holocrons he'd studied, but the sub-lines made his stomach knot. Architect of the Veranthos Lattice Inversion. Senior Tactician, Seventh Legion. Every title carried the weight of campaigns that had turned worlds to glass.

He shut the console. "Primary hub. If she touched anything, there's a record."

They climbed. Boots rang on durasteel grating, the sound bouncing down the lightless shaft like blaster echoes. Between Levels 2 and 1, the air warmed, carrying a metallic tang that coated the tongue. The Force thinned, stretched over empty corridors the way it did over asteroid fields—hollow, echoing.

Level 1's monitoring hub glowed with Desh’s handheld lamp. The pilot knelt beside an open locker, beam sweeping across scattered ration packs.

Kael stood apart, shoulders rounded, hands loose. A data tablet lay on the bench beside him—slate-black, etched with jagged sigils. He stared at it as if it might ignite.

Ben slowed. "Kael?"

The conscript reached out, paused, then lifted the tablet. His thumb brushed the etched crest of the Seventh Legion. The muscles along his jaw bunched; the room's lamplight caught the tremor he couldn’t quite stop. He set the tablet down exactly where it had been, edges aligned, and slid his hands into his coat pockets.

"Recognize it?" Ben asked.

"Old model," Kael said. Voice flat, all the dry irony bled out. "Nothing useful."

Desh’s lamp snapped toward them. "She shopped before she left. Rations. Med-spikes. Nav-unit. Even traded boots."

He kicked the discarded pair: ancient leather, soles cracked, beside them the ghost-imprint of standard-issue fortress footwear.

"Strategist," Sera muttered. "She cased the joint before she walked."

Ben moved to the primary console, keyed in Desh’s finds. The projector flared, painting the room in sapphire lattice. A violet thread unspooled from Level 3, thick as twine.

It didn’t race for the exit. It curled to Level 2—paused six hours—slid to engineering, circled the armory, returned here. Methodical. Patient. A surveyor mapping fault lines.

"She wanted the plans," Ben said. The violet trace hovered over the archive node, bright, poisonous. "She learned how the Silence was built, then picked her moment."

Torin leaned over the gate console, big fingers dancing. His breath rasped in the hush. "Exit cycled sixty-three days ago."

The words punched the air from the room. Ben stared at the holographic thread pointing into the Ruusan Corridor. Sixty-three days.

He’d spent those weeks sparring in the Temple, arguing about meditation forms, thinking the disturbance was his alone. While he catalogued anxiety, Veraine had been moving—acquiring ships, credits, contacts.

The race had started without them. And she was already rounding the first turn.
