Ben’s guard dropped a half-second too late.

The training blade kissed his shoulder with a hot pop. Plasma filament, low-output, still enough to sting. He hissed, blade wavering.

Third time this week the wrongness had clipped his timing. Not fatigue. Not distraction. Something in the Force itself slipped a cog. The universe hiccuped.

“Ben?” Aris shut his weapon down. Violet died. The salle swam in Coruscant’s amber evening cycle. “That’s twice in ten minutes. You’re off.”

Ben rolled the ache, flashed the easy Skywalker grin that usually bought space. “Temple stim-tea. Droids are using coolant instead of leaves.”

“Skywalker, you’re sweating through your tunic and you just let me hit you twice. That’s not a tea problem.” Aris wiped sweat from his brow. “You’ve been twitchy since the Rim run. Master Tionne says some Knights still feel Abeloth’s echo.”

“Great. Medical diagnosis by historian.” Ben thumbed his hilt—snap-hiss, blue, snap-gone. The sound felt cheap. “Look, I’m fine. Just overtrained.”

“Overtrained is falling asleep in your soup. You’re jumping at shadows.” Aris stepped closer, voice dropping. “You’re not the only one who felt that thing in the Maw. But the Council keeps saying it’s residual—”

“Residual doesn’t make you late on a block.” Ben’s voice cracked like a whip. He caught himself, forced the grin back into place. “I’ve got this, Aris. Really.”

Aris studied him. Long moment. “You’re bleeding timing in a way I’ve never seen. Whatever’s eating you, handle it before you spar someone who’ll aim for your head instead of your shoulder.”

The doors hissed shut behind him. Silence poured in, thick as the stone walls.

Ben stayed. Boots whispered across synth-weave until he stood dead center. No blade this time. Eyes closed. He began Shien—slow, deliberate. Calves burned. Spine locked. Weight shifted. His body knew the way.

He reached.

Not for light or dark. For structure. The lattice that hummed beneath reality. Skywalkers grew up inside that architecture. Ben had learned to read load-bearing truths before he could spell them.

He pushed past Temple static—sleeping Jedi, basement generators, the city-planet’s pulse.

There.

Flat note. A dropped pressure plate inside his inner ear.

Not darkness. He’d swum in Jacen’s oil, Abeloth’s scream. This was absence. One vital string had been clipped from the galactic chord. Picture stayed. Depth vanished. The wrongness tugged southwest, a faint vacuum headache behind his eyes.

He froze. Arm half-extended.

He knew this tilt. Same tilt the floor had taken when Jacen started calling himself Caedus.

Breath shallow, he opened his eyes. Not tired. Not broken. Witness.

Council couldn’t see this yet. They’d hand him meditation beads and a father-son talk: Mara’s boy chasing ghosts again.

Handle it. Diagnose the missing note before the song collapsed.

He started Shien again. Faster. Sweat beading. Every time he reached the thin spot, he adjusted. Compensated. Rewrote his own combat map around a fraying reality. Skywalker rule: if the Force faltered, you patched the break—even blind.

The wrongness sharpened. Not louder. Just more wrong. Like trying to breathe through a hole that kept moving.

His knee buckled on the next pivot. He caught himself, palm slapping stone, and laughed—sharp, bitter. “Great. I’m getting my ass kicked by physics.”

Doors hissed.

Ben held the finish. Chest hammering. In the doorway stood a warm, familiar sun dimmed by the room’s thinning air.

Luke Skywalker said nothing. Travel cloak on. Datapad in hand. Thumb resting on the edge—General face. Not father face. Mission incoming.
