The structure wasn't ruins. It had power.

Ben Skywalker's boot scraped basalt as he leaned over the ravine's edge. The wind stole his breath, but what lay below stole his words—a bunker of fused stone pulsing in slow, mechanical rhythms that traveled up his spine. Not the bright chord of a temple. Not the crackle of a Sith crypt. Something that had waited centuries for visitors and found them wanting.

"Bearing's dead on," Desh Rolan called, voice cutting through the gale. He slapped his chrono hard enough to leave dust streaks on the metal. "Closer we get, the faster she runs. Physics just surrendered."

Ben's throat worked. From orbit, the site had felt like torn fabric. Here, it felt like standing inside the wound itself. The Force stretched thin as ice over a lake—something massive moving beneath, waiting for the crack.

He started downslope. Desh followed, blaster holstered but thumb riding the strap. Each boot-scrape sent sparks skittering into the void. Ben's lightsaber stayed clipped. His palm didn't.

The bunker rose—sheer planes, no ornament, no welcome in the stone. A blockhouse built for war, the kind the Reformations had tried to erase from memory. The kind that refused to die.

"Door seal's breathing," Desh said, finger leveled at a circular plate set into the rock. Same sigil as the ruins: twin circles split by a vertical slash. Here it pulsed sapphire.

Ben stepped up. A slit opened. Blue light poured out, slid across his face, chest, knuckles—cold, clinical, peeling back layers. It paused on his lightsaber, snapped back to his eyes. The glow curdled to amber. Somewhere inside, metal teeth ground against each other.

"Doesn't like your flavor."

"Looking for a vintage they don't make anymore." Ben's voice stayed steady, but his pulse hammered against the light's touch. He reached—not with flesh. Calm. Balanced. The way his father had shown him. Peace like still water.

The amber flared crimson. The floor kicked beneath their boots.

"Ben—"

He understood. Still water wouldn't open this lock. He gathered the Force—not as a gift, but as a weapon—and drove his signature against the mechanism like a battering ram. Authority instead of serenity.

Red wavered. Bled back to blue. Settled into hard white.

Stone screamed. The door rose, coughing stale air and dry rot into their faces.

"Reluctant," Desh muttered, sidearm clearing leather. He slipped past, sweeping the barrel down a throat of green floor-strips and dust. "Like it had to dig through the archives to decide you still rate."

"I’m not the guest it expected." Ben's words tasted like failure. Like showing up to a party centuries late and finding the house had been waiting to judge his worth.

He followed. Lights flickered alive underfoot—sickly viridian—revealing durasteel ribs in the walls, weapon racks long stripped, consoles drowned in grey ash. Their footsteps rang like coins on a drum, each echo asking: *Are you worthy? Are you sure?*

Ben halted in the foyer. Air tasted of centuries and disappointment. The hum in his bones was the sound of empires outlived, of guardians who'd died at their posts still waiting for relief that would never come. Awe opened inside him—cold, bright, terrible.

Desh's voice reeled him back. "Power cells are humming." He stood in an archway, helmet lamp stabbing into a room of sleeping generators. "Closed loop—pulling juice from the planet and feeding itself. Been running solo since before either of us had ancestors."

He rejoined Ben, checked his chrono again. "Now three seconds fast. Stay an hour and I'll miss my own funeral. Every Jedi digs its own physics grave?"

"This wasn't a retreat." Ben's hand shook as he wiped dust from the central console. The toggles felt wrong under his fingers—too large, too certain. "It's a picket. First line of a perimeter that probably no longer exists."

He set his palm on the plate.

The bunker woke roaring. Ceiling projectors flared, white light bleaching every shadow into chalk. Screens scrolled frantic glyphs, letters stuttering across dead glass like dying men trying to speak. Somewhere aft, a klaxon howled three discordant notes—a sound meant for minds tuned to the Force.

Desh dropped to a knee, blaster trained down-corridor. "We triggered movement?"

"Broadcast." Ben's fingers fought the toggles as they bucked under his touch. The system shoved back—protocols fossilized since Sith Empires fell. He felt machine desperation, an automated need to scream at admirals who had turned to dust centuries ago. *Help us. Stop them. The seal is failing.*

"Frequencies are dead," he said. "Sub-space bands mothballed since Ruusan. It's been yelling into vacuum the whole time."

"Translate?"

"Syntax is High Galactic, pre-Reformation." He forced a bridge. The console spat sparks, then surrendered with a sound like breaking bone.

Screens blacked. The klaxon choked. Silence landed like a slab.

One monitor blazed. Letters jittered, struggling to render modern Basic.

They steadied.

Two words: SEAL FAILING.
