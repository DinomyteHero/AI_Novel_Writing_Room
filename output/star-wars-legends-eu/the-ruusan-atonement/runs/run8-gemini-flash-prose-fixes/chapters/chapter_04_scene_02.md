The air in Level 1 clung to Ben’s skin, thick and stale. Recycled oxygen had died here a thousand years ago, leaving dust that coated every surface in a uniform shroud. His boots carved crisp black furrows through it as he walked.

A bass note thrummed in his molars—not machinery, but the mountain itself, vibrating like a struck bell that wouldn’t stop ringing.

"Power’s dead," Desh said, his voice muffled by the breather. His glow-rod swept the room, illuminating dead monitors and comm headsets reduced to bone-white frames. "Emergency reserves bled out before the Republic was founded."

"Not all of them." Ben stopped at the primary tactical station. No switches. He closed his eyes and sent a needle of intent through the floor plating. Capacitors waited below—dry, brittle, clinging to a ghost of charge. He nudged them in sharp Morse pulses.

The console groaned. A copper spark spat from a seam. The primary display flickered awake in sickly green.

Desh was already moving, datapad raised, snapping high-res frames of every readout. "Look at the angles, Skywalker."

Ben looked. The circular room aimed its blast doors and repeating-blaster slits inward, at the stairwell—not outward at sky or mountain path.

"They weren’t stopping a drop from orbit." Desh’s thumb clicked as he mapped exits. "They were stopping whatever tried to climb out. This isn’t a command post. It’s a lid."

Ben’s stomach clenched. "A lid for what?"

"Ledger’s downstairs." Desh didn’t look up. "Level 2. Prison keeps records."

They spiraled down. If Level 1 was a tomb, Level 2 was a library under siege.

Near the entrance, holocron pedestals stood in perfect ranks—Gavran’s handwriting etched in stone. Ten meters farther, the order collapsed. Data-tablets lay in avalanche piles. Vacuum-sealed flimsi bulged from half-closed lockers. Encrypted canisters had rolled like loose ammunition, spilling crystal wafers across the floor.

Ben picked up a tablet. Its casing still held battery warmth. He thumbed the screen. High Galactic scrolled past in forgotten shorthand: Sector-Lord, Pyre-Guard, ranks the modern Temple had never filed.

"I can’t parse half of this," he said. "They rewrote the language."

"Try the biometric terminal." Desh didn’t look up from the crate of blueprints he was scanning. "Center pedestal."

Ben pressed his palm to cold alloy. For a heartbeat, the plate resisted. Then it recognized the Skywalker signature from the gate. A hologram sputtered to life, pixels misfiring like dying stars.

He requested construction logs. Red text slapped the air: ACCESS DENIED. LEVEL 4 CLEARANCE REQUIRED. GAVRAN-AUTHORIZATION ONLY.

"Force-lock," Ben said, jerking his hand back. "Break it wrong and I might trigger anything."

"Skywalker." Desh’s voice dropped to a growl. "Here. Now."

Ben crossed to the table Desh had cleared with his sleeve. Beneath the dust lay a glass surface pulsing slow amber.

"I thought it was a starchart," Desh said. "History lesson."

Ben watched fresh telemetry crawl across the lower bezel—timestamps updating in real time. "It’s live."

Outer Rim sectors divided by jagged borders that ignored every hyperspace lane Ben knew. Twelve nodes dotted the grid. Nine glowed dead red. Three beat amber, steady as a pulse. The central node, Darrpa Sector, convulsed violet, hemorrhaging light across the map. A single word flashed, bruising the dust purple:

BREACH.

"That’s us," Desh said, tapping the nearest amber dot. "And that—" he pointed at the violet wound, "—is whatever got loose sixty-three days ago."

"Containment network," Ben said. The mountain seemed to tilt beneath him. "Twelve anchors holding something in. Nine are dead. We’re one of the last three still biting down."

The wonder he’d carried inside the front door dissolved, replaced by the cold certainty that he was standing inside a failing machine built to keep a wound shut.

Violet crept toward the next amber node, slow as infection.

"Level 3," Ben said. "Power source is still breathing. Map says so."

"That violet looks like gangrene, Ben."

"We came to read the past." Ben’s voice was flat. "Turns out it’s the present."

They wove through overturned canisters until the rear doors loomed—armored slabs wedged open a hand’s width. Blue-tinged emergency light flickered up the stairwell beyond, a slow, steady pulse against steel.

Ben took the first step. The railing burned cold under his palm. Air rising from below carried metal shavings and static. The hollow he’d tracked across the galaxy—the structural absence—was gone. Something dense pulsed below, thick as syrup, alive.

He shut his eyes and opened the other set.

Three separate rhythms thudded against his senses, each one warm, weighted, deliberate. Not battle-ghosts. Not fossil impressions.

People. Living people, sealed under the mountain and still breathing.
