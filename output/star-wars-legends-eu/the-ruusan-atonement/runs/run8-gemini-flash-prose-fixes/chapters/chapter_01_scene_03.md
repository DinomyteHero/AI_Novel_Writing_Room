The cockpit of the *Steady Returns* reeked of scorched wiring and stale recycled air. Ben Skywalker ran the pre-flight by muscle memory, fingers flying across switches that felt more like armor than instruments. Beyond the transparisteel, the Temple hangar yawned—amber lights, clanking droids, the deep pulse of a departing bulk freighter shaking dust from the overhead beams. All that shared purpose and ancient tradition pressed against the viewport, held at bay by a finger-thick sheet of crystal.

He yanked the datapad free. The roster scrolled: Knight Aris Vane, Soresu stylist, report-happy. One swipe, gone. Tionne Solusar, scholar—she’d log barometric pressure before she noticed the hollow in the Force that set his teeth on edge. Gone. Jaden Korr, bright beacon in the Force—exactly the distraction Ben didn’t need. Gone.

Another Jedi would hear the same off-key note, want to talk about it, "center," report back. A mirror reflecting Jacen’s calm monster, Vestara’s smiling blade. Ben killed the list, opened his private frequencies, and punched the unregistered code.

Static spat. Then a rhythmic clank-hiss of hydrospanner on metal.

"You’re late," Desh Rolan said, voice gravel through cheap comm. "Owes me twenty credits, Skywalker. Dock foreman bet you’d whine at 0300."

The knot between Ben’s shoulders eased a millimeter. "Council ran long. You’d hate it."

"I hate anything with robes." Something heavy crashed behind him, followed by Corellian curses that would’ve bought a week of dish duty in the Temple kitchens. "Hold this seal’s got a grudge."

Ben listened to honest labor: metal on metal, no mystic overtones. Desh didn’t hum in the Force; he was a warm pocket of silence, the only quiet Ben trusted.

"Job?" Desh asked, breathing hard.

"Survey. Ruusan Corridor. Sensor ghosts."

"Survey." Desh snorted. "Right. You call when the gauges lie. How many shoot at us by day three?"

"Might get messy. Need eyes that aren’t glow-sticks. No backup."

A pause—not meditation, just two men remembering red-lit corridors and the scent of burnt promises. Desh never asked why no Master, no task force. He knew.

"Terminus Station?" Desh asked.

"Forty-eight hours."

"Fuel pods topped, secondary scanners dialed. Bring the plain kit—gold leaf won’t stop what makes nav-comps twitch."

"Already packed."

"See you in the dark, kid."

The line clicked dead. Silence flooded the cockpit. Efficient. Streamlined. Safe.

Ben ignited the repulsors. The *Returns* lifted, vibration climbing the frame like an alert climbing a spine. He eased her past the hangar mouth, past balconies where Masters sometimes stood. No looking.

Coruscant’s atmosphere thinned; color drained from sky to star-pierced black. He set the jump to Darrpa Sector, hand on the silver lever. Always, at this point, the Force opened wide, vacuum-clean. Not tonight. The sour note stayed, now pointing like a compass needle swung true.

He pulled. Stars smeared, warped, dragged toward a mass he couldn’t see. Hyperspace wrapped the hull in blue fire—and the wrongness rode the tunnel with him, aimed.
