The datapad’s blue glow sliced through the amber twilight of the Grand Master’s office. Luke didn’t hand it over—he slid it across the desk, stopping it one thumb-length from Ben’s reach.

Ben let it sit. His pulse still thrummed from the Shien drills in the salle, and the office air carried the papery scent of old flimsi and the toasted bite of Luke’s stim-tea. Beneath that comfort ran the sour tang Ben had been tasting for weeks—metal left too long in acid rain, a fracture in the Force’s frame where a beam should be.

He lifted the pad.

“Seventeen flagged reports,” Luke said, Council-voice, General-face. “Darrpa Sector, Ruusan Corridor. Sensor ghosts, nav drift, localized Force fluctuations.”

Ben flicked through the noise—mineral claims, crop yields, pirate tallies—until three entries glowed. Not ghosts. Gaps.

He zoomed on WR-7741. Gravity held steady, yet the pilot’s log read: *Falling upward.* In the Force, the telemetry wasn’t dark—it was hollow, a missing rivet in reality’s plating.

“I’ve tracked the bearing three months,” Ben said, matching Luke’s tone. He didn’t mention the stumble in his footwork, the tilt that kept pulling him toward the Rim. “Drift’s linear.”

“Three months?” Luke’s presence flared—sun-warm, then knife-sharp. “You filed nothing.”

“Thought it was echo.” A half-truth to dodge med-eval. “Abeloth. The Maw. Wanted a vector before I buried the Council in noise.”

He set the pad down. The casing clicked too loud. Coruscant’s night grid glittered below, a trillion lights pretending the galaxy was whole.

Luke’s chair creaked. “Survey mission. Six to eight weeks. Quiet. Someone who can see past the sensors.”

Ben met his own reflection—Skywalker jaw, Skywalker eyes—while the sour pull drummed behind his temples. “Solo?” he asked the glass.

“Unless you’ve a partner in mind.”

Aris would report every breath. Tionne would catalogue the emptiness until it drowned in footnotes. Any knight would drag the Council along, and the Skywalker shadow would eclipse the hole before he measured it.

“No,” Ben said. “Solo’s faster.”

He turned. The sour note pulsed, a command disguised as mission.

“Do you feel it, Master?” He let the title fence them apart. “Not the reports—the thing itself?”

Luke studied the prosthetic hand resting on the desk. “A tremor. Storm building beyond the horizon—dark-side pressure, maybe Tribe residue.”

Cold pooled in Ben’s chest. *Dark-side pressure.* The textbook answer. He’d swum that sea when Jacen fell, tasted Abeloth’s oily hunger. This wasn’t presence. It was absence—one chord struck and never answered, a song left limping.

Luke couldn’t hear the missing note. His perception spanned galactic architecture; he hunted cracks while Ben stared at the vanished wall.

“A tremor,” Ben echoed. To explain the structural failure and the mission would morph into crisis—task force, father tagging along, shadow devouring the mystery. He tucked the dissonance behind his teeth.

“Unsettling,” he added. Tactical silence. He thumbed the pad dark. “I’ll start at WR-7741. If it’s resonance, I’ll flag it for cleanse.”

Luke’s gaze drilled, gentle and ancient. A brush of warmth reached through the Force—father asking son to share weight.

Ben neither recoiled nor leaned in. He stood Knight receiving orders, the missing chord locked in private silence.

“Be careful,” Luke said. The General mask slipped, revealing the father underneath for a heartbeat. “Outer Rim isn’t the Temple. If it feels bigger than a tremor—”

“I’ll call.” He wouldn’t, not until he understood. “Wheels up at 0600.”

He straightened, datapad against his thigh. The office shrank: three paces of polished floor, a desk, two Skywalkers pretending distance was professional instead of necessary.

Luke nodded, slow, not pushing. Perhaps he recognized the quiet for what it was—declaration of independence wearing mission brief clothes.

“May the Force be with you, Knight Skywalker.”

“And with you, Grand Master.”

Ben left without naming a partner. Luke didn’t ask. Neither spoke the cost of that silence.
