Sera Varik didn’t wait for permission. She crossed the Level 2 archive like a blade sliding home, boots ringing against fused stone. Ben had wrestled with the console for twenty minutes—dead circuits, High Galactic glyphs he could barely parse. Sera brushed past him. The machine purred.

Six notes, bright as vibroblade strikes, rang from the panel.

“System recognize: Knight Varik, Sera. Authorization: Aegis-Prime.”

Projectors coughed alive overhead. Sapphire ribbons knifed through dust, sketching a galaxy that wasn’t his. Trade routes vanished. Whole sectors wore the colors of extinct kingdoms. The map hovered, rotating slow as a sleeping predator.

Torin stepped into the light. His scarred face turned translucent, eyes reflecting a violet bruise that throbbed across the Darrpa Sector.

“Still spreading,” he murmured. “Look how far.”

Ben’s hands found the edge of the console, knuckles whitening. For months, the wrongness had gnawed his sleep—an itch behind every meditation. He’d blamed leftover Sith taint, told himself it would fade.

“That’s it,” he said. The words tasted like copper. “That’s what I’ve been feeling.”

Sera’s reflection shimmered in the stain, her jaw set like she was biting through something.

“Lattice Inversion. Kaan’s alchemists peeled reality at Veranthos. Wanted the Force raw—an ocean to drown the Republic.”

“But the Thought Bomb ended it,” Ben said, the lesson drilled into every Temple child. His voice cracked on the last word.

Kael Drenn detached from the shadows.

“The Bomb was mercy.” He flicked a wrist through the hologram. Stars stretched; violet threads reached toward the Core like cracks in transparisteel. “Your map lies, Varik. Jedi telemetry ignores draw-weight.”

“Brotherhood succeeded,” Kael said, almost cheerful. “Veranthos wasn’t failure—it was collapse. Jedi slapped a lid on a smoking crater and called it victory.”

Ben’s stomach lurched. Dates slid. Fixed points turned liquid. He could feel his father’s voice in his head—*The Reformations were our finest hour, son. We chose service over sovereignty.* The words crumbled like dried leaves.

“Then the Reformations—” He swallowed hard. “My father said they chose peace. Laid down crowns to serve.”

Torin knelt at a stone plinth. The lid recognized his palm-print, hissed open. Inside, a Codex shard lay wrapped in dull lead-foil. Silence poured from it—not calm, absence. The kind of quiet that made Ben’s ears ring.

“Reformations weren’t choice,” Torin said. His voice had gone gentle, the way Ben’s mother got when delivering bad news to families.

He lifted the shard. Shadows drank the room’s glow.

Sera’s fingers drummed against her thigh—three quick taps, stop, three more.

“Lattice tore. Force leaked, unraveling physics. Stars drifted. People screamed without sound.” She paused. “Council authorized the Silence.”

Ben’s throat worked, trying to find moisture that wasn’t there.

“Which was?”

“Lobotomy,” Torin answered, voice soft as old blood. “We didn’t fight darkness with light. We carved the Force out. Left vacuum so absolute not even Sith could breathe.”

Desh barked a laugh that echoed wrong in the archive’s acoustics.

“You switched off the energy field binding the galaxy because someone got noisy?”

Sera’s glare could have cut armor.

“Alternative was dissolution.”

Ben felt archives shift under his feet. Temple floors turned to sand. The room tilted slightly. He realized he was leaning harder against the console.

“They buried it,” he said. “Reformed. Renamed. Forgot. Turned atrocity into humility. They lied from my first lesson.”

“They had to,” Torin said. “Truth was poison. Amnesia became foundation.” His eyes found Ben’s—paternal, understanding—and it made Ben want to put his fist through something.

The violet patch pulsed faster. Heartbeat of something waking.

Ben watched his own face reflected in the hologram, distorted by the wound’s light.

“Cover-up,” Ben whispered. His voice cracked. “Seal’s failing.”

Sera returned to the console. Pallor made her freckles stand like ash on snow. Her hands moved with the precision of someone dismantling a bomb.

“Hole that size needs anchors.”

She keyed. Galaxy folded into a ring schematic—twelve nodes around Ruusan Corridor.

Kael tapped amber icons.

“Can’t bolt reality with droids. Need living rebar.” He gestured to the display. “Jedi Lords took twelve—strongest from both armies—wired them into structure.”

Display flickered. Dying lumas casting last light.

Torin counted dead nodes.

“Nine burned out in the dark. Galaxy forgot their names.”

Three icons throbbed, weak but steady.

Ben looked from Sera to Torin to Kael. Weight built in his chest like a scream he couldn’t voice.

Desh pointed at the twelfth—jagged red, crawling across sectors, trail of unraveling space behind it.

“Breach didn’t start because machinery broke,” Sera said, finger shaking against the glass. “She woke first.”
