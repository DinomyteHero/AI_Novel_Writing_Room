The ice blurred the markings.

Ben knelt on the frozen surface, breath fogging the air. The ruins here pushed up through permafrost instead of desert sand—different climate, different geology, different everything except the carvings.

Same hand. Same circles. Same jagged slash through them.

His glove followed the grooves. Cold should’ve numbed his fingers, but the resonance came first—that familiar vibration, like stone recalling hands that had touched it centuries ago. The weight pushed back, patient and waiting behind a door he couldn’t see.

He measured the distance to the ice ridge: forty meters, maybe forty-five. His Force-sense insisted it was close enough to touch.

The parallax hit like vertigo. Two maps of the same space, misaligned and bleeding together. He yanked his hand back. The ridge snapped into place. His stomach lurched with it.

"Found something?" Desh’s voice cut through the wind.

"Same markings." Ben stood, knees cracking against the cold. "Same everything."

Desh crunched closer, stopping beside the pillar with that familiar stance—the one that meant he was running threat assessments and exit routes in his head.

"Same carvings. Different rock. Different planet."

"That’s the point."

Desh’s jaw clenched. "You saying someone’s been tagging worlds like a graffiti artist?"

"I’m saying it’s not random."

"Orange fungus in winter doesn’t happen by accident either." Desh scanned the ridge, then the frozen plain. "Temperature’s wrong. Sound’s wrong. Gravity’s wobbling like it’s drunk."

Ben followed his gaze. The growths clustered across the rocks like autumn had lost its way. Out of sync. Like the seasons forgot their place.

"Back to the ship."

"Copy that." Desh was already moving, hand hovering near his holster despite the empty landscape. "This place gives me the same itch I got before Christophsis. Felt wrong then too."

They moved fast across unstable ground, boots finding grip where there shouldn’t be any. The Steady Returns nestled in a shallow depression—sensor shadow, Desh had called it, the only flat spot that hadn’t registered as a gravity anomaly.

The ramp sealed with a hiss that echoed in the sudden quiet. Warm air replaced the tundra’s bite, but Ben’s shoulders stayed locked. Desh peeled off his outer layer heading for the cockpit, movements sharp with unspoken tension.

"Pre-flight’s running." Desh’s fingers flew across the console. "Atmospheric readings are garbage. Gravimetric variance tripled since the first site."

"Stronger here."

"No kidding." Desh pulled back on the throttle. The ship lifted through thin atmosphere. "Most survey missions catalog rocks and call it a day. Maybe find some pottery. Very civilized. Very dull."

"Disappointed?"

"Starting to think you’re bad luck, Skywalker."

The joke landed flat, but it was a joke. That counted.

Ben dropped into the co-pilot’s seat and pulled up the nav holotable. Blue light carved shadows across the cockpit. He loaded both sets of coordinates, let the system overlay them.

Two points. Two worlds separated by parsecs of nothing. Between them, only the bearing he’d chased for months.

Desh finished orbital calculations and turned. "What are we looking at?"

"Two sites. Same markings. Same wrongness." Ben highlighted the first coordinate. "WR-7741." The second. "Here."

"The bearing between them."

Ben traced the vector line. "Matches the directional pull I’ve been tracking."

"Two points make a line." Desh leaned closer. "But they don’t make a pattern. Not yet."

"Every Jedi outpost I’ve studied follows the same logic—position, supply lines, communication relays." Ben’s finger traced the map. "These two break that logic. They’re not defensive. They’re not logistic."

"They’re in the middle of nowhere."

"By design." The words came out sharper than he meant. "Someone hid these. Put them where no one would look."

Desh pulled up the historical archive overlay. Old Republic bases dotted the Mid Rim and Inner Territories in familiar patterns—forward posts, signal towers, garrison worlds. A defensive web.

"Neither site connects to any of this." Desh circled the anomalous coordinates. "They’re off the grid entirely."

Ben’s hand hovered over the console. He pulled up the survey route file Luke had given him before departure—seventeen worlds, eight weeks, a bearing into the Outer Rim. He’d thought it was routine reconnaissance.

He overlaid it on the holotable.

The line between the sites ran exactly along Luke’s route.

Ben stared at the display. His uncle’s careful planning. The specific coordinates. The particular worlds chosen from all the anomalous reports. It wasn’t a survey. It was a map.

Desh watched him. The pilot missed nothing.

"You okay?"

"Fine." The word came out too fast. Ben closed the route file and extended the bearing forward past their position. The vector cut through the Outer Rim’s edge and kept going.

Into black chart-space.

No hyperspace lanes. No recorded systems. No navigational data. Just empty void where stars should’ve been charted.

Desh exhaled. "That’s not on any chart I’ve ever seen."

"No."

"Someone erased it."

"Or it was never mapped." Ben stared at the void. The wrongness hummed at the edge of his awareness, patient and directional, pulling toward that empty space like a compass finding north. "Or someone made sure it stayed blank."

The bearing pointed toward a cluster of systems that didn’t exist on any navigation chart. Ben reached for the nav computer.

Coordinates appeared—numbers stretching into uncharted space, past the Republic’s mapped boundaries, into the dark where the wrongness waited.

Desh watched him enter the final digits. "What’s out there?"

Ben’s finger hovered over execute. The nav computer blinked, waiting for confirmation. No hyperspace lanes. No safety net. No way to know what they’d find.

He pressed it.

The Steady Returns shuddered as the hyperdrive engaged. Stars streaked into blinding lines outside the viewport.

Something waited out there.
