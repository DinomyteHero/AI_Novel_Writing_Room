The training blade struck Ben square across the shoulder. A sharp crack vibrated down his arm. He stumbled back two steps, boots squealing against the mat. Third time this week.

His partner's practice saber dropped to standby. The salle echoed with nothing but his own breathing.

"That's it?" Mika's voice carried the kind of disappointment reserved for senior Knights dealing with prodigies who'd lost their shine. "We're done?"

Ben rolled his shoulder, testing the bruise already blooming beneath his tunic. "Long day."

"Long week, you mean." Mika deactivated her blade with a theatrical snap-hiss that made Ben's teeth ache. "Look, if something's throwing off your timing—"

"I'm fine." The words came out sharper than intended. Skywalker perfectionism at work—admit weakness, invite questions. Questions led to conversations he couldn't afford.

Mika studied him for a long moment, weighing the cost of pushing. Whatever she saw made her shrug. "Your funeral, Skywalker."

She left him alone with the salle's empty mirrors and his reflection—a Jedi Knight who should have been fast enough to block a basic training strike. The wrongness hummed beneath his thoughts like a note played just flat enough to make his jaw clench.

Ben ignited his practice blade. The green glow painted stripes across the floor. Form III, the defensive foundation every Jedi learned. He moved through the opening sequence without the remote, muscle memory carrying him through blocks and pivots. The blade felt wrong in his hands—too light, too slow, like moving through thick water while the rest of the galaxy ran at normal speed.

He switched to Form V, his mother's preferred style. Practical. Efficient. No wasted motion. The blade carved precise arcs through air that tasted metallic with sweat. Still wrong. Still that fraction-of-a-second delay between intention and action, like the Force itself had developed lag.

The remote sat deactivated in the corner, its photoreceptor dark. Ben stared at it, weighing the cost of another round versus the humiliation of another failure. His reflection stared back from the mirror wall—same sharp features, same Skywalker eyes that made people expect impossible things. Same shoulders that carried bruises from training blades because he'd lost his edge somewhere between Abeloth's death and trying to pretend the galaxy had healed.

He deactivated the saber. The silence felt accusatory.

Ben walked the perimeter of the salle, counting steps. Twenty-three meters lengthwise, eighteen across. He'd measured it before, back when he'd first made Knight rank and thought proving himself meant training harder than everyone else. Back when the Force had sung instead of wheezing like a broken moisture vaporator.

His boot caught a seam in the mat. He stumbled, caught himself on pure reflex, and felt his face flush hot. Graceless. That was new. The wrongness wasn't just in his combat timing anymore—it was bleeding into basic coordination.

The door chimed.

Ben straightened automatically, shoulders squaring into parade rest before he recognized the familiar presence on the other side. Great. Perfect timing for a paternal check-in. Nothing said "I've got everything under control" like lurking in empty training rooms at twenty-three-hundred hours.

Luke Skywalker stepped through, datapad in hand and that particular expression that meant he'd found something interesting and potentially suicidal. The one that usually preceded Ben getting volunteered for missions involving hostile environments, political disasters, or both.

"Busy?" Luke asked, though his tone suggested he already knew the answer.

Ben glanced at the deactivated remote, the empty salle, the practice blade hanging at his belt. "Living the dream."

Luke's mouth twitched—almost a smile, almost sympathy. "We've got a situation."

The wrongness in the Force pulsed, sharp and familiar as a toothache. Ben felt his shoulders relax for the first time all week. Finally, something he could hit.
