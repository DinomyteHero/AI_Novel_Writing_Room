The hangar stank of engine wash and cold durasteel, Ben the only soul inside.

His fingers traced the *Clarity*’s hull—a Defender-class light corvette, decades past its prime—thumb skimming seams. Not because he expected flaws. Because his hands had to move while his eyes kept drifting to the datapad tucked under his left arm.

He crouched to inspect the portside landing strut. Solid. Moved to the fuel coupling seal. Also solid. Stopped at the boarding ramp and finally looked at the screen.

Three names glowed back at him. He’d pulled their files before leaving Luke’s office—all solid, all available, all the kind of reliable that looked good on paper. He read the first. Then the second.

His thumb skipped past all three and opened his personal contacts instead.

"Practical choice," he muttered, boarding. "Outer Rim run. Off the charts. Not a Jedi problem."

The excuse left a copper tang on his tongue.

He sealed the ramp behind him and dropped into the pilot’s seat. The cockpit enclosed him—familiar, snug, his—and he powered up the comm board.

"Rolan."

Tinny. Flat. Ben had once watched Desh deliver that same greeting while bleeding from a gash above his ear in a Coruscant underworld corridor, so the tone told him nothing.

"It’s Ben. I’ve got a job."

"Of course you do." A pause. Glass clinked. "Unknown Regions?"

"How’d you—"

"Because you only call me when it’s either the Unknown Regions or you need someone to lie to a customs official. And you sound too calm for customs."

Ben’s fingers tapped the flight controls. "Six weeks. Maybe eight. I’ll cover the kit."

"Coordinates?"

"I’ll send them when you’re aboard."

The line hissed through three relay stations. Somewhere on Nar Shaddaa, Desh was probably staring at a wall the way Ben stared at his viewport.

"You’re not bringing a Jedi."

Ben’s hand froze over the controls. The silence answered for him.

"Right." Desh exhaled. "Temple docks. Four hours. Don’t leave without me."

The line went dead.

Ben exhaled, spine settling into the seat. His shoulders dropped a full inch before he caught himself. He reached for the pre-launch sequence, fingers moving by muscle memory.

Four hours to file the departure notice. Four hours to avoid the Temple’s other Knights like they carried something catching.

He filed the notice. Skipped the food.

---

Desh arrived at three fifty-two with a duffel and a thermos that smelled like caf laced with engine degreaser.

"You look like hell," he said, dropping into the co-pilot’s seat.

"Flattery will get you everywhere." Ben released the docking clamps. "Strap in."

The *Clarity* lifted clean, rotating on its axis. Coruscant’s light grid sprawled around them—traffic lanes stacked a hundred levels deep, each level a different flavor of desperation. Ben slipped between a bulk freighter and a diplomatic shuttle without checking the proximity alert.

"So." Desh swirled his drink. "What kind of nightmare are we walking into?"

"Unknown Regions. Possible Force anomaly. Could be nothing."

"But isn’t."

"Probably isn’t." The sky shifted from orange-gray to violet to black in under a minute. "The Council thinks it’s residual post-Abeloth interference."

Desh barked a laugh. "The Council thinks a lot of things. What do you think?"

Ben’s grip tightened on the controls. "I’ve felt post-Abeloth interference. This is different."

"Different how?"

"Like a scar versus a drain."

Desh considered it, sipping his toxic brew. "Still don’t know what that means."

"Neither do I yet." Ben entered the coordinates. "That’s the job."

"Right." Desh leaned back. "Wake me if we’re about to die."

"Standard protocol."

The navicomputer chimed ready. Ben wrapped his hand around the hyperdrive lever—cool metal, familiar resistance—and pulled.

The stars stretched.

The *Clarity* punched through.

Hyperspace settled into its steady blue-white tunnel. Instruments hummed. Structural vibration dropped to cruise frequency.

Everything normal.

Except.

Ben’s hand stayed on the lever. The wrongness didn’t fade—it sharpened, like a knife catching light at the perfect angle. Southwest through the tunnel, pulling like a magnet aligning.

His breath turned shallow. Beside him, Desh’s breathing had already steadied, oblivious.

He’d thought he was choosing this mission. Running toward the anomaly on his terms, controlled variables, trusted partner.

The pull strengthened southwest, steady and inexplicable as gravity.

Ben stared at the viewport and realized he’d just volunteered to chase something he didn’t understand to a place he couldn’t name, with no idea what waited at the other end of that invisible thread.

The wrongness had direction now.

And it was pointing straight at him.
