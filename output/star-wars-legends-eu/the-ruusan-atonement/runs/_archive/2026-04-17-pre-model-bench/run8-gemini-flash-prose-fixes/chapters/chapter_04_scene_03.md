The lift groaned, metal shrieking against metal until the doors parted on a smell that clawed at the back of Ben’s throat—coolant gone sour, air scrubbed so hard it tasted thin.

Level 3 opened into a ring. Twelve chambers stood shoulder-to-shoulder around a central drop, their viewports blind with frost. A dying blue pulse traveled the floor, slow as a heartbeat bleeding out. The vibration settled into Ben’s bones and wouldn’t leave.

Desh’s glow-rod cracked the dark. “Capacitors are screaming. You feel that?”

Ben was already moving. One stride, two, then his glove scraped frost away. A man floated inside—tunic of the old wars, skin the waxy gray of drowned parchment. Ben reached for the spark that should have danced inside the shell. Nothing answered.

“Dead,” he said and walked on.

He counted aloud, voice flat. “Three. Four. Five.” Each chamber offered the same exhibit: corpse as power cell, battery drained. Jedi Lords had wired living Force into bedrock and left the husks to cool.

“Six. Seven. Eight.”

At the ninth chamber the blue light steadied and the hum rose to a clean, level note. Ben wiped the glass. A woman hung motionless, face intact, the Force around her sealed and pressurized—discipline honed to a blade.

“Live one,” he called.

Desh jogged over, boots clanging. “Cryo at ninety-eight percent. Hardware’s a thousand years old and still topping itself off.” He whistled. “Like finding a warm speeder on an empty moon.”

Ben looked ahead. Two more chambers burned steady. He felt them now—three rhythms pressing against the hush. One slow as tectonic rock, one fluttering like a caged thing beating its own skull against the bars.

“Three survivors,” he said. “Three anchors still holding.”

“And a load of dead weight,” Desh answered. He started toward the last tank, number twelve, set hard against the shaft. “If this circuit was balanced—”

He stopped. The glow-rod froze with him.

Ben crossed the floor in four strides.

Twelve hadn’t failed; it had exploded outward. Transparisteel teeth littered the deck. The frame bowed as if a charge had detonated from the inside, yet no scorch, no soot.

Desh crouched, light spearing the metal. Gouges ran in parallel—claw-wide, systematic—something had gripped the interior and pulled until the alloy tore.

“They didn’t break in,” Ben said.

“Something broke out,” Desh said. He snapped a cable into the port at the base. Red digits spilled across his pad. “Pressure spike inside first. Lattice flagged breach after breach—sent a hundred alerts nowhere.”

“Timestamp,” Ben said.

“Sixty-three days ago.”

The temperature in the room dropped inside his skin. Two months. Whatever had been caged here had walked free long enough to learn every corridor of the fortress.

“Veraine,” he whispered. The name from the archive finally had a door kicked open in its honor.

Power conduits still glowed, drinking from the three living chambers and diving into the central pit.

“Load’s being balanced,” Ben said. “Someone’s still steering.”

“The warden,” Desh answered.

They abandoned the lift. Ben swung over the rail, let the Force braid under his boots, and sank into the dark throat of the shaft. Desh followed, boots skimming ladder rungs, the metallic clatter chasing Ben down.

Level 4 pressed in—low ceiling, close walls, a single seat swamped by cables that dripped from overhead like vines seeking an anchor. In that seat a man waited, silver hair neat, eyes shut, hands fused to the controls. Peaceful. Preserved. Finished.

Ben’s own pulse sounded loud in the hush. He stepped close enough to see the final log line blinking on the console, cursor winking like a metronome stuck on the last beat of a song.

*Anchor 12 lost. Lattice stability at 42%. I will hold the remaining three until—*

The cursor blinked on, refusing to admit the sentence was already over.

“Gavran,” Ben said. The name felt too small for what filled the room: duty that had kept breathing long after its owner could not.
