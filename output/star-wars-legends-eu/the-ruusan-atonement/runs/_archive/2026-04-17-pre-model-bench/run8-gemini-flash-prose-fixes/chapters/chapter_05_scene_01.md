Ben’s fingers closed around the lever. The metal clung to his glove, biting cold. He could walk away—leave them frozen, let the fortress keep its secrets. Instead, he yanked it down.

The lever groaned. A hiss tore through the chamber like a dying breath. Blue light fractured into static, skittering across Ben’s skin, crackling in his ears.

Sera Varik launched herself outward.

She landed in a crouch, one hand already groping for a weapon that wasn’t there. Bloodshot, wild eyes locked onto Ben. “Identification. Name, rank, sector-lord.” The words snapped out, syllables bitten off.

Desh’s blaster rose. “Easy, lady. You’ve been out awhile.”

Ben stepped between them, palms raised. No saber. Just the Force—he reached for calm, but her presence struck back: a blade of disciplined ice, nothing like the warm currents he knew. A weapon forged for an age that devoured Jedi.

“We’re here to help,” he said, voice steady. “Ben Skywalker. We found the fortress. We found you.”

Sera’s gaze flicked to the nine dark pods, then to the empty twelfth. “Skywalker? Unknown house. Where is Gavran? Where is the Pyre-Guard?”

Metal shrieked. The second pod hissed open, spilling Torin Hal. He caught the frame, fingers denting the alloy, and coughed hard enough to shake the chamber. Silver hair clung to his skull; his uniform hung in charred strips.

“Sera,” he rasped, words rolling like a royal decree despite his trembling knees. “Stand down. Look at them.”

She didn’t move. “They wear Order robes. Cut is wrong. Hangar ship is wrong. Everything is wrong.”

Torin straightened, still denting metal. His eyes—ancient, amused, dangerous—swept Ben from head to toe. Heat radiated off him, but beneath the welcome simmered something jagged, humming like broken machinery. “You carry light, boy. Thin, though. Like song through a wall. Who trained you?”

“My father. Luke Skywalker. Master of the New Jedi Order.”

Sera’s brow furrowed; Torin’s head tilted. The titles meant nothing. A thousand years had erased them.

The third pod exhaled open.

Kael Drenn stepped out, silent, back sliding along the curved wall. No stumble. No questions. Just eyes measuring Desh’s blaster, the ceiling seams, the lift shaft, Ben. His Force-signature was a knot pulled tight—survivor’s math, nothing wasted.

Ben’s throat tightened. He’d told the Council he could bridge eras. Right now, he felt twelve, standing in rubble he couldn’t decipher.

“The seal,” Sera said, voice cracking like overstressed cable. She advanced on Ben, hands trembling. “Gavran promised we’d hold until the wound closed. How long? How fares the Corridor?”

Desh’s datapad glowed amber. “Corridor’s failing,” Ben said. “That’s why we’re here. Seal’s at fourteen percent.”

“Fourteen?” Torin’s grip slipped an inch. “Anchors were built for decades. Even with casualties—” His gaze swept the nine dead pods; color drained from his face. “How many years since Ruusan?”

Ben hunted for a calendar they’d recognize. “A thousand years since the Battle of Ruusan. Republic fell. An Empire rose and fell. We rebuilt what we could.”

The words landed like a physical blow. Sera’s stance didn’t break—it dissolved. She slid down the pod, knees drawn, eyes fixed on nothing. “A thousand years?”

Torin’s hands turned over, as if expecting ash. “Everyone we knew. Every city. Every war we fought.” His voice cracked on the last word—grief disguised as inventory.

Kael spoke from the shadows, dry and soft. “Gone. We’re the epilogue no one read.”

Ben tasted metal. He’d come for tactics, coordinates, a way to save his own time. Instead, he’d dragged three people from their graves to tell them history had moved on without footnotes.

Responsibility settled, cold and absolute. No Council. No father. No Senate. Just him and three ghosts inside a dying fortress.

Torin rallied last. Shock hardened into tactical clarity. He shoved off the frame, limped past the nine coffins, and stopped before the twelfth.

Glass lay shattered outward. Alloy peeled back like wet film. Parallel gouges scored the wall—claw marks, systematic, alchemical.

Torin traced one groove, then looked at Ben.

“Who got out?”
