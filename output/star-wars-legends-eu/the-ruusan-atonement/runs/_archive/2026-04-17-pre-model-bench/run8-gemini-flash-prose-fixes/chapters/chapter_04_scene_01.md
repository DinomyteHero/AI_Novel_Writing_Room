The wind on the plateau didn’t howl—it bit. Crystalline grit scoured Ben’s face until his cheeks burned raw. Each breath seared his throat, leaving him lightheaded.

Desh climbed beside him—steady, unrelenting, breath snatched from his rebreather. No complaints. Just boots finding purchase on slate-gray shale.

"Up there." Desh’s voice cracked through the mask, finger jabbing at a spur of massif frozen mid-crash.

Ben squinted. At first, only stone. Then the shadows refused to shift. Straight edges. Right angles.

The mountain became architecture.

Closer, the illusion peeled away. Fortress and peak were one fused spine—no seam, no crest, no welcome. A thousand-year patina dulled the metal to the color of old blood. No Jedi sigils. No invitation. Just a block of silence hammered into the world.

"Defensible," Desh muttered, hand resting on his holster. "Clear kill-box. No cover. One gun on that ridge ends the conversation."

Ben’s teeth vibrated as the wrongness seeped through stone. His skin prickled with the sensation of being watched by something that had waited centuries for company.

"No back door either." Desh kicked a shard over the drop. "Built to keep something in."

"Or to make sure it never had to leave." Ben’s voice came out thin. The air thickened, pressing against his lungs.

He stepped to the wall. A palm-sized dimple waited, wind-polished. No handle. No keypad.

He set his hand against the metal. Ice bit his skin. He reached for the calm Luke had taught him—open, radiant—and pushed.

Amber light flared around his fingers. One pulse. Two. The door stayed shut.

The Force snapped back as if slapped.

Desh unclipped a detonator. "Stress fracture in the seam. I can drop the frame."

"No." Ben flexed tingling fingers. "Blow a Lords-era seal and the whole mountain might sit on us."

"Cold porch, then." Desh’s breath misted as he leaned against rock, watching.

Ben’s jaw ached from clenching. He was a Skywalker. He’d faced Abeloth. Fought Sith on every rim. A slab of antique alloy dared judge his worth?

He pressed again, harder. Amber answered, stubborn.

Desh scanned the ridge lines. "Clock’s ticking, Skywalker."

Ben shut his eyes. The records they’d found flashed—loss quotas, war-math. These builders hadn’t wanted serenity. They wanted obedience.

He stopped asking.

He dragged the full, unfiltered current of his bloodline up through bone and nerve—Anakin’s storm, Luke’s fire, his own spark—and slammed it into the lock.

Amber shattered. Colors whirled, searching. The mountain rang like a bell.

Green—harsh, targeting-laser green—locked on.

Counterweights the size of X-wings groaned. The seal retracted, grinding metal against metal until a throat of darkness yawned.

A breath of air rolled out—colder than the plateau, thick with the taste of centuries. Desh already had his glow-rod up, blaster ready.

Ben’s ears popped. The wrongness wasn’t a hum now. It was a weight pressing against his chest, measured and patient, as if the mountain itself inhaled.

Inside, the corridor waited, absolutely still.

Something inside exhaled back.
