Desh Rolan jammed the pry-bar into the seam of a floor-to-ceiling access panel and heaved. Metal screamed—oxidized durasteel shearing in protest. Two kicks sent the panel crashing forward. The echo rolled through the freezing vault like distant artillery.

"Old tech's like a drunk uncle," he muttered, peering into the dark cavity. "Won't listen till you shout."

He pulled a standard data coupler from his belt and forced the modern connector into a jagged port older than hyperdrive tech. Blue sparks snapped. The first kissed his glove. The second scorched stone and filled the air with melted insulation stink.

Red. Amber. Green. The coupler steadied. Somewhere under the floor, ancient machinery woke with a bass heartbeat that climbed Ben's boots.

Ben stepped back. Hand near his belt, but not on his saber. He shut his eyes and let the cold slide off him. The Force here felt like a holorecording played too many times—colors bleached, sound warped. He tasted dust and old sweat. The click of armored boots echoed where none should be. This bunker had never been a monastery. It had been a watchtower built to keep something in. Or out.

"I'm in," Desh said, voice flat as a combat report. "Talking to a ghost. File structure's pre-Ruusan—logic gates older than the Reformations."

The ceiling projector coughed. A jagged star map bloomed between them, constellations drifting a thousand years off true. Labels etched in High Galactic. Desh waved a hand through the light, fingers dancing like a sabacc dealer.

"Not a library," he said, tagging a ring of icons. "Look at the routing—encrypted handshakes, high-bandwidth, low-latency. Command-and-control node. They parked a fleet here."

"Army of Light," Ben murmured. The scrolling glyphs felt sharp against his eyes. "They were babysitting something else."

Desh yanked a fragmentary log through the coupler. Letters stuttered like a dying droid.

...CONTAINMENT INTEGRITY: NOMINAL...
...SEAL STABILITY: DEGRADING...
...WARNING: ANCHOR 04 OF 12 SILENT...

"Still running diagnostics," Desh said. The glow painted sweat on his upper lip. "Still screaming the fence is falling."

He hovered a thumb over the kill-switch. "We bounce a tight-beam, the Council lands in forty-eight hours. Your father sees this first."

Ben stared at SEAL FAILING until the letters blurred. He pictured the Council chamber: beads clicking, datapads sliding, Luke's General-face locking the mystery into a neat doctrinal box. Veraine already weeks ahead. Bureaucrats would chew those hours into minutes.

"No." The word cracked like a spine.

Desh's caution voice clicked in. "Galactic-scale alert, Ben. Two men with a flashlight."

"Protocol's for a galaxy that isn't bleeding out." Ben moved closer to the map. Boots crunched frost. "She reaches the door before a committee finishes breakfast. We jump now or we watch her open it."

Desh studied him, hunting for the reckless kid. Found only Skywalker calculus: risk weighed, bridge already torched.

"Your cremation," he said, and turned back to the console. He bypassed a corrupted sector. The cache dumped in a rush of light.

The hologram zoomed out hard enough to stagger them. One green dot shrank to nothing as the view swallowed the Darrpa Sector, then the Rim, then the Ruusan Corridor. Twelve new lights flared, snapping into a perfect ring around a black heart. Golden threads pulsed between them—once-strong, now strobing amber.

Ben's lungs forgot their rhythm. A perimeter the size of a sector, built when the Republic still used coin. Every thread flickering toward dark.

"A dam," he breathed. "They walled off the Corridor."

"And the mortar is turning to sand." Desh tapped the center coordinate resolving—a single blinking crimson mark. "Fortress commands the ring. That's where the heartbeat is."

Ben snapped his datapad into the secondary port. Fingers flew: copy route, copy logs, copy every screaming byte. His mind already in hyperspace, counting fuel cells and lies he'd have to tell his father.

"We're going."

"Long walk in the dark, Skywalker."

"Then we run."

Coordinates locked. Destination: uncharted void, no lane, no chart, no name. Something meant to stay lost.
