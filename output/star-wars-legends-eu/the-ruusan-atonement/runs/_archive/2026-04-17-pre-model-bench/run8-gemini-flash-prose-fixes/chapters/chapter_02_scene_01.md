The *Steady Returns* didn’t just drop from hyperspace—it stumbled.

Starlines snapped to starpoints with a shudder that rattled bolts loose. Ben’s caf mug launched from its holder, spun a perfect half-twist, and splashed across the copilot console and his thigh.

"Son of a—" The harness punched the breath from his lungs.

"Static spike on the motivator," Desh said, fingers flying over the yoke. "Or the old girl recognized who’s at the controls and tried to murder us."

Ben wiped the console with his sleeve. Burnt coffee mingled with the freighter’s permanent metallic tang.

"You’re paying for the cleanup," he muttered, scraping grounds from a seam.

"Scanners were already lying. I’m doing them a favor." Desh stomped a loose deck plate back into place. "Use a lid next time, Skywalker. No temple droids out here."

Ben glanced over. In the Force, Desh was a vacuum—no pulse, no echo, no fracture. Just a man with a wrench and a hatred of authority that smelled like honest labor.

"Don’t miss the droids," Ben said. "Miss gravity that can’t make up its mind."

"You picked the Rim. Gravity’s a fairy tale out here, same as honest politicians."

Desh’s gloved fingers danced over the nav keys—clack, clack, pause—banter as armor against the silence growing between them.

"Remember that sweep by the belt?" he asked. "Buoy thought it was a detonator."

Ben’s spine locked. "Black kits. Extra pockets, zero oversight."

Neither said *Guard*. Neither said *Jacen*. The words stayed sheathed, a shared blade they’d agreed never to draw, but the weight of it settled in Ben’s chest anyway.

He stared at the stars, thumb tracing the armrest’s edge. "We were younger. And drier."

"Speak for yourself. I was soaked after your ice-nevel piloting."

Desh let the joke die. "Recal’s hanging. Look."

The sensor feed stuttered—ghost mass, vanishing wells. A dotted line of phantoms pointing dead ahead.

"It’s matching the bearing," Ben murmured.

"The what?"

"The thing we’re chasing."

Desh exhaled through his teeth. "Computer calls it instrument error. Space-time hiccups it can’t categorize, so it pretends they don’t exist."

"Instruments aren’t broken. They just don’t know how to lie to themselves."

"Great. Certified nightmare." Desh’s knuckles whitened on the yoke. "If the nav can’t lock, we smear across a parsec."

"We won’t smear."

The nav chimed. A grey-green sphere blinked onto the scope—first survey world, Darrpa Sector.

Wrongness punched through the hull like silent decompression. Ben’s vision tunneled; his fingers fused to the armrest as the thing they’d hunted finally had a shape he could point to and say *there*.
