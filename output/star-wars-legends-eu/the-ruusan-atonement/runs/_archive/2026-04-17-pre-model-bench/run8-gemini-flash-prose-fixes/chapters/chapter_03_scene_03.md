The haptic delete glyph pulsed. Ben Skywalker stared at the cursor devouring his third attempt at a report to Luke. The screen reflected his face—hollow, pale under emergency red.

*Master Skywalker. Father.*

None of it fit. The words clung like a flight suit two sizes too small. He couldn’t squeeze the Ruusan Corridor’s screaming urgency into bullet points.

“You’re not sending that.”

Desh Rolan leaned over the copilot headrest, his shadow sliding across the nav console. Recycled air and scorched thrusters clung to his clothes. His gaze—cold, assessing—scanned the empty text field.

Ben eased back; the chair hissed. “The Council takes three weeks to verify a sunrise. Flag this Grade-One and they’ll convene a subcommittee. Want sensor logs, Temple archives, a second opinion—”

“While the seal drops to twelve percent,” Desh finished. Not an argument—logistics.

“Exactly.” Ben scrubbed his jaw, stubble scraping. “Veraine has sixty days on us. If I wait for the Bureau to process it, we’re writing the post-mortem.”

True enough. Beneath the math, another pulse: the old Skywalker instinct that said only he saw the pattern. Handle it alone and he kept the controls. Bring in the Order and he became another cog in a machine that had already lost the galaxy’s pulse.

Desh remained standing. He reached past Ben, fingers flying over the nav-computer’s keys—not the comms, but the fortress coordinates. The unit whined, processors straining, cooling fans shrieking. Mapping uncharted space was sprinting blind through a room of vibroblades.

“Void without a chart,” Desh muttered. “Makes the paperwork entertaining.”

Ben watched numbers scroll. “We’re tailing a ghost.”

“We’ve done it before.”

Silence settled, thick with memory: black-ops sweeps, off-books extractions, the understanding that law could delay rescue. They’d answered to Jacen then. Now Ben answered to his own shadow.

Desh adjusted the inertial compensator. “No committee for ‘urgent’ in the old days. We just went. Usually ended in fire, but we went.”

Heat burned up Ben’s neck. “This isn’t that. It’s containment. Tactical.”

“Sure.” Desh slapped the final vector. Amber shifted to steady green. A jagged line pointed into the Darrpa Sector’s dead zone.

Ben reached for the Force, hunting the familiar warm thread. Instead, he got a throb behind his eyes, a note a half-step flat. Wrongness, close enough to taste copper.

“Route locked.” Desh’s hand hovered over the hyperdrive lever. He let the silence stretch until the life-support hum felt deafening, then turned. His stare cut through Knight’s bearing straight to the Skywalker underneath. “You’re not reporting this.”

Border crossing. No going back.

Ben glanced at the comm, then at the nav line. He saw Luke’s General-face: safety first, study the survivors, meditate while the galaxy bled. “Not yet.”

The words lifted a weight he hadn’t realized he carried. Cold clarity took its place. His choice. His burden.

Desh nodded—recognition between conspirators. He yanked the lever.

The *Steady Returns* shuddered. Stars didn’t blur; they splintered, white spears snapping apart before the mottled blue tunnel swallowed them.

Hyperspace should have muffled the disturbance. It didn’t. The wrongness spiked, deck plates seeming to ripple like water, violet bruises swirling through the blue. They weren’t escaping; every second dragged them deeper into the thing’s throat.

Ben sat rigid, pulse hammering. The Force felt like a roomful of voices screaming in different tongues. They were heading straight for the source.
