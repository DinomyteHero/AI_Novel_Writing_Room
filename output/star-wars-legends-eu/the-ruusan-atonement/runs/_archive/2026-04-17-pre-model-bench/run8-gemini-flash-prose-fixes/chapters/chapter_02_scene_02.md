The wind on WR-7741 carried the taste of pulverized silt and ancient, exhausted heat. It whistled through gaps in the jagged basalt outcroppings—a thin, mourning sound that never settled into rhythm.

Ben Skywalker checked his datapad again, squinting against the glare of a sun too close to the horizon. "Official Republic Survey, forty-four years ago," he rasped, the words scraping his dust-coated throat. "Record status: Uninhabited. Geologically stable. Zero archaeological signatures."

Desh Rolan’s boots crunched on shifting gravel as he hauled himself up the ridge’s last meters. He paused, hands on knees, breathing hard through his nose. One swipe across his forehead left a brown smear of grit. "Then somebody’s been busy with the stone-shapers since then. Or the surveyor was drunk."

Below them, nestled in a natural bowl of orange-stained rock, sprawled a half-buried complex of stone. Not a city. Not an outpost—something in between. Monolithic slabs driven into the earth at aggressive angles. Wind had softened their edges, but not their weight.

"Jedi records match the Republic’s," Ben murmured, starting down the slope. "Nothing in the High Republic archives. Nothing from the Sith Wars. This planet’s supposed to be a blank spot."

"Maps lie, Ben. Usually because the cartographer wanted to go home." Desh unclipped his blaster’s safety, eyes scanning the shadows beneath the pillars. "Stone doesn’t. Look at the weathering on those base layers. That’s not decades. That’s millennia."

They reached the perimeter. The air thickened, like the moment before lightning strikes—every hair standing, every instinct screaming you can’t outrun what’s coming. Ben walked to the nearest pillar. Dark igneous rock flecked with minerals that caught the dying light like embers. He brushed away fine sand, revealing tool marks. Not laser-chisel precision. Not primitive masonry’s rough bite. The stone looked *persuaded*—edges microscopically smooth, almost fused.

"Not Jedi Lords," Ben said, tracing the seam. "They liked grandeur. This is… functional. Like a machine carved from mountains."

Desh stayed five meters back, shoulders squared to the ruin instead of Ben. "I don’t like the acoustics. Sounds don’t bounce right. I hear my own heartbeat. Not yours."

Ben closed his eyes and reached. Ancient sites usually hummed like faded holograms—echoes of lives, residual warmth or cold. Here, only a hollow resonance. A frequency just below hearing that rattled his teeth without sound.

"It’s a thin place," he whispered.

"Speak Basic, Skywalker."

"The Force is stretched. Like fabric wearing thin."

"Great. Another Outer Rim gem." Desh’s gaze flicked to a deep, squared-off archway. "Your dad going to want this in a museum or buried under a kilometer of durasteel?"

"Standard protocol first," Ben said, pulling the high-resolution scanner. "Catalog. Then hypothesize."

He moved deeper, toward a central slab squatting like an altar—or capstone—in the ruin’s heart. Its shadow stretched too long, pointing back at the ridge they’d crossed. As Ben approached, pressure clamped his chest. His inner ear stuttered. The world tilted a degree off true.

On the slab’s face, someone had carved a knot of interlocking circles stabbed by a jagged vertical line. Seal or warning—maybe both. Ben crouched, leveled the scanner. The device whined, screen jittering with static.

"Giving you trouble?" Desh asked. He’d followed after all, blaster holstered but hand resting.

"Sensors are jumping. Stone reads like it’s in two places at once."

"Maybe it’s shy." The joke died halfway, Desh’s jaw clenched white. "Ben, look at the sun. Hour of light left. We shoot the pictures and move. This place feels like it’s waiting for us to stop."

Ben didn’t answer. The jagged line pulled him, shadows pooling in its groove. The wrongness he’d chased across the sector screamed here—silent but urgent.

He needed to know.

He reached out—not with the Force. With his hand. Fingertips slid into cold stone. The horizon slammed into his face, then flung itself away. The slab became a shaft plunging planet-core deep. His Force-spatial map shattered and reassembled into nonsense. He clung to a needle in a void.

Air turned to vacuum against his eardrums. He yanked his hand back and hit the ground hard, pulse hammering.

"Ben!" Desh’s blaster swept for targets. "Trip a charge?"

Ben’s voice came dry as dust. "Static discharge. Mineral buildup." He forced himself upright, legs unsteady, and locked the tremor deep inside.

Desh studied him—long enough for Ben to feel the weight of the assessment. "Static discharge doesn’t make a Jedi look like he saw his own ghost. We’re done, right?"

"Right." Ben retrieved the datapad. The scanner had captured the image before feedback fried calibration.

He stared at the screen. Flag it Class-One Force Anomaly. Request Master Tionne. Wait for committees, slow-motion bureaucracy that would turn this blade of fear into a research grant.

He opened the transmission packet, addressed to Luke Skywalker and the Council. Attached visuals, coordinates, survey discrepancy.

Cursor blinked in the *Force Impressions* field. He pictured his father’s General-face, the weeks of deliberation. They’d tell him to wait.

He typed: *Visual confirmation of unrecognized ruins. No active Force signatures detected. Ambient readings consistent with standard Outer Rim background radiation. Moving to next survey point.*

Send. Progress bar sprinted to one hundred percent. The lie leapt into the relay net toward Coruscant.
