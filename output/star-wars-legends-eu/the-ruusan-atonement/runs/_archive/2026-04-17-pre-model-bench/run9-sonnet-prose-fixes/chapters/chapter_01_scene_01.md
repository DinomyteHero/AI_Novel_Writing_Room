The training blade cracked across his shoulder hard enough to leave a welt beneath the vest.

Ben retreated a step. Third hit this week. Same half-second lag. The Force hiccupped—gear tooth skipping—and his guard arrived nowhere useful.

"Point," Tass announced, redundant.

Ben thumbed his saber off. The practice blade whined to silence.

Tass—four years older, shoulders like cargo containers—studied him the way medtechs check fuel lines: professional, expecting rupture.

"You’re protecting that side."

"Protecting my pride. Shoulder’s fine."

"Again?"

"Pass." Ben clipped the hilt to his belt, rolled the joint once to prove it worked. "I’m aging. Reflexes ossify. Tragic, really—once had potential."

"Twenty-three."

"Jedi geriatric case. We burn fast." He scooped his towel, pressed the cool cloth to his neck. "Go sleep, Tass. I’ll close up."

Tass hesitated. Swallowed the question. Gathered his gear and left.

The doors hissed shut.

Quiet.

Temple presence murmured around him—Padawans two floors up, a Knight drilling in Salle Three, maintenance droids humming through ducts. White-noise home. He knew every tremor the way a sailor knows hull plates.

Beneath it: the flat note.

Three weeks now. Private label. Not dark-side pressure—nothing that tidy. A single choir voice a hair off pitch, just enough to snag the ear. Most wouldn’t notice. Those who did could dismiss it as tinnitus of the soul.

Ben had tried. Dismissal hadn’t stuck.

He set the towel down. Ran Ataru openings—no blade, no Force—bone and tendon only. Ten thousand reps should autopilot.

They did. Crisp pivots. Balanced landings. Zero hesitation.

Soresu next. Djem So. A mash-up sequence he’d stitched together after the last war, designed to expose lazy footwork. Clean.

He stopped center-floor, sweat threading into his collar. Shoulder didn’t ache. Body obeyed. Training held.

He flattened his palm to the durasteel wall. Reached.

Not for data—just the room tone. Sleeping apprentices. A maintenance worker whose null signature floated like warm black space amid the glow. Familiar. Correct.

Except.

Under everything, a structural absence. Load-bearing silence where resonance should be. Not corruption—corruption has flavor. This was hollow core behind perfect paneling.

Briefings called it echo. Abeloth residue. Tribe fallout. Normalizing baseline. Masters he respected spoke of dissipating pressure. He’d kept quiet because what could he say?

*It’s not easing. It’s widening.*

*It’s in the framing, not the paint.*

Memory ambushed him: standing beside Jacen while the Force bent, feeding something that never fed back. This wasn’t that. But the DNA matched.

The Order filed it under background noise.

Ben lifted his hand.

Folded the towel. Exact corners.

Telling Dad meant slides. Charts. A task force. Follow-up meditations—process devouring urgency while the silence spread, joint by joint.

He’d seen files stamped. Archived. Forgotten.

The doors opened.

Luke stood there, datapad angled like a ticket to somewhere already decided. Not Council voice. Mission face.

Ben straightened. Let the brood slide off. Met his father’s eyes.
