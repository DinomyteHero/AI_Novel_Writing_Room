The hangar reeked of hot metal and spent fuel. Ben sat in the *Steady Returns*'s cockpit, staring at his datapad.

The Jedi Knight deployment roster glowed blue in the dim cabin. Three names near the top—all qualified, all available. Tass Merrik, six Outer Rim surveys logged, clean reports. Yela Cord, whose spatial Force-sense outshone Ben's on a bad day. Raan Yul, who asked no personal questions—one of the few things Ben liked about him.

His thumb hovered over Tass's name, then slid past all three as if burned.

He closed the contacts and set the pad face-down on the co-pilot seat. Fuel line pressure gauge instead.

Ninety-two percent. Fine. He logged it anyway.

Beyond the cockpit glass, the hangar stretched forty meters of polished duracrete. Three other ships in pre-flight chaos. A coolant cart rolled toward the far bay, pushed by two technicians. The Temple's mechanical breath—ventilation cycling with the slow rhythm of something vast and alive—moved through it all. He'd grown up with that sound. It had always meant safety.

Now it felt like surveillance.

*You need a mechanic*, he told himself, initiating the repulsorlift warm-up sequence. *Someone practical. Someone who can fix the ship three jumps past any yard. Someone who won't critique your navigation choices the entire trip.*

The repulsorlifts hummed through the deck plates. He pulled the hangar clearance request and sent it before the thought finished forming—before he could question whether it was true or just a convenient shape of truth.

Clearance arrived in fourteen seconds. Faster than usual. Even the hangar crew knew a Skywalker departure required little deliberation.

He dialed the long-range comm frequency from memory.

Static. Then metal clattering, and a voice.

"Rolan."

"It's Ben."

"I know your voice." A pause. The clatter resolved into the rhythmic turn of a socket wrench. "You sound like you're in a cockpit."

"Temple hangar."

"Hm." The wrench kept turning. "What'd you break?"

Ben's shoulders eased a fraction. "Outer Rim survey crew. One spot. Eight waypoints, twelve weeks. Standard pay, full clearance."

"What kind?"

"Anomalous Force readings. Bearing runs through rough space. No established lanes past the third waypoint."

The wrench stopped. Desh set a tool down. "You want a mechanic."

"Among other things."

"You've got Jedi."

Ben's grip tightened on the yoke. Voice steady. "I've got a survey route and a timeline."

Another pause. The coolant cart rolled past his ship's nose. Ben tracked it, resisting the urge to calculate Desh's response geometry—the shape of what he hadn't said.

"Terminus Station's two days out," Desh said. "You passing through?"

"I can."

"I'll have my kit packed." The wrench resumed. "Bring decent caf. Yours tastes like engine housing."

"That's because it was."

"Right." A beat. "See you in two days, Skywalker."

The comm clicked off.

Ben sat with the silence. The tension behind his sternum—since Luke's office, since the third Darrpa Sector report, since the private calculations at 0200—had eased, if only slightly. Not gone. Just... less.

Desh hadn't asked why Ben hadn't called a Jedi.

He never did.

Ben brought the repulsorlifts to full. The *Steady Returns* lifted off the deck.

The ship rose through the hangar's open roof into Coruscant's lower atmosphere. Below, the city sprawled—skylanes stacking in layers, running lights threading through smog like glowing veins. He climbed through traffic lanes, then merchant corridor. A freighter the size of a city block lumbered past starboard, hull close enough to read dock numbers on its loading bay doors.

He thought about Tass Merrik's name on that roster. Another Jedi in the co-pilot seat. Their Force-presence constant. Their interpretations layering over his until he couldn't separate his readings from theirs. He thought about how that had felt with Jacen, early on, when training together and Ben had mistaken his cousin's confidence for wisdom.

He stopped thinking about it.

Coruscant's upper atmosphere thinned. Gravity released its grip. Stars sharpened. The planet curved below, nightside lit like a circuit board. Ben set the navicomputer to the first waypoint—bearing refined over six weeks.

The calculation finished. He checked it. Checked again.

He pulled the hyperdrive lever.

Starlines.

The *Steady Returns* punched into hyperspace. The cockpit filled with the muted blue-white tunnel of faster-than-light travel—the quiet that followed a jump, the Force softening like a signal fading at range.

The instruments flickered.

Not a surge. Not a fault. Readouts dipped and recovered in under a second. The wrongness remained—the same pressure tracked for three months, the same absence-that-wasn't-absence—but now it persisted in hyperspace. It had no right to. It had a point of origin.

Ben's hands stilled on the yoke. The tunnel stretched ahead. Somewhere along his bearing—past the third waypoint, past established lanes, past every chart he'd pulled—the wrongness waited. Patient. Certain. Waiting a very long time.

He wasn't heading toward an anomaly.

He was heading toward a source.

The instruments steadied. The tunnel held. Ben stared at the navicomputer's readout. The bearing settled into him like a compass needle finding true north—not a guess, not a theory, not three months of data points that might mean something.

A direction. Specific. Cold. Unmistakable.

He reached for his pre-departure caf. Took a sip. Made a face. Desh was right. Tasted like engine housing.

He kept flying.
