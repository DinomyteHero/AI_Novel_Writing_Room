The datapad clicked against the desk, its cobalt glow crawling up Luke Skywalker’s face like frostfire.

"Seventeen reports," Luke said. "Eight months."

Ben lifted the device. Light leaked between his fingers as the Temple’s familiar rhythm—his father’s presence, warm as sun-baked durasteel—carried an undertone of decay he’d been tasting for weeks. Even here. Even in the Grand Master’s sanctum.

He kept his expression neutral and read.

Dry phrasing. Analyst’s stamp. The filing code that meant *nobody knows, so slap residual on it.* Seventeen missions, seventeen identical labels: *post-Abeloth disturbance.* Nobody had bothered to create a new category.

He pinched-zoomed three. Bearing data filled the screen.

"These three." He angled the display. "Same trajectory."

Luke dipped his chin—already logged, already mapped.

Ben scrolled through the remaining fourteen. A *routine* follow-up on a case that had stumped field teams for eight months. Right. In his experience, *routine* was just catastrophe wearing a polite face.

"Vector runs deep," he said. "Past the Rim."

"Unknown Regions." Luke leaned back, fingers relaxed. "Ankhural Codex coordinate. Archivists verified it last week."

Ben set the datapad down. The bitter tang flared—overripe citrus—then settled. He rode the breath through it.

"You feel it too." Not a question.

"I do." Luke’s voice stayed steady. "An imprint. Ancient. Dark—the kind that clings after the wound scabs over. Scar tissue. Ruusan, perhaps. Older."

Ben studied his father’s face.

Scar tissue. Imprint.

He thought of Jacen. Months when the Force had bent like refracted light around an uncharted singularity. Months when Abeloth’s white-noise shriek had leaked through every channel. He’d paid tuition in that academy—tuition no ledger recorded.

What thrummed now wasn’t an echo. Echoes faded. This one sharpened.

"It doesn’t read like scar tissue," he said. "Scar tissue is still flesh. This is—" He hunted the word. "Void. Striking a chord that should resonate and getting silence. Not dead air. Absence."

Luke watched him. Traffic hummed through the transparisteel, Coruscant’s endless breath.

"A void," Luke said.

"A hollow where something used to exist."

Luke turned the datapad once. "The Codex calls it *lattice extraction.*" He spoke as if sampling a spice with no name. "Theoretical. Pre-Reformation."

"But."

"But the coordinate kisses the vector." He placed the pad between them. "And residuals fade. These are intensifying."

Ben said nothing. Three nights ago, he’d plotted the curve himself; the line headed the wrong direction.

"I want you to follow the bearing," Luke said. "Eyes on the coordinate. Bring back something the Council can’t dismiss as *apocrypha and paperwork.*"

Something in the phrasing snagged. *Bring back.* Not *repair.* Not *resolve.* Just observe. Report. Language tailored to fit a frame.

Ben stood, moved to the window.

Coruscant sprawled in every direction, an ocean of luminescence that became sky when the city ended. He’d stood here as a boy—city endless; as an apprentice—city solvable; now as a Knight—city white noise to a rent in the Force.

The bitter trace lived here too. Thin. Persistent.

His father was handing him a real mission—not escort duty, not archive drudgery. A tear with a vector and a question mark at the far end. He would take it. Had always intended to.

He also knew Luke was nudging open the door he’d been pacing outside—offering distance masquerading as duty.

The shadow measured seventeen reports wide, eight months deep, and Ben’s boots stood within it.

He turned back to the room.

"Three days," he said. "Route and Codex mark."

"Already loaded." Luke’s tone held something parked between relief and unease. "The three bearing reports are flagged. Reference points along the way."

Ben returned, took the pad. Blue radiance washed his palm—files ordered, course plotted. His father had prepared this before stepping through the door.

Of course.

"Check-ins at Rim waypoints," Ben said.

"Good."

He tucked the datapad under his arm. Luke wore the expression that had concluded every departure—warm, measured, freighted with unsaid sentences.

Ben thought: *Dad.*

He asked, "Anything else?"

"Read the Codex passage before you lift."

"I will."

A final pause. Somewhere below, apprentices drilled, practice blades flicking light across a distant courtyard.

"May the Force be with you," Luke said.

"And with you."

Ben met his father’s gaze long enough to feel the weight of everything unspoken, then dropped his eyes to the pad.

Course: southwest. Seventeen markers. One apocryphal number.

No wingmate.

Luke didn’t ask.
