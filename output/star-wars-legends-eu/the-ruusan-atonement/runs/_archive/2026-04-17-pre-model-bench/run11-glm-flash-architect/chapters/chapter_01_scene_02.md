The datapad's blue glow cut a rectangle across Luke's desk. Outside the viewport, Coruscant's evening cycle had muted the Temple to amber standby, but the screen's light threw sharp edges against the holocomm, the stacked flimsi, the empty caf mug that had likely sat there since last shift.

Ben stopped at the threshold. No summons—just the wrongness clinging to him from the salle, trailing down three corridors until it pooled outside Luke's open door. An unspoken invitation. The Skywalker way.

"Seventeen." Luke didn't look up. "Same anomaly flag. Identical readings. Different sectors."

Ben crossed to the desk. His thumb found the datapad's seam, pressing until the plastic bit. Grounding. Habit. The pad weighed more than a few grams should.

He scrolled. The first six reports showed standard variances—statistical noise any long-range survey collected. Solar interference. Gravitational lensing corrupting data. Explanations that worked in isolation.

The seventh stopped him. Darrpa Sector. The same flat note he'd heard for three months—not in the data, but beneath it. A foundation crack visible only from the right angle.

"Seventeen," Ben said. "And the Council filed them under what heading?"

"Solar flare activity. Background echo residue." Luke's voice carried the flatness of too many committee meetings. "Thin places in the Force. Standard post-Abeloth artifacts."

"Standard." Ben scrolled faster. Eighth. Ninth. Each sharper, more pronounced. The data's jaggedness mirrored what he'd felt in the salle—that note played flat, that wall ready to collapse. "The Council wouldn't spot a Sith Lord sitting on the Chancellor's lap."

Silence. Luke's mouth twitched—not quite a smile, but its shadow.

"Your mother said something similar. Different chancellor."

Tenth. Eleventh. The Perlemian reach showed the pattern compressed—denser, tighter. Ion trails converging.

He set the datapad down. The casing cracked against wood.

"These aren't thin places." His voice dropped. The analytical part stopped processing data and started processing shape. "Thin places don't cluster. They don't form bearings. This—" He tapped the coordinate overlay. "This is a line. Something's pulling them."

Luke leaned back. Leather creaked. In the Force, his presence filled the room—vast, warm, a hearth-fire burned so long most Jedi stopped noticing. But tonight Ben caught the edges. Where the warmth stopped short.

"Eight coordinates," Luke said. "Standard survey route. Six to eight weeks, depending on early findings."

A mission. Of course. The datapad wasn't information—it was a briefing. Already decided.

Ben's thumb found the seam again. Pressed until skin blanched. "You're sending me alone."

Not a question. Luke didn't treat it as one.

"The Council's on the Korellian dispute. Corran's team is still in the Unknown Regions. The younger Knights don't have the field experience for deep Outer Rim work." A pause. "And you've been hearing it too."

The room went quiet. Not silent—the Temple hummed, recyclers cycled, traffic vibrated through permacrete—but quiet in the way spaces do when truth lands.

Ben studied his father. Really studied, for the first time since entering. Luke's face was older than years accounted for, carved with lines that had nothing to do with age. The Grand Master's mask was solid—most Jedi couldn't see the seams—but Ben had been reading that face his whole life.

"I feel it too." Luke stood. Circled the desk. Not close—never crowding—but present. "The wrongness. I've felt it since Abeloth. Maybe before."

Ben waited. The admission hung between them like held breath.

"It's a faint echo of old battles," Luke continued. "Residue that lingers after major conflicts. I've felt it after Endor, after the Vong War. The Force remembers. Those memories fade."

Old battles. Echoes. Residue.

The words settled in Ben's chest and curdled.

Luke was wrong.

Ben's shoulders tightened. The realization wasn't thunder—it was quieter. A door clicking shut. His father, who'd rebuilt the Order from ash, was reading the wrongness through history. What he felt and what Ben felt were the same note, but Luke heard memory. Something already happened. Something the galaxy would heal.

Ben heard something that hadn't happened yet. A foundation cracking. A wall about to give.

Because he'd felt this before. Not echoes. Not residue. The flat note, the structural wrongness—he'd felt it in the worst year of his life, standing in Jacen's shadow. Abeloth had torn holes. This wasn't either.

But it rhymed.

If he didn't go, no one would see it. Not the Council with their background noise. Not Luke with his old battles and healing galaxy. The Order would file reports, schedule reviews, and the wrongness would keep pulling.

Ben set the datapad on the desk. Deliberate. Final. Crossed to the viewport.

Coruscant spread below—millions of lives pressing into the Force, a galaxy's activity condensed into skyline. The wrongness was there too. Faint. A sourness under the harmonics, like a harmonic out of phase. The Temple's presence should have drowned it. Instead, the wrongness waited underneath, patient, for the harmony to thin.

"I'll take the *Steady Returns*." He kept his back to Luke. Voice level. Professional. The way you discussed mission parameters, not the way you told your father you were walking into something neither understood.

Behind him, Luke said nothing.

The silence stretched. Ben felt his father's presence—that vast, familiar warmth—and under it, something careful. Restrained. The particular quiet of a man who wanted to say more and knew better.

No partner offered. No Master assigned. No suggestion this was too big for one Knight, one ship, one Skywalker running a bearing into black.

Ben didn't ask.

The viewport showed Coruscant's lights, the Temple's reflection, and beyond both, the black where the Outer Rim began. The wrongness pulled toward that black like a compass finding north.

Luke still hadn't spoken. Neither had Ben.

The choice made itself in the space between them. Without words.
