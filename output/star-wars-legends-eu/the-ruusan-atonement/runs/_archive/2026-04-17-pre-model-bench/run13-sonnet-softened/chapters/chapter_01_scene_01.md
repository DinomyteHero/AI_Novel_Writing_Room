The training blade cracked across his shoulder. Third time.

Not pain. Not surprise. Just the tally. Third time this week the Force had skipped like a holorecorder with a scratched crystal, leaving him moving on old data.

Vesh lowered her weapon, breath steady.

“Third time this week.” She studied him the way Jedi studied things—no cruelty, no pity. “You left your right side open before I even committed. What happened?”

Ben rolled the joint, testing the sting. Training setting. No burn. Only the insult. “Edge is dulling. Twenty-three going on ninety. Tragic.”

“Ben.”

“I’m fine, Vesh.”

“You’re half a beat slow.” She stayed where she was, giving him space to lie. “Council chew you out yesterday? If Trebick—”

“Not Trebick.” He thumbed his saber off. The hum died. The Temple’s ordinary background noise rushed back in—ventilation, distant footwork, the living murmur of three thousand beings—like water filling a hole. “Late nights. Early drills. You know the drill.”

“I know the look of someone swallowing a problem whole.”

He snagged his towel, scrubbed the sweat from his neck. Recycled air carried the usual metallic bite—ten thousand people breathing the same ducts. Familiar. Usually grounding. Tonight it shrank the room.

“Session’s done.” He set the training saber on the bench, picked up the real one. “Tomorrow I’ll be sharp.”

She held his gaze a beat—long enough to confirm she didn’t buy it, short enough to prove she wouldn’t chase it—then gathered her gear and left.

The door hissed shut. Quiet settled. Hollow as an empty helmet.

Ben set the training saber on the bench. Picked up the real one.

Didn’t ignite. That wasn’t the test.

He snapped into Djem So’s opening guard and held it—hilt’s weight, soles against durasteel. Then he let the form run: guard, pivot, rising slash, redirect. No Force. Just muscle.

Clean. Every angle drilled ten thousand times landed exactly where it should. He pushed into the advanced flow, the one that required precognition to thread the tempo. Body kept up. Mechanics intact.

What failed was the whisper.

Usually, a hairsbreadth before an opponent twitched, the Force hummed a warning—thread pulled taut, giving him the beat he needed. Automatic as heartbeat. Three times this week the thread had gone slack. Not snapped. Just silent. He’d pivoted on stale intel, and Vesh’s blade had kissed him for it.

He slowed the kata to a crawl.

Body: fine. Training: fine.

The Force stuttered.

He froze mid-turn, hilt loose, and finally let himself name it. He’d been circling the bruise for months, afraid to press. Standing alone with no audience, he pressed.

The wrongness had started faint—a single note tuned flat beneath the galaxy’s chord. Easy to blame on fatigue, stress, leftover grime from things he didn’t discuss. He’d filed the report. Council logged it as echo residue: *You’ve seen a lot, Knight Skywalker. Give it time.*

He’d tried. The discord had thickened. Not louder. Not darker. Structural. A support beam quietly shearing.

He’d tasted that flavor twice before. Jacen at the end, when the space around him had stopped sounding right. Abeloth, when every frequency had rattled loose. Both times the damage had gone deeper than dark.

He wasn’t presenting that theory. He could already hear the gentleness in their replies—*Trauma echoes, natural association*—true enough to dismiss him. And they’d do it. Because dismissal was easier than admitting the Force might be cracked. And he the only one who could hear the leak.

He finished the final strike. Let the hilt rest against his thigh. Salle lights hummed. Someone three rooms away worked footwork drills—scuff, slide, scuff.

The wrongness waited beneath it all. Patient. Off-key. Recently he’d realized it pointed somewhere. A compass only he could read. He didn’t know the destination. Knew he’d have to follow. Alone—because Skywalkers handled their own messes. And because explaining consumed time he didn’t want to spend.

He exhaled, long and measured. Set the hilt down.

The door slid open.

Luke Skywalker filled the threshold, datapad in hand, posture all business. The look that had launched a thousand sorties.

Ben knew that look.

It meant wheels up.
