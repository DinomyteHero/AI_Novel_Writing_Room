The *Steady Returns* felt wrong in the way that normal now felt wrong—too much, too present, too loud. Sublight engines hummed at a frequency Ben had stopped hearing weeks ago, but after hours inside the node's dead air, the vibration pressed against his skin. He could smell the recycled atmosphere. He hadn't been able to smell anything in that bunker. His nose had simply stopped registering input, as if the thin place had decided olfaction was unnecessary.

He sat at the fold-down table in the crew compartment, datapad propped against a water container, and tried to write.

*Battlefield Node—confirmed structural decay in nexus infrastructure. Ancient installation, pre-Reformation. Significant—*

He deleted the last word.

*Significant* implied he understood the scale. He didn't. He understood pieces: the empty stasis chamber, the boot prints in thousand-year-old dust, the log entries with their clinical arithmetic about acceptable losses. He understood that the Jedi who built the Ruusan seal had done something the modern Order had no framework for and no interest in remembering. He did not understand what to do with that knowledge.

He started again.

*Anomalous energy signatures consistent with previous survey findings. Ancient infrastructure of unknown origin. Recommend continued assessment of Corridor nodes before escalating to Council review.*

He read it back. Every word was true. Every word was a door closed on what he'd left standing in that archive room.

The justification assembled itself without effort. That was the part that bothered him. He wasn't lying to Luke. He was managing information—giving his father time to prepare, preventing the kind of reactive Council response that had turned Jacen's early warnings into silence and then catastrophe. He was doing the responsible thing. This was how a seasoned Knight handled field conditions too complex for a summary comm.

He'd heard that logic before. He'd heard it from the wrong side.

Ben set the datapad face-down and pressed both palms flat against the table. The metal was warm from the ship's ambient heat. Solid. Real. He focused on the sensation until the echo of the archive's cold stopped living behind his sternum.

The problem wasn't that the report was incomplete. The problem was that he knew exactly what he was leaving out—the lattice inversion, the Jedi origin of the technique, the name *Veranthos*—and had chosen the omission. He was already building the architecture of reasons why that was fine. The Jedi Lords had done the same thing. They had filed it under *necessary sacrifice* and closed the drawer.

He picked the datapad back up. His thumb hovered over the send command. The cursor blinked. Once. Twice. He sent the report.

The confirmation code appeared almost instantly. Luke had been waiting.

***

"Got something." Desh's voice drifted from the cockpit. "Come look at this."

Ben folded the table up and moved forward. Desh had the nav-display pulled wide, the next node's approach data overlaid in amber against the star chart. He leaned back in the pilot's seat with his arms crossed, a posture that meant he'd already reached a conclusion and was waiting for Ben to catch up.

"Energy signature at the next stop," Desh said. "Different from the first node."

"Different how?"

"Cleaner. Concentrated." He tapped the display. "First node showed residual decay—old activity, weeks of slow bleed. This one has a pattern. Someone spent time there. They weren't just passing through."

Ben studied the readings. The Force-sensitive instruments the Temple had installed in the *Steady Returns* were civilian-grade, but they were picking up something the nav-computer flagged as interference. It wasn't interference. He knew what interference looked like.

"How recent?"

"Can't say exactly. But the signature density—" Desh pulled up a secondary overlay. "—it's an order of magnitude stronger than the first node. Whatever Veraine was doing there, she was doing a lot of it."

Ben looked at the flickering readout at the edge of the display. The Force ahead felt wrong in a way that was harder to categorize than the thin place—not offset, not displaced, but *intermittent*. Like a glow-rod cycling through its last reserves. He'd thought the thin place was an environmental effect, localized to the Battlefield Node. The readings suggested the instability was deepening as they moved toward the southwestern terminus.

He was still looking at the display when he sensed the doorway.

Torin didn't announce himself. He simply occupied the space between the crew compartment and the cockpit, one shoulder against the frame, arms loose. A big man moving without sound was always unsettling in close quarters. His presence arrived before he did—warm, like campfire heat, but with an underlying quality Ben hadn't found words for yet. It was out of register. Like a note played at the right pitch but in the wrong key.

Desh glanced at him once, then found something interesting to do with the navigation console.

Torin's eyes moved to the datapad in Ben's hand. The encrypted comm-link indicator was still active on the screen—the confirmation that the message had transmitted, the Temple's acknowledgment code waiting in the queue.

"Long report?" Torin asked.

Ben's grip tightened on the device. "Short one."

Torin nodded slowly. Not satisfied, not pressing. Just noting. He had the patience of someone who had learned that silence was a better tool than most weapons. The Jedi Lords hadn't been trained to wait; they had learned it the hard way.

"What will you tell them?" he said.

The question sat in the recycled air like a physical weight. Ben felt it land—not accusatory, but precise. Torin wasn't asking about the report. He was asking about the next one. The one after that. The one Ben was already composing in his head, already editing for consumption.

Ben turned back to the nav-display, but the numbers blurred. "The next node's showing heavy activity. We need to adjust our approach vector—come in from the upper ecliptic. We can't broadcast our drive signature if Veraine left monitoring in place."

Desh pulled up the approach calculation without comment. The sound of fingers on the console filled the silence Torin had left behind.

"Right," Torin said. He stayed in the doorway a moment longer—Ben could feel him there, that warm presence with its wrong note—then moved away. The soft displacement of air marked his passage back toward the crew compartment.

Ben kept his eyes on the nav-display. The flickering at the edge of the readout had worsened—not a malfunction, not sensor noise. The Force ahead was dropping in and out like a signal losing its source. And overlaid on that decay, Desh's energy signature reading burned steady and concentrated.

Not a footprint. A worksite.

Ben marked the approach vector. His hands moved with the precision that required no thought. Thinking meant circling back to the question still hanging in the air. *What will you tell them?*

Not Luke. Them. The Council. The Order. The institution that had already forgotten one catastrophe and was building the conditions for another.

The *Steady Returns* fell toward the next node. The Force ahead flickered. Veraine had been there for a long time, and Ben was already learning how to live with that.
