The nav-computer's holographic display cast a pale blue wash across Desh's hands. He traced the Corridor's length with one finger, hovering just above the projection.

"Fourteen nodes total." He toggled the status overlay. Red dominated the map—eleven dead markers strung across the route like charred bulbs. Three glowed amber. "Degraded but still drawing power. And these two—" His finger shifted toward the far end of the string, where two nodes pulsed white, irregular and insistent. "Recent activity. Within the last three weeks."

Ben stood at the cockpit’s edge, shoulder braced against the doorframe. Behind him, the others had arranged themselves with the unconscious geometry of people who didn't yet trust each other with their backs. Sera stood rigid near the secondary console. Torin occupied the center of the deck as though he’d been briefed on the room's architecture in advance. Kael had found the one spot equidistant from everyone; he leaned against the bulkhead with arms folded, watching the display through narrowed eyes.

"So she's moving toward the end of the line," Sera said.

"Moving *through* it," Ben corrected. "She's not stopping to examine the nodes. She's using them."

Sera’s posture sharpened. "Targeting the relay points. She isn't just accelerating the decay—she's directing it." She pulled the Corridor overlay onto the secondary display, her hands moving with the efficiency of a veteran archivist. "We go node by node. Systematic assessment. If we can stabilize what's still functional, we limit her resources."

"That takes time we don't have." Torin's voice remained pleasant, his usual tone for disagreement. "The active nodes are at the far end. She's already there. By the time we work through the degraded sites, she'll have finished her work and vanished."

"And if we bypass the degraded sites and she doubles back?"

"She won't. She's not running a repair operation."

"You don't know that."

"I know how a strategist moves." Torin gestured at the display. "She goes to Veranthos. We go to Veranthos. We stop her there."

Sera turned to face him fully. "You're describing a direct engagement with a Sith alchemist who has had a millennium to study the wound's mechanics. We have five people and no backup."

"I'm describing decisive action over prolonged exposure to a deteriorating situation."

"Decisive action." Sera's voice didn't rise, but it honed to a fine edge. "You mean reckless. You're gambling that your instinct outweighs a thousand years of preparation."

The cockpit air grew heavy. Ben watched them. In the GAG, this friction would have been smoothed over by rank or suppressed entirely—neither of which had produced good outcomes. Here, the friction was the information. Sera's certainty met Torin's, and neither would yield.

"You're both assuming we know what she's doing," Kael said.

The observation landed without emphasis, much like his earlier comment about the strength of the caf. Both Sera and Torin turned. The shift in their attention was physical, a door slamming on their argument.

"We know she's visiting the active nodes," Kael continued, still rooted to the bulkhead. "We know the timing. We don't know the method, and we don't know the sequence." He looked at the display. "You're planning a chase. I'd rather know what we're chasing *toward*."

Ben felt a click of recognition. It wasn't agreement, but a third position—one that didn't require either Sera or Torin to concede ground.

"The nodes are physical sites," Ben said. "If she's been there in the last three weeks, she left a trail. Residue. Modifications. Something we can read."

Kael gave a small, affirming nod.

Sera's hands stilled. She worked through the logic, reframing the assessment not as caution, but as intelligence gathering. Her shoulders dropped a fraction of an inch.

"So we run the Corridor," Ben said, looking at Sera. "Your route. Node by node." He turned to Torin. "But we're not stopping to repair what's broken. We're reading her work. By the time we reach Veranthos, we'll know her method instead of guessing at it."

Torin’s expression remained neutral, but his eyes betrayed the calculation. The direct approach and the systematic approach had merged.

"Battlefield preparation," Torin said.

"Something like that."

"It's slower than a direct burn."

"Yes."

Torin inclined his head—an acknowledgment of a man outmaneuvered but not defeated. "Then we'd better move."

Sera was already back at the console, pulling up the first node's coordinates. She didn't offer approval; she didn't need to. Her hands moved with purpose.

Desh entered the new heading, and the *Steady Returns* adjusted course.

***

The first node appeared in the viewport forty minutes later—a dark mass against the star field. Whatever structure it once held had been reduced to a skeleton of support pylons and collapsed prefabrication. Dead. Red on the display.

Ben stood at the forward viewport while Desh ran the passive scans. He reached out with the Force.

The Force was there. He felt the ship, Desh’s quiet presence at the console, and the distinct signatures of the survivors. Everything accounted for.

But the geometry was wrong.

He tried to center himself—the familiar drop into stillness—and instead found himself reaching for a handhold that wasn't where his mind expected it to be. Not absent. Displaced. It felt six inches to the left of habit, forcing him to adjust his grip before he realized there was nothing to adjust.

He held that sensation. The Force hadn't disappeared. It had shifted.

The part of the Force he'd always used without thinking—the foundation, the pressure underfoot—had developed a slight give, like a floor where the supports have begun to separate from the joists.

"Nothing on active sensors," Desh reported. "The structure's been cold for a long time."

"Right." Ben pulled his attention back to the viewport. The node drifted past, dark and inert. He kept his hand loose at his side. "Mark it and move on."

The display updated. Red.

Thirteen more to go.

He kept his eyes on the stars and did not try to center himself again.
