The hangar reeked of hot metal and recycled air—just like every Jedi Temple hangar Ben had ever known. The *Steady Returns* sat in bay fourteen, running lights blinking through pre-departure sequence, engines idling in a low, steady hum. He'd fueled her the night before. Efficiency, he'd told himself.

He was still telling himself that.

Ben dropped into the pilot's seat and pulled up the nav-console. The familiar weight of the controls settled under his hands. Green across the board. Motivator charge at ninety-four percent. He flagged it, accepted, moved on. Pre-flight checklists were useful. Something to focus on besides the datapad on the co-pilot's seat.

He looked at it.

The Jedi Partner Roster remained open from the briefing. Three names, all Knights, all available for extended survey assignments. Maret Osk, two years in the Outer Rim with a knack for environmental Force-reading. Reth Carnor, whose tracking instincts outshone even his father's. Thea Sollis, who'd worked with Ben twice before and never once pressed him on a reading he didn't want to share.

His thumb hovered.

Osk would need to meditate on the readings before committing to a bearing. Carnor would file reports at every stop—reports Ben would have to justify. Sollis would sense the wrongness in the Force and study him with that look, the one that meant she was weighing his read against her own. She'd stand there calculating while he sat gripping the controls, fighting the urge to take them away from her.

He could already feel it—the moment of surrender, of letting someone else's judgment matter as much as his own.

His thumb slid past the roster and opened his contacts.

Desh Rolan's comm tag sat third in the list.

Ben plugged in the Outer Rim survey coordinates as the call connected, watching the navicomputer begin its preliminary hyperspace calculations. The route was long. Too much empty space between the Temple's clean air and the bearing's destination. He needed a co-pilot who could handle the ship while he slept, someone who wouldn't interrupt his rotations with questions about Force sensations.

The comm crackled. A long pause—long-range lag.

"You're calling at six hundred," Desh said. His voice came through tinny, flattened by distance. "Either something's burning or you need a co-pilot."

"Survey route," Ben said. "Six weeks, maybe eight. Outer Rim, Darrpa Sector bearing. Standard pay plus hazard."

"Hazard." Not a question. An acknowledgment.

"Precautionary."

Another pause. Ben heard metal clatter in the background—Desh in the Terminus Station workshop, or one of the maintenance bays he'd been contracting through since the—

Neither finished the thought.

"When?" Desh asked.

"I'm in the hangar now. You'd need to meet me at Terminus in forty-eight hours."

"Forty-eight." A chair scraped. "You couldn't have called yesterday?"

"I'm calling now."

"Right." A beat. "Standard kit or are we expecting trouble?"

"Bring the kit," Ben said. "And the other kit."

The pause shifted—a recognition that lived between them, old and unspoken. Desh had been Galactic Alliance Guard. Ben had been the Temple liaison when the Guard dissolved. They'd worked together in that narrow window before everything fell apart. Before Jacen. Before Vestara. Before Ben learned trusting Force-users meant watching them burn.

"Copy that." No elaboration. No *why are you going alone, why didn't you call a Jedi, why use the word hazard like it's nothing.* Just the sound of Desh already moving, already calculating. "See you in forty-eight."

The line went dead.

Ben sat with the dead comm for a moment. His shoulders had dropped during the call without him noticing. The tension he'd carried since the wrongness started—that constant pressure behind his sternum—had eased just enough to register.

He set the datapad aside—the roster still open, the three names still visible—and reached for the repulsorlift controls.

The *Steady Returns* lifted.

The bay fell away beneath him. Floor crew stepped back without prompting. He angled up through the hangar's magnetic containment field and into Coruscant's lower traffic lanes, slotting between a bulk freighter and a government shuttle. The city sprawled below—towers and bridges beneath the permanent amber haze of ten trillion lives. He'd grown up beneath that haze. He'd once called it home.

He climbed without looking down.

Traffic thinned as he ascended. Lower lanes gave way to mid-orbit shipping, which gave way to orbital platforms and the first true darkness of space. Coruscant's terminator line curved below, nightside glittering, dayside a pale smear of atmosphere. The *Steady Returns* broke through the last traffic corridor and he was clear.

He logged the departure. Survey mission, Outer Rim, eight weeks estimated. Desh listed as crew. He didn't note the Force anomaly.

The navicomputer chimed—calculations complete, route plotted, the Outer Rim corridor laid out in a long arc across the display. He studied it like he'd studied telemetry in his father's office. The bearing. The direction. The gap in the charts where survey notation simply ended.

He pulled the hyperdrive lever.

The stars stretched. Elongated. Snapped.

Hyperspace swallowed everything in mottled blue, and the *Steady Returns* settled into the tunnel's current. Instruments flickered through standard transition recalibration. Normal. Expected.

What happened in the Force wasn't.

Hyperspace usually blurred it—the living current muffled, distant, like hearing through a wall. Ben had spent enough time in transit to stop noticing the ship's ventilation after the first hour.

The wrongness didn't blur.

It sharpened.

Through the tunnel, through the physics that should have smeared Force perception into white noise, the wrongness pulled toward the Outer Rim with navicomputer precision. Not ambient. Not diffuse. Directional, like a current's pull—not pushing, but making clear which way the water ran.

Ben's grip tightened on the controls. The wrongness had been a weight for three months. Now it had a vector. Now it was pulling him somewhere specific, and he'd already committed—called Desh, cut the Temple loose.

He'd told himself the survey route was logical. He'd told himself the bearing followed the data. He'd told himself Desh was the practical choice.

The wrongness disagreed.

Ben kept his hands on the controls and watched the hyperspace tunnel scroll past. He didn't glance at the co-pilot's seat where the datapad sat, roster still visible. Three Jedi he'd chosen not to trust. Three who would have asked questions he couldn't answer, made judgments he couldn't control.

Desh wouldn't ask. Desh would arrive, run the ship, watch Ben's back the way he always had. Desh wouldn't need the Force to know something was wrong.

The *Steady Returns* held course. The wrongness held its direction. Somewhere in the Outer Rim, the bearing waited.
