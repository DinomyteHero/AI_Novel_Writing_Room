The void had no floor.

Ben knew that intellectually — had known it since the first time the resonance pulled him across the threshold — but his body kept expecting one anyway. His boots found nothing. His weight distributed into nothing. The Force was present here the way blood was present in a bruise: real, wrong, under pressure.

He held position at the boundary and did not go further. That had been the agreement. Kael was behind him somewhere in the half-light of the Scar's rim, anchoring the perimeter with the flat, efficient focus that had stopped surprising Ben two days ago. Ben's job was to brace the resonance from outside and watch Sera work.

Watching was harder than working.

She had already located Torin. Ben felt her presence in the void the way he might feel a hand pressed flat against glass — distinct shape, muffled warmth, the pressure of intent without the texture of contact. She was moving toward him. Ben tracked her by the way the void reacted to her passage, the unstructured Force parting and closing around her like water around a hull.

Torin stood at the Scar's edge.

Ben had expected something dramatic — a man straining against an impossible weight, posture bent with effort, some visible evidence of the cost. There was none of that. Torin stood straight, both hands pressed to the wound's surface, and his Force presence was being drawn inward the way smoke was drawn up a chimney. Steady. Constant. Not violent. The worst kind of going.

His eyes were open. They did not see Sera.

Ben's stomach dropped. He pressed his feet harder against nothing and stayed where he was, which was the correct tactical decision and felt exactly like cowardice. The impulse to go in himself was immediate and specific — he could feel the geometry of it, the angle of approach, the way he'd position himself to pull from the outside while Sera worked the inside. He had the reach. He had the training. He had a Skywalker's reflexive certainty that he should be doing something.

He stayed. He'd learned, at some cost, what that certainty was worth.

*Trust the people you brought.* The thought came hard. *That's the whole lesson. That's the only lesson.* He was getting very tired of the whole lesson.

Sera reached Torin. Her hands came down over his.

The void lurched.

Not physically — Ben's body didn't move, didn't stagger — but through the Force the reversal landed like a step onto a surface that shifted underfoot. Sera's training had been built for one direction: seal, compress, contain, lock. She was running it backward now, unsealing where she had always sealed, and the technique resisted the way any thousand-year-old mechanism resisted being operated wrong. Ben felt the resistance as pressure behind his sternum, a structural protest radiating outward from the Scar's edge.

The wound pulled harder.

He planted himself and pushed back through the resonance, feeding stability into the seal from outside, and felt Kael do the same behind him — no communication, no coordination, just two people who had watched Sera work long enough to know when to brace. Ben had no idea when he'd started trusting Kael's timing. Somewhere between the third crisis and the fourth, he supposed.

Inside the void, Sera's presence held steady. The technique fought her and she did not adjust her principle, only her application — the same discipline, the same precision, pointed in the opposite direction. Ben could feel it in the way the Force moved around her hands: the careful, deliberate unsealing of locks that had been set to last a millennium. Each one released with a sensation like pressure equalizing, a small wrongness becoming a small rightness, and beneath it the constant pull of the wound trying to take more of Torin before it lost its grip.

Torin's presence was still going. Still being drawn in. But slower now. The wound had his hands and his signature and the long habit of his willingness, and Sera was unpicking all three simultaneously.

Ben's chest ached with the effort of holding the resonance stable. He was dimly aware that this was probably not a sustainable amount of bracing, that at some point the seal would reassert itself or it wouldn't, and that his role in that outcome was limited to not letting go before Sera finished. He focused on not letting go.

He noticed, distantly, that he had stopped thinking about what he would do if this failed. At some point in the last thirty seconds, that question had become irrelevant. There was only the resonance and the hold and Sera's hands over Torin's.

The wound gave.

It wasn't a sound. Ben would have said it was a sound if anyone had asked him to describe it afterward, but it wasn't — it was the Force-equivalent of a sound, the sudden release of sustained tension in a medium that didn't carry vibration. He felt it in his back teeth. He felt it in the base of his skull. Kael made a short, involuntary noise behind him that suggested Kael had felt it too, which Ben found oddly comforting.

Torin's Force presence tore free from the wound.

The adhesive image was wrong and right at the same time — it was more like watching something integral to a structure be removed and realizing only afterward that the structure had been built around it. The wound sealed behind the gap Torin left. The new resonance rushed in to fill the space. The seal held.

Torin did not hold.

Ben felt it before he saw it: the quality of Torin's presence in the Force, changed. Not absent — he was alive, breathing, his signature present and legible — but reduced in a way that had nothing to do with injury. Parts of him had been in that wound for too long. When Sera pulled him free, those parts didn't come with him. The wound had taken its percentage, the way any long occupation takes something of the occupied.

Nausea moved through Ben in a wave. He swallowed against it and did not look away.

Sera and Torin came out of the void together. There was no ceremony to it — one moment the void held them, the next it didn't, and they were standing on the Scar's rim in the flat, grey light of Veranthos, Sera's hands still wrapped over Torin's, both of them upright, both breathing. Ben stepped back to give them room and immediately had to recalibrate his balance, because his legs had apparently been operating on borrowed certainty for the last several minutes and were now requesting a formal accounting.

He stayed on his feet. Small victories.

Torin's eyes were still open.

For a long moment they focused on nothing, tracking across the middle distance with the mild, untroubled attention of someone who had not yet remembered what attention was for. Ben watched Sera's face during that moment — the controlled stillness of someone who had prepared for the worst and was not yet sure which version of the worst had arrived.

Then Torin's eyes found her face.

The warmth was immediate. Unmistakable. It crossed his features the way light crossed water — quick, genuine, the specific warmth of recognition rather than the general warmth of consciousness returning. He knew her. Ben saw it. For one heartbeat the man who had laughed easily and told stories with self-deprecating humor and treated Ben with a paternal affection he hadn't known he needed — that man was present, looking at Sera with the full weight of knowing her.

Then it faded.

Not to nothing. Not to blankness. It faded to something quieter: recognition without the resonance behind it, warmth without the depth that had made it warm. His eyes stayed on her face. He knew her. He would go on knowing her.

But the way he knew her was different.

Ben made himself observe this without looking away. That was all he could do — witness it, hold it, not diminish it by turning his attention elsewhere. Torin was alive. The wound was contained. Sera had reversed a thousand years of technique and pulled the man she loved free from the thing that was consuming him, and the cost was not his life but the specific texture of who he had been, the parts the wound had already taken before she reached him.

The caldera hadn't consumed him. It had replaced things.

Kael appeared at Ben's shoulder. Said nothing. The silence was the right call; Ben filed that away alongside all the other times Kael had turned out to be right about when not to speak.

Sera had not let go of Torin's hands.

She was looking at his face the way someone looked at a familiar place that had been changed while they were away — searching for the version they remembered inside the version that remained, cataloguing what was there against what was gone. Her expression gave nothing to the outside. Ben suspected that was costing her more than anything else she'd done today.

He did not say *I'm sorry*. He did not say *we did everything we could*. Both were true and neither was what this moment needed.

Torin was alive. His eyes found Sera's face.

For a moment — just a moment — the warmth returned. Then it settled into something quieter. He knew her. But the way he knew her was different now. Diminished. Present but smaller. The caldera hadn't consumed him.

It had replaced the parts that made him Torin.
