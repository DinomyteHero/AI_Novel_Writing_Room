The void had no depth until Sera stepped into it.

Ben stood at the threshold with his boots dug into black glass scored by old heat and newer fractures, one hand braced against the pull of the resonance they had built around the Scar. There should have been shape inside the wound. Distance. Direction. Some cue his senses could use.

There was none.

The Force in there had no grain. No current. No edge for his awareness to catch on. Reaching into it was like reaching into air after the air had been taken away. His skin prickled. His tongue tasted copper and stone dust. Behind him, outside the worst of the breach, Kael held the perimeter with both hands spread and shoulders set, as if he were leaning into a gale only he could feel.

Sera kept moving.

At first Ben tracked her by memory alone: where she'd been a heartbeat earlier, where a trained body would place its next step, the uncompromising line of her intent. Then the formless blank drew taut around a single point, and the Scar took shape from the inside out.

Not a place. A wound under pressure.

Torin stood at its lip.

His back was to them. Both hands were pressed against nothing Ben could see and everything he could feel. The wound drank from him in a steady draw. Not a violent yank. Worse than that. A patient taking. His presence ran into the Scar in long strands, pulled thin and thinner still, threaded into the containment the way wire vanished into transparisteel.

His eyes were open.

They fixed on the blank ahead of him and saw none of it.

Ben's stomach knotted hard enough to fold him forward. For one raw second he almost went in after her. The impulse hit his muscles before it hit thought; his left foot slid, his hand came off the resonance line, and the seal gave a warning lurch under his palm.

No.

He slammed his hand back into place and locked his knees.

If he broke the resonance now, Sera and Torin were gone and the Scar went with them. Heroic impulse. Great instinct. His father would have had a speech about trust. Luke would've been annoyingly right.

Ben bared his teeth and fed more of himself into the seal instead.

Sera reached Torin without hesitation. Inside that dead, directionless blank, her presence cut clean and narrow, the way she always did when she'd chosen a line and decided the galaxy could adjust. She didn't call his name. Didn't try to turn him.

She stepped close enough that her shoulder nearly brushed his back, raised both hands, and laid them over his.

The instant she made contact, the wound convulsed.

Ben felt the technique engage under her touch, old training waking with all the stubbornness of habit. Force-locks. Built to close. Built to bind. Built by people who had looked at a catastrophe and answered it with chains.

Sera turned them backward.

The reversal was not elegant. It was intimate and ugly, like taking a blade apart inside a wound while the wound was still using it. The containment bit at her, tried to route her effort into the old pattern, tried to make her hands finish what they had been trained to do for a thousand years.

She refused.

The Scar's pull sharpened. The resonance line bucked under Ben's grip and a shock ran up his arm into his shoulder. He staggered, caught himself, widened his stance. Kael hissed through his teeth somewhere to Ben's right and shifted with the pressure, anchoring the perimeter before the ripple could run wider.

Inside the void, Sera's presence tightened further. Not harder. Clearer. Ben knew enough now to feel what she was doing without naming each step. The old seals wanted closure. Compression. Fix the breach, trap the sacrifice inside it, call that duty.

She took the same discipline and opened its clenched fist.

The wound fought her. Torin's presence strained between them, still sunk deep in the Scar, still feeding it. Ben's own hands twitched with the urge to do something impossible and stupid, like reach through space and steady hers. His fingers flexed against empty air. He settled for pouring more weight into the resonance until his forearms trembled.

The void lurched.

Black glass cracked under Ben's left boot with a sharp report. Dust spat up and scratched his throat. For one sick instant the new seal sagged inward, all the balanced pressure around the Scar slipping half a step out of line.

Kael moved first. Of course he did. Quiet, watchful Kael, who always looked half a second from wandering off and always turned out to be exactly where the structure needed him. He shifted his right hand, cut across the buckle in the perimeter, and pinned it in place. His face had gone gray with effort.

Ben gave him a grim flash of teeth that might have been a smile in less catastrophic conditions. Great. They were improvising metaphysics over a hole in reality. His mother would call this a learning experience. His mother was a menace.

Then Sera pulled.

Ben felt it before he understood it. A drag across his own chest from the inside, adhesive giving way a fraction at a time. Not clean separation. Not release. Extraction.

Torin came free in strips.

The wound had him too deeply for anything gentler. Every piece Sera unsealed tried to snap back into place. Every fragment she drew out left a raw impression in the resonance, a missing contour the Scar wanted to reclaim. Ben's vision blurred at the edges. His stomach rolled. He locked his elbows and held on while nausea climbed his throat and his heartbeat thudded against his ribs hard enough to hurt.

Torin's presence changed under Sera's hands.

Not all at once. Piece by piece.

Ben had known Torin as warmth under command, a steady field with iron hidden in it, laughter held ready even in bad ground. That shape was still there while Sera tore him loose, but thinner each time the wound let go. Threads failed to come back with the rest. Some remained embedded in the seal, fused into the new containment with old damage around them. Some just stopped.

Ben swallowed acid and fed another surge into the resonance.

The perimeter narrowed. Kael matched him without looking. Between them the seal cinched around the place Torin had occupied, not restoring what had been there, only keeping the Scar from taking the empty space as an invitation. The strain set fire to Ben's shoulders. Sweat tracked down his spine under his jacket despite the cold deadness pouring off the void.

Sera did not stop.

She had both hands locked over Torin's, body braced behind the motion, head bowed as if she were listening to the resistance and rejecting every answer it offered. Ben had never seen her bend on principle. Had never seen her make room for compromise just because it hurt less. This wasn't compromise. This was something harder.

She was using the thing that had imprisoned all of them to refuse one more imprisonment.

Torin's hands tore free first.

Ben felt the break of contact like skin ripping from sealant. The shock hit the whole resonance ring. Kael grunted. Ben's knees dipped. The Scar gaped for one bright wrong instant in the Force, a pressure drop that made the world seem to tilt under him, and then the new seal slammed tight around it.

Not smooth. Not stable in any comforting way. Stable enough.

Sera stumbled backward with Torin's weight coming into her.

He did not help her.

His arms hung where she had dragged them. His head stayed angled toward the wound for a beat too long, as though some part of him was still listening to it. Then the last strands came loose. Ben felt them peel away from the Scar and slide across the resonance into the shape of a man.

The ache in his own chest sharpened, then broke.

Air rushed back into the space behind Ben's teeth. He hadn't been holding it. The void had simply taught his body not to expect breath. He sucked in recycled cold, stone grit, the bitter tang of heated circuitry from the failing equipment around the caldera rim, and nearly coughed on it.

Kael dropped to one knee but kept one hand up, maintaining the perimeter. Sweat darkened his collar. He looked at Ben with flat, exhausted eyes that said, in perfect silence, You wanted exciting.

Ben would have answered if his mouth had remembered how.

Inside the void, Sera had both arms around Torin now, not in an embrace, not yet, but in the practical grip of someone hauling an injured man out of bad terrain. She drew him back from the lip one step at a time. Each pace gave the blank around them a little more shape, or maybe Ben's senses were finally finding purchase again because Torin was no longer part of the Scar.

The difference hit harder than relief should have.

Torin was there. Alive. Present in the Force. Ben could track him now without the wound distorting the edges.

And wrong.

Reduced was too neat a word for it. This was not absence. Not damage you could point to and map. His presence had shrunk inward, as if whole rooms had been sealed off and the lights extinguished behind the doors. The warmth Ben associated with him still existed, but at a remove, behind layers, dimmed by distance that had not been there before.

Ben's throat closed.

He had wanted Sera to get him back. Wanted it hard enough that some part of him had still been bargaining with the Force like a child: take the dramatic rescue, skip the bill. Let this one break in the merciful direction.

The galaxy declined.

Sera brought Torin across the threshold of the worst of the void. The dead blank receded from around them, leaving black glass, the raw heatless pressure of the Scar beyond the new seal, and three exhausted people trying not to let reality split again under their hands.

Torin's boots scraped once against the ground. He blinked.

That, more than anything else, hit Ben. Not the extraction. Not the tearing. The blink. Small, human, ordinary. The body remembering itself after being used as infrastructure for something no living being should have touched.

Sera eased him another half step back.

Torin's head turned.

His eyes found her face.

For an instant the old Torin was there so clearly Ben almost laughed from the sheer meanness of hope. Warmth lit his features, recognition deep and immediate, the same open affection that had made him dangerous in all the best ways and foolish in at least two fatal ones.

Sera froze as if even breathing might break it.

Then the light thinned.

Not gone. Ben wished, wildly and pointlessly, that it had gone. Loss would have been cleaner. This settled into something quieter, smaller, still aware of her and altered in the knowing. He knew Sera. Ben could feel that much. The bond remained. The pull of it remained.

But pieces had been left in the wound. Pieces that had held his presence in the galaxy with all its former size and color and reach.

Torin pulled free. Alive. His eyes find Sera's face. For a moment — just a moment — the warmth returns. Then it fades to something quieter. He knows her. But the way he knows her is different. Diminished. Present but smaller. The caldera didn't consume him. It replaced the parts that made him Torin.
