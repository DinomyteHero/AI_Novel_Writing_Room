The void had no edges.

Ben stood at the threshold where the Scar opened into something that refused to be a place, and the word *threshold* was already wrong, already too architectural for what this was. The wound in the Force didn't begin anywhere. It simply was, and then everything else wasn't, and the boundary between those two states was something you felt in the back of your molars rather than saw with your eyes.

Kael stood three meters to his left, arms loose at his sides, attention focused inward. Anchoring. He'd taken the position without being asked, which meant either he'd figured out the geometry of what they needed or he was doing something else entirely and Ben was choosing to trust the coincidence. Given everything, he was choosing to trust it.

Sera was already inside.

Ben tracked her the way you track a sound when the room goes dark — not seeing, exactly, but knowing. Her presence in the Force moved through the void with the particular quality he'd come to recognize over the last seventy-two hours: controlled, dense, like a fist held closed. She'd gone in without hesitation. He'd watched her step through and disappear, and the void had closed around her like water, and now all he had was the thread of her through the Force and the effort of not going in after her.

He found Torin through her.

Torin was standing at the wound's edge — Ben registered this as pressure rather than image, a pull in the Force that had direction the way a drain has direction, and Torin was at its center. His hands were on the Scar. Ben couldn't see them. He felt them the way you feel a door held shut from the other side, the weight of someone leaning into it, and what Torin was leaning into was the wound itself, and the wound was taking everything he offered.

His Force presence was being drawn inward. Slowly. The way light gets drawn toward a gravity well — not consumed, not destroyed, just redirected until there's nothing left pointing outward.

His eyes were open. Ben knew that without knowing how he knew it. They didn't see anything.

*Torin.* The thought was not a call. He didn't reach through the void; he'd learned better than that in the last two minutes of standing here watching Kael's face go very still and feeling the resonance hold and knowing that any additional presence he shoved into the equation would be one more variable for Sera to compensate for. He was a Skywalker. He was standing outside. He was helping by not helping, which was, if he was being honest with himself, the single most difficult thing he'd done on this entire mission and possibly in his life.

He could feel when Sera reached Torin.

The moment had a texture — her presence pressing close to his, and then her hands over his hands, and what happened next was not something Ben had words for because he'd never felt a Force-lock run backward before. The technique was designed to seal, to close, to draw the lattice tight around a wound and hold it. Sera had spent a thousand years learning to do exactly that. What she was doing now was the same motion in reverse, the same discipline reading the same mechanism and choosing to unseal instead, and the resistance was immediate and total.

The wound *pushed back.*

Ben's knees didn't buckle. He wanted credit for that. The ripple came through the resonance like a subsonic shock, not sound but pressure, and it hit the lattice at every point where they'd anchored it, and for two seconds the new seal felt like paper. He planted his feet and poured himself into the resonance and didn't think about what would happen if Sera's reversal destabilized the containment before Torin came free, because thinking about it would not help and he was not going to be useful by catastrophizing.

Kael made a sound — a short, controlled exhale that was not quite a curse. He'd felt it too.

"Holding," Kael said.

Ben didn't answer. He was busy.

The resistance wasn't uniform. He could feel it through Sera's thread — the wound's pull was concentrated around Torin's hands, around the points where his Force presence had bonded with the containment structure over however many minutes he'd been standing there. Not minutes. It felt like longer. It felt like the wound had been waiting for someone to offer themselves and had taken the offer with both hands.

Sera's reversal worked the way a lock-pick works — not force, not speed, but precision. She found the seal points where Torin's presence had fused with the wound's edge and she unsealed them one by one, and each release sent another ripple through the lattice, smaller than the first but cumulative, and Ben rode each one and let them pass and held the resonance and did not think about the fact that he could feel how much of Torin was already gone.

Not dead. Not consumed. Just — redirected. Drawn inward. The parts of his Force presence that faced outward, that laughed and told stories and noticed when someone needed warmth — those parts had been feeding the wound, and the wound had been keeping them.

The separation, when it came, was not clean.

Ben felt it as a tearing in his own chest, which was not his chest, which was Torin's Force presence pulling free from the Scar's edge — the wound releasing its hold not because it was finished but because Sera refused to let it keep him. The adhesion broke in stages. First the hands. Then the arms. Then the deeper points where the integration had gone further than the hands, where the wound had found purchase in the parts of Torin that were most willing to give.

The last point held longest.

Ben was gripping the resonance so hard his actual hands had gone white at the knuckles, which was absurd, which was the body doing what the body does when the mind is somewhere else entirely. He had a brief, wild thought that if he survived this he was going to need to explain to his father that he'd strained his hands by holding them too tight while standing in a field on Veranthos, and his father was going to make a face, and it was going to be fine.

The last point released.

The void exhaled — not sound, not pressure, just an absence of the pull that had been there for so long Ben hadn't registered it as pull until it stopped. The resonance snapped back into shape around the wound, the new seal reasserting itself, and the lattice steadied, and the Scar was contained, and Kael made a different sound — not relief, exactly, but the sound of someone who has been carrying weight and has just been allowed to set it down.

Ben's legs were shaking. He noted this as information and chose not to act on it.

They came out of the void together, Sera and Torin, and *together* was doing work in that sentence because they were not the same together they'd been when Sera went in. Torin's feet found the ground. His hands — Ben could see his hands now, actual hands, not Force-sense, not pressure — fell to his sides. He was breathing. He was upright.

His eyes found Sera's face.

Ben watched it happen. The warmth came back — not slowly, not by degrees, but all at once, the way a light comes on, and for a moment Torin was entirely himself. The man who laughed too easily and told stories with his hands and had treated Ben with a kind of steady, uncomplicated affection that Ben hadn't known he needed until he'd had it and now didn't know what to do with what he was watching. That man was there. Present. Recognizable.

Then the moment passed.

The warmth didn't disappear. It receded. Settled into something smaller and quieter, like a fire burning down to coals — still warm, still real, but no longer the thing that had lit the room. Torin's eyes stayed on Sera's face and he knew her, Ben could feel that, the recognition was complete and genuine. But the way he knew her was different. The wound had taken something and the wound had kept it, and what Sera had pulled free was Torin, but not all of Torin, and the parts that were missing weren't the ones that would heal.

Ben looked away.

Not from grief, or not only from grief. He looked away because what was happening between Sera and Torin was theirs, and he was standing close enough to witness it, and witnessing it felt like a violation of something that had no name in any language he knew.

He looked at Kael instead. Kael was watching the Scar, his expression doing the thing it did when he was thinking and had decided not to share the thought. His hands had stopped shaking before Ben's had, which Ben was choosing not to take personally.

The resonance held. The seal held. The wound was contained.

Torin was alive.

Ben turned back. Torin's eyes were still on Sera's face, and the warmth was still there — smaller, quieter, present. The caldera hadn't consumed him. It had replaced the parts that made him Torin.
