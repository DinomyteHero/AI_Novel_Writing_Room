The void had no texture. It was absence given shape, a pocket of space where the Force had been pulled out like oxygen from a room. Ben stood at its threshold, his boots planted on the cracked stone of the caldera floor, and felt the resonance he and Kael had built thrumming through his bones. It was a net, a lattice of their own making, holding the edges of the wound closed. Inside that net, the void waited.

Sera stepped past him without a word. She moved like a blade parting water, her focus so absolute it left a wake in the air. Ben felt her determination as a physical pressure against his chest. She walked into the formless dark, and for a heartbeat she vanished—not from sight, but from sense. The void swallowed her Force signature whole.

Then he felt her locate the Scar.

It wasn’t a place, not exactly. It was a point of violent negation, the raw edge where the lattice had been torn. And there, pressed against that edge, Ben sensed Torin.

His stomach clenched.

Torin stood with his hands flat against the nothingness, his palms splayed as if holding a pane of invisible glass. His Force presence wasn’t just touching the wound; it was being drawn into it, thread by thread, like ink bleeding into blotting paper. His eyes were open. They stared ahead, unblinking, unseeing. The warmth that usually radiated from him was gone, replaced by the cold, systematic pull of the containment lattice. He was already half-gone, a man becoming infrastructure.

Ben’s hands twitched. The impulse to reach into the void, to grab Torin’s shoulder and yank him back, burned through his nerves. He locked his knees. Sera was already moving. He had to hold the resonance. The thought landed with the dry, familiar weight of Skywalker legacy: *Stand here. Watch. Hold the line while someone else pays the price.* He pushed it aside. That wasn’t this. Sera wasn’t paying a price; she was refusing one.

Inside the void, Sera reached Torin. Ben felt it as a shift in the pressure—not sight, but a sensed proximity. She didn’t hesitate. She placed her hands over his, her fingers slotting between his splayed ones, palm to knuckle. A healer’s gesture. A lockpicker’s.

Then she reversed a thousand years of training.

Ben’s breath caught. He knew Force-lock techniques—complex weaves meant to seal, to bind, to contain. What Sera did was the precise opposite. He felt the discipline turn inside out. Where a lock would click shut, she unclicked it. Where a seal would fuse, she pried it apart. It was intimate violence, a surgical undoing. The wound fought back. The lattice resisted, its structural integrity rejecting the reversal. Ben sensed the strain as a physical lurch, a shudder that traveled up his spine. The new seal wavered, the resonance net stretching thin. For a terrifying second, he thought the whole containment would rip open.

Kael, standing ten meters to Ben’s left, didn’t flinch. Ben felt the other man’s focus like an anchor line thrown into a storm—steady, unyielding, utterly pragmatic. Kael held the perimeter. Ben poured more of himself into the resonance, muscles trembling with the effort. The net held.

Sera pulled.

It wasn’t a clean extraction. Torin’s Force presence had started to integrate with the lattice, his signature woven into the wound’s ragged edge. Separating it was like tearing adhesive. Ben felt the rip in his own chest, a sympathetic ache that hollowed out his lungs. He gritted his teeth, shifting his stance to channel more focus, sweat beading along his hairline. The cost hit him in a wave of nausea. This was the price. Not in grand sacrifice, but in this slow, brutal tearing.

Torin’s presence came free.

It didn’t snap back whole. It peeled away, leaving fragments behind. Ben felt the moment of separation—a sudden, sickening release of tension, followed by a diminishment. Torin’s signature was still there, still alive, but it was… less. Thinned. Like a color washed out by too much light.

The new seal reasserted itself around the wound. The resonance settled, the net tightening back into place. The void remained contained.

Sera stepped back, pulling Torin with her. They emerged from the formless dark together, stumbling onto the solid stone of the caldera. Torin’s legs buckled. Sera caught him, her arms wrapping around his chest, holding him upright. His hands, now free, hung limp at his sides.

Ben let out a breath he hadn’t realized was trapped. The resonance hummed steadily now, almost peaceful. The immediate danger had passed. He took a step toward them, then stopped.

Torin’s head lifted. His eyes, which had stared blankly into the void, now moved. They found Sera’s face.

For a moment—just a moment—the warmth returned.

It flickered behind his eyes, a spark of familiar recognition, of affection, of *Torin*. Ben saw it. Saw the man’s lips part slightly, saw the lines around his eyes soften. Saw Sera’s own breath catch, her grip tightening, hope raw on her face.

Then it faded.

The warmth didn’t vanish. It settled. It banked itself, cooling into something quieter, something smaller. Torin looked at Sera, and he knew her. But the way he knew her was different. The depth was gone. The shared history, the thousand years of camaraderie and tension and unspoken love—it was present, but it was a silhouette, a shape remembered rather than felt. His eyes held recognition, but not the weight of it.

He was alive. He was free.

He was diminished.

Sera’s expression didn’t crumple. It stilled. She had known the cost. She had chosen it anyway. She lowered Torin gently to the ground, kneeling beside him, one hand still on his shoulder. He looked up at her, then past her, his gaze drifting across the caldera with a mild, detached curiosity. Like a tourist seeing a famous ruin for the first time.

Ben’s throat tightened. The grief that rose in him was hollow, quiet. This was the victory. Not a restoration, but a rescue with scars. The caldera hadn’t consumed Torin. It had replaced the parts that made him Torin. What remained was a man who had given himself to a wound and come back less than he was.

Love had pulled him free. Love couldn’t make him whole.

Ben looked at Sera, still kneeling in the dust, her shoulders straight, her face a mask of calm acceptance. He looked at Torin, alive and breathing and forever altered. He felt the resonance holding, the wound contained, the immediate crisis passed.

The cost sat between them, silent and absolute.

Torin pulled free. Alive. His eyes find Sera’s face. For a moment—just a moment—the warmth returns. Then it fades to something quieter. He knows her. But the way he knows her is different. Diminished. Present but smaller. The caldera didn’t consume him. It replaced the parts that made him Torin.
