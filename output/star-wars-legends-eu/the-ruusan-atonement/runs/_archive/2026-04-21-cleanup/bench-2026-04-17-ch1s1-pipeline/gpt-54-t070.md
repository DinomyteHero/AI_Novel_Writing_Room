Ben’s guard came up late.

Not by much. Half a second, maybe less. Against most Temple sparring partners, that should still have been enough.

The training blade cracked across his right shoulder with a sting that bit through sweat and tunic, and the jolt shut his arm down for an instant. His own saber dipped. The red-orange practice beam guttered as his grip shifted.

Kriff.

His partner had already stepped back, blade angled down in a clean reset. “Point.”

“Yeah.” Ben rolled his shoulder once, buying the motion before the ache settled in. “I noticed.”

The other Knight gave him the polite look people used when they were deciding whether to ask if he was all right or whether that would be insulting. Ben saved him the trouble. He thumbed off his training saber. The hum cut out, and the salle felt too large without it.

“Again?” the Knight asked.

Ben wiped his palm against his pants. The sweat was worse than it should have been for a late session drill. “Not tonight.”

His partner glanced at the deactivated hilt. “You sure?”

“Unless you’ve got a secret technique for making me younger and less tired.”

That got the expected short huff of amusement. “Fair enough.”

Fatigue. Nice, ordinary, boring fatigue. Better than saying Sorry, the Force went off-key for a second and my body followed it right off a cliff.

The Knight clipped his own saber to his belt. “See you in the morning.”

“If I survive the walk back to my quarters.”

“Risky.”

Ben lifted two fingers in a half salute. The other man headed for the door, boots tapping against the salle floor, then fading into the Temple’s evening quiet.

Silence settled by degrees. Ventilation whispered through the high room. Sweat cooled at the back of Ben’s neck. Somewhere beyond the walls, lifts moved, distant doors slid open and shut, the Temple carrying on with its usual business while his shoulder throbbed under a hit he should never have taken.

Third time this week.

He looked down at the practice saber in his hand as if it might have an opinion. It didn’t. Very inconsiderate of it.

Ben crossed to the center line again, stopped, and replayed the exchange. Two feints, low cut, pivot, guard up—

The wrong note had hit just before contact. Not loud. Not dramatic. A faint flattening that slid under everything else and pulled his timing off by a fraction. Enough to matter.

Everyone had excuses for disturbances in the Force these days. The aftermath of Abeloth. The long bruises left by too many crises stacked too close together. Residue from things no one wanted to put names to during dinner. Background noise. Weather.

Ben had heard all of them. He’d even repeated a few when he wanted the conversation to end.

None of that explained why it kept catching him like this.

He set the saber aside on a bench and started through the first form without igniting it. Empty hand around an absent blade. Guard high, shift, cut, recover. Boots whispered over the salle floor. The old patterns sat in his muscles where they belonged, drilled too deep to shake loose. Shoulders aligned. Hips turned. Wrist angle clean.

Again.

He moved faster. The second sequence flowed into the third, then broke apart as he forced himself to stop relying on reflex and watch the timing piece by piece. If his body was failing him, he wanted proof. If it was the Force, he wanted that too.

The room answered with the soft slap of soles against polished floor and the distant vibration of the Temple around him, thousands of lives passing through stone and metal and light. Usually that background sat where it belonged, a steady presence he could tune with half his attention.

Tonight it scraped.

Ben checked a strike halfway through, irritation tightening the back of his throat. He reset and tried again. This time he kept his awareness narrower, close to skin and breath and the pull in his healing shoulder. Pure mechanics.

Better.

He ran the same combination three times. Clean each pass.

“Great,” he muttered to the empty room. “So I’m not losing my mind. That really narrows it down.”

His voice came back at him from the high ceiling, thin and dry.

He paced to the far end of the salle, turned, and started another form. No blade, no target, just line and balance. The wrongness brushed him again, faint as a bad tuning fork held somewhere behind his teeth. His next step landed a fraction too hard.

Not emotion. That was the problem.

Fear had a shape. Anger had heat. Grief dragged. Corruption had its own greasy pressure, ugly and obvious once you’d met it up close and survived. Ben knew those. He’d spent enough of his life neck-deep in Skywalker family disasters to qualify for a private lecture circuit.

This was different. Cleaner. Emptier.

His hands shifted through the next guard position, and memory opened where he didn’t want it.

Jacen, in the years when he had started becoming hard to read, not because Ben had known him so well but because some parts of him had stopped fitting. Abeloth, worse by orders of magnitude, tearing at every instinct that was supposed to tell a Jedi where the line was. Not darkness exactly. Not the usual way. More like standing in a room where some essential pressure had been removed and your body knew before your mind did that breathing there came at a cost.

Ben stopped moving.

The salle lights hummed overhead. A bead of sweat rolled down his temple and caught at his jawline. His shoulder ached where the practice blade had tagged him, a sharp useful pain. Real. Measurable.

He stared at the blank hilt lying on the bench.

No. He wasn’t giving that comparison more room than it deserved.

Because if he was right, then he’d missed something building under all their noses. If he was wrong, then he was jumping at ghosts left over from years when the galaxy had tried very hard to kill his family in inventive ways.

Neither option was comforting.

He drew in a slow breath and let it out through his nose. Skywalkers handling their own messes had a long, glorious tradition. Also a casualty list.

That thought almost made him smile.

Almost.

Ben walked back to the center circle and set his feet shoulder-width apart. Meditation stance, stripped down for practicality rather than ceremony. Knees loose. Spine straight. Hands open at his sides. The old contemplative drills came easily; Master Cilghal had once told him he approached stillness like a mechanic diagnosing a hyperdrive fault. Ben had taken it as a compliment. She might not have meant it that way.

He let the salle arrange itself around him. Ventilation first. Air moving across warm skin. The faint medicinal sting from the training mats. His own pulse settling. Distant footfalls beyond the corridor wall, too far away to matter. He reached outward, careful, not pressing, only listening.

The Temple usually met him with layered life: Jedi in meditation chambers below, initiates asleep two levels up, technicians on the night rotation, traffic threading through Coruscant far beyond the walls. Threads, currents, pressure changes. Familiar.

Tonight one thread dragged flat against the rest.

Ben held still.

There.

A tiny dissonance, not in any one presence but under them, as if some hidden support had gone crooked and everything above it had adjusted without admitting it. He didn’t push deeper. The first rule of sensing something you didn’t understand was not to announce yourself to it like an idiot.

His mouth thinned.

Structural.

The word arrived uninvited and stayed.

Ben opened his eyes. The salle swam into focus again: padded wall panels, low benches, the sheen of use on the floor. Ordinary things. Good things. Things that obeyed rules.

His reliability as a Jedi Knight should have belonged in that category too.

Instead he was standing alone in an empty training room, trying to decide whether his instincts were warning him or replaying old damage with better production values.

He flexed his right hand once. The fingers answered. Solid. No tremor.

“Excellent,” he said to the room. “Very healthy internal process. No concerns.”

The room, unhelpfully, remained silent.

He picked up the saber from the bench but didn’t ignite it. He ran through the opening sequence of Soresu one more time, slower now, letting each movement test the edges of that conclusion. The form held. His footing held. The wrong note stayed where it had been, low and persistent, and the memory of Jacen and Abeloth sat beside it with enough precision to make his stomach tighten.

Not the same. Close enough to matter.

Which meant he had two choices. Mention it again and watch people nod in that careful way that said post-crisis sensitivities were understandable, Ben, thank you, keep monitoring. Or keep it to himself until he had something harder than instinct and a bruised shoulder.

He already knew which option he preferred. Control the variables. Get data. Handle it.

Classic family behavior. A terrible sign.

Ben lowered the hilt and looked toward the door as a shift in the corridor pressed against his attention before the panels opened. Familiar presence. Steady. Centered. Hard to mistake even when Ben was annoyed enough to want to.

Luke appeared in the doorway with a datapad and an expression Ben knew meant a mission.
