The void had no depth until Sera entered it.

From the caldera's rim, Ben had been holding the resonance in place with both hands spread toward nothing, knees bent against a pressure that came from no direction at all. The air across the Scar tasted dead, stripped clean of heat and dust and all the usual grit of a volcanic world. Sound went thin here. Even the grind of shifting stone under his boots came back wrong, clipped short.

Ahead of him, where the wound opened in the world, there should have been shape. Distance. Edge. Instead there was absence pressed into a circle, a blank so complete his eyes slid off it and his Force-sense found no purchase. A hole without darkness. Darkness at least was something.

Sera stepped into that blank and became the only point inside it.

Ben caught her through the resonance first: a hard, narrow line of intent, clean as a drawn blade. No hesitation. No prayer. Just purpose aimed straight at the center.

Then the void gave him one more point.

Torin stood at the Scar's edge with both hands braced against what could not be touched. His eyes were open. Fixed. Empty of recognition. His body leaned forward a fraction, as if he were listening to a voice too distant for anyone else to hear, and all of him—muscle, breath, presence—fed into the wound in a steady pull.

Ben's stomach knotted.

Half-gone, his mind supplied, cold and fast.

He took one step before he stopped himself. His hands twitched toward the void, stupidly human, as if he could just reach in and haul Torin back by the shoulders. Great plan, Skywalker. Throw yourself into the thing everyone is currently trying to keep from eating reality. That would look fantastic in the report.

He locked his knees, planted his feet wider, and did not move again.

Kael was off to his left, a dark shape at the edge of Ben's vision, holding the perimeter with that same infuriating economy he brought to everything. No wasted motion. No visible strain. Ben could feel him only because the resonance included him; outside that, Kael's presence stayed folded in on itself, compact and watchful.

Sera crossed the last distance to Torin.

Inside the void, movement had no texture. She did not seem to walk so much as arrive, one controlled step after another into a place that gave nothing back. Her robes hung wrong in the blankness, without wind or weight. Torin didn't turn.

His hands were on the wound.

Ben couldn't have said what that meant in any language that made sense outside the Force. Torin's palms met emptiness, but the resonance translated the contact into something Ben's body understood. Pressure. Drag. A slow peel of living presence being taken apart one layer at a time and fed inward.

Sera lifted her hands.

For one beat she hovered there, just above Torin's.

Ben felt memory in the shape of her preparation. Not his memory. Not hers in any clear image. Just the old discipline embedded in the technique itself—contain, bind, fix, hold. The whole ruin of it. A thousand years of Jedi making prisons and calling them necessity.

Sera set her hands over Torin's.

The void lurched.

Ben's teeth clicked together. The new seal bucked under his grip, the resonance warping hard enough to pitch him half a step sideways before he caught himself. Stone bit through the sole of his boot. Heat flashed up his calf. The wound pulled once, ravenous, and the caldera answered with a groan deep enough to shake dust loose from the broken rim.

She was reversing it.

Not patching. Not reinforcing. Turning the lock inside out.

Ben felt the instant the old discipline hit resistance. The technique wanted closure. Compression. Sacrifice made permanent. Sera drove it the other way, and every line of training in that ancient pattern fought her for it.

The strain punched through the resonance and into his hands. His fingers cramped. He shoved more of himself into the seal, less elegance and more brute insistence, the Force moving through his shoulders and spine until his whole body became one long brace.

Inside the void, Sera did not yield an inch.

Her posture stayed upright, but tension spread through her in harsh, exact lines: wrists fixed over Torin's, shoulders locked, head bowed just enough to put all of her weight behind the reversal. Ben couldn't hear her breath from here. He could see the rhythm of it in the small movements at her ribs. In. Hold. Drive.

Torin's presence jerked.

Not free. Not yet. But the smooth inward feed broke apart into catches and snags, a stream hitting stones.

The backlash ripped across the lattice. Ben swallowed hard against a surge of nausea as the wound tried to widen under the disruption. For an ugly second the resonance thinned, and he saw too much at once—raw formlessness, the old anchor-scars buried around the caldera, the way the new seal sat over all of it like hands trying to close a split seam while pressure kept pushing from beneath.

Kael shifted.

That was all. One step, maybe less. But the perimeter tightened around the break, dry and unsentimental as a mechanic slamming a failing panel back into place. Ben felt the support hit the resonance and took it, grateful enough that under any less catastrophic circumstance he might have said something insulting to cover it.

Instead he leaned harder.

Sera pulled.

The sensation came through the Force with hideous intimacy. Adhesive separating. Not a clean cut. Not release. A long, stubborn tearing of one thing from another after they had been pressed together too long.

Ben's chest seized with it. He sucked air through clenched teeth and nearly lost the rhythm of the seal. Every muscle in his arms went rigid. The wound answered with a pulse that scraped across his nerves like static without sound.

Torin's head lifted a fraction.

His eyes were still open. Still blind. But some part of him had met resistance in the other direction.

Sera kept pulling.

There was no flourish to it. No visible blaze of power. The force of the act lived in refusal. In her absolute decision that this would not stand, not one second longer, not even if the discipline she had built her life on had to be broken across her own hands to do it.

Ben had spent enough time around Skywalkers to recognize that kind of refusal on sight. Usually right before something exploded.

He would have laughed if his lungs had been doing anything useful.

The second tear was worse.

Torin's presence came free another notch, and the wound clamped down in answer, trying to hold what it had already taken. Ben felt the bite of it in his own sternum, a hollow pressure that made his vision blur at the edges. He shifted his stance, one foot sliding back in black volcanic grit, and fed the resonance every spare scrap of focus he had left.

Hold, he thought at the seal, at Kael, at himself, at the whole impossible arrangement of trust and timing keeping this from collapsing. Hold for her.

The caldera trembled. Fine cracks skittered through a slab of glassy rock near the rim. A hot mineral smell surged up from somewhere below, sharp and bitter under the blood taste at the back of Ben's throat.

Inside the void, Sera's hands stayed locked over Torin's.

Then the pattern changed.

Not in the wound. In Torin.

The steady draw inward faltered. Ben caught fragments of him where before there had been only a draining stream: warmth, command, that broad steady presence Torin had always carried into a room as if making space for everyone else was as natural as breathing. It surfaced in broken pieces. One held. Another slipped. Another tore loose.

The pain of it hit Ben hard enough to bend him forward.

He didn't go down. His knees tried. He told them no.

Kael's support pressed in from the side again, spare and exact. Ben caught the shape of him through the resonance—still rooted at the perimeter, still saying nothing, still doing the ugly necessary work without pretending it was noble. Ben filed away a fresh batch of reluctant respect for later, when the galaxy was less busy ending.

Sera pulled with both hands, both feet planted, whole body committed.

And Torin came free.

The separation slammed through the caldera. Ben felt it in the soles of his boots and in the cavity under his ribs, a final wrenching peel followed by a recoil so abrupt his head snapped up. The wound surged wide for one terrifying beat. The new seal dropped over it at once, resonance snapping back into place under Ben and Kael and whatever Sera had left to spare. The impact rattled Ben's bones.

Then it held.

The void did not close. It thinned. Collapsed inward from featureless infinity to a single scar in the air and then to the open caldera again, black stone, trembling heat, the harsh geometry of real space returning all at once.

Sera staggered backward out of the blankness with Torin in her grip.

Ben nearly let go of the seal from pure reflex. He caught himself, locked the resonance one final time against the wound's sullen pressure, and only then looked straight at them.

Torin would have fallen if Sera hadn't held him up.

His hands hung slack at his sides, fingers curled as if they still remembered pressure. Color was coming back into his face in slow degrees. The blank fixed stare was gone. So was the terrible forward lean, that posture of living surrender to the Scar. He was breathing on his own now, shallow and rough.

Alive.

Ben's first reaction was relief so fierce it hurt.

The second was the shape missing around it.

Torin's presence remained, but diminished was too simple a word for what Ben felt. This wasn't weakness. Not exhaustion. Pieces had been taken out and the empty places had settled. The broad warmth that had always sat at Torin's center like banked coals was there for a heartbeat when his gaze found Sera—

and Ben saw it, clear as sunlight through stormcloud, the man he had been. Recognition. Affection. That easy human warmth that had made command feel like shelter instead of weight.

Sera saw it too. Ben knew she did from the way she went utterly still.

Then the warmth receded.

Not gone. Reduced. Drawn inward to a smaller, quieter point that still knew her face and still answered to it, but without the depth that had once stood behind his eyes. He looked at Sera like a man waking in a room he recognized from long illness: aware, present, intact enough to know what mattered, and altered in ways no one could yet measure.

Ben's throat closed around a curse he didn't say.

Torin pulled free. Alive. His eyes found Sera's face. For a moment—just a moment—the warmth returned. Then it faded to something quieter. He knew her. But the way he knew her was different. Diminished. Present but smaller. The caldera didn't consume him. It replaced the parts that made him Torin.
