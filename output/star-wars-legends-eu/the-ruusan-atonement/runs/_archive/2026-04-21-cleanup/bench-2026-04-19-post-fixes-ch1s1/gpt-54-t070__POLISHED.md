His guard came up late.

The training blade cracked across Ben's right shoulder hard enough to sting through the padded tunic, and the contact tone chirped from both sabers a beat later, cheerful and insulting. He had time for one useless thought—again—before he killed his blade and stepped back.

Across from him, the other Knight lowered his own saber, eyebrows lifting. "You all right?"

"Sure." Ben rolled his shoulder once. The spot burned. "I just thought I'd try a bold new strategy where I let you hit me until your confidence gets dangerous."

The Knight snorted, still watching him a little too closely. Not suspicious, exactly. More the look people got when a lift stopped between floors for half a second and then kept going. "That's three."

Ben knew. He also knew he should have blocked all three. The first one he could have called distraction. The second, sloppy footwork. The third had run out of friendly explanations.

He thumbed the deactivation switch on his saber though it was already dark, a small pointless motion to keep his hand busy. Sweat had turned his palm slick on the hilt. Around them the training salle hummed with late-evening quiet: overhead lights, the faint buzz from inactive remotes docked along the wall, boots scuffing somewhere out in the corridor beyond the sealed doors. The Temple never really slept. It just changed shifts.

"Want another round?" the Knight asked.

Ben almost said yes on reflex. Win the next exchange, smooth it over, walk out with the story repaired. Then the wrongness brushed the edge of his awareness again—late, dragging, like the room had answered after he'd already moved—and his fingers tightened.

"Not tonight." He clipped the saber to his belt. "I'm running on bad caf and less sleep."

"Mm." The Knight gave him a look that said he didn't quite buy it but respected rank enough not to push. Ben was a Knight too, after all, not some apprentice who needed to be marched off to meditation because he'd taken a practice hit badly. "You sure?"

"If I stay, I'll embarrass the Order further."

That got a grin. "I'll try not to report the collapse of Skywalker standards."

"Thanks. Quiet heroism like that is what keeps the Temple running."

The Knight dipped his head in mock gravity, retrieved his water flask from the bench, and headed for the door. It hissed open on the corridor's recycled-air draft and the passing murmur of late foot traffic. A cleaning droid trundled by, gave the threshold an offended beep at having to route around a living being, and kept going. Then the doors sealed again.

Silence settled back over the salle.

Ben stood in the middle of it with the sting in his shoulder and the taste of the lie still on his tongue. Fatigue. Fine. Sure. He could almost admire the efficiency. Short, plausible, and complete nonsense.

His shoulders sagged a fraction once nobody was there to see it.

He crossed to the edge of the mat, stripped off his outer gloves, and set them on the bench beside a towel someone had forgotten earlier. The floor was cool through the thin soles of his training boots. He listened for a moment, not to the corridor this time but to that off-rhythm thread he'd been trying all week to ignore into submission.

It answered him the way it always had lately: not absent, not broken, just wrong. The Force around the Temple should have come to him with a familiarity so ingrained he never had to think about it, the same way he never thought about the timing of a stair step. Instead it kept arriving late, a half-beat behind where his body expected the world to be.

Maybe he was tired. Maybe he was carrying more residue from the last few years than he liked to admit. Maybe everyone who had told him to give it time, meditate, sleep, eat something that had once been alive, and stop glaring at the walls had a point.

Maybe, he thought, he should also consider taking up dejarik because his judgment was clearly flawless.

He exhaled through his nose and stepped back onto the mat.

No blade this time.

He drew the first stance empty-handed except for the saber hilt, inactive and weightless compared to a live line of plasma. Left foot angled. Right shoulder loose despite the ache. He started slowly, moving through the opening sequence of a form he'd known since childhood, letting muscle take over where thought only got in the way.

Step. Turn. Guard high.

For two motions it held.

Then the lag tugged.

Not in his arm. Not in the old training pathways worn deep enough to survive half-asleep. The pull came somewhere less honest, at the place where instinct and the Force usually met cleanly. His wrist turned a fraction late. His weight followed after it should have landed. The next cut traced the correct path and still felt borrowed, like hearing your own voice played back with the timing nudged wrong.

Ben stopped.

The room gave him back the quiet hum of lights and distant lift chimes. Somewhere beyond the walls, someone laughed in the corridor and the sound faded.

He reset and tried again, faster.

The form snapped out sharper this time. He drove through the sequence, boots whispering against the floor, shoulder protesting on the back swing. One, two, pivot, low guard, recover—

There.

The half-beat slipped under him and stole a timing he would once have trusted without looking. He missed nothing visible; anyone watching would have seen a competent run through a standard exercise. Ben felt the gap anyway. It opened and closed between one breath and the next, small and vicious. More than enough to lose a point. More than enough to lose a fight if the wrong opponent chose that moment.

His bare fingers dug into the hilt. He hadn't remembered taking the glove off his saber hand too.

He paced to the edge of the mat, turned, and came back harder. Again. Empty blade, full form. Test the body. Strip out the obvious excuses. If the problem was his shoulder, it would show. If it was fatigue, his footwork would degrade, his balance would smear, the whole thing would go loose around the edges.

Instead the body held.

The miss lived elsewhere.

A cold knot tightened low in his gut with each pass. By the fourth run his feet were gripping the floor so hard his calves had started to burn. He forced himself to slow down before frustration turned the exercise into plain stupidity. The Temple air cooled the sweat on the back of his neck. He could hear his own breathing now, not ragged yet, but heading there.

This week: a mistimed catch during levitation drills. A meditation session that had refused to settle no matter how long he sat with it. A briefing where he'd lost the thread of another Knight's words because the Force around the room had slipped sideways for one maddening instant and left him listening for a note nobody else seemed to hear.

And now this.

He stood still in the center of the salle, saber hilt hanging at his side, and let the last run replay itself through muscle and memory. Not fatigue. Not a bad night. Not the shoulder. He tried the words on in silence and disliked all of them.

The part he disliked most wasn't the problem. It was the next step.

Say something. To whom? The Council? A healer? His father, which came with its own collection of complications. Hello, I know everyone's been chalking up background wrongness in the Force to everything we've all recently survived, but mine has progressed to losing sparring exchanges I should win, and I'd prefer if we could discuss that without anyone wondering whether I'm still carrying damage I haven't admitted to.

That would go well.

He barked out one short laugh, humorless enough to scrape. "Great plan," he muttered to the empty room.

The words vanished into the high ceiling.

Ben lifted the hilt again and set his feet one more time, slower now, almost cautious. He hated that. Caution in a training form felt like apologizing to the floor. But he needed one clean answer, if only for himself.

He moved.

The sequence flowed. Step. Turn. Guard. The salle's familiar presence brushed him late again, the same dragging half-beat, and this time he didn't try to deny it or outrun it or file it under one more temporary inconvenience. He let the truth land where it wanted.

His next motion faltered.

The hilt stopped in mid-cut. Ben held there, arm trembling from the arrested movement, and stared at nothing.

All right, then.

Not fatigue.

The words sat heavy and unwelcome, but cleaner than anything he'd had all week. They stripped away the last of the easy cover stories and left a colder problem in their place. If this had gotten deep enough to reach his timing, deep enough that another Knight could see the result even if he couldn't see the cause, then keeping it private had stopped being discipline somewhere behind him and tipped into stupidity.

He lowered the hilt, slow this time.

Out in the corridor, footsteps approached the salle and paused. Ben turned toward the doors, pulse settling from exertion into something tighter and steadier. Irritation was still there, and the old instinct to get himself under control before anyone looked too closely. But under both of them, thin as wire, another current flickered to life.

Maybe he didn't have to carry this one by himself after all.

The doors slid open.

Luke stood in the doorway with a datapad in one hand and an expression Ben knew meant a mission.
