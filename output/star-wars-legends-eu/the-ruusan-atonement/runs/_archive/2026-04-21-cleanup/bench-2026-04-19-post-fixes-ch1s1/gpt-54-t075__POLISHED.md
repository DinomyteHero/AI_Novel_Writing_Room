Ben's guard dropped half a second late, and the training blade slapped across his right shoulder hard enough to sting through the padded tunic.

He was already turning into the block when it hit. That was the part that made it worse.

The blue practice blade snapped away with a sharp hum. Across from him, the other Knight froze in follow-through, eyebrows lifting before he schooled his face back into something polite.

Ben lowered his own saber a fraction. Sweat had slicked his palm against the hilt. "Well," he said, "that was embarrassing."

The Knight let out a short breath that might have been a laugh if he wasn't trying not to be rude. "Point stands."

"Clearly."

They reset by habit, boots whispering over the salle floor. The overhead panels cast everything in clean white light, too bright for excuses. Ben rolled his shoulder once. The contact still burned. So did the fact that this made three times in a week.

Not three losses. He could live with losing. His father had spent years making sure he learned that early and often.

Three misses.

Tiny ones. A hand arriving late. A pull in the Force showing up behind where it should have been, like the Temple itself had fallen out of step and nobody else had noticed. He'd told himself the first time was lack of sleep. The second time was distraction. The third time was getting difficult to market, even to himself.

The Knight brought his training saber up in salute. Ben mirrored him.

They moved.

A low cut, a pivot, bind, disengage. Ben's body knew the pattern before thought got near it. The salle had been teaching him variations on this rhythm since he was small enough to need both hands on a practice hilt. Boots thudded. Sabers hissed and cracked together. Sweat cooled along his spine under the recycled-air chill.

For four passes, he was fine.

On the fifth, the wrongness brushed the edge of his attention again. Not loud. Not dramatic. Just there—late, always late, the Force in the room arriving a beat behind the motion that ought to have called it. His partner shifted left. Ben read right for one sliver of a second too long.

The Knight's blade tapped his ribs.

"That's two," the man said.

Ben stepped back and cut power. The blue blade collapsed with a pop and a fading whine. "And that's enough for me."

His partner lowered his weapon. "You sure?"

"Unless your long-term training goal is proving I can lose count in public, yes." Ben clipped the hilt to his belt and flexed his fingers. They wanted to stay tight around something. "I'm done."

The Knight hesitated. "You don't usually—"

"Stay up late all week and make excellent life choices?" Ben gave him a brief smile. "Apparently I contain multitudes."

That got the laugh this time, quick and unwilling. The Knight relaxed a little. "You want another round tomorrow?"

"Assuming I recover from this crushing defeat."

"Mm-hm."

Ben bent to collect his water flask from the bench along the wall. The metal was cold and damp against his hand. He took a swallow. The water tasted flat.

When he straightened, the other Knight was already clipping his saber away. No push, no concern dragged into the open where Ben would have to deal with it. One of the perks of Temple life: everybody had learned how to pretend not to notice each other's cracks unless invited.

"Get some sleep," the Knight said on his way to the door.

Ben lifted the flask in acknowledgment. "I'll add it to the schedule."

The door slid shut behind him. The sound seemed too loud in the emptied room.

Silence never lasted in the Temple. Even this late there was movement out in the corridor—muffled foot traffic, the distant chime of a lift, a cleaning droid whining somewhere far enough away to be harmless. Life going on. Shifts changing. People with actual reasons to be tired.

Ben set the flask down again and stood in the middle of the salle under the lights.

Fatigue, he told himself.

That excuse had worn thin enough to see through.

He unhooked the training saber but didn't ignite it. The hilt rested across his palm, familiar in the way old scars were familiar. He took two slow breaths, let them out, and started the first form.

No blade. No opponent. Just lines and turns and the measured placement of his feet on the floor.

If this was muscle, he'd know.

His left foot slid back. His shoulders turned. The hilt traced the first cut through empty air. Barely a whisper from his sleeves. The floor had a little give under the soles of his boots, enough to grip. Good. Grounded.

Second movement. Third.

He reached for the Force the way he always did—not hauling at it, not making a production out of it, just opening the door inside himself that had never really shut—and the room answered wrong.

Late.

His hand had already moved when the answer came, a fraction behind, small and ugly and impossible to pin down. Not absence. Not pain. More like someone in an ensemble clapping on the wrong count and ruining the whole line.

Ben stopped mid-turn.

He stayed that way for a second, hilt angled across his body, listening.

The Temple pressed back around him in all its familiar pieces: the distant traffic in the corridor, the dry edge of filtered air, the faint buzz from the light panels overhead, his own pulse in his wrists. Under all of it, the Force should have sat where it always sat, woven through everything so cleanly his body read it before he had words for it.

Tonight it lagged.

He set his jaw and started again.

First form, slower. Strip it down. No room for excuses if he built the thing one move at a time.

Step. Turn. Cut.

Late.

Recover. Lift. Guard.

Late.

Advance. Rotate. Lower—

The beat slipped under him.

His foot landed a shade too hard. Balance went with it. Not much, not enough to fall, but enough to jar up his leg and bite into his hip. Ben hissed between his teeth and broke off before the next movement.

He looked at the inactive hilt in his hand as if it had betrayed him personally.

"All right," he muttered to it. "That one's getting harder to blame on being dramatic."

The joke landed flat in the empty room.

He paced once across the salle and back. The floor cooled through the thin soles of his boots now that he wasn't moving enough to stay warm. He scrubbed his free hand over the back of his neck. His skin felt too tight there.

This week had been full of almosts. Almost hearing a call before it came through. Almost catching a dropped glass in the refectory before it hit the table. Almost reading an opening in a briefing discussion before someone else took the line. Little misses, the sort no one else would mark because no one else knew where his timing usually sat.

He knew.

That was the problem.

A Skywalker was not supposed to lose track of the Force in the middle of a training exchange in his own Temple. He could hear Mara's voice in his head, dry as sandpaper: Nice theory. Now do something useful with it.

Useful would have been telling someone on day two.

Instead he'd done what any intelligent, well-balanced Jedi Knight with excellent family habits did when something might be wrong with him: kept it private and hoped it got embarrassed enough to leave.

Ben blew out a breath through his nose and moved back to center.

One more time.

He widened his stance and closed his eyes, not for mysticism, just to cut away the easy distractions. The hilt settled into both hands. He let the room find him by sound and pressure and memory. Boots in the corridor. Ventilation in the ceiling. The faint electrical bite in the air from the training remotes docked along the far wall.

Then he moved.

The first three actions held.

On the fourth, the lag brushed him again, closer this time. Not stronger. Closer. His body committed to the turn before the Force arrived with its warning, and the mismatch hit him like missing a stair in the dark. His stomach dropped. Every muscle in his back locked to compensate. The hilt jerked off line.

Ben's eyes snapped open.

He didn't finish the form.

For a long moment he stood there breathing through his nose, hands clamped around the inactive saber, shoulders pulled tight enough to ache. Sweat trickled from his temple to his jaw. The room had not changed. The lights still glared. The corridor still murmured beyond the door. The Force still answered a half-beat late, as steady in its wrongness as the metronome of a bad joke.

Not fatigue, then.

The thought landed and stayed.

His grip eased by degrees. Not because he was calmer. Because squeezing harder wasn't fixing anything.

A cleaning droid rolled past the open transom outside with a brief mechanical chirp, then continued on. Ben watched its reflection smear across the polished wall panel and vanish. The ordinariness of it nearly made him laugh.

He should report it. He should have already. He should also have done a dozen things this week that didn't involve losing to a perfectly competent Knight because the Force had decided to come in late.

The word traumatized never appeared whole in his mind. It didn't have to. It lived in all the gaps around the problem, in other people's careful voices after Abeloth, in the way concern could slide into watchfulness if you gave it room.

Ben lowered the hilt to his side.

The door at the far end opened.

Luke stood in the doorway with a datapad in one hand and an expression Ben knew meant a mission.
