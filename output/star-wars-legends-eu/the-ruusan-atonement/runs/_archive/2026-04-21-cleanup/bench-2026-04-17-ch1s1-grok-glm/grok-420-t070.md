Ben's training blade met the other in a sharp crack. He pivoted, footwork crisp on the polished floor, and pressed the advantage. The salle's air tasted of salt and recycled oxygen. His opponent's saber hummed low, a steady counterpoint to the slap of boots and the distant murmur of other Jedi moving through the Temple corridors.

The wrongness hit like a missed beat in a drumline.

His guard lagged half a second. The training blade whipped past his defense and connected with his left shoulder. Impact jolted down his arm. Ben staggered back, teeth clenched.

"Point," the other Knight said, lowering his weapon.

Ben straightened. The sting radiated outward, but it was nothing compared to the flat, off-key vibration still echoing through him. Third time this week. He deactivated his saber. The hilt clicked off with a soft hiss.

"Enough for tonight," he said. "I'm wiped."

The Knight studied him a moment, then shrugged. "You sure? You usually eat those."

"Body's cashing checks my sleep schedule can't cover." Ben forced a grin that felt like a cracked mask. "Go get dinner. I'll close up."

A nod, a quick salute with the training hilt, and the other man headed for the doors. Footsteps faded. The salle's heavy doors sealed with a pneumatic sigh, leaving Ben in silence broken only by the low drone of the Temple's life-support systems and the persistent, sour note in the Force.

He rolled his shoulder. Muscle protested, but the joint moved fine. No real damage. That wasn't the problem.

Ben set the training saber on the rack and began to pace. The floor tiles felt cool through his boots. Sweat cooled on his neck, his back. He moved to the center of the open space, empty now except for the echo of his own steps. No blade. Just forms.

He dropped into the first sequence. Feet sliding, weight shifting, hands cutting precise arcs where a lightsaber would have been. Muscle memory answered without hesitation. His body knew the patterns cold. Years of repetition had carved them into bone and sinew.

The second sequence flowed smoother. He accelerated, adding spins, low sweeps, explosive transitions. Sweat flew from his hair. His breathing settled into the familiar rhythm of exertion. Everything physical checked out. Heart rate elevated exactly where it should be. Balance perfect. Reflexes sharp enough to shave with.

He moved into the third form. The one Jacen had drilled into him during those long afternoons on Ossus.

The wrongness spiked again.

His left foot dragged a fraction. The timing between hip rotation and shoulder follow-through fractured. Ben caught himself before he actually stumbled, but the error burned. He stopped dead in the middle of the salle, chest heaving.

Not the body. The body was fine.

He stood still, hands loose at his sides. The vibration lingered, a discordant pulse that didn't match anything around him. The Temple's ambient Force presences hummed along in their usual register. Other Jedi meditating in distant chambers, initiates practicing in lower levels, the quiet collective focus of an Order trying to rebuild after too many wars. All of it normal.

Except for that one flat note threaded through everything. Like a string tuned a quarter-step off in an otherwise perfect orchestra. It pulled at his reactions the way a bad hyperdrive motivator pulled a ship off vector. Subtle until it wasn't.

Ben wiped sweat from his eyes with the back of his wrist. The gesture bought him three seconds of ordinary physical sensation. Then the wrongness returned, stronger.

He knew this flavor.

Not the raw chaos of Abeloth. Not the seductive fracture Jacen had worn like a second skin toward the end. Those had been emotional wounds, personal cataclysms that bled into the Force like open arteries. This was different. This felt... engineered. A deliberate removal of something foundational. The way a surgeon might excise a tumor and leave the patient alive but altered.

His stomach tightened. Skywalkers and structural problems in the Force. Family specialty. He could almost hear his father's voice in the back of his head, calm and measured, suggesting meditation, perspective, a longer view.

Ben didn't want a longer view. He wanted the note to stop.

He resumed the forms, slower this time. Bare hands tracing the paths of invisible blades. Each movement precise. Each breath deliberate. He isolated the variables the way his mother had taught him during those brutal GAG years: test one component at a time. Body. Reflexes. Timing. Memory.

All of them passed.

The failure lived somewhere else.

The realization settled over him like permacrete dust after a building collapse. He was the only one who seemed to notice it at this intensity. The other Knights felt the aftershocks of Abeloth, the lingering scars from the Lost Tribe, the normal background radiation of a galaxy that had been through too much. They wrote this off as more of the same.

Ben couldn't.

He kept moving through the sequences. The salle's overhead lights cast long shadows that shifted with him. His pulse beat steady in his ears. The wrongness continued its flat, persistent drone, disrupting nothing visible and everything that mattered.

He had spent years proving he wasn't his cousin. Years learning to trust his own eyes, his own instincts, after Jacen had twisted so many things inside out. Now those same instincts screamed that something fundamental was coming apart. And the Order treated it like background static.

Great.

Ben finished the last form and stood motionless in the center of the salle. Sweat trickled down his spine. The vibration in the Force refused to fade. If anything, it felt closer. More intentional.

He didn't know whether to feel relieved that his perception wasn't failing or terrified that it wasn't.

Boots sounded in the corridor outside. Measured, familiar cadence. The doors opened.

Luke stood in the doorway holding a datapad. His expression was the one that always meant a mission.
