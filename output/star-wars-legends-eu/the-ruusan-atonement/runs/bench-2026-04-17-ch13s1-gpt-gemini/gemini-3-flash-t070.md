The *Steady Returns* hung in low orbit over a world that looked like a postcard for the Expansion Region. Through the viewport, the planet was a vibrant marble of sapphire oceans and emerald forests, marbled with the white wisps of a stable atmosphere. It looked healthy. It looked alive.

"Visuals show a Class-M paradise," Desh Rolan said from the sensor suite. His voice was flat, the tone he used when the data made no sense. "But the spectrograph is reading high-energy static where the carbon signatures should be. The thermal imaging says the surface temperature is forty degrees Celsius, but the atmospheric pressure readings are fluctuating like we’re skimming a gas giant."

Ben Skywalker leaned closer to the transparisteel. "The settlement at the equator. Can you get a lock on the landing pad?"

"I can give you coordinates," Desh said. "But the sensors can't decide if there's a landing pad there or a hole in the crust. The ship's computer is flagging every scan as a hardware error."

"It’s not the hardware," Sera Varik said. She stood behind Ben, her arms crossed tightly over her chest. Her face was a mask of archaic severity. "It is the node. Veraine didn't just damage it. She tore it open."

Torin Hal joined them, his presence usually a warm weight in the Force, a steadying influence that reminded Ben of his father. Today, Torin looked pale. "We have to go down. If this is a field test, we need to know the parameters of the failure."

Kael Drenn remained in the shadows at the back of the bridge, leaning against a bulkhead. "I'm the only one who thinks 'let's go stand in the middle of a reality-warping disaster' is a bad plan? Just checking."

"Noted, Kael," Ben said. He turned away from the deceptive beauty of the planet. "Desh, bring us down. Manual thrusters if the nav-computer starts seeing ghosts."

The descent was unnervingly smooth. There was no turbulence, no screech of ionized air against the hull. They settled onto a duracrete pad that looked pristine. When the boarding ramp hissed open, the air that rushed in smelled of damp earth and blooming jasmine.

Ben stepped out first. He stopped at the base of the ramp.

The settlement was a collection of pre-fab modular homes and a central administrative hub. Everything was intact. No craters, no scorch marks, no signs of struggle. But the vegetation was wrong. Thick, oily vines climbed the walls of the buildings, but they weren't seeking the sun. They grew in thick clusters toward the shadows, twisting into the dark corners beneath the eaves. Large, pale flowers bloomed under the shade of a cargo transport, their petals translucent and pulsing with a slow, rhythmic light.

"The birds," Sera whispered, stepping down beside him. "Listen."

Ben listened. There was no wind. No rustle of leaves. No chirping of insects or the distant cry of a predator. The silence was heavy, sitting on his eardrums like water.

"Maybe they evacuated," Torin suggested, though he sounded like he didn't believe it.

"Without their ships?" Desh pointed to a row of airspeeders parked in a neat line. "Without their personal gear?" He held up a handheld chrono. "Ben, look at this."

The second hand on the chrono jerked forward three seconds, paused for five, then spun backward. 

"Time is... drifting," Desh said. He rubbed his chest, his brow furrowed. "Do you feel that? It’s like I’ve lost something important, but I can’t remember what it was. It’s like grief, but there’s nothing to be sad about."

Ben didn't answer. He was looking at a tree near the edge of the pad. Its leaves were a deep, healthy green, but as he watched, one leaf detached and floated upward, drifting toward the clouds until it vanished from sight.

He needed to center himself. He needed to know what he was standing in. 

Ben closed his eyes and reached out for the Force.

Usually, the Force was a river. Sometimes it was a storm, sometimes a calm pool, but it always had a direction. It had a texture—the cool clarity of the Light, the oily heat of the Dark. It was a lattice that connected the leaf to the tree and Ben to the galaxy. It provided a sense of *ought*. This is right. That is wrong. This way leads to peace.

He reached out, and the world vanished.

The Force was there. It was everywhere. It was a roaring, limitless ocean of power, more present than he had ever felt it in his life. But it had no shape. It was like being submerged in a tank of light with no objects to reflect it. There was no 'up'. No 'down'. No sense of the people standing next to him. 

He tried to find Sera’s disciplined spark or Torin’s warmth. He found nothing but raw, unmediated energy. The moral compass he had relied on since he was a child—the intuitive nudge that told him where he stood in the universe—simply stopped existing. 

He felt a terrifying surge of vertigo. Without the lattice to hold him, Ben felt like he was falling into himself. The energy around him was a tool with no handle, a fire with no heat. He could pull on it, he could use it to level the buildings or shatter the stars, and the Force would give him no feedback. It wouldn't resist him. It wouldn't warn him. It was just... potential.

"Ben?" 

Sera’s voice sounded like it was coming from the bottom of a well. He opened his eyes and saw her hand on his arm, but he couldn't feel her presence in the Force. She was just a shape. A collection of matter. 

"It's gone," Ben whispered.

"The Force?" Torin asked, his voice tight with alarm.

"No," Ben said, and the sound of his own voice frightened him. It was too clear. "The Force is here. But the rules are gone. The handrail is gone."

He looked at his hands. He could feel the power humming in his marrow, but it was deaf and blind. He could kill everyone in this plaza and the Force wouldn't scream. It wouldn't even ripple. It would just be.

"I don't like this," Kael said, his usual sarcasm replaced by a jagged edge of panic. "I feel like I'm dissolving. Like if I stop looking at my feet, they'll just stop being there."

"We're in the wound," Sera said. She looked at Ben, her eyes searching his. "This is what Veraine wants. A world where the Jedi don't matter because the Light doesn't exist. Where the Sith don't matter because there is no Dark to crave."

Ben tried to perform a basic breathing exercise, a foundational Jedi meditation to ground his mind. *There is no emotion, there is peace.*

The words felt hollow. They were just sounds. Without the Force to echo the truth of the mantra back to him, it was just a lie he was telling himself. 

He looked at Torin Hal. The older man was staring at the ground, his shoulders slumped. Torin had spent his life believing in the nobility of the Jedi Lords, in the inherent rightness of their burden. In this silence, that burden looked like a pathetic delusion. 

Ben realized he was thinking about how easy it would be to leave them here. If he took the *Steady Returns*, he could get back to the Alliance, back to his father. He could leave the survivors of a dead era to rot in their own failed history. 

The thought was sharp and cold. Usually, the Force would have buffered a thought like that, a slight nudge of shame or a reminder of his connection to others. Now, there was nothing. No judgment. No correction. 

He looked at Desh, who was still staring at his skipping chrono with a look of profound, hollow sadness. Desh wasn't a Jedi, but he was feeling the loss of the lattice too. The universe had lost its rhythm. The music had stopped, and everyone was just standing in the dark, waiting for a beat that would never come.

Ben realized the true cost of what Veraine was doing. She wasn't just killing people. She was removing the context of their lives. She was stripping away the invisible architecture that made sense of suffering and joy alike.

He was alone. Truly, completely alone inside his own skull. 

In the silence of the de-structured Force, Ben hears himself think without the safety net of Force intuition for the first time. The thoughts are his. All of them. Including the ones the Force usually softens.
