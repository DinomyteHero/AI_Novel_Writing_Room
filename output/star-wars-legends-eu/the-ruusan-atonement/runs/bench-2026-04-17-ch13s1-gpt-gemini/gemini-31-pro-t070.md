The planet filling the *Steady Returns*’ primary viewport offered an image of absolute tranquility. Swaths of emerald forest broke against deep blue oceans, marbled with pristine white cloud cover. From orbit, the Breach Zone looked like a vacation world, a temperate paradise untouched by the relentless industry of the core systems.

Desh Rolan tapped the primary sensor display, his knuckles rapping sharply against the glass. "The eyes lie."

"Define lie," Ben said. He leaned over the co-pilot's chair, studying the scrolling telemetry.

"Atmospheric composition reads as a toxic soup of heavy metals on the primary band," Desh said, his voice carrying the dry, practical cadence of a man who trusted numbers over aesthetics. "Switch to the secondary band, and it says the atmosphere is pure, unadulterated nitrogen. The thermal imaging is completely inverted. According to this console, those blue oceans are currently boiling, and the equatorial jungles are sitting at absolute zero."

Ben checked the backup array. The screens flickered, throwing contradictory graphs across the illuminated dash. "Equipment failure?"

"I ran a full diagnostic ten minutes ago. The hardware is flawless." Desh crossed his arms. His tactical jacket rustled in the quiet cockpit. "The environment is broken. The sensors don't know how to interpret a reality that changes its own rules every three seconds."

Torin Hal moved into the cockpit, projecting his usual effortless authority. He placed a heavy, reassuring hand on the back of the pilot's seat. "Veraine didn't just damage this node. She annihilated it. We are looking at the aftermath of a localized lattice collapse."

"A field test," Sera Varik corrected. She stood in the doorway, her spine rigidly straight. Her archaic robes remained immaculate, a physical manifestation of her refusal to compromise. "Before she attempts the same at Veranthos. We need to see exactly what she accomplished here, and what it cost the local space."

Kael Drenn slouched against the aft bulkhead. He didn't look at the viewport. He kept his gaze fixed on the deck plates. "Do we? Because landing a ship on a planet that actively breaks sensor logic sounds like a spectacular way to become a permanent part of the local geology."

"We have to know what we are up against, Kael," Torin said. He offered the younger man a warm, paternal smile. "I will take point once we are down. I'll sweep the perimeter. Stay close to me, and you'll be fine."

Ben watched Sera's posture stiffen. Torin always assumed the burden of leadership, always positioned himself as the invincible shield. It was a habit born of a thousand years of Jedi Lord entitlement, a reflexive need to control the board by taking all the risk. He couldn't see how much it grated on the others.

"Nobody is taking point alone," Ben said, keeping his tone even. "We move as a single unit. Desh, find us a stable landing footprint in the main settlement plaza. Bring us down."

The repulsorlifts whined in protest as the *Steady Returns* descended through the atmosphere. Turbulence rattled the hull, not from wind, but from localized gravity shears that tugged the ship in conflicting directions. Desh wrestled the yoke, his face an impassive mask of concentration, until the landing struts finally slammed into the cracked permacrete of the settlement plaza.

The boarding ramp lowered with a heavy, metallic clank.

Ben walked down the incline, his boots echoing in the stillness. The air smelled of damp earth and crushed leaves. It lacked the ozone tang of a storm or the sulfurous reek of industrial decay. It just smelled like dirt.

The settlement stood largely intact. Modular housing units formed neat, utilitarian rows along the perimeter of the plaza. Weathering marked the facades, but no blast damage, scorch marks, or structural collapses marred the architecture. It looked entirely peaceful.

Yet the longer Ben looked, the more the geometry of the place fractured.
