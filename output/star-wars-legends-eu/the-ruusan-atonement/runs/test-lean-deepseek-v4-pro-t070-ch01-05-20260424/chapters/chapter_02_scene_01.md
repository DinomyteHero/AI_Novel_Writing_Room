The hyperspace reversion hit like a physical blow.

The *Steady Returns* shuddered out of the tunnel with a groan that ran through the deck plates and up into Ben's spine, and the caf he'd been holding — lukewarm, third cup of the transit, wedged against the copilot's console in a spare mug that had seen better decades — sloshed across the instrument panel in a brown wave.

"Kriff," Ben said, and meant it.

Desh's hands were already moving across the nav controls, steady despite the rattle. "That wasn't me."

"I didn't say it was."

"You were thinking it."

Ben grabbed the sleeve of his tunic and started mopping caf off the console before it could seep into anything important. The fabric soaked up liquid and left streaks across the status displays. The chrono flickered — once, twice — and then steadied. The nav readout cycled through a recalibration sequence that Ben had seen Desh run a hundred times and had never seen take this long.

The cockpit smelled like burnt caf and recycled air and the faint copper tang of that power coupling Ben had noted on the preflight. The hyperspace blue had vanished from the viewport, replaced by the starfield — unfamiliar stars, Rimward stars, the kind of stars that didn't show up on standard nav charts unless someone had paid for the update.

Desh tapped the sensor panel with two fingers. The motion was patient. The kind of patient that meant he'd already seen something he didn't like.

"Diagnostic says the jump coordinates were clean," he said. "Nav computer didn't burp. Power coupling's throwing the same fluctuation you flagged back at the Temple, but that's not what kicked us out."

"What did?"

"Good question." Desh pulled up the sensor log and let it scroll. Numbers Ben didn't need to read. "Chrono drift. Started about twenty minutes into the jump. Small at first — microseconds. Then it started accumulating."

Ben finished wiping the console and sat back. His sleeve was soaked. The caf had gotten into the seam between two panels, and he could smell it starting to burn off the housing. "Chrono drift doesn't kick a ship out of hyperspace."

"This one did." Desh highlighted a section of the log. "It's not random. Look at the bearing."

Ben looked. The drift wasn't scattered across the sensor array — it was clustering along a single vector, a narrow cone pointing Rimward and slightly spinward, the same direction the *Steady Returns* had been flying since they'd cleared the Hydian Way.

The same direction the wrongness had been pulling since Coruscant.

He didn't say that. He sat with his caf-soaked sleeve and the smell of burnt wiring and the pressure in his chest that had stopped being ambient somewhere between the Temple hangar and the first jump point and had started being directional instead. A compass needle. A hand on his shoulder. A thing he couldn't shake and couldn't name and couldn't put into a sentence that wouldn't sound like he was losing his grip.

"Ben."

"Mm."

"That's the third time the sensors have flagged that bearing since we left." Desh's voice was dry. Casual. The voice of someone who'd learned to deliver bad news without making it sound like bad news, because panic was a luxury intelligence operatives skipped. "First time it was a blip. Second time I thought it was the coupling. Third time, it's a pattern."

"Patterns have causes."

"This one has a bearing." Desh reset the sensor array and watched the drift re-form along the same vector. "It's not random noise. It's directional. Consistent. And it's getting stronger the further Rimward we go."

Ben's jaw tightened. He could feel the wrongness pressing against the edge of his perception — not pain, not urgency, just *presence*, the way a sound you'd stopped noticing would suddenly resolve into a voice. His body had been tracking it since Coruscant. His shoulders had been tight since the Temple hangar. His hands had been white on the controls since the jump to hyperspace, and he'd told Desh he was fine, and he'd said it twice, and neither time had been true.

"Same bearing as the flagged reports," Ben said. His voice came out level. Good. "The ones Luke loaded."

"Same bearing as the survey route." Desh turned in his seat. The copilot's chair creaked — old ship, old components, the kind of wear that came from a vessel that had carried people for a long time. "You want to tell me what we're actually looking for, or should I keep guessing?"

Ben didn't answer right away. He was watching the sensor display, the drift clustering along its vector, the chrono still flickering at the edge of his vision. The wrongness pulled along the same line. He could feel it now — not as a general sourness but as a specific pressure, a physical thing, the way a missing handrail felt when you reached for it in the dark and found nothing.

"I don't know," he said. "Something's wrong."

"That's what you said before we left."

"It's still true."

Desh looked at him for a long moment. The cockpit hummed around them — the environmental systems, the power coupling, the faint vibration of a ship that had just been yanked out of hyperspace by something its nav computer couldn't explain. Through the viewport, the starfield hung motionless. The wrongness pressed against Ben's perception like a second gravity.

"All right," Desh said. "Let me run the full diagnostic. We're not jumping again until I know what kicked us out."

"Fine."

"And you're going to sit there and not tell me why your knuckles are white."

Ben looked down at his hands. They were gripping the armrests. He hadn't noticed.

"That's the third time," Desh said. "Since we left. White knuckles."

"I'm fine."

"That's the fourth time you've said that. It wasn't true the first three."

The diagnostic chimed. The sensor log kept scrolling. Ben's caf had dried on the console in a brown stain that would probably be there for the rest of the run, because Desh never cleaned the cockpit and Ben was too tired to care. The wrongness pulled along the bearing — the same bearing, the one his body had been tracking since Coruscant, the one he'd been trying to ignore by staying in motion.

Desh tapped the sensor panel again. The drift re-formed. Same vector. Same intensity. Same quiet refusal to be anything other than what it was.

"It's got teeth," Desh said. His voice was still dry, still casual, but there was something underneath it now — the specific tone of someone who'd served in something the Order had dissolved, who knew what it meant to watch a problem get worse and not have the vocabulary to name it. "Whatever it is. It's not going to stop."

Ben's hands tightened on the armrests.

The nav display pinged. The first survey world had resolved on the long-range scanners — a small, unremarkable dot at the edge of the sensor cone, right along the bearing. The drift intensified. The chrono flickered again.

Ben didn't move. He sat with the pressure in his chest and the wrongness pulling toward the world on the scanners and the weight of something he couldn't name settling into place like a door closing behind him.

The bearing was the one he'd been carrying since Coruscant. The one he'd been trying to ignore. The one that had stopped being ambient and started being a vector, a destination, a thing that could not be outrun.

Desh was watching him. The sensor log was still scrolling. The first survey world hung on the display like a question Ben didn't know how to answer.

His hands tightened on the armrests.
