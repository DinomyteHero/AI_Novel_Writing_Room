Ben's guard dropped.

It was a half-second late — the kind of mistake a Knight didn't make, not in a training salle, not against Rendell, not in the third exchange of a form Ben had been running since he was fifteen. The training blade caught him across the shoulder with a sharp *crack* and the salle's ambient light flickered blue against the walls.

Ben stepped back, saber still humming, and registered the hit the way he'd been trained to: clean failure, own it, move on. His shoulder stung. The wrongness pulled at his attention like a note played slightly flat — there and gone, a half-beat lag in the ambient field that had been tugging at the edges of his perception all week.

Rendell deactivated his blade. "You all right?"

Third time this week. The first had been in solo practice — easy to dismiss. The second had been against a training remote Ben had been beating since he was a Padawan — embarrassing but private. This was neither.

"Fine." Ben thumbed his own saber off and rolled his shoulder. "Late night. Let's call it."

Rendell hesitated. He was a solid Knight, a few years older than Ben, the kind of sparring partner who pushed hard enough to be useful without turning every session into a statement. He didn't press. He also didn't look convinced.

"Sure." He clipped his saber to his belt. "Same time next week?"

"Count on it."

Ben kept his tone light — the specific register of a man who'd been tired all week and wasn't going to make it anyone else's problem. It was almost true. He *had* been tired all week. The wrongness had been costing him sleep, sharpening his attention at odd hours, pulling his focus sideways during meditation until he'd stopped trying to meditate at all. Fatigue was a reason Rendell would accept. Fatigue was normal.

The wrongness was not.

Rendell gave him a nod and headed for the door. His boots echoed on the salle floor — a clean, professional sound, a Knight leaving a training session at the end of a long shift. The door slid shut behind him.

Ben stood alone in the empty salle.

The Temple at this hour was quiet but not silent. Somewhere down the corridor a cleaning droid hummed past, its motivator a low whine against the stone. The turbolift chimed — someone heading up from the archives, probably, or a late-shift Knight catching a meal before the refectory closed. Coruscant's night cycle pressed against the Temple's walls, a city that never stopped breathing, and the salle held the residue of a dozen earlier sessions — sweat and ozone and the faint scorched-durasteel smell of training blades.

Ben didn't reignite his saber.

He moved instead to the center of the floor and settled into the opening stance of Form III — feet set, weight balanced, empty hand extended as though the hilt were still in his grip. He'd run this sequence a thousand times. His body knew it the way it knew how to breathe.

He began.

The first movement was clean. The second, precise. His body flowed through the form without the blade's weight, without the hum, without anything but muscle memory and the cool recycled air of the salle. He let his attention drift outward, past his body, past the floor, into the ambient field that saturated the Temple — the living presence of the Force as it moved through the stone and the night and the city beyond.

And there it was.

The lag.

It arrived between the third movement and the fourth — a half-beat where the Force should have been present and wasn't, a note that arrived late, a rhythm his body had known by reflex for years suddenly missing its downbeat. His foot landed. His arm completed the block. But the field hadn't been there to meet him. It had come after.

Ben stopped moving.

He stood in the center of the salle, breathing hard, and felt the wrongness settle into his chest like a tightening he couldn't shake. Not fatigue. Not a bad week. Not the residue of Abeloth or the Vong War or any of the other crises the Order had been attributing the background disturbance to.

Something was wrong with the Force.

He didn't articulate it. He didn't have the shape of it yet — couldn't have put it into words if Rendell had walked back through the door and asked. But his body knew. His body had been telling him all week, and now it had told him in front of a witness, and the only thing worse than knowing something was wrong was knowing it alone.

The cleaning droid hummed past again in the corridor. The turbolift chimed. The Temple went about its night, indifferent.

Ben let his empty hand drop to his side.

The door slid open.

Luke Skywalker stood in the doorway with a datapad in one hand and an expression Ben had seen a dozen times before — the specific look of a Jedi Master who had a mission and had already decided who was taking it. He was dressed simply, no robes, just the tunic and belt of a man who'd been working late and hadn't bothered to change. His presence in the Force was steady and warm, the way it always was, and Ben felt the wrongness recede slightly — not gone, but pushed back by the sheer familiar weight of his father's attention.

"Ben."

"Luke."

Luke glanced at the salle — the empty floor, the unlit training sabers on the rack, the hour. "Still training?"

"Done, actually." Ben kept his voice even. "Rendell left a few minutes ago."

Luke nodded. He didn't ask how the session had gone. He didn't ask why Ben was standing alone in an empty salle with no blade ignited. He just held up the datapad.

"I need you for a survey run."

Ben felt something shift in his chest — relief and reluctance tangled together, the hope that he might not be alone with this and the cost of what it would take to say so. He didn't say so. Not yet.

"What kind of survey?"

"The kind that leaves tonight." Luke stepped into the salle and handed him the datapad. "There's a route I want you to look at. Outer Rim. Nothing urgent, but I'd rather have your eyes on it than someone else's."

Ben took the datapad. The screen was already lit — a star chart, a plotted course, a bearing that ran out toward the Rim and kept going.

The wrongness pulled at his attention.

It wasn't ambient anymore. It had a direction.
