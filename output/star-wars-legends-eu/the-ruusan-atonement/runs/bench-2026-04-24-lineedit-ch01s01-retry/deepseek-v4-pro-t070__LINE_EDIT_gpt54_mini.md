Ben’s guard dropped a half-second late.

Rendell’s training blade clipped him across the shoulder with a sharp crack that cut cleanly through the salle’s quiet. It hit harder than a sparring touch should have, the sort of contact that meant the strike was already committed before Ben’s block even started to move.

He stepped back and rolled the shoulder once, just enough to work the sting out. The fault tugged at him even now — not a sound exactly, more like the salle’s steady Force presence arriving after his body expected it, one beat behind the rhythm he’d trusted for years. A note played flat. A note played late.

Third time this week.

Rendell thumbed his training saber off and pushed damp hair off his forehead. “You all right?”

“Fine.” Ben kept his voice light and clipped his own blade to his belt in one practiced motion that was meant to look effortless. “Call it fatigue. Long week.”

It wasn’t quite a lie. The week had been long. He’d been tired since Tuesday, or maybe since the first time the disturbance started catching at the edge of his awareness like a question he couldn’t quite hear. Fatigue was the sort of answer another Knight accepted. It didn’t require explanation, or honesty, or the irritating business of admitting he couldn’t pin the problem down.

Rendell hesitated, saber still in hand. Ben could feel the question in the pause before it became words.

“Another round?” Rendell asked.

“Tomorrow.” Ben gave him a smile that was more reflex than humor. “Let me get some sleep first.”

Rendell nodded, apparently satisfied, or at least willing not to push, and crossed to the rack to set his saber back in place. His boots thudded softly on the padded floor. In the corridor outside, a cleaning droid purred past with a faint mechanical whine, and farther off a turbolift chimed as it arrived at another level. Late-shift traffic still moved through the Temple — Knights, Masters, support staff — the building settling into the quiet stretch between evening and night.

Ben stayed where he was until Rendell’s footsteps faded.

Then the smile fell away.

His shoulder ached where the training blade had landed. Not true pain; the blades were tuned to sting, not injure. It was the hard, clean flash of a mistake his reflexes should never have allowed. He’d been blocking that strike since he was fourteen. The form was muscle memory, the timing something his body knew before his mind caught up.

Tonight his body had arrived late, and the gap had been enough.

He paced once around the salle, then again, marking out the space by habit. The room was empty now — no witness, no one waiting for him to laugh it off, no rematch to force, no explanation to offer. Just Ben, the quiet hum of recycled air, and that half-step delay in the background of everything.

He stopped in the center of the training circle and lifted his saber without igniting it.

The form was an old one, something his father had taught him years ago, a slow sequence built on precision rather than speed. Ben moved into the first stance, then the second, his body sliding into the positions from memory while his attention stayed on the field around him. The salle felt steady, familiar, the same quiet current he’d known since he was old enough to notice it.

He reached the third stance and felt the lag.

Not dramatic. Just a half-second slip between his movement and the Force’s response — like hearing accompaniment come in behind the beat, the notes right and the timing off. He finished the sequence anyway, moving through each position slowly, and the delay followed him from one transition to the next.

It wasn’t in him.

The realization struck less like thought than impact — a tightening in his chest, a narrowing of focus that felt almost like the first breath before a fight. He’d been carrying the problem all week as if it were his own failure, some frayed bit of perception, some aftereffect of old troubles the rest of the Order had already set aside. But his body knew this form. His body had been doing it since before he could read. His body was on time.

The Force wasn’t.

Ben stood still in the middle of the training circle, unlit saber in hand, and kept himself from putting a name to what he felt. *Dread* was too neat, too polished — the kind of word that belonged in a report, not in an empty salle with a cleaning droid humming somewhere down the corridor. He wasn’t ready to say it. He wasn’t sure he wanted to.

But he stopped pretending it was fatigue.

Something was off in the Force itself, something that had been nagging at him since the week began and had now cost him beats he had no business missing. He still didn’t know what it was, or why nobody else seemed to notice. He didn’t know whether the trouble was out there in the galaxy or in his own head — whether he was sensing something real or whether the years since Abeloth had left a crack in him that was only now showing through.

The not-knowing felt worse than the miss.

He ran the form again, faster this time, and the delay was still there — a half-beat behind every transition, the Force reaching its own rhythm late. His body adjusted on instinct, compensating for what was actually happening instead of what should have been there, and even that felt like failure.

He stopped mid-stance and lowered the saber.

The salle had gone quiet. The cleaning droid had moved on. The turbolifts had stopped chiming, and the late-shift foot traffic was thinning as the hour grew later. Ben stood alone in the empty training circle, breathing evenly, and let the problem sit there without trying to talk himself out of it.

He didn’t know what it was. He didn’t know why he was the only one who felt it. He didn’t know whether saying it out loud would make it real or just make him sound like a Knight who couldn’t trust his own senses.

But it wasn’t fatigue. It wasn’t him.

The door hissed open behind him.

Ben turned. Luke stood in the doorway, datapad in one hand, the other loose at his side. His expression was calm — the sort that meant he’d already looked at whatever was on the datapad and was thinking several steps beyond it. His presence in the Force was steady and warm, broad in the way it always was, and for one brief moment Ben felt the lag recede as if the weight of his father’s attention had shoved it back.

Then Luke met his eyes, and the expression shifted. Barely. Just enough.

Mission.

Ben clipped the saber to his belt and faced the door.
