The hangar bay smelled of scorched coolant and recycled air, the metallic tang common to every military-grade facility Ben had ever known. He moved through the space with the ease of long habit, running his pre-flight on the *Steady Returns* while the bay crew flowed around him like water around stone.

His datapad sat propped against the landing strut.

He had been staring at the contacts list for four minutes.

Three names. All Jedi Knights. All qualified—more than qualified, if he were being precise. Fen Karris had completed two Outer Rim surveys in the last year. Darro Vel had field experience with anomalous Force phenomena stretching back a decade. Lira Sond had partnered with Ben twice before; she knew his operational rhythm without needing it explained.

His thumb hovered over Fen's name. The contact record glowed soft blue in the hangar's shadows. One tap and Fen would say yes. Fen always said yes.

He scrolled past.

Darro's file opened automatically, the Temple's predictive interface attempting to be helpful. Ben closed it. He knew the contents: three papers on Force anomalies and a reputation for meticulous analysis. Darro was the kind of partner who would want to discuss implications.

Lira was the obvious choice. Familiar. Competent. Already briefed on his style. Ben's thumb stopped on her name for three seconds before he forced it to move.

The rationalization assembled itself without effort. He needed someone grounded. Someone who wouldn't spend the flight parsing survey data through Temple dogma. A Jedi would want to report back, to loop in the Council, to debate the philosophical weight of every new reading. He had just come from a briefing; he didn't need a second one on board.

The logic was clean. It felt like the truth the way a well-fitted lie always does.

He scrolled further.

The name he stopped on lacked a Jedi rank. It lacked almost everything—just a comm frequency, a sector, and the word *private* where the affiliation field should have been.

*Desh Rolan.*

Ben's chest tightened. He recognized the sensation: the moment before stepping off a ledge he had been circling. He tapped the call icon before the feeling could resolve into a reason to stop.

***

The comm connected after six seconds. Audio came through tinny and compressed, the signature lag of a long-range relay bouncing off multiple nodes.

"Rolan." Flat. Awake, despite the hour differential between Coruscant and wherever Desh was currently hiding.

"It's Ben."

A pause. Not surprise—Desh didn't do surprise, or at least he never let Ben see it. "What'd you break?"

The question landed like a test. Desh was asking what was wrong instead of asking who was calling. "Nothing yet. I need a co-pilot. Outer Rim survey run. Two, maybe three weeks."

"Survey run." The skepticism in those two words was heavy. Desh knew Ben didn't call for routine jobs. Desh knew Ben didn't call *him* for routine jobs.

"Survey run," Ben confirmed, keeping his voice level. The hangar noise continued around him—indifferent, constant.

Another pause, shorter this time. Something mechanical clattered in the background—a rhythmic knock, perhaps a diagnostic cycle or an engine running rough. Desh was always elbow-deep in machinery. "Sector?"

"Darpa."

The mechanical sound stopped. In that silence, Ben heard what Desh wasn't saying: *That's not a sector anyone surveys.*

"No one goes there for fun," Desh said.

"Good thing I'm not doing it for fun."

Desh made a sound that wasn't quite a laugh. "What's the kit list?"

"Standard. Sensor package, long-range comm array, emergency medical. Nothing that draws attention."

"Timeline?"

"I leave in forty minutes. Meet me at Terminus Station. Forty-eight hours from now, give or take."

The mechanical sound resumed. Ben listened to the clatter and waited. He had made the pitch. Either it landed or it didn't. The waiting was the point—the space where Desh decided whether to ask the question Ben was braced for.

"I'm wrapping a job in Tion," Desh said. "Nothing I can't hand off."

Relief flooded Ben's shoulders, immediate and unwelcome. He pushed it down. "I know."

"Rate's gone up since last time."

"I know that too."

"Just checking." A pause followed—the specific quality of a man who had already decided and was taking the time to let the other person think he hadn't. "Terminus Station. Forty-eight hours."

"I'll send the berth coordinates."

"Don't bother. I'll find the ship." The comm clicked off.

Ben set the datapad against the landing strut. His hand was steady. He returned to his pre-flight checks.

***

Desh hadn't asked why he wasn't bringing a Jedi.

Ben had been ready for the question. He had prepared three different answers, each technically accurate, each designed to redirect before the real answer surfaced. He had been braced for it the way one braced for a door that always stuck—the automatic lean-forward before touching the handle.

Desh hadn't asked.

He never did. That was the thing about Desh: he worked with what was in front of him, not with what was missing from the picture. It was the quality that had made him dangerous in the old security apparatus and useful afterward. He didn't need the whole story to work with the parts he had.

And that was the problem. That was exactly the problem.

Ben sealed the aft cargo bay and moved forward. The hangar noise surrounded him—the whine of repulsorlifts, the clang of a tool dropped three bays over, a ground crew arguing about load distribution in clipped technical shorthand. Ordinary sounds. The Force sang in the Temple's bones overhead, generations of Jedi compressed into a warmth at the base of his skull, as familiar as a callus.

Underneath it, something else.

Three months of it. It didn't respond to the Temple's harmonics. It sat below the song like a weight pressed against the underside of a hull plate—constant, structural, wrong in the specific way things are wrong when they are about to give.

He climbed into the cockpit and ran the final sequence.

He had watched others follow their own perceptions into darkness. Jacen had followed them. Vestara had followed them. He knew where that path led. He had stood close enough to see the drop.

The pre-flight finished clean. Ben requested departure clearance.

He was not like them. He was not bringing a Jedi because he needed someone grounded, someone who wouldn't—

Temple Control approved his clearance. He guided the *Steady Returns* out of the bay.

***

Coruscant fell away in layers. The hangar's recycled warmth gave way to the cold of the upper atmosphere, then to the dark beyond. The planet's light-grid shrank to a curve and then to a point as he climbed. He held the ship steady through the traffic lanes and let autopilot take over at the boundary marker.

The wrongness remained.

He had half-expected it to ease with distance from the Temple. Some Force phenomena were site-specific, tied to a location's history or a structure's accumulated resonance. This one didn't ease. It sat at the base of his skull and pressed, patient and structural.

He checked his heading. Confirmed the coordinates for Terminus Station. Set the hyperdrive to calculate the jump.

The calculation finished. He wrapped his hands around the levers.

The starlines stretched.

In the moment between realspace and hyperspace—the fraction of a second where the ship existed in neither—the wrongness shifted.

It didn't grow louder or quieter. It moved. The static pressure resolved into something with an axis, a bearing, a pull. It was the way a sound previously heard as noise suddenly sorts itself into a word.

Hyperspace closed around the ship. Blue light. Silence.

The wrongness held its direction.

Ben stared at the tunnel of light ahead. His hands remained on the controls. He had left Coruscant. He had made a practical choice about his crew and filed his departure clearance and pointed himself toward Terminus Station.

He had not expected the wrongness to know where he was going before he did.
