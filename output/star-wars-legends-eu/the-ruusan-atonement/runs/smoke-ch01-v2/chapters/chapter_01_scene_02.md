The datapad landed on the desk between them with a soft click, blue light washing up across Luke's face.

Ben picked it up.

Seventeen survey reports, all flagged with the same classification: *anomalous Force reading, origin undetermined, priority: routine.* The archivist who'd processed most of them had signed off with a single line of notes each. He scrolled through the first few — Outer Rim coordinates, dates spanning eight months, the dry language of Knights who'd logged something strange and moved on because they didn't have a framework to explain it.

His father's office was quiet around him. The holocomm on the desk showed a standby light. A half-empty cup of something sat near Luke's elbow, long gone cold.

"Pellan's report is in there," Ben said. Not a question.

"Third from the bottom."

He found it. *Absent signal rather than corrupted signal.* He'd read it twice already, in the archives, and had thought at the time that Pellan had gotten closer than anyone else to naming the thing accurately. Seeing it here, in a file his father had assembled independently, tightened something in his chest.

He zoomed in on three of the readings — Pellan's, and two others from the previous month, one near Bonadan and one from a surveyor who'd been charting the Perlemian corridor. All three had the same quality when he mapped them against his own perceptions: not distortion, not damage. Removal.

"These three," Ben said. "They're not reading disturbance. They're reading absence."

"That's one interpretation." Luke's voice was even. "The archivist flagged them as post-crisis resonance. Lingering effects from—"

"From Abeloth. I know." Ben set the datapad on the desk. "That's what I assumed for the first month."

A pause. Outside the high window, Coruscant's perpetual nightglow pressed against the transparisteel — orange and white, the city running its routines regardless.

"You've been tracking this for a month," Luke said.

"Six weeks."

He watched his father absorb that without reaction. Luke's presence in the Force was what it had always been — vast and warm and steady, the thing Ben had navigated by since childhood, the fixed point he'd always been able to find. Underneath it, barely perceptible unless you knew to look, the flat note was there too. Present even here, in the center of the Temple, a faint sourness under everything.

"You feel it," Ben said.

Luke's hands folded on the desk. "Yes."

"And you think it's residual."

"I think it's consistent with what we've seen from sites like Nathema. The Force doesn't recover cleanly from that category of wound. There can be echoes for—"

"This isn't an echo." Ben kept his voice level. "Echoes are noise. This is the opposite of noise. I've felt noise — I've felt what Jacen left behind, what the Maw was like during the Abeloth crisis, what Vestara's training grounds felt like when we found them. All of that was loud. All of it pushed back." He paused. "This doesn't push back. It just isn't there."

Something moved in Luke's expression. Not disagreement. Closer to the look he got when he was testing whether someone had actually thought something through versus arrived at a conclusion they wanted.

"That's a meaningful distinction," Luke said.

"It's the only distinction that matters."

The silence that followed wasn't hostile. It had the quality of two people who'd had versions of this conversation before, who knew how to sit with each other's assessments without one of them having to concede immediately. Ben had grown up in that silence. He'd learned to read it the way other people learned to read faces.

His father believed him. That was the part he hadn't been sure of.

Luke reached over and turned the datapad to face him, pulling up a star chart overlay. The flagged reports formed a loose corridor — Outer Rim, tracing roughly along the Perlemian before curving toward the Deep Core approaches.

"I need someone to survey the bearing in person," Luke said. "Ground-truth the readings. The reports we have are incidental — Knights who noticed something while they were already in the area. I need systematic observation, Force-sensitive, experienced enough to distinguish signal categories." He looked up. "The mission profile is a survey run. Twelve systems, four weeks, standard reporting protocol."

Ben looked at the chart. The corridor was clean, no active conflict zones, nothing that would require more than a single ship. Practical. Efficient.

*He's giving me something to do with it,* he thought. *He's been sitting on this for weeks too, and he's giving both of us a direction.*

"Understood," Ben said.

He walked to the window. Coruscant spread out below in its usual indifference — towers and transit lanes and the deep canyons between levels where the light never reached. He'd stood at windows like this his whole life and watched his father make decisions from behind desks. It had taken him years to understand that the desk wasn't distance. It was just how Luke worked.

"I can have a departure itinerary filed by morning," Ben said.

"There's no urgency on the timeline. Take a day to—"

"Morning is fine."

Behind him, the slight pause. He knew what was in it. The same thing that had been in every version of this conversation they'd had over the past three years — the offer that didn't quite become an offer, the question that didn't quite become a question, both of them too careful with each other to push past the professional frame they'd built.

Ben kept his eyes on the city.

His father's presence in the Force was right there, steady and warm and close enough that he could have reached for it the way he had at nine years old, at fourteen, at twenty-two when everything had been burning and Luke had been the one fixed point in a galaxy that kept rearranging itself. He didn't reach for it. He stood at the window and looked at Coruscant and kept his breathing even.

"The survey corridor," Ben said. "Is there anything in the preliminary data that suggests I should be looking for a specific origin point, or am I mapping the extent first?"

A beat.

"Map the extent first," Luke said. "If you find a concentration, you'll know it."

"Copy that."

He turned from the window. Luke was watching him with that expression — the one from the salle doorway, the one that had looked like a mission briefing from a distance and felt like something else up close. Ben picked up the datapad, tucked it under his arm.

"I'll send the itinerary to your queue," he said. "Anything else for the briefing?"

"No." Luke's voice was quiet. "That covers it."

Ben nodded once, the way a Knight nodded to the Grand Master when a mission had been assigned and the logistics were his to handle. Professional. Clean.

He crossed to the door.

"Ben."

He stopped.

"Be careful with the readings," Luke said. "If the absence is as localized as you're describing, I want to know the edges before you go looking for the center."

"Understood."

The door opened. He walked through it.

Neither of them had mentioned a partner. Luke hadn't pushed. Ben hadn't asked.

The door hissed shut behind him, and the corridor stretched ahead, and the flat note was still there, faint and persistent beneath everything, the same as it had been for six weeks.

He kept walking.
